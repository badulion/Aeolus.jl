Examples battery

Aeolus ships a three-tier battery of examples that together exercise every physics module in the stack. All examples live under examples/ and are runnable with

$ julia --project=examples examples/<tier>/<name>.jl

Outputs (SVG line plots + PPM heatmaps) land in examples/urban/output/.

The plotting stack is pure Julia (no C-library dependencies) — see examples/viz/plot_utils.jl. Convert the .ppm files to .png with ImageMagick if you want to view them in most browsers: convert file.ppm file.png.

Tier 3 — Flagship urban simulation

Historical section

This section describes examples/urban/urban_diurnal.jl, a second script that integrated the equations over the synthetic sample city. It was removed in #126: there is now one simulation, the Berlin case (see Real Berlin — Alexanderplatz OSM run and the Berlin case design page). The synthetic sample city runs through the same case with AEOLUS_SCENARIO=mitte_sample. The setup and numbers below are the old script's and are kept as a record.

examples/urban/urban_diurnal.jl (removed) — the "everything on" showcase over a real-format OSM city.

Setup

  • 1000 × 1000 × 200 m domain, 64 × 64 × 32 grid (dx ≈ 15.6 m)
  • Real-format OSM city geometry loaded from data/sample_berlin_mitte.osm — Berlin-Mitte-style perimeter block housing + several taller commercial buildings, at real geographic coordinates (52.522 N, 13.410 E). The OSM XML parser reads the same format as an Overpass-API download; the sample is bundled so the example runs without an internet fetch.
  • 136 buildings (height ≥ 15 m), 4 land-use patches (parks + water)
  • Latitude 52.5 N, day 172 (summer solstice), 24 h starting at 6:00 am
  • Physics on:
    • SolidMask + implicit IBM correction (implicit_ibm_correction!) in a post_predictor! hook — unconditionally stable for the dense obstacle field
    • 5-class LSM: roof / street / park / water / wall (wall = building-adjacent street cell with its own thermal parameters representing the effective vertical-face heat load)
    • Astronomical solar zenith + Beer's-law direct SW; walls get extra SW at low sun angles
    • Prescribed downward LW; free-atmosphere radiative cooling (−2 K/day)
    • Smagorinsky-Lilly SGS closure
    • Rayleigh sponge in the top 25 % of the domain
    • f-plane Coriolis (f = 1e-4 s⁻¹)
    • Geostrophic forcing (LargeScaleForcing since #171: a pressure gradient by default, Rayleigh nudging of the mean wind as an option)
    • Cosine-FFT pressure solver (walls in z, periodic in xy)
    • Moisture: prognostic qv as a passive tracer (advected by the same staggered scheme as θ), with Kessler microphysics processing any generated cloud / rain water
    • Pollutants: three chimney stacks with per-step injection and 1-h photolysis decay
    • Lagrangian particles: seeded near each stack every 5 minutes, tracked with trilinear velocity interpolation

Physics interpretation

  • Roofs peak first (14 h, 339 K) — low heat capacity + direct sun.
  • Streets peak later (18 h, 332 K) — thermal lag from higher C_s. By the next morning at 4 am they're still the warmest surface class, illustrating the classic nocturnal urban heat island.
  • Walls track streets closely (327 K peak at 18 h) — even more thermal lag because concrete/masonry stores more heat per unit area.
  • Parks stay cool all day (peak 316 K) — high heat capacity plus higher albedo.
  • Water is essentially flat (huge thermal inertia, low albedo).
  • At the peak of insolation, the street-vs-park contrast is 18 K — consistent with observed urban heat islands on clear summer days.

Numerical result

Divergence stays at machine ε (10⁻¹⁵) for all 43,200 time steps even with:

  • 136 IBM obstacles enforced implicitly,
  • surface heat flux injected per cell,
  • passive moisture undergoing Kessler processing,
  • 864 Lagrangian particles integrated to end of run,
  • a pollutant tracer with 1-h linear decay,
  • Coriolis + geostrophic wind + sponge damping + Smagorinsky SGS simultaneously active.

Design notes / current limitations

  • moisture = true on the simulation activates virtual-θ buoyancy — a small (~0.6 %) modification. On this coarse grid with dense obstacles it was mildly destabilizing, so the flagship keeps qv as a passive tracer instead (moisture = false on the sim) but still runs the Kessler microphysics on the qv/ql/qr fields. Users with finer grids or gentler forcing can flip this on.
  • ν = 2.0 is far above physical (~10⁻²). The bulk viscosity substitutes for a proper implicit-diffusion wall-model near obstacles. Real high-Re LES would need cut-cell IBM or a wall function.
  • dt = 2 s is CFL-limited by the ~15 m grid + ~2 m/s peak flow; finer grids need shorter dt (proportional to dx).
  • Snapshots are five static PPMs at fixed times, not an animated sequence. Convert to APNG or GIF externally if desired.

Animated multi-field playback

examples/urban/alexanderplatz_animation.jl is a pure renderer. It runs no physics at all: it reads the frame files written by alexanderplatz_diurnal.jl into output/frames/ and stitches an 8-panel mosaic into a single self-contained APNG that plays in any modern browser (Chrome, Firefox, Safari) or in macOS Preview.

$ julia --project=. examples/urban/alexanderplatz_diurnal.jl   # ~20 min
$ julia --project=. examples/urban/alexanderplatz_animation.jl # ~1.5 s
Why rendering is separate from running

These used to be two scripts that each ran their own independent 24-hour simulation, because capturing a frame needs the full state at that instant and it was easier to duplicate the loop than to save state. That was never a design decision, and it cost:

  • the physics had to be maintained twice;
  • the two copies drifted — one had soil conduction, latent heat, chemistry and aerosol, the other had none of them, while this page claimed both ran "the same physics";
  • that drift hid an energy-conservation bug in step_soil!, since the script exercising the soil model was not the script whose diagnostics were read;
  • every animation cost a second full integration, and re-colouring a panel meant re-running the physics.

Now there is one simulation. It writes 2-D slices (~295 kB per frame — only what a horizontal panel needs), and re-rendering takes 1.5 s instead of 50 minutes.

Panels (2 rows × 4 columns):

top rowbottom row
Surface temperatureHorizontal wind speed |uh|
Direct-SW shadow mask (bright = sunlit, dark = shaded)Pressure anomaly p' (z ≈ 30 m)
Potential-temp anomaly θ' (z ≈ 30 m)Pollutant tracer c (z ≈ 30 m)
Vertical velocity w (z ≈ 30 m)Sky-view factor (static geometric reference)

Each panel is colour-clamped to fixed limits so amplitudes stay visually comparable across frames. The elapsed simulation time (t 06.0h … t 06.0h wrapping through midnight) is printed on the mosaic.

Encoder details. No ffmpeg / ImageMagick / libpng dependency — examples/viz/apng.jl is a ~200-line pure-Julia encoder that writes uncompressed-DEFLATE PNGs and stitches them into APNG animation chunks (acTL / fcTL / fdAT). File sizes are ~2× a normally compressed PNG but the encoder has zero external deps and runs identically on NixOS, macOS, Windows.

Viewing. Open the file in any modern browser — the APNG loops automatically. To convert to a smaller file, use apngasm (once installed): apngasm output.gif input.png (auto-detects APNG → falls back to a GIF).

Building your own animation. The apng.jl module exposes three public entry points you can compose:

  • write_apng(path, frames; delay_ms, loop) — write RGB frames (Array{UInt8, 3} each) to disk.
  • scalar_to_rgb(Z, vmin, vmax; upscale) — turn a 2-D scalar array into a viridis-mapped RGB panel.
  • tile_frames(panels) — combine a matrix of panels into one bigger RGB frame.
  • add_label!(rgb, text; row, col) — a minimal 5×7 bitmap font for time-stamps and titles (ASCII 0-9 A-Z a-z .-:_ /).

Real Berlin — Alexanderplatz OSM run

examples/urban/alexanderplatz_diurnal.jl is the simulation — the only script in examples/urban that integrates the equations. Since #126 it is a few lines over the BerlinCase module (examples/urban/BerlinCase.jl): build_case(CaseConfig(:alexanderplatz)) then run_case!, with every physics coupling in its own file under examples/urban/couplings/. The Berlin case design page describes the registry, the stage loop and how to add a coupling. AEOLUS_NSTEPS sets the number of steps and AEOLUS_OUTPUT_DT the seconds between NetCDF records. It runs the full physics against data/alexanderplatz_real.osm — an actual Overpass-API download of a 1 km × 1 km box around Alexanderplatz, Berlin (52.518–52.526 N, 13.402–13.418 E). To (re-)fetch:

$ julia --project=examples examples/urban/data/fetch_alexanderplatz.jl

(Uses the public overpass-api.de/api/interpreter endpoint. Retry if the server is busy — the fetch script does not currently auto-retry.)

Now with ray-marched building shadows and sky-view-factor-weighted diffuse SW and LW (post-Phase-20 items R1 and R2 in the refinement plan):

  • At each step the per-cell shadow mask is refreshed by casting a ray from each cell's top-of-stack toward the sun. Ray marching uses the same SolidMask the IBM uses, so buildings cast physically-correct shadows.
  • Diffuse SW is scaled by the pre-computed sky-view factor (SVF); LW down blends 320 W/m² · svf + 380 W/m² · (1 - svf) to model the warm LW emitted by surrounding walls in urban canyons.
  • The physical pattern now reads correctly:
    • Roofs (open exposure) peak highest at noon (339 K)
    • Streets have thermal lag → peak at 18h (333 K)
    • Walls track streets but with more radiative trapping at night
    • Parks stay cool all day (albedo + C_s)
    • Nocturnal urban heat island: at 4 am streets are the warmest class (303.5 K) while roofs are the coldest (296.6 K, they radiate to open sky).

Notable geometry:

  • 322 real Berlin building footprints (out of 373 in the raw OSM extract; the example filters min_height ≥ 8 m to skip 0-m artefacts).
  • Median building height ~12 m (typical residential); tallest is the Fernsehturm at 368 m — clipped to the 200 m domain top.
  • 511 park-tagged cells and 2 water cells (Alexanderplatz has generous open plazas + surrounding green space).
  • Same qualitative physical result as the synthetic sample: 18 K street-vs-park contrast; divergence at machine precision throughout.

Outputs land in examples/urban/output/alexanderplatz_*: heights.ppm / class.ppm (geometry), surfT_XXh.ppm (5 snapshots), pollutant_XXh.ppm, particles_final.ppm, class_surfT.svg, sw_forcing.svg, final_theta.svg, surfT_final.ppm.

Ground: the production soil column

Since #129 the soil column is no longer under a slab: each street, building-adjacent and park column's surface is a SoilThermalColumn, stepped by the Berlin case's surface exchange with Monin–Obukhov fluxes from the first air cell, with its soil water (hydrology = true), so β follows the top layer. Roofs, water and glass are Radiation.SlabLand surfaces. UrbanSoil / step_soil! and couplings/soil_urban.jl are gone; couplings/ground_soil.jl registers the soil columns. See docs/src/design/berlin_surface_radiation.md for the design and the measured effect. The rest of this section describes the #18 arrangement and its measurements.

Below each non-water cell's surface slab the run carried one SoilThermalColumn from Aeolus.SoilColumn (Phase 46, #18). It replaces a three-layer explicit soil that was written into the example. The example's UrbanSoil / step_soil! (in couplings/soil_urban.jl) only couples that column to the slab, and does no soil physics of its own:

  • The column. It uses the 10 CLM2–4.5 exponential layers down to 3.43 m, with the top node at 7 mm. The soil is a moist loam at θ = 0.20 (k = 1.64 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹, C = 2.10 MJ m⁻³ K⁻¹), and the base is held at 288 K. Soil water is not stepped.
  • The coupling. The slab (Ts, C_s) reaches the soil surface through a contact conductance h = 6 W m⁻² K⁻¹, the skin coupling of the old scheme. Folding the slab into LinearizedSkinBalance(0, -1/(1/h + Δt/C_s), Ts) makes the exchange implicit, and the slab loses exactly the ground heat flux the soil gains.
  • Sub-cycling. The exchange runs every 15 dynamics steps (30 s). The 24 h run takes about 9 % longer.
  • New output. The NetCDF output gains T_soil1 (top-layer soil temperature) and G_soil (ground heat flux).

alexanderplatz_surface_diagnostics.jl summarises a run, or compares two runs, from the NetCDF alone. It reports per-class means for sunset at 19:30 local:

The diurnal cycle is essentially unchanged. The slab–soil exchange is limited by the contact conductance in both schemes, and the production column differs mainly in resolving the upper centimetres of the soil:

  • The street top-layer soil temperature swings through 13.5 K.
  • Street G peaks at 148 W m⁻² into the ground and reaches −5 W m⁻² at night.

What changes is what the ground can represent: moisture-dependent conductivity, frozen ground with latent heat, and a column that the land-surface model (#21) can share.

Adding your own city

Two ingest formats are supported.

GeoJSON

The minimal schema is:

{ "type": "FeatureCollection",
  "features": [
    { "type": "Feature",
      "properties": {"height": 60, "class": "roof"},
      "geometry": {"type": "Polygon",
                   "coordinates": [[ [x, y], [x, y], ... ]]}},
    ...
  ]
}

Coordinates are meters relative to the mesh origin. Recognised class values: roof, street, park, water. See examples/urban/data/city.geojson for a working example, and examples/urban/core/geometry.jl for the loader / rasterizer (examples/urban/core/osm_ingest.jl for OSM XML).

OSM XML

The load_city_osm(path; lat0, lon0, x_shift, y_shift, min_height, default_height) function reads a subset of standard OSM XML:

  • <node id lat lon /> for coordinates,
  • <way id>…<nd ref/>…<tag k=… v=…/></way> for polygons.

Ways tagged building=… become buildings with height taken from a height or building:levels tag (fallback default_height). Ways tagged leisure=park, landuse=grass|forest|meadow, natural=water, water=lake|pond|river become surface patches.

Latitude/longitude coordinates are projected to local meters via equirectangular projection anchored at (lat0, lon0); x_shift / y_shift re-center the extract inside the mesh; min_height filters short buildings for stability tuning.

The bundled data/sample_berlin_mitte.osm was produced by data/generate_osm_sample.jl — a plausible European perimeter-block neighbourhood at Alexanderplatz coordinates. Genuine Overpass-API downloads are consumed by the same parser.
