Leaf photosynthesis

Transpiration is the largest latent-heat flux over vegetated land, and stomata open to let CO₂ in. Before the land surface can make evaporation respond to light, CO₂, humidity and soil water, it needs the rate at which a leaf uses CO₂. This page develops that rate as implemented in src/Photosynthesis.jl; stomatal conductance, the land column and the resolved canopy build on it.

1. Three limitations of a C3 leaf

Farquhar, von Caemmerer & Berry (1980) describe C3 photosynthesis as the smallest of three biochemical capacities. Rubisco either carboxylates RuBP (fixing CO₂) or oxygenates it (photorespiration, releasing half a CO₂ per oxygenation). The ratio of the two is set by the CO₂ and O₂ at the enzyme, so net carboxylation vanishes at the photorespiratory compensation point

\[\Gamma^* = \frac{o_i}{2\,S_{c/o}},\]

with $S_{c/o}$ the Rubisco specificity factor. $\Gamma^*$ is proportional to O₂, which is why low-O₂ air raises C3 photosynthesis (the Warburg effect).

Rubisco-limited — enough RuBP, too little enzyme:

\[A_c = V_{cmax}\,\frac{c_i - \Gamma^*}{c_i + K_c(1 + o_i/K_o)} .\]

RuBP-regeneration (light)-limited — the electron transport rate $J$ limits how fast RuBP is regenerated; four electrons per carboxylation and eight per oxygenation give

\[A_j = J\,\frac{c_i - \Gamma^*}{4 c_i + 8 \Gamma^*}, \qquad \Theta J^2 - (I + J_{max}) J + I J_{max} = 0, \quad I = \tfrac12 \Phi_{PSII} Q,\]

$J$ being the smaller root: linear in absorbed photons $Q$ at low light (one photon per photosystem per electron, hence the ½), saturating at $J_{max}$.

Export (TPU)-limited — the chloroplast cannot export triose phosphate faster than $T_p$: $A_p = 3T_p$.

Net assimilation subtracts mitochondrial respiration in the light, $A_n = A - R_d$.

TPU with CLM defaults. $A_j$ is bounded by $J_{max}/4$ ($c_i \to \infty$). CLM sets $T_{p25} = 0.167 V_{cmax25}$ so that $3T_p = 0.501 V_{cmax}$, and $J_{max25} = 1.97 V_{cmax25}$, so $J_{max}/4 = 0.4925 V_{cmax} < 3T_p$: at 25 °C the default C3 leaf is never TPU-limited. TPU limitation appears when $J_{max25}/V_{cmax25} > 2.004$, e.g. for a Kattge-Knorr leaf grown below ~ 16 °C.

2. Co-limitation

The transitions between limitations are not sharp in real leaves. Following Collatz et al. (1991) and CLM, the gross rate is a nested smooth minimum, each the smaller root of a quadratic,

\[\Theta_{cj} A_i^2 - (A_c + A_j) A_i + A_c A_j = 0,\qquad \Theta_{ip} A^2 - (A_i + A_p) A + A_i A_p = 0,\]

with $\Theta_{cj} = 0.98$, $\Theta_{ip} = 0.95$ (C3). $\Theta = 1$ recovers the Farquhar minimum and $\Theta = 0$ the rectangular hyperbola. The root is evaluated as $2ab/[(a+b) + \sqrt{(a+b)^2 - 4\Theta ab}]$, free of cancellation.

Because this smooth minimum is homogeneous of degree one, the C3 rate is written $A_i = (c_i - \Gamma^*)\,\mathrm{colim}(V_{cmax}/(c_i+K_m),\, J/(4c_i+8\Gamma^*))$. Above $\Gamma^*$ this is identical to CLM; below it, it continues the curve to the physically correct negative gross rate (photorespiration exceeding carboxylation), where CLM clips.

3. C4 leaves

C4 plants concentrate CO₂ around Rubisco with PEP carboxylase, which removes photorespiration and saturates the leaf near ambient CO₂. Collatz et al. (1992) replace the three rates with

\[A_c = V_{cmax},\qquad A_j = \alpha Q,\qquad A_p = k_p\,c_i ,\]

co-limited with $\Theta_{cj} = 0.80$, $\Theta_{ip} = 0.95$ (CLM) or 0.83 / 0.93 (the paper). The CO₂ compensation point $R_d/k_p$ is ≈ 1.25 µmol mol⁻¹ for CLM parameters, against ≈ 54 for C3.

4. Temperature

Michaelis constants and $\Gamma^*$ follow Arrhenius functions with the activation energies of Bernacchi et al. (2001), $f(T) = \exp[\Delta H_a(1 - T_{25}/T)/(R T_{25})]$. Capacities ($V_{cmax}$, $J_{max}$, $T_p$, $R_d$) additionally deactivate at high temperature:

\[f_H(T) = \frac{1 + \exp[(T_{25}\Delta S - \Delta H_d)/(R T_{25})]} {1 + \exp[(T\,\Delta S - \Delta H_d)/(R T)]}, \qquad T_{opt} = \frac{\Delta H_d}{\Delta S - R\ln[\Delta H_a/(\Delta H_d - \Delta H_a)]}.\]

Two parameter sets are provided. Bonan et al. (2011) (CLM4.5 Table 8.2) does not acclimate; its $V_{cmax}$ optimum is 33.3 °C. Kattge & Knorr (2007) lets the entropy term follow the growth temperature $t_g$ (°C, clamped to 11–35), $\Delta S = 668.39 - 1.07 t_g$ for $V_{cmax}$ and $659.70 - 0.75 t_g$ for $J_{max}$, and lowers $J_{max25}/V_{cmax25} = 2.59 - 0.035 t_g$; the $V_{cmax}$ optimum moves from 29.2 °C (grown at 11 °C) to 41.5 °C (grown at 35 °C).

A caveat worth knowing. The deactivation term of the Bonan set is not negligible at 25 °C — it already removes 13 % of $V_{cmax}$ there — and normalising to 25 °C hides that. The peaked curve therefore lies 10–15 % above Bernacchi's pure Arrhenius fit between 10 and 20 °C and falls below it above 25 °C (−18 % at 30 °C). The activation energies agree; the shapes are different by construction.

C4 capacities use CLM's $Q_{10} = 2$ with low- and high-temperature inhibition (CLM4.5 eqs. 8.12–8.14), not renormalised at 25 °C (the C4 $V_{cmax}$ factor there is 0.871); the optimum is 36.2 °C.

5. Plant functional types

CLM45_PFT_PHOTOSYNTHESIS carries CLM4.5 Table 8.1: pathway, canopy-top $V_{cmax25}$, leaf C:N, fraction of leaf N in Rubisco and specific leaf area for 14 natural and 5 crop types (rainfed rows; the irrigated rows are identical). $V_{cmax25}$ in that table is itself derived, $V_{cmax25} = F_{LNR} F_{NR} a_{R25} / (CN_L\,SLA_0)$; recomputing it from the other columns reproduces every row to within 0.1 %, which is how the transcription is checked.
