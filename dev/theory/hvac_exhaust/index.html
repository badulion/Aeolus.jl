HVAC demand and exhaust heat

An air-conditioned building does not remove heat from the city. It moves the heat outdoors and adds the compressor work: every joule of cooling leaves the condenser as $1 + 1/COP$ joules. The hotter the street, the larger the cooling load and the lower the COP. Both push more heat into the street. This is the air-conditioning feedback that amplifies urban heat islands (Salamanca et al. 2014; de Munck et al. 2013). src/BuildingEnergy.jl (issue #32) closes this loop for the zone model of Building energy: indoor air and thermal mass.

1. Ideal loads from the affine zone step

step_zone! solves the zone implicitly. Every column is stepped twice, at the old air temperature $x_0$ and at $x_0 + 1$ K, so the air balance is one linear equation

\[\mathcal{L}\,(x - x_0) = R + Φ_{hvac},\]

where $\mathcal{L}$ collects $C_a/Δt$, the surface, glazing and ventilation conductances, and $R$ collects everything else. The free-floating end-of-step temperature is $x_f = x_0 + R/\mathcal{L}$. An IdealLoadsHVAC then supplies

\[Φ_{req} = \begin{cases} \mathcal{L}(T_{heat} - x_0) - R & x_f < T_{heat},\\ \mathcal{L}(T_{cool} - x_0) - R & x_f > T_{cool},\\ 0 & \text{otherwise}, \end{cases} \qquad Φ_{hvac} = \mathrm{clamp}(Φ_{req}, -Q_{cap}, Q_{heat,cap}).\]

This is exact, not iterated. When the capacity suffices, the air temperature ends the step on the setpoint to round-off. The shortfall $Φ_{req} - Φ_{hvac}$ is reported as Q_unmet. ISO 13790 §C.4.2 does the same for the 5R1C model by interpolating between a free step and a 10 W m⁻² step (iso13790_ideal_load!). Both are linear, so the interpolation is exact there too.

Humidity. When ZoneForcing carries the outdoor specific humidity, the zone vapour balance is advanced implicitly after the temperature:

\[M\frac{q^{n+1}-q^n}{Δt} = M n (q_{out} - q^{n+1}) + \frac{Φ_{lat}}{L_v} + W_{hvac},\]

with $M = ρV$ and $n$ the total outdoor-air change rate. Heat recovery is sensible only, and hygroscopic buffering is not modelled. With dehumidify = true, $W_{hvac} < 0$ removes the vapour that would raise the relative humidity above RH_max at the new air temperature. The coil load is then $Q_{cool} = Q_s + L_v |W_{hvac}|$. Capacity goes to the sensible load first. Re-heat is not modelled.

2. Equipment performance

Cooling is a direct-expansion unit in the DOE-2 / EnergyPlus Coil:Cooling:DX:SingleSpeed form. Two biquadratic modifiers, normalised to 1 at the AHRI 210/240 "A" point (26.7 °C dry bulb / 19.4 °C wet bulb indoors, 35 °C outdoors), scale the rated values:

\[COP = \frac{COP_{rated}}{EIR_{FT}(T_{wb,i}, T_{c,i})},\qquad Q_{cap} = Q_{rated}\,CAP_{FT}(T_{wb,i}, T_{c,i}).\]

Here $T_{wb,i}$ is the thermodynamic wet bulb of the zone air, from MoistThermodynamics.wet_bulb_temperature. $T_{c,i}$ is the air entering the condenser. For an air-cooled condenser it is the outdoor dry bulb. For an evaporatively cooled condenser it is (EnergyPlus Engineering Reference)

\[T_{c,i} = T_{wb,o} + (1-ε)(T_{db,o} - T_{wb,o}),\qquad ε = 0.9 \text{ by default}.\]

Two published curve sets are shipped, both copied verbatim from the EnergyPlus test files. DX_CURVES_WINDOW_AC comes from WindACAuto.idf and DX_CURVES_PACKAGED_AC from HeatPumpAuto.idf. The operating point is taken from the start-of-step state. Part-load degradation and fan power are not modelled.

Heating can be a boiler (fuel $Q/η$, $η = 0.9$ by default), electric resistance, or a heat pump with a constant $COP_h$.

3. Exhaust heat

The plant obeys the first law:

\[Q_{exh,s} + Q_{exh,l} = P_{heat} + P_{cool} + Q_{cool} - Q_{heat}.\]

  • Cooling. The condenser rejects $Q_{cool}(1 + 1/COP)$. An air-cooled condenser rejects all of it as sensible heat. An evaporative condenser or cooling tower rejects a fraction latent_fraction (default 0.85) as evaporated water, $W_{evap} = Q_{exh,l}/L_v$. The 0.85 is the cooling-tower rule of thumb: evaporation of 0.00085 of the circulating flow per °F of range, against 0.001 if every joule went into evaporation. Published values span 60–95 %. This split is not the one EnergyPlus uses. The COP follows the EnergyPlus pre-cooling-pad condenser, whose water use is $\dot m_{cond}\,Δw$ across the pad and scales with the condenser air flow, not with the rejected heat. The latent/sensible split follows the wet-tower rule. So $W_{evap}$ is an estimate within that band. The total $Q_{exh,s} + Q_{exh,l}$ does not depend on the split.
  • Heating. A boiler's flue loss $Q(1/η - 1)$ is sensible exhaust. A heat pump's $-Q(1 - 1/COP_h)$ is negative: it cools the outdoor air.

HVACBudget reports these terms separately so that the canopy coupling (#29, Anthropogenic heat from the building energy model) can place them: anthropogenic_heat_maps puts them at the building's roofs.

4. The feedback loop

Take a canyon air volume exchanging heat with the rural air $T_r$ through a conductance $K$, with background heating $Q_0$. Surround it with a zone of heat-loss coefficient $H$ and internal gain $G$, cooled to $T_{set}$ by an air-cooled unit at constant COP. The steady canyon is

\[K(T_c - T_r) = Q_0 + (1 + 1/COP)\bigl(H(T_c - T_{set}) + G\bigr) \;\Rightarrow\; T_c = \frac{T_r + [Q_0 + (1+1/COP)(G - H T_{set})]/K}{1 - g},\quad g = \frac{(1 + 1/COP) H}{K}.\]

The exhaust-induced warming is therefore amplified by $1/(1-g)$ over the open-loop estimate. A temperature-dependent COP adds $Q_{cool}\,\partial(1/COP)/\partial T_c$ to the loop gain.

Not validated against observations. No metered building or measured COP data were used. The equipment behaviour is that of the published EnergyPlus curves, and the feedback is checked against its own linear theory.
