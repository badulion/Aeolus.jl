P3 microphysics

The Predicted Particle Properties (P3) scheme of Morrison & Milbrandt (2015) represents all ice by a single category whose bulk properties — rime fraction, rime density, and hence the mass–dimension relation — are prognosed rather than fixed by assigning particles to "cloud ice", "snow", "graupel" or "hail". This page develops the pieces as they are implemented in Aeolus.P3Microphysics (Phase 40); the API is in Clouds and microphysics.

Gamma size distributions and their moments

Every hydrometeor population in P3 is a complete gamma distribution in maximum dimension $D$,

\[N(D) = N_0\, D^{\mu}\, e^{-\lambda D},\]

with intercept $N_0$ (m$^{-4-\mu}$ for number per m³ of air; kg$^{-1}$m$^{-1-\mu}$ for number per kg of air, as P3 carries it), shape $\mu$ and slope $\lambda$ (m$^{-1}$). Its $k$-th moment is

\[M_k = \int_0^\infty D^k N(D)\,dD = N_0\,\frac{\Gamma(\mu+k+1)}{\lambda^{\mu+k+1}} ,\]

so the number is $M_0$, the mass of spheres of density $\rho$ is $(\pi/6)\rho M_3$, and the Rayleigh reflectivity factor of liquid spheres is $M_6$. Given two moments $M_i, M_j$ and a fixed $\mu$, the slope follows from their ratio,

\[\lambda = \left[\frac{M_i}{M_j}\, \frac{\Gamma(\mu+j+1)}{\Gamma(\mu+i+1)}\right]^{1/(j-i)},\]

and $N_0 = M_0 \lambda^{\mu+1}/\Gamma(\mu+1)$.

Truncated moments

P3's mass–dimension relation $m(D)$ is a piecewise power law, with different prefactors and exponents on either side of the thresholds $D_\mathrm{th}$, $D_\mathrm{gr}$, $D_\mathrm{cr}$. Every bulk integral therefore splits into moments truncated to one regime:

\[\int_{D_1}^{D_2} D^k N(D)\,dD = N_0\,\frac{\Gamma(a)}{\lambda^{a}} \left[P(a,\lambda D_2) - P(a,\lambda D_1)\right], \qquad a = \mu+k+1,\]

where $P(a,x) = \gamma(a,x)/\Gamma(a)$ is the regularised lower incomplete gamma function and $Q = 1 - P$ its complement. When the interval sits in the upper tail ($\lambda D_1 > a$) the difference is formed as $Q(a,\lambda D_1) - Q(a,\lambda D_2)$ so that a small tail contribution does not vanish in the cancellation of two numbers near one.

Numerical evaluation

SpecialFunctions does not build on every target, so the functions are implemented in-package following Press et al. (2007, Numerical Recipes 3rd ed., §6.1–6.2):

  • $\ln\Gamma(x)$ by the 14-term Lanczos approximation for $x \ge 1/2$ and Euler's reflection $\Gamma(x)\Gamma(1-x) = \pi/\sin \pi x$ below; $\Gamma$ at positive integers is the exact factorial product.
  • $P(a,x)$ by its power series for $x < a+1$ and $Q(a,x)$ by its continued fraction, evaluated with the modified Lentz algorithm, otherwise. The other value is the complement. The tail that can become very small ($P$ for $x \ll a$, $Q$ for $x \gg a$) is therefore always computed directly and keeps its relative accuracy. One limitation: for $a < 1$ and $x < 1$, $Q$ is of order $a$ and is formed as $1-P$. Its relative error is then about $\epsilon/Q$: 1.8e-12 at $a = 10^{-3}$, 4.2e-9 at $a = 10^{-6}$. P3 moments have $a = \mu+k+1 \ge 1$, so they never reach this range.

Truncated moments are clamped at zero. Over an interval only a few ulp wide, $P(a,x_2) - P(a,x_1)$ is pure rounding and could otherwise come out negative, down to $-1.5\times10^{-14} M_k$.

Mass–dimension relation and predicted rime density

P3 carries four ice prognostics per kg of air: total ice mass $q_i$, number $N_i$, rime mass $q_\mathrm{rim}$ and rime volume $B_\mathrm{rim}$. Two ratios follow,

\[F_\mathrm{rim} = \frac{q_\mathrm{rim}}{q_i}, \qquad \rho_\mathrm{rim} = \frac{q_\mathrm{rim}}{B_\mathrm{rim}} ,\]

with $\rho_\mathrm{rim}$ bounded to 50–900 kg m$^{-3}$. Carrying the volume means density is never combined by a rule: accreting rime mass $\delta q$ at density $\rho_\mathrm{new}$ adds $\delta q/\rho_\mathrm{new}$ to $B_\mathrm{rim}$, and the result is exactly the mass-weighted harmonic mean of the parts.

with $\rho_i = 917$ kg m$^{-3}$, $\alpha_{va} = 0.0121$ kg m$^{-\beta}$, $\beta_{va} = 1.9$ (Brown & Francis 1995, as corrected by Hogan et al. 2012) and $\gamma = 0.1315$, $\sigma = 1.88$ (Mitchell 1996). For unrimed ice only the first two regimes exist. Each threshold is where the neighbouring masses agree, so $m(D)$ is continuous:

\[D_\mathrm{th} = \left(\frac{\pi\rho_i}{6\alpha_{va}}\right)^{1/(\beta_{va}-3)} \approx 66\ \mu\mathrm{m},\quad D_\mathrm{gr} = \left(\frac{6\alpha_{va}}{\pi\rho_g}\right)^{1/(3-\beta_{va})},\quad D_\mathrm{cr} = \left(\frac{6\alpha_{va}}{\pi\rho_g(1-F_\mathrm{rim})}\right)^{1/(3-\beta_{va})}.\]

The area is not continuous at the thresholds (the disc and aggregate relations are independent fits), so neither is the single-particle fall speed; this is as in Morrison & Milbrandt (2015).

Graupel density in closed form

Graupel is rime plus the unrimed mass that filled in around it, $\rho_g = F_\mathrm{rim}\rho_\mathrm{rim} + (1-F_\mathrm{rim})\rho_d$, where $\rho_d$ is the mean aggregate density over $[D_\mathrm{gr}, D_\mathrm{cr}]$. Morrison & Milbrandt, and the P3 reference code, solve this coupled system by fixed-point iteration. It is linear in $\rho_g$: with $r = D_\mathrm{cr}/D_\mathrm{gr} = (1-F_\mathrm{rim})^{-1/(3-\beta)}$, $\rho_d = g\,\rho_g$ where

\[g = \frac{r^{\beta-2}-1}{(\beta-2)(r-1)} \quad\Rightarrow\quad \rho_g = \frac{F_\mathrm{rim}\,\rho_\mathrm{rim}}{1-(1-F_\mathrm{rim})\,g}.\]

p3_mass_dimension evaluates this directly, summing the numerator's series for small $\ln r$ because its $O(1)$ and $O(\ln r)$ terms cancel exactly, and with the common factor $\ln r$ divided out analytically so that nothing underflows even at subnormal $F_\mathrm{rim}$. It agrees with a 256-bit fixed-point iteration to 5.1e-16. Two limits follow. As $F_\mathrm{rim}\to 0^+$, $\rho_g \to \tfrac23\rho_\mathrm{rim}$ for any $\beta$, and the graupel regime shrinks to zero width. At $F_\mathrm{rim} = 1$, $\rho_g = \rho_\mathrm{rim}$ and $D_\mathrm{cr} = \infty$. Because $g \le 1$, $\rho_g \le \rho_\mathrm{rim} \le \rho_i$, which gives the ordering $D_\mathrm{th} \le D_\mathrm{gr} \le D_\mathrm{cr}$.

Size distribution and slope

The mean particle mass of $N(D)\propto D^\mu e^{-\lambda D}$ is a sum of one truncated gamma moment per regime (see above), so it is exact to incomplete-gamma accuracy. It decreases strictly in $\lambda$, and p3_slope inverts it for $\lambda$ given $q_i/N_i$ with $\mu$ fixed (regula falsi in $\log\lambda$). The round trip recovers $\lambda$ to 1.1e-14 over 225 cases. MM15 diagnose $\mu$ from $\lambda$ (Heymsfield 2003); that coupling is left to the caller.

Fall speed

Single-particle fall speeds follow Mitchell & Heymsfield (2005), the formulation behind the P3 lookup tables. From the Best number $X = 2 m g \rho_a D^2/(A\eta^2)$,

\[Re = \frac{\delta_0^2}{4}\left[\left(1+\frac{4\sqrt{X}}{\delta_0^2\sqrt{C_0}}\right)^{1/2}-1\right]^2 - a_0X^{b_0}, \qquad V = \frac{\eta\,Re}{\rho_a D},\]

with $\delta_0 = 5.83$, $C_0 = 0.6$ and $\eta$ from Sutherland's law. The turbulence correction $a_0X^{b_0}$ is off by default ($a_0 = b_0 = 0$), as in the P3 reference lookup-table code. MH05 fit $a_0 = 1.7\times10^{-3}$, $b_0 = 0.8$ to aggregates; applied to dense graupel it outgrows the first term, and 900 kg m$^{-3}$ graupel would peak at 11 m s$^{-1}$ near 2 cm and fall at zero speed from 5 cm. Without it, $V(D)$ of such graupel rises monotonically (14.4 m s$^{-1}$ at 1 cm, 33.8 m s$^{-1}$ at 5 cm, 600 hPa). The price is the known Stokes-limit bias of the MH05 fit, $Re \to X/(\delta_0^2C_0)$, 17.7 % above $X/24$. The bulk number- and mass-weighted speeds $V_N$, $V_M$ are integrated by composite Gauss–Legendre quadrature, with panels split at the regime thresholds.

Continuity: no category jump

The point of P3 is that bulk properties change continuously as ice rimes. We sweep $F_\mathrm{rim}$ from 0 to 1 at $\rho_\mathrm{rim}$ = 50, 400 and 900 kg m$^{-3}$, both at fixed $\lambda$ and at fixed $q_i/N_i$. The largest relative step in mean mass, bulk density or $\lambda$ between neighbouring samples is 1.6e-2 at $\Delta F = 10^{-3}$ and 1.6e-3 at $\Delta F = 10^{-4}$: it shrinks in proportion to the spacing, so the curves have no jump. The $\rho_\mathrm{rim}$ sweep behaves the same way (1.5e-2 and 1.5e-3), and so do $V_N$, $V_M$ (1.1e-2 and 1.1e-3 at $\Delta F$ = 2.5e-3 and 2.5e-4). At fixed $q_i/N_i = 10^{-7}$ kg, full riming ($\rho_\mathrm{rim} = 400$) raises $V_M$ from 1.31 to 3.02 m s$^{-1}$, the snow-to-graupel transition without a category boundary.

Process rates

Each microphysical process is written as one non-negative rate that moves mass between exactly two species (vapour $q_v$, cloud $q_c$, rain $q_r$, ice $q_i$, with the rime $q_\mathrm{rim}$ a part of $q_i$). p3_tendencies maps the rates linearly onto every prognostic variable, so total water is conserved identically, and the heating is fixed by which phases a rate connects:

\[c_{pm}\frac{dT}{dt} = -L_v\frac{dq_v}{dt} + L_f\frac{dq_i}{dt},\]

that is $L_s = L_v + L_f$ for deposition, $L_f$ for riming, $-L_f$ for melting and nothing for autoconversion or shedding. Latent heats, saturation vapour pressures, $c_{pm}$ and the (isobaric, enthalpy-conserving) saturation adjustment are those of the MoistThermodynamics contract; vapour diffusivity, thermal conductivity, the liquid growth resistances and the drop fall speed are those of Superdroplet; the KK2000 autoconversion is AerosolCloud.autoconversion_rate. The P3 part-file adds only the integrals over the ice and rain size distributions and the bookkeeping of number and rime.

Condensation

Cloud water is condensed or evaporated by the contract's process saturation adjustment, MoistThermodynamics.isobaric_saturation_adjustment: vapour and cloud water exchange mass at constant pressure and constant moist enthalpy $h$ (moist_enthalpy) until the parcel is exactly saturated over liquid, or until all cloud has evaporated. Cloud water stays supercooled liquid below 0 °C and becomes ice only by explicit processes. Rain and ice are passed as non-adjusting condensate: their mass does not change, but they are part of $c_{pm}$, so the heating satisfies exactly

\[c_{pm}(q_\text{final})\,\Delta T = L_v(T_0)\,\Delta q_c ,\]

with $c_{pm}$ including $q_r$ and $q_i$. The tests measure this ratio at $1 \pm 10^{-12}$ at 1000, 900 and 600 hPa, and $|\Delta h|/h < 10^{-14}$. With 5 g kg$^{-1}$ of rain (ice) present, $\Delta T$ per unit condensate is 1.56 % (0.54 %) lower than it would be if they were left out of $c_{pm}$.

Before issue #82 this step used the θli diagnosis `MoistThermodynamics.saturationadjustment, which conserves the contract's approximate\theta_{li}(its Exner exponent follows the partition) rather thanh, and passed onlyq_v + q_c. That gavec_{pm}\Delta T/(L_v \Delta q_c)= 0.998 at 1000 hPa, 1.012 at 900 hPa and 1.064 at 600 hPa, and it left rain and ice out ofc_{pm}. The two contract functions are different operations. Condensation during a step is a process and conservesh. RecoveringTfrom a prognostic\theta_{li}is a diagnosis and has to invert\theta_{li}`.

Warm rain

The warm-rain processes follow Khairoutdinov & Kogan (2000), the formulation P3 uses:

\[\left.\frac{\partial q_r}{\partial t}\right|_\mathrm{auto} = 1350\,q_c^{2.47}N_c^{-1.79}, \qquad \left.\frac{\partial q_r}{\partial t}\right|_\mathrm{acc} = 67\,(q_c q_r)^{1.15},\]

($N_c$ in cm$^{-3}$). Autoconversion is off below $q_c = 10^{-8}$ kg kg$^{-1}$ and creates drizzle drops of 25 μm radius. The measured exponents are 2.470000000000 and −1.790000000000. Rain self-collection and breakup follow Seifert & Beheng (2001) with the Seifert (2008) breakup efficiency, as in the P3 code: $\partial N_r/\partial t = -\varphi\,k_{rr}\rho_a q_r N_r$ with $k_{rr} = 5.78$ and $\varphi = 2 - \exp(2300(D_m - 280\,\mu m))$ above 280 μm. Breakup balances self-collection at $D_m = 581.4$ μm.

Diffusional growth with ventilation

A particle of capacitance $C$ grows as $dm/dt = 4\pi C f_v S/(A+B)$. Here $S$ is the supersaturation over water (rain) or ice, and $A$, $B$ are the heat-conduction and vapour-diffusion resistances. For ice, $C = D/2$ for spheres and graupel and $0.48\,D/2$ for nonspherical ice. The ventilation coefficient $f_v(Sc^{1/3}Re^{1/2})$ is Hall & Pruppacher (1976) for ice and Beard & Pruppacher (1971) for drops, in their piecewise forms, which keep $f_v \ge 1$. The population rate $N\,4\pi\,E_N[C f_v]\,S/(A+B)$ is integrated over the P3 PSD, with $Re$ from the Mitchell–Heymsfield fall speed.

The checks behave as expected. Small ice spheres (mean 3.3 μm) reproduce the analytic $N 2\pi(\mu+1)/\lambda \cdot S/(A+B)$ to 1.5e-4. Snow with a 0.46 mm mean size is ventilated by a factor of 2.32. Near-monodisperse 40 μm drops evaporate at 1.0076 times the unventilated single-drop rate. Ice grows by deposition only below 0 °C and when $S_i > 0$. Sublimation occurs only when $S_i < 0$ and leaves the rime fraction and density unchanged. Rain evaporates only when $S_w < 0$.

Riming, shedding and Cober & List rime density

Ice collects cloud water with the geometric kernel of the ice particle, $\partial q_c/\partial t = -E_{ci}\rho_a q_c N_i E_N[AV]$, and rain with $A|V_i - V_r|$. Below 0 °C the collected water becomes rime: $q_\mathrm{rim}$ grows, and $B_\mathrm{rim}$ grows by $dq/\rho_\mathrm{rime}$. At or above 0 °C, collected cloud water is shed as 1 mm rain drops. The density of cloud rime follows Cober & List (1993) in the P3 form, as a function of the Macklin parameter $R_i = -r_c[\mu m]\,V_\mathrm{impact}/T_s[°C]$:

\[\rho_\mathrm{rim} = \begin{cases} (0.051 + 0.114R_i - 0.0055R_i^2)\times10^3 & 1 \le R_i \le 8\\ 611 + 72.25(R_i - 8) & 8 < R_i \le 12 \end{cases}\]

The formula is continuous at $R_i = 8$ (611 kg m$^{-3}$). It is bounded by 159.5 and 900 kg m$^{-3}$, inside the P3 limits of 50–900. Rime from rain has a density of 900 kg m$^{-3}$.

Melting

Above 0 °C, the heat conducted to the particle and the latent heat of vapour diffusion, both ventilated, plus the sensible heat of collected water, melt the ice (Mason 1971; Rutledge & Hobbs 1983):

\[\frac{dq_i}{dt}\Big|_\mathrm{melt} = -\frac{N_i 4\pi E_N[Cf_v]\,[K_a(T-T_0) + L_v D_v(\rho_v - \rho_{vs}(T_0))] + c_w(T-T_0)(\mathrm{coll}_c + \mathrm{coll}_r)}{L_f}\]

The rate is floored at zero. In dry air at +0.5 °C (30 % RH), evaporative cooling prevents melting. Each melted particle becomes one drop, and the rime melts in proportion.

Size limits

Both size distributions have a mean-size limiter, and each has one definition that every part of the scheme uses.

Rain. The mean (number-weighted) diameter $(\mu_r+1)/\lambda_r$ is kept in $[10\ \mu\mathrm{m}, 2\ \mathrm{mm}]$ (P3_RAIN_D_MEAN_MIN, P3_RAIN_D_MEAN_MAX). These are the limits of get_rain_dsd2 in the P3 reference code (microphy_p3.f90, v4.4.0 through v5.5.2): lammax = (mu_r+1.)*1.e+5 and lammin = (mu_r+1.)*inv_Drmax with inv_Drmax = 1./0.002. Earlier P3 versions used 0.8 mm (lammin = (mu_r+1)·1250), which the code keeps as a comment marked "old value". When a limit is hit, P3 keeps $q_r$ and resets $N_r = 6\lambda^3 q_r / (\pi\rho_w(\mu_r+3)(\mu_r+2)(\mu_r+1))$. Here that is p3_rain_limit_number, followed by p3_rain_size_distribution. The process rates (evaporation, self-collection, collection by ice, the clean-up of $N_r$), sedimentation and radar reflectivity all call these two functions, so they see the same $(N_0, \lambda_r)$ for the same $(q_r, N_r)$.

Ice. The mean size $(\mu_i+1)/\lambda_i$ is kept in $[2\ \mu\mathrm{m}, 5\ \mathrm{mm} + 20\ \mathrm{mm}\cdot F_\mathrm{rim}^2]$ (create_p3_lookupTable_1.f90: Dm_min = 2.e-6, Dm_max1 = 5000.e-6, Dm_max2 = 20000.e-6, dum = Dm_max1+Dm_max2*Fr**2). The internal _p3_ice_λ_limits in massdim.jl holds these numbers. The process rates, the $N_i$ clean-up and ice sedimentation all call it.

Before this was consolidated, the process rates had their own copies. The ice copy was identical. The rain copy bounded the mean-volume diameter to 20 μm to 5 mm, which for $\mu_r = 0$ is $\lambda_r \in [363, 90\,856]$ m$^{-1}$. Sedimentation used $[1250, 10^5]$ m$^{-1}$. At $q_r$ = 1 g kg$^{-1}$, $N_r = 10$ kg$^{-1}$ and 70 % relative humidity, the rain evaporation rate changes from 1.17e-7 to 1.84e-7 kg kg$^{-1}$ s$^{-1}$ with the shared limiter.

The source-term step

p3_step evaluates the rates, limits them, applies a forward-Euler update, cleans up and then adjusts to saturation. The limiter scales each process by the factor for the one species it drains. The vapour processes are also limited so that they cannot overshoot liquid or ice saturation. This limit includes a psychrometric factor, and the saturation values are taken after the step's riming and melting heat. Sublimation and rain evaporation share the ice-saturation deficit (evaporation first, since ice stops sublimating once the air reaches ice saturation), so the two vapour sources together cannot overshoot it either.

Sedimentation, size sorting and radar reflectivity

One speed per moment

Each prognostic moment falls at its own bulk speed. Number falls at the number-weighted $V_N$, mass (and with it rime mass and rime volume) at the mass-weighted $V_M$, and a reflectivity moment at $V_Z$:

\[V_N = \frac{\int V N\,dD}{\int N\,dD},\qquad V_M = \frac{\int V m N\,dD}{\int m N\,dD},\qquad V_Z = \frac{\int V m^2 N\,dD}{\int m^2 N\,dD}.\]

$V(D)$ increases with $D$, so $V_N < V_M < V_Z$ for any broad size distribution, and the three are equal only when all particles have the same size. The gap $V_M - V_N$ is size sorting. In a falling shaft, mass runs ahead of number, so the leading edge carries a few large particles and the small ones are left behind. For exponential rain with $\lambda = 2000$ m$^{-1}$ the three speeds are 1.86, 6.04 and 8.01 m s$^{-1}$. As $\mu \to \infty$ at a fixed mean size of 1 mm, $(V_M-V_N)/V_N$ falls from 2.6e-2 ($\mu = 100$) to 2.6e-3 ($\mu = 1000$), the expected $O(1/\mu)$ rate.

Ice speeds come from the Mitchell–Heymsfield fall speed above. Rain uses the drop fall speed of Superdroplet.terminal_velocity (Stokes, then Beard 1976, then Atlas et al. 1973), so the package has a single raindrop fall speed. It is corrected for air density by $(\rho_0/\rho_a)^{0.54}$ (Foote & du Toit 1969). The quadrature is checked against a power law $V = aD^b$, whose weighted speeds are gamma-function ratios. They agree to 1e-12.

Column scheme

The column is updated in flux form with first-order upwinding. Cell $k = 1$ is at the surface. Over a sub-step $h$,

\[\rho_k\psi_k\Delta z_k \leftarrow \rho_k\psi_k\Delta z_k - hF_k + hF_{k+1}, \qquad F_k = \rho_k\psi_k V_k .\]

$h$ is chosen so that the Courant number $V_k h/\Delta z_k$ stays at or below 1 in every cell. This makes the scheme conservative and non-negative. The outflow is also capped at the cell content, so round-off cannot make a value negative. Fall speeds are recomputed at every sub-step, as in the P3 reference code.

When a whole column is advanced in one 900 s step the Courant number reaches 162; the scheme takes 162 sub-steps and conserves mass to 5.7e-16. With a constant speed and a Courant number of exactly 1, it shifts a profile by exactly one cell per sub-step. The ice column ($q_i$, $q_\mathrm{rim}$, $B_\mathrm{rim}$, $N_i$) conserves all four fields to 4.6e-16. It carries $F_\mathrm{rim}$ and $\rho_\mathrm{rim}$ unchanged to 1e-12, because rime mass and rime volume fall with the mass.

Two-moment upwind sedimentation sorts sizes too strongly. At the leading edge, number trails mass by whole cells, so a few cells hold mass with almost no number. Their diagnosed drop size then has no upper bound. We measured 5.4e2 m after 300 s of a 1 km rain layer ($q_r$ = 1 g kg$^{-1}$, $N_r = 10^4$ kg$^{-1}$). P3 bounds this by limiting $\lambda_r$ so the mean size $(\mu_r+1)/\lambda_r$ stays at or below 2 mm, and raises $N_r$ to match (p3_rain_limit_number; see Size limits). Mass stays exact and number is no longer conserved. With the limiter, the mass-mean diameter falls steadily from the leading edge to the top of the shaft: 3634, 3147, 2926, 2824, 2394, 1449, 773, 387, 236, 148 and 61 μm, sampled every 400 m (the initial value was 576 μm); the edge sits on the cap, $\Gamma(4)^{1/3}\cdot 2$ mm = 3634 μm, and has fallen 3125 m in 300 s (10.4 m s$^{-1}$). A near-monodisperse shaft ($\mu_r = 2000$) keeps a uniform size to 4.6e-3.

Radar reflectivity

For rain, the Rayleigh reflectivity factor is the sixth moment, $Z = \int D^6 N\,dD = N_0\Gamma(\mu+7)/\lambda^{\mu+7}$, reported in mm$^6$ m$^{-3}$. It agrees with Simpson quadrature to 3e-14. Ice uses the equivalent reflectivity (Smith 1984). A particle of mass $m$ is treated as a sphere of solid ice with the same mass:

\[Z_e = \frac{|K_i|^2}{|K_w|^2}\left(\frac{6}{\pi\rho_\mathrm{ice}}\right)^2 \int m(D)^2 N(D)\,dD,\qquad \frac{|K_i|^2}{|K_w|^2} = \frac{0.176}{0.93}.\]

A particle of density $\rho_p$ therefore contributes $(\rho_p/\rho_\mathrm{ice})^2$ of the $D^6$ that a solid sphere of the same size would. $m^2$ is a piecewise power law, so $Z_e$ is a sum of truncated gamma moments with no quadrature. It matches quadrature to 3.8e-15. Melting the same particles into drops raises $Z$ by exactly $(|K_w|^2/|K_i|^2)(\rho_\mathrm{ice}/\rho_w)^{2}$, which is +6.48 dB.

The standard check is a Marshall–Palmer distribution ($N_0 = 8\times10^6$ m$^{-4}$), with the slope solved so that the rain rate computed from our own fall speeds is 5 mm h$^{-1}$. That gives 33.93 dBZ, against 34.19 dBZ from the MP relation $Z = 200R^{1.6}$. At 1, 10, 25 and 50 mm h$^{-1}$ the differences are +0.67, −0.59, −0.92 and −1.11 dB.

The target in the issue for this part was "a ~5 mm h⁻¹ rain rate gives 35–45 dBZ". The lower edge of that window sits above the standard Z–R relations themselves: $200\cdot5^{1.6} = 2627$ mm$^6$ m$^{-3}$ (34.2 dBZ) for Marshall–Palmer, and $300\cdot5^{1.4} = 2856$ mm$^6$ m$^{-3}$ (34.6 dBZ) for the WSR-88D convective relation. The expectation was wrong, not the code. The test therefore checks agreement with the MP value to within 1 dB and $30 <$ dBZ $< 40$, rather than the 35–45 window.

Secondary ice: Hallett–Mossop rime splintering

Ice riming supercooled drops between about −3 and −8 °C ejects small ice splinters — about 350 per milligram of rime at the optimum near −5 °C and none outside the window (Hallett & Mossop 1974; Mossop 1976). P3 carries it as a number source proportional to the riming rate (p3_hallett_mossop), in the triangular form of the P3 reference code:

\[\frac{dN_i}{dt}\Big|_\mathrm{HM} = 3.5\times10^{8}\,\mathrm{kg^{-1}}\; f(T)\,\left(\phi_c\,\mathrm{rime}_c + \mathrm{rime}_r\right), \qquad f(T) = \begin{cases} (T - T_{-8})/3\,\mathrm{K} & -8 < T < -5\ ^\circ\mathrm{C}\\ (T_{-3} - T)/2\,\mathrm{K} & -5 \le T < -3\ ^\circ\mathrm{C}\\ 0 & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases}\]

The riming rates are those of p3_process_rates — the same numbers that feed $q_\mathrm{rim}$ and $B_\mathrm{rim}$ — and are not recomputed. $\phi_c = 1$ in the P3 code; with P3HallettMossop(D_drop_min = 25e-6) it is the fraction of cloud water in droplets larger than 25 μm (Mossop 1976), $Q(\mu_c + 4, \lambda_c D_\min)$ for a gamma droplet distribution with the Martin et al. (1994) shape used by P3 — the geometric sweep-out collects each droplet in proportion to its volume.

Two conditions of the P3 code (one ice category) select the rimers that splinter: the ice must already carry rime ($q_\mathrm{rim} \ge q_\mathrm{small}$) and its mean diameter $D = (6 q_i/(\pi N_i \rho_\mathrm{bulk}))^{1/3}$ (p3_hm_rimer_diameter) must be at least D_ice_min = 250 μm — a population of small crystals rimes but does not splinter.

A splinter is a 10 μm-diameter ice sphere, $m_\mathrm{spl} = \tfrac{\pi}{6}\,900\,(10\,\mu\mathrm{m})^3$ = 4.71×10⁻¹³ kg (P3_HM_SPLINTER_MASS, Dinit_HM of the P3 code) — not the 2 μm nucleated-crystal mass P3_NUCLEATED_ICE_MASS. It is ice that already exists — a fragment of the freezing rime — so $q_i$, the other species and $T$ are unchanged; the splinter mass leaves the rime mass (the P3 code books it to $q_i$ but not $q_\mathrm{rim}$), and the rime volume leaves with it at the density of the rime being formed, so the new rime keeps its density. The splinter mass is 1.65×10⁻⁴ of the rime.

Splintering is opt-in in p3_step (hallett_mossop = P3_DEFAULT_HM) so that existing callers keep their results; the Arctic stratus column (Phase 43, run_arctic_stratus) turns it on.

Terminal-velocity spectrum and sub-grid precipitation fraction (Phase 44)

No single fall speed

An audit of src/ and examples/ for precipitation that fell at one speed found two places, both now delegating to the P3 rain functions above (no new fall-speed law):

  • Kessler (apply_kessler_step!, Phase 12) fell at a constant Vt (default 5 m s⁻¹; the examples used 3, 5 and 6 m s⁻¹), with a silent Courant cap of 0.5. Its default is now Vt = :spectrum: the single-moment rain is closed with the Marshall–Palmer spectrum that Kessler (1969) assumes, $N(D) = N_0 e^{-\lambda D}$, $N_0 = 8\times10^6$ m⁻⁴, so that

    \[\lambda = \left(\frac{\pi \rho_w N_0}{\rho_a q_r}\right)^{1/4}, \qquad N_r = \frac{N_0}{\lambda \rho_a},\]

    and each cell falls at the mass-weighted speed $V_M$ of that spectrum (kessler_fall_speed, via p3_rain_sedimentation_velocities) through the sub-stepped, conservative p3_sediment!. $V_M$ = 3.77, 5.66, 6.57 and 7.48 m s⁻¹ at 0.1, 1, 3 and 10 g m⁻³ of rain, within 10 % of — and at 1–3 g m⁻³ within 0.2 % of — the Klemp & Wilhelmson (1978) fit $V = 36.34 (\rho_a q_r)^{0.1346}$ ($\rho_a q_r$ in g cm⁻³). A numeric Vt keeps the legacy kernel bit for bit.

  • The urban two-moment warm rain (examples/urban/moisture_addons.jl) used one cell-wise speed $6.5 (q_r \rho_a/N_r)^{1/6}$ with a 4 m s⁻¹ floor, and its upwind update took the inflow from above at the receiving cell's Courant number, which does not conserve mass where the speed varies (+0.068 % in 20 s for a 9-level shaft). It now calls p3_sediment_rain! (mass at $V_M$, number at $V_N$). That scaffold (TwoMomentState) was never called by the Berlin run and was deleted with the rest of the dead moisture add-ons in #126; P3 enters the Berlin case as a microphysics provider (#143).

Not changed, because they are not a single precipitation fall speed: Superdroplet (each droplet at its own terminal_velocity), P3 ice and rain (already per-moment), inp_sediment! (Stokes settling of aerosol) and the aerosol V_sed of the urban air-quality add-on.

Precipitation fraction down the column

The fraction $f_p$ of a level covered by rain is the union of the rain area arriving from above, $P$, and the cloud of the level, $c$ (where new rain forms), carried from the model top down (p3_precipitation_fraction):

\[\begin{aligned} \text{maximum:}\quad & f_p = \max(P, c),\\ \text{random:}\quad & f_p = P + (1-P)\,c,\\ \text{maximum–random:}\quad & f_p = P + (1-P)\,\frac{\max(c,\tilde c) - \tilde c}{1-\tilde c}, \qquad \tilde c = \min(c_\text{above}, P), \end{aligned}\]

the last being Geleyn & Hollingsworth (1979) as used for precipitation by Jakob & Klein (2000) and the ECMWF IFS (Tiedtke 1993): maximum overlap between vertically adjacent cloudy layers, random across clear gaps. All three are nondecreasing in $P$ and ordered max ≤ max-random ≤ random at every level, hence all the way down (for four gap-separated decks of 0.3, 0.4, 0.5, 0.2: 0.500 ≤ 0.832 ≤ 0.994 at the surface). Where the rain above has evaporated completely the area is reset; rain with neither cloud nor rain above it gets $f_p = 1$.

Process rates in the precipitating fraction

p3_fractional_process_rates follows the P3 code in E3SM/SCREAM: cloud and rain are maximally overlapped inside the cell, giving four regions — cloud ∩ rain (area $\min(f_c, f_p)$), cloud only, rain only, clear. Each region is given in-cloud $q_c/f_c, N_c/f_c$, in-rain $q_r/f_p, N_r/f_p$, and vapour

\[q_{v,\mathrm{cld}} = \min(q_s, q_v/f_c), \qquad q_{v,\mathrm{clr}} = \frac{q_v - f_c\, q_{v,\mathrm{cld}}}{1 - f_c},\]

(Tiedtke 1993; grid-mean vapour kept) with the ice uniform, and the cell rate is the area-weighted sum of p3_process_rates of the regions. The tendency map, and so water conservation, is unchanged. Nonlinear rates change by the factors their exponents imply: KK2000 accretion $67 (q_c q_r)^{1.15}$ by $a\,(f_c f_p)^{-1.15}$ (×1.2311 for rain on a quarter of a cloudy cell), autoconversion $\propto q_c^{2.47} N_c^{-1.79}$ by $f_c^{0.32}$ (×0.6413 at 25 % cover; the 0.06 % from $0.25^{0.32}$ is the air density of the cloudy region, whose condensate P3's $\rho_a$ counts). Rain under cloud does not evaporate — the cloudy air is saturated — while rain in the clear part evaporates at the clear-sky deficit. Rates linear in $(q_r, N_r)$ at fixed $\lambda_r$ (evaporation, rain riming) are unchanged by $f_p$ up to that same density effect (6×10⁻⁵).

The forward-Euler limiter (p3_limit_rates) is applied to each region against that region's own contents and saturation state, not to the grid-mean sum. A grid-mean limiter lets rain on 10 % of a fully cloudy cell collect 64 % of the cell's cloud water in a 60 s step, although only 10 % of it lies under the rain; limited per region the loss is 10.02 %. It would also switch off deposition on ice in the water-saturated cloudy part whenever the grid mean is subsaturated with respect to ice.

The option is off by default: p3_step(s, dt) is bit-identical to the grid-mean step; p3_step(s, dt; cloud_fraction, precip_fraction) and p3_step_column switch it on. Sedimentation needs no fraction, since the fall speeds depend only on $q_r/N_r$. The saturation adjustment of step 5 remains the grid-mean, all-or-nothing one.
