Urban chemistry and deposition

Aeolus.Chemistry carries a reduced NOx–O3–VOC mechanism for urban boundary-layer air quality, together with resistance-model dry deposition and below-cloud wet deposition. All mixing ratios are in ppb. The Berlin case runs it on five advected tracers (src/components/urban/aq_chem.jl, #135).

Mechanism

reactionratesource
R1 NO2 + hν → NO + O3$J = l μ_0^m e^{-n/μ_0}$, $l = 1.165×10^{-2}$ s⁻¹, $m = 0.244$, $n = 0.267$MCM v3 J4 (Saunders et al. 2003)
R2 NO + O3 → NO2 + O2$3.0×10^{-12} e^{-1500/T}$JPL 19-5 (Burkholder et al. 2019)
R3 VOC + O3 → products$1.03×10^{-14} e^{-1995/T}$ (isoprene)IUPAC (Atkinson et al. 2006)
R4 VOC + OH → ν NO→NO2$2.7×10^{-11} e^{390/T}$ (isoprene), ν = 2IUPAC
R5 NO2 + OH (+M) → HNO3Troe form, $k_0 = 1.8×10^{-30}(T/300)^{-3}$, $k_∞ = 2.8×10^{-11}$, $F_c = 0.6$JPL 19-5

Bimolecular constants (cm³ molecule⁻¹ s⁻¹) become ppb⁻¹ s⁻¹ through the air number density, $k_{ppb} = k\,M\,10^{-9}$, $M = p/(k_B T)$. OH is prescribed: the mechanism has no HOx budget. In R4 the peroxy radical and the HO2 it yields each convert one NO to NO2, so each VOC oxidised by OH makes two Ox. This is the mechanism's ozone production.

The triad, exactly

R1–R2 conserve $NO_x = NO + NO_2$ and $O_x = O_3 + NO_2$. With $y = [NO_2]$ they reduce to a Riccati equation

\[\dot y = k (NO_x - y)(O_x - y) - J y = k (y - y_1)(y - y_2),\]

with roots $y_1 ≤ \min(NO_x, O_x) ≤ y_2$. Its solution over a step is

\[y(t) = y_1 + \frac{(y_0 - y_1) E}{1 - (y_0 - y_1)(1 - E)/r}, \qquad r = y_2 - y_1,\; E = e^{-k r t}.\]

nox_triad evaluates it with the cancellation-free root $y_1 = 2 k NO_x O_x/(B + D)$ and takes the degenerate limit $r → 0$ (night titration with $NO_x = O_x$) through expm1. The integrator is unconditionally stable and positive, and conserves NOx and Ox to round-off for any time step. The test compares it with a 200 000-step RK4 reference to 1e-9. The photostationary state $y_1$ is where the Leighton ratio

\[φ = \frac{J [NO_2]}{k [NO][O_3]}\]

equals 1. The relaxation time is $1/(k r)$, about 40 s at noon. With peroxy radicals the steady state is instead $φ_{ext} = 1 + ν k_4[OH][VOC]/(k[NO][O_3])$.

R3–R5 are applied first, as pseudo-first-order losses over the step with their partners frozen (exact exponentials), then the triad. This is first-order operator splitting. photochemistry_cell returns the Ox production and loss, so that $Δ O_x = P - L$ and $Δ NO_x = -Δ HNO_3$ hold exactly per cell.

Dry deposition

The deposition velocity is three resistances in series (Wesely & Hicks 1977; Seinfeld & Pandis 2016, ch. 19):

\[v_d = \frac{1}{r_a + r_b + r_c}.\]

  • $r_a = 1/(C_H U)$ is the scalar aerodynamic resistance between the surface and the first air cell. The caller supplies it; the Berlin case takes it from SurfaceExchange.solve_surface_layer.
  • $r_b = \frac{2}{κ u_*}(Sc/Pr)^{2/3}$ is the quasi-laminar resistance. $Sc = ν/D$ uses the water-vapour diffusivity of Massman (1998) and the $D_{H_2O}/D_x$ ratios of Wesely (1989).
  • $r_c$ is the surface resistance per species and surface kind (surface_resistance). For O3 and NOx the values are the reciprocals of the total deposition velocities the Berlin example used to carry, now read as the surface conductance. They are of the order of Wesely's midsummer values but are not a fit. HNO3 uses Wesely's floor of 10 s m⁻¹. The lumped VOC does not deposit.

apply_dry_deposition! removes $c(1 - e^{-v_d Δt/Δz})$ from the first air cell of each column. This is the exact solution of the surface-flux sink over the step. The first air cell is a per-column level, so the sink sits above the roof on building columns.

Wet deposition

Below-cloud scavenging by rain is $dc/dt = -Λ c$ with $Λ = s\,q_r$, integrated exactly. For HNO3, $s = 1$ (kg/kg)⁻¹ s⁻¹ gives $Λ ≈ 10^{-4}$ s⁻¹ at $q_r = 10^{-4}$ (about 2 mm h⁻¹). That is the order of magnitude of below-cloud scavenging for a highly soluble gas, not a fit. For NO, NO2 and O3 the Henry's-law solubility is so small that $s = 0$.

Emissions

emission_ppb_flux(F, M) converts a mass flux (kg m⁻² s⁻¹) to a mixing-ratio flux, $10^9 (F/M)/(p/RT)$ ppb m s⁻¹. Spread over a first cell of depth $Δz$, this raises the mixing ratio by $F_{ppb}Δt/Δz$. The emission inventory itself (traffic NOx, park isoprene) is case data and lives in the Berlin coupling.
