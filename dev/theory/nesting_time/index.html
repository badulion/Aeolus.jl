Nested time coupling: subcycling and temporal interpolation

The nesting operators move a field set between a parent and a child grid. This page covers how the two grids are advanced in time together: NestedRun and step_nested! in src/nesting/nested_run.jl (N-nest-c, #257).

The pieces

A NestedRun holds the parent, the child, the NestedGrid, three NestInterpState buffers (the parent prolongated at the start and the end of the parent step, and a scratch blend), one advance closure per domain, the schedule, the feedback mode and the statistics.

Each domain is advanced by a DomainAdvance, built with domain_advance(core, args...; kwargs...). It carries the hooks and keywords that domain's steps need (body_force!, post_predictor!, momentum_advection, sgs, sgs_stress, …) and calls the core through dispatch: step! (Boussinesq, or the anelastic / pseudo-incompressible step of a MassWeightedSimulation), step_rk3!, step_imex! with ARK2() or Rosenbrock2(), or a user function. No step logic is copied. The run adds one hook of its own, chained after the domain's post_predictor!, which refreshes the child's halos (see below).

Scheduling

Parent step. $Δt_p$ is the parent's adaptive_dt (per-cell advective, diffusive, buoyancy and Coriolis limits, the growth limiter and the clamps of its CFLConstraints). It can also be passed as a fixed dt.

Child substeps (sync = :subcycle, the default). The child lands exactly on $t_p + Δt_p$. It starts with

\[n = \left\lceil \frac{Δt_p}{Δt_c^{\max}} \right\rceil ,\]

where $Δt_c^{\max}$ is the child's own adaptive_dt. Before every piece the count is re-evaluated against the current child limit, $n_{\rm rem} \leftarrow \max\!\big(n_{\rm rem}, \lceil R/Δt_c^{\max}\rceil\big)$, and the piece is $Δt_c = R/n_{\rm rem}$, where $R$ is the time left. The last piece takes exactly $R$. The ceiling uses a relative tolerance of $10^{-9}$, so a ratio that is an integer up to round-off is not rounded up: a child whose limit is exactly $Δt_p/r$ takes $r$ substeps, not $r + 1$. The test shows this for $r = 2, 3, 4, 5$. At the end the child clock is set to the parent's. The discrepancy before that is kept in stats.max_clock_error, and it was 0 over 1000 adaptive parent steps.

Shared step (sync = :shared_min_dt). Both domains take one step of $\min(Δt_p^{\max}, Δt_c^{\max})$. This is PALM's scheme (Hellsten et al. 2021, §3.1): every domain uses the smallest time step of all domains. It is simpler, but the parent then runs at the child's step.

Rejections. A step is rejected when it throws, leaves a non-finite prognostic, a non-finite bound scalar or a speed above max_velocity, or (parent only) exceeds divergence_tol. A rejected step is rolled back with the TimeIntegration StateSnapshot contract, plus the bound scalars and the SGS closure's memory. The parent is stepped and validated before any child substep, so a parent rejection never costs child work. A rejected child piece rolls back only the child. That piece is redone at half its size and the remaining time is re-divided. The parent is never advanced twice.

Temporal interpolation

The child's boundary data at a time $t$ inside the parent step is the linear blend of the parent field set prolongated at the two ends of the step:

\[B(t) = (1-τ)\,\mathcal P\,φ_p(t_p) + τ\,\mathcal P\,φ_p(t_p+Δt_p), \qquad τ = \frac{t-t_p}{Δt_p}.\]

boundary_time(run, t) evaluates it over the whole field set (u, v, w, θ, qv, extra scalars), at the points the run stores (the halos and boundary face planes only, without a sponge: #329). Linear interpolation of a smooth $φ(t)$ has the error

\[\lvert B(t) - \mathcal Pφ(t)\rvert \le \frac{Δt_p^2}{8}\max\lvert \ddot φ\rvert ,\]

which is second order. With $φ = 300 + A\sin ωt$ and $A = 1.5$, $ω = 2π$, the measured maximum error over the child's stage times was $1.824\times10^{-2}$ at $Δt_p = 1/20$. The bound is $1.851\times10^{-2}$. Halving $Δt_p$ divided the error by 3.957, and halving it again divided it by 3.989.

Boundary data at the stage times of every core

A multi-stage core reads its halos once per stage. Imposing the data at the step start for all stages loses accuracy (Carpenter et al. 1995). The halos must hold $B(t_0 + c_i Δt)$ when stage $i$ computes its tendencies. The $c_i$ are given by stage_times. Since N-nest-d (#261) the child's nest faces are OpenBoundary faces fed by a NestBoundaryData, and every core refreshes them itself, once per projected stage, before the projection (update_open_boundaries!, N-open-b #255), with the time in the last column (nested boundary conditions). Before, the run wrote the halos through a post_predictor! hook with the same times.

core$c_i$boundary data refreshed at the end of stage $k$
step! (Boussinesq or sound-proof)$0$$B(t_0 + Δt)$
step_imex!, ARK2 (Ascher et al. 1997)$0, γ$$B(t_0 + h_k)$, with $h_k = γΔt, Δt$ the stage increments
step_imex!, Rosenbrock2$0, a_{21}$$B(t_0 + h_k)$, with $h_k = a_{21}Δt, Δt$
step_rk3!, SSP-RK3 (Shu & Osher 1988)$0, 1, \tfrac12$$B(t_0+Δt),\ B(t_0+2Δt),\ B(t_0+\tfrac32Δt)$

The SSP-RK3 row needs explaining. step_rk3! combines whole arrays, halos included, and the open-boundary buffers with the same weights: $u^{(2)} = \tfrac34 u^n + \tfrac14 \tilde u$ and $u^{n+1} = \tfrac13 u^n + \tfrac23 \tilde u$. Because $B$ is linear in time, the values in the last column make the combined halos

\[B(t_0+Δt),\quad \tfrac34 B(t_0) + \tfrac14 B(t_0+2Δt) = B(t_0+\tfrac12Δt), \quad \tfrac13 B(t_0) + \tfrac23 B(t_0+\tfrac32Δt) = B(t_0+Δt) .\]

These are exactly the stage times and the step end. A write past $t_p + Δt_p$ is a linear extrapolation of the same line.

The boundary face of the normal velocity on a west, south or bottom nest face is the child's interior face index $H+1$; it belongs to the OpenBoundary and is written with the halos (N-nest-c had to re-impose it with an impose_interface option, now removed). A periodic child direction has no open face and keeps its own interior face.

Measured. The parent was advanced analytically, linear in $x, y, z$ and $t$, and the child's halos were checked at every substep and every stage against the analytic value. The maximum relative error was $4.4\times10^{-16}$ for step! and step_rk3!, $4.6\times10^{-16}$ for ARK2 and $4.4\times10^{-16}$ for Rosenbrock2. boundary_time reached $3.8\times10^{-16}$.

A child that picks its own steps (N-berlin-nest, #265; NEST-p3, #483)

The urban site advances its child through a whole stage loop (surfaces, radiation, chemistry, transport, then the dynamics) and picks every step itself (its adaptive time stepper retries its own pieces), so it does not take the run's own subcycling. The model's nest engine (Components.ModelNest) steps it in the same window as any child, and lets it take its own steps inside:

  1. the parent takes its step $Δt_p$ (its own adaptive pick; for a site with a fixed step, a whole number of the site's steps, Components.window_dt), and the window opens: the prolongation at $t_p$ (reused in a one-way run) and at $t_p + Δt_p$, the child's boundary window $[t_p, t_p + Δt_p]$;
  2. the site's steps, which land on nest_window_end(run): subcycle_dt(R, limit) is the rule above ($R/n$, $n = \lceil R/\mathrm{limit}\rceil$ with the $10^{-9}$ tolerance), and every core reads the blended parent at its own stage times; a two-way site books each step between the link's _begin! and _commit!;
  3. the window closes: the clock check and snap, the two-way feedback, the statistics.

test/era2_nest_external.jl drives a window this way (the engine's internals of Aeolus.Nesting) and checks that it is step_nested!(run) bit for bit (one- and two-way, four windows of three substeps). The same file runs the physics of a child that picks its own steps on synthetic boxes, with adaptive child steps that do not divide the window (limit $0.37\,Δt_p$): a parent at rest with a hydrostatic θ(z) leaves the child at rest (max $|u|$ = 1.0e-14 m/s after 12 steps, θ unchanged); a parent at (2, 1) m/s leaves the child at (2, 1) m/s exactly; the net boundary volume flux after the correction stays at 8.1e-18 $UA$ and max $|∇·u|$ at 5.7e-14 s⁻¹; a +1 K bubble near the lid of a calm child loses 5.0 % of its heat through the faces in 80 s and the time integral of boundary_scalar_flux books it to 8.9e-12 of the anomaly's heat (the periodic box keeps it to 6.8e-12); the parent's geostrophic tendency is exactly $(-fV_g, fU_g)$ and the child's IBM hook runs in every child step. The urban site's link has no NestSponge (which acts only inside the run's own subcycling); the site relaxes toward the parent's mean profile with its core's own top sponge.

The parent stepped concurrently (SC-c, #403; NEST-p3, #483)

In a one-way run the parent step of window $n+1$ reads nothing but the parent at the end of window $n$: it does not wait for the child, so it can run on its own task while the child subcycles through window $n$. The model's nest engine does this for a one-way parent below the root (Components.nest_prefetches, NEST-p2 #482), bit for bit the sequential run. The root is not prefetched (its next step is the model's next window, and the run reads every domain at every window's end), so the Berlin nest, whose parent is the root, steps its parent and its site one after the other since NEST-p3 (#483), where NestedRun's own window API and its prefetch (prefetch_parent_step!, the Berlin path of SC-c) went.

A child tracer the parent does not carry (in Berlin: ql, qr, the chemistry species) still needs a face condition: its plane is added after the core is built (add_open_scalars!) with the :inflow halo — the parent's value (here zero) where the face's normal velocity enters, the zero-gradient mirror where it leaves.

Feedback

  • :two_way (the default since ONE-f, #455, or a TwoWayFeedback): the self-nesting default, an Aeolus parent driving an Aeolus child, as in PALM. After the child reaches $t_p + Δt_p$, its field set is anterpolated onto the covered parent cells outside a buffer zone, the interface flux of the open faces is corrected, the scalars are refluxed from flux registers booked at every stage of both domains, and the parent is re-projected; see the two-way nesting page. The conservation ledger is read with nest_conservation_error.
  • :one_way, per child. The parent never sees the child. The parent of a one-way run is bit-identical to a standalone parent run with the same $Δt$ sequence; the test checks this over 20 adaptive steps. The boundary data at the start of a step is reused from the end of the previous one. A mixed core pair (Boussinesq with sound-proof) and a child without a conservation box can only be nested one-way, and so is a child driven offline, by a dynamic driver instead of a running parent. The urban site's Berlin nest ([nesting] mode) keeps one-way as its default until it becomes a [[domain]] config: its two-way validation is #331.

The historical step_nested!(parent, child, nest, dt; …) is a thin wrapper. It is a two-way NestedRun with a fixed $Δt$, a fixed substeps (default $\max r$) and no step validation.

Cost for the Berlin child

benchmarks/bench_nested_run.jl runs a 192 × 192 × 120 parent (6 × 6 × 3 km, Δx = 31.25 m, uniform Δz = 25 m) and a 1 × 1 km child at $(2, 2, 4)$. Both use Smagorinsky SGS and a 5 m/s westerly with noise. The run takes 12 adaptive parent steps of $Δt_p = 1.44 - 1.80$ s on one core. The model's nest engine (NEST-p2, #482)

A model of several [[domain]] blocks is stepped by one engine, ModelNest and nest_window!, built on the links of a NestHierarchy (the operators, boundary data, registers and feedback above). One model step is one window, a step of the root:

  1. a domain steps through stages 1–11 of its own pipeline;
  2. the boundary data of each child are prolongated at both ends of that step, and each child takes $n$ equal substeps to land on its end, recursively (Berger & Oliger 1984);
  3. the two-way children feed back (stage 12, :nest_feedback);
  4. the domain finishes with stage 13 (:budgets, :checks, :output), so its budgets close after the feedback and its checks and output see the state after it.

Step sizes. With a fixed dt a child takes $n = \max r$ substeps; the engine is then the hierarchy's step_nested!, bit for bit (tested on one level, Euler and RK3, one- and two-way, and on three levels with a sibling). Adaptive, the root takes its own stability limit (its CFL limits, its components' limits, the growth limit from its last step; step_adaptive!(model)), and each child, at the start of every window of its own, the smallest whole number of equal substeps that keeps it within its limit,

\[n = \left\lceil \Delta t_p / \Delta t_c^{\max} \right\rceil ,\]

chosen once per window from the child's state at the window start (after the window opening). Measured on the 16 × 16 × 8 / 16 × 16 × 8 nest with a jet in the child: $n = 4, 2, 2, 2, 3, 3, 3, 3$ over eight windows of 12–25 s; on a 64 × 64 × 32 root with a 64 × 64 × 32 child at $r = 2$ the root takes 8 s windows with $n = 3$ (fixed: 2 s with $n = 2$), at 0.065 s per window against 0.050 s for the fixed one, so 6.1 times cheaper per simulated second.

Window retries. A step that fails (a non-finite core or nest-bound field, or a numerical failure, is_numerical_failure) redoes the window from the state saved at its start: with half the root step when the root failed, with twice the failing child's substeps when a child did (the floor holds for the rest of the window), up to max_retries (3), after which the window start is restored and an error raised. The saved state is the structural record of the whole hierarchy (every core, component, budget, register, statistic and generator), refreshed in place every window (IO.refresh_structural_state!): 9.7 ms and 0.5 MiB allocated on the 64 × 64 × 32 + 64 × 64 × 32 nest, against 72 ms for a fresh record. The restore is complete: a window whose root failed once is bit for bit the window taken at the halved step from the same start, and a one-way parent is bit for bit unaffected by its child's retry. A child's retry repeats its parent's step too (the parent is restored with everything else), which costs one parent step but changes nothing.

Concurrency. With more than one thread, sibling children step in parallel (they read only their own boundary data, write their own domains and links, and feed back afterwards in link order), and a domain below the root whose children are all one-way steps ahead of them: its substep $s + 1$ runs on a task while they subcycle through substep $s$ (nest_prefetches). Both are bit for bit the sequential result (tested at 1 and 4 threads, fixed and adaptive). Measured on a 64 × 64 × 32 root (minimum of 4 × 5 windows): two one-way 48 × 48 × 32 siblings ×1.25, ×1.37 and ×1.41 at 2, 4 and 8 threads; a three-level one-way nest ×1.08 to ×1.12. The root itself is not prefetched: its next step is the model's next window, and the run reads every domain at the end of each window (output, checks, the monitor, checkpoints).

Mixed cores. A Boussinesq domain and an anelastic one may be nested either way round, two-way included; the density conversion is on two-way nesting.

Current limits

These are owned by other issues of the nesting epic (#246).

  • Resolved by N-nest-d (#261). The child's projection now sees the parent's boundary flux (open faces, all-Neumann solve, the single mass-flux correction), and a wall below a nest face above is a supported child (the Berlin shape). With N-nest-c's periodic child projection a Taylor-Green child blew up within about 10 steps unless the normal velocity was re-imposed and a sponge added; it now runs the 1000-step tests with neither, and the benchmark child has an open top.
  • Resolved by N-nest-e (#263): two-way with walls. The interface flux correction and the refluxing act on the child's open faces only; a child wall that is not the parent's wall is reported (the composite integral cannot be conserved across it).
  • Resolved by #329: the sponge and ∇·u. A NestSponge relaxes the child velocity on the predicted velocity of every stage, before the pressure solve (post_predictor!), so the child stays divergence-free ($2.8\times10^{-14}$ s⁻¹, against 1.6 s⁻¹ when relaxed after the step); the scalars are relaxed after the substep. A user core must call post_predictor! for the velocity part to act.
  • Resolved by #329: memory and allocations. Without a sponge the run's three field sets are halo-only (storage = :shell, 42 % of the child-sized sets for a $32^3$ child, 24 % for $64^3$); the rollback state of each domain and the two-way scratch are allocated once. Per nested step ($32^3$ parent, $32^3$ child at $r = 2$, step!), the nesting layer allocates 24 KiB one-way and 44 KiB two-way on top of the cores, against 2.9 MiB and 6.6 MiB before (the composite momentum integral alone allocated 858 KiB per call, twice per two-way step); the periodic halo fill no longer allocates, so a 32³ step! went from 1.6 MiB to 0. The state is concretely typed (no Any, Vector{Any}, Vector{Field} or Dict{Any,Any}), as the KernelAbstractions port (#291) needs.
  • Resolved by #329: refluxing at the parent boundary. The correction of a child face lying on the parent's own boundary goes to the wrapped cell across a periodic seam and nowhere across a non-periodic boundary (it used to wrap on every axis, so a child at the parent top would have corrected the parent's lowest layer); apply_flux_correction! takes the parent's periodic axes (nest_periodic_axes). Since #349 the periodic keyword of the cell-centred apply_flux_correction! and of reflux! is required: its triply periodic default was wrong for every parent with a wall or an open face.
  • Resolved by #328: hierarchies, siblings, non-aligned and stretched children. A NestHierarchy advances a tree of domains by recursive subcycling, every link a NestedRun; children may have a rational ratio, edges off the parent faces or a stretched z. See nest hierarchies, non-aligned and stretched children.
