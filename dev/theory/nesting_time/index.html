Nested time coupling: subcycling and temporal interpolation

The nesting operators move a field set between a parent and a child grid. This page covers how the two grids are advanced in time together: NestedRun and step_nested! in src/nesting/nested_run.jl (N-nest-c, #257).

The pieces

A NestedRun holds the parent, the child, the NestedGrid, three NestInterpState buffers (the parent prolongated at the start and the end of the parent step, and a scratch blend), one advance closure per domain, the schedule, the feedback mode and the statistics.

Each domain is advanced by a DomainAdvance, built with domain_advance(core, args...; kwargs...). It carries the hooks and keywords that domain's steps need (body_force!, post_predictor!, momentum_advection, sgs, sgs_stress, …) and calls the core through dispatch: step! (Boussinesq, or the anelastic / pseudo-incompressible step of a MassWeightedSimulation), step_rk3!, step_imex! with ARK2() or Rosenbrock2(), or a user function. No step logic is copied. The run adds one hook of its own, chained after the domain's post_predictor!, which refreshes the child's halos (see below).

Scheduling

Parent step. $Δt_p$ is the parent's adaptive_dt (per-cell advective, diffusive, buoyancy and Coriolis limits, the growth limiter and the clamps of its CFLConstraints). It can also be passed as a fixed dt.

Child substeps (sync = :subcycle, the default). The child lands exactly on $t_p + Δt_p$. It starts with

\[n = \left\lceil \frac{Δt_p}{Δt_c^{\max}} \right\rceil ,\]

where $Δt_c^{\max}$ is the child's own adaptive_dt. Before every piece the count is re-evaluated against the current child limit, $n_{\rm rem} \leftarrow \max\!\big(n_{\rm rem}, \lceil R/Δt_c^{\max}\rceil\big)$, and the piece is $Δt_c = R/n_{\rm rem}$, where $R$ is the time left. The last piece takes exactly $R$. The ceiling uses a relative tolerance of $10^{-9}$, so a ratio that is an integer up to round-off is not rounded up: a child whose limit is exactly $Δt_p/r$ takes $r$ substeps, not $r + 1$. The test shows this for $r = 2, 3, 4, 5$. At the end the child clock is set to the parent's. The discrepancy before that is kept in stats.max_clock_error, and it was 0 over 1000 adaptive parent steps.

Shared step (sync = :shared_min_dt). Both domains take one step of $\min(Δt_p^{\max}, Δt_c^{\max})$. This is PALM's scheme (Hellsten et al. 2021, §3.1): every domain uses the smallest time step of all domains. It is simpler, but the parent then runs at the child's step.

Rejections. A step is rejected when it throws, leaves a non-finite prognostic, a non-finite bound scalar or a speed above max_velocity, or (parent only) exceeds divergence_tol. A rejected step is rolled back with the TimeIntegration StateSnapshot contract, plus the bound scalars and the SGS closure's memory. The parent is stepped and validated before any child substep, so a parent rejection never costs child work. A rejected child piece rolls back only the child. That piece is redone at half its size and the remaining time is re-divided. The parent is never advanced twice.

Temporal interpolation

The child's boundary data at a time $t$ inside the parent step is the linear blend of the parent field set prolongated at the two ends of the step:

\[B(t) = (1-τ)\,\mathcal P\,φ_p(t_p) + τ\,\mathcal P\,φ_p(t_p+Δt_p), \qquad τ = \frac{t-t_p}{Δt_p}.\]

boundary_time(run, t) evaluates it over the whole field set (u, v, w, θ, qv, extra scalars), at the points the run stores (the halos and boundary face planes only, without a sponge: #329). Linear interpolation of a smooth $φ(t)$ has the error

\[\lvert B(t) - \mathcal Pφ(t)\rvert \le \frac{Δt_p^2}{8}\max\lvert \ddot φ\rvert ,\]

which is second order. With $φ = 300 + A\sin ωt$ and $A = 1.5$, $ω = 2π$, the measured maximum error over the child's stage times was $1.824\times10^{-2}$ at $Δt_p = 1/20$. The bound is $1.851\times10^{-2}$. Halving $Δt_p$ divided the error by 3.957, and halving it again divided it by 3.989.

Boundary data at the stage times of every core

A multi-stage core reads its halos once per stage. Imposing the data at the step start for all stages loses accuracy (Carpenter et al. 1995). The halos must hold $B(t_0 + c_i Δt)$ when stage $i$ computes its tendencies. The $c_i$ are given by stage_times. Since N-nest-d (#261) the child's nest faces are OpenBoundary faces fed by a NestBoundaryData, and every core refreshes them itself, once per projected stage, before the projection (update_open_boundaries!, N-open-b #255), with the time in the last column (nested boundary conditions). Before, the run wrote the halos through a post_predictor! hook with the same times.

core$c_i$boundary data refreshed at the end of stage $k$

The SSP-RK3 row needs explaining. step_rk3! combines whole arrays, halos included, and the open-boundary buffers with the same weights: $u^{(2)} = \tfrac34 u^n + \tfrac14 \tilde u$ and $u^{n+1} = \tfrac13 u^n + \tfrac23 \tilde u$. Because $B$ is linear in time, the values in the last column make the combined halos

\[B(t_0+Δt),\quad \tfrac34 B(t_0) + \tfrac14 B(t_0+2Δt) = B(t_0+\tfrac12Δt), \quad \tfrac13 B(t_0) + \tfrac23 B(t_0+\tfrac32Δt) = B(t_0+Δt) .\]

These are exactly the stage times and the step end. A write past $t_p + Δt_p$ is a linear extrapolation of the same line.

The boundary face of the normal velocity on a west, south or bottom nest face is the child's interior face index $H+1$; it belongs to the OpenBoundary and is written with the halos (N-nest-c had to re-impose it with an impose_interface option, now removed). A periodic child direction has no open face and keeps its own interior face.

Measured. The parent was advanced analytically, linear in $x, y, z$ and $t$, and the child's halos were checked at every substep and every stage against the analytic value. The maximum relative error was $4.4\times10^{-16}$ for step! and step_rk3!, $4.6\times10^{-16}$ for ARK2 and $4.4\times10^{-16}$ for Rosenbrock2. boundary_time reached $3.8\times10^{-16}$.

A child driven by its caller (N-berlin-nest, #265)

The Berlin case advances its child through a whole stage loop (surfaces, radiation, chemistry, transport, then the dynamics), so it cannot hand the child to the run's own subcycling. step_nested!(run) is therefore three calls, and a caller can take the middle one itself:

  1. begin_parent_step!(run; dt) — the parent step $Δt_p$ (its own pick, or dt), the prolongation at $t_p$ (reused in a one-way run) and at $t_p + Δt_p$, and the child's boundary window $[t_p, t_p + Δt_p]$; it returns window, with window.t_child_end;
  2. the caller's child steps, which must land on window.t_child_end: subcycle_dt(R, limit) is the rule above ($R/n$, $n = \lceil R/\mathrm{limit}\rceil$ with the $10^{-9}$ tolerance), and every core reads the blended parent at its own stage times;
  3. end_parent_step!(run, window) — the clock check and snap, the two-way feedback, the statistics.

step_nested!(run) is exactly these with the run's own subcycling in between; test/era2_nest_external.jl checks the two are bit-identical (one- and two-way, four windows of three substeps). The same file runs the physics of an externally driven child on synthetic boxes, with adaptive child steps that do not divide the window (limit $0.37\,Δt_p$): a parent at rest with a hydrostatic θ(z) leaves the child at rest (max $|u|$ = 1.0e-14 m/s after 12 steps, θ unchanged); a parent at (2, 1) m/s leaves the child at (2, 1) m/s exactly; the net boundary volume flux after the correction stays at 8.1e-18 $UA$ and max $|∇·u|$ at 5.7e-14 s⁻¹; a +1 K bubble near the lid of a calm child loses 5.0 % of its heat through the faces in 80 s and the time integral of boundary_scalar_flux books it to 8.9e-12 of the anomaly's heat (the periodic box keeps it to 6.8e-12); the parent's geostrophic tendency is exactly $(-fV_g, fU_g)$ and the child's IBM hook runs in every child step.

A child tracer the parent does not carry (in Berlin: ql, qr, the chemistry species) still needs a face condition: its plane is added after the core is built (add_open_scalars!) with the :inflow halo — the parent's value (here zero) where the face's normal velocity enters, the zero-gradient mirror where it leaves.

Feedback

  • :one_way (default, the Berlin default). The parent never sees the child. The parent of a one-way run is bit-identical to a standalone parent run with the same $Δt$ sequence; the test checks this over 20 adaptive steps. The boundary data at the start of a step is reused from the end of the previous one.
  • :two_way (or a TwoWayFeedback). After the child reaches $t_p + Δt_p$, its field set is anterpolated onto the covered parent cells outside a buffer zone, the interface flux of the open faces is corrected, the scalars are refluxed from flux registers booked at every stage of both domains, and the parent is re-projected; see the two-way nesting page. The conservation ledger is read with nest_conservation_error.

The historical step_nested!(parent, child, nest, dt; …) is a thin wrapper. It is a two-way NestedRun with a fixed $Δt$, a fixed substeps (default $\max r$) and no step validation.

Cost for the Berlin child

benchmarks/bench_nested_run.jl runs a 192 × 192 × 120 parent (6 × 6 × 3 km, Δx = 31.25 m, uniform Δz = 25 m) and a 1 × 1 km child at $(2, 2, 4)$. Both use Smagorinsky SGS and a 5 m/s westerly with noise. The run takes 12 adaptive parent steps of $Δt_p = 1.44 - 1.80$ s on one core. Since N-nest-d (#261) the child's sides and top are open nest faces (OpenBoundary) over a ground wall, with no sponge; the boundary column includes the per-stage refresh of the open-boundary buffers:

The child costs 0.16× the parent at 32 levels and 0.40× at 64 levels (N-nest-c, with a wall lid, a sponge and the periodic-projection child: 0.24× and 0.50×). In this case $n$ exceeds the horizontal ratio 2 because the child's vertical spacing is 4× finer, so the vertical term of the per-cell CFL sum weighs more. The two-way case now runs with the interface flux correction: N-nest-c had to switch it off, because it forced the parent's flux through the child's wall lid to $w = 0$.

Current limits

These are owned by other issues of the nesting epic (#246).

  • Resolved by N-nest-d (#261). The child's projection now sees the parent's boundary flux (open faces, all-Neumann solve, the single mass-flux correction), and a wall below a nest face above is a supported child (the Berlin shape). With N-nest-c's periodic child projection a Taylor-Green child blew up within about 10 steps unless the normal velocity was re-imposed and a sponge added; it now runs the 1000-step tests with neither, and the benchmark child has an open top.
  • Resolved by N-nest-e (#263): two-way with walls. The interface flux correction and the refluxing act on the child's open faces only; a child wall that is not the parent's wall is reported (the composite integral cannot be conserved across it).
  • Resolved by #329: the sponge and ∇·u. A NestSponge relaxes the child velocity on the predicted velocity of every stage, before the pressure solve (post_predictor!), so the child stays divergence-free ($2.8\times10^{-14}$ s⁻¹, against 1.6 s⁻¹ when relaxed after the step); the scalars are relaxed after the substep. A user core must call post_predictor! for the velocity part to act.
  • Resolved by #329: memory and allocations. Without a sponge the run's three field sets are halo-only (storage = :shell, 42 % of the child-sized sets for a $32^3$ child, 24 % for $64^3$); the rollback state of each domain and the two-way scratch are allocated once. Per nested step ($32^3$ parent, $32^3$ child at $r = 2$, step!), the nesting layer allocates 24 KiB one-way and 44 KiB two-way on top of the cores, against 2.9 MiB and 6.6 MiB before (the composite momentum integral alone allocated 858 KiB per call, twice per two-way step); the periodic halo fill no longer allocates, so a 32³ step! went from 1.6 MiB to 0. The state is concretely typed (no Any, Vector{Any}, Vector{Field} or Dict{Any,Any}), as the KernelAbstractions port (#291) needs.
  • Resolved by #329: refluxing at the parent boundary. The correction of a child face lying on the parent's own boundary goes to the wrapped cell across a periodic seam and nowhere across a non-periodic boundary (it used to wrap on every axis, so a child at the parent top would have corrected the parent's lowest layer); apply_flux_correction! takes the parent's periodic axes (nest_periodic_axes).
  • Open (#328): one parent–child pair per run; nest hierarchies, sibling nests, non-aligned parents and stretched children.
