Water table and unconfined aquifer

Issue #17 gives the soil column a prognostic water table. Without one, the base of the column is a fixed condition (free drainage, bedrock or a fixed head). With one, rain that percolates through the column is stored in an aquifer and drains laterally at a rate set by how high the water table stands, and a high water table in turn keeps the soil wet from below. The model is the SIMTOP groundwater scheme of Niu et al. (2005, 2007) as used in CLM4.0 (Oleson et al. 2010, §7.5), implemented as UnconfinedAquifer, a stateful AbstractSoilBottomBoundary of SoilWaterColumn in src/SoilColumn.jl.

using Aeolus, Aeolus.SoilColumn

col = SoilWaterColumn(clm5_soil_grid(), carsel_parrish_texture(:silt_loam);
                      bottom = UnconfinedAquifer())
set_water_table!(col, 5.0)            # hydrostatic start, table 5 m down
step_soil_water!(col, 86400.0; water_input = 5e-7)
water_table_depth(col), aquifer_baseflow_rate(col), total_water_storage(col)

1. Aquifer storage and the water table

The aquifer is a layer of thickness $D$ (default 25 m) below the base of the column $z_b$, with depth $z$ positive downward. It holds a water depth $W$. While the water table $z_\nabla$ is below the column, the aquifer drains by gravity with specific yield $S_y$ (0.2):

\[z_\nabla = z_b + D - \frac{W}{S_y}, \qquad W \le W_{max} = S_y D\]

(CLM4 eq. 7.165, where $W_{max}$ = 5000 mm and the default $W$ = 4800 mm places the table 1 m below the column.) When the aquifer is full the water table is inside the soil column. The aquifer can then store water only elastically, with the column's specific storage $S_s$, and its pressure head at the base is $\psi_b = (W - W_{max})/(S_s D)$. Hydrostatics then puts the table at $z_\nabla = z_b - \psi_b$. As a result $W \mapsto z_\nabla$ is continuous and monotone across both regimes, piecewise linear with capacity $C = -dW/dz_\nabla = S_y$ or $S_s D$.

CLM4 handles the table-in-column case differently. It keeps two stores and freezes the aquifer, extracting baseflow directly from the saturated soil layers and diagnosing the table from their air-filled porosity (eq. 7.167). Here the Richards solver already represents saturated layers, so a single store is enough. When the table is in the column, baseflow leaves the aquifer, and the aquifer draws water from the saturated column through the base.

2. Recharge

Recharge $q$ is the flux across the column base, positive downward. It is the steady Darcy flux between the bottom soil node ($\psi_N$ at $z_N$) and the water table ($\psi = 0$ at $z_\nabla$). When the table is in the column it is instead the flux to the saturated base ($\psi_b$ at $z_b$). CLM4 uses a virtual node halfway to the table (eq. 7.147–7.148), and Niu et al. (2007) use a single conductivity. Both replace the unresolved unsaturated zone by one mean conductivity. That works for downward drainage but fails badly for capillary rise from a deep table, because $K$ varies by orders of magnitude along the path.

This implementation instead takes $K$ exponential in $\psi$ between the two points, $K \propto e^{\alpha\psi}$ with $\alpha = \ln(K_2/K_1)/(\psi_2 - \psi_1)$, and integrates $q = K(1 - d\psi/dz)$ exactly (Gardner 1958):

\[q = \frac{K_1 - K_2 e^{-\alpha L}}{1 - e^{-\alpha L}}\]

(gardner_two_point_flux). This expression has three properties:

  • It is exact for a Gardner soil.
  • It reduces to Darcy's law when $K$ is uniform.
  • It vanishes for the hydrostatic pair $\psi_1 = \psi_2 - L$ in any soil, so hydrostatic equilibrium is a steady state of the discrete system.

Take a Gardner soil with $\alpha = 2$ m⁻¹, a node at $\psi = -10$ m and the table 5 m below it. The exact capillary rise is $4.5\times10^{-10}$ m s⁻¹. A logarithmic-mean conductivity gives $5.0\times10^{-7}$, 1100 times too much.

3. Baseflow

SIMTOP's topographically controlled lateral drainage (Niu et al. 2005; CLM4 eq. 7.159) is

\[q_{drai} = (1 - f_{imp})\, q_{max}\, e^{-f z_\nabla},\]

with $q_{max} = 5.5\times10^{-3}$ kg m⁻² s⁻¹ (5.5e-6 m s⁻¹) and $f = 2.5$ m⁻¹, the CLM4.0 global values (Oleson et al. 2010, below eq. 7.160). CLM4.5 replaced $q_{max}$ by $10\sin\beta$ kg m⁻² s⁻¹ ($\beta$ the mean topographic slope) and $f_{imp}$ by an ice impedance factor; those variants are not implemented. The frozen-soil impermeable fraction (CLM4 eq. 7.160, $\alpha = 3$) is

\[f_{imp} = \frac{e^{-\alpha(1-F)} - e^{-\alpha}}{1 - e^{-\alpha}},\]

where $F$ is the thickness-weighted ice mass fraction of the layers from the water table down to the base. $F$ stays zero until the freeze–thaw model (#16) fills θ_ice. Niu et al. (2005) derive $q_{max}$ from the grid cell's mean topographic index. That derivation is not implemented: $q_{max}$ is a parameter.

4. Time integration

Each accepted sub-step of the Richards solver proceeds in two stages.

  1. Implicit coupling. For the current bottom-node potential, the aquifer solves the backward-Euler balance $W_1 = W_0 + \Delta t\,[q(\psi_N, W_1) - q_{drai}(W_1)]$. In this balance the target point is frozen at its start-of-step depth, and its potential is the hydrostatic $z_t - z_\nabla(W_1)$. The balance is strictly increasing in $W_1$, so a safeguarded Newton iteration on a bracket always converges. The aquifer then hands the solver the tangent $q \approx a + b\,\psi_N$, including $dW_1/d\psi_N$. Without that term the coupling is unstable when the aquifer is full, because the elastic capacity is only $S_s D$ = 2.5e-4 m per m of head.
  2. Exact aquifer update. After convergence, $W$ is advanced by the exact solution of $dW/dt = q - q_{drai}(W)$ at the converged recharge. Within a regime $z_\nabla$ is linear in $W$, so $u = e^{f z_\nabla}$ obeys the linear equation $du/dt = (f/C)(q_0 - q u)$. Its solution is followed up to the crossing of $z_b$, if there is one, and then continued in the other regime. Both regimes relax towards the same $u_\infty = q_0/q$, so a path crosses at most once.

Baseflow over the sub-step is $q\Delta t - \Delta W$. Soil plus aquifer water (total_water_storage) therefore changes by exactly infiltration − extraction − baseflow. With no recharge, the scheme reproduces the SIMTOP recession

\[q_{drai}(t) = \frac{q_0}{1 + t/\tau}, \qquad \tau = \frac{S_y}{f q_0}, \qquad z_\nabla(t) = z_0 + \frac{1}{f}\ln\left(1 + \frac{t}{\tau}\right)\]

for any step length. Under steady recharge $R$ the table settles at $z_\nabla = \ln(q_{max}/R)/f$.
