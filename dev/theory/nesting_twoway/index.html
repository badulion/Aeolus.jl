Two-way nesting: anterpolation, refluxing and the secondary circulation

In a one-way nest the parent never sees the child. In a two-way nest the child's solution is fed back onto the parent once per parent step, after the child has subcycled to the parent's time (N-nest-e, #263). This page describes that feedback, TwoWayFeedback, and what it costs in conservation and stability. The pieces it is built from:

restriction (volume / mass measure, solid-aware)restrict_to_parent! (N-nest-a #250, N-nest-b #256)nesting

The maintainer's decision for the Berlin case is one-way nesting. Two-way is implemented and tested on synthetic boxes (below); N-berlin-twoway (#267) couples it to the Berlin run as an option, options[:nesting].mode = :two_way, which is unvalidated in the urban setting until the long-run stability campaign #331 (see the Berlin nesting page). Only after #331 validates it does two-way become the default for self-nesting.

The feedback, step by step

After the child substeps of a parent step, two_way_feedback!:

  1. anterpolates the child field set (u, v, w, θ, qv, tracers) onto the covered parent cells of the feedback mask;
  2. replaces the parent's velocity on the interface planes of the child's open faces by the child's flux (the interface flux correction; a wall or periodic child face is not an interface);
  3. refluxes θ, qv and the declared tracers from flux registers;
  4. re-projects the parent, so $∇·u = 0$ holds in the parent again.

At the start of the next parent step the child is projected onto its new boundary data (the parent changed after the previous window's end data; without this projection the child's first stage starts divergent near its open faces, and SSP-RK3, which combines that start state into its result, kept a third of it: $|∇·u|$ grew to $5\times10^{-9}$ in 200 steps of the vortex harness).

Anterpolation

Clark & Farley (1984) and PALM (Hellsten et al. 2021, §3.5, Eq. 15) set a covered parent value to the mean of the child values inside it,

\[φ_P \leftarrow ⟨φ_C⟩ = \frac{\sum_{c \in P} W_c\,φ_c\,ΔV_c}{\sum_{c \in P} W_c\,ΔV_c},\]

the conservative restriction of the nesting page: volume-weighted for the Boussinesq core ($W = 1$), reference-mass weighted for a sound-proof pair ($W = ρ̄$ or $ρ̄θ̄$), and solid-aware (building cells and faces are left out; a parent cell with no fluid child keeps its value). Velocities are area- (mass-flux-) weighted face means. It is idempotent: a second application changes nothing.

Relaxation (our extension; PALM replaces directly). With a time scale $τ$,

\[φ_P \leftarrow φ_P + \frac{Δt_p}{τ}\bigl(⟨φ_C⟩ - φ_P\bigr),\]

so a covered-parent deviation decays as $(1 - Δt_p/τ)^n$, with an e-folding time $-Δt_p/\ln(1 - Δt_p/τ) = τ\,(1 - Δt_p/2τ + …)$. $τ = Δt_p$ (and τ = nothing, the default) is direct replacement.

Where the parent is not anterpolated

feedback_mask leaves out:

  • the buffer zone: the outermost buffer parent cells next to every open face of the child (default 2, as in PALM). Next to its boundary the child's solution is still mostly its boundary data, i.e. the parent itself; feeding it back closes a loop through which noise at the interface can grow;
  • the canopy layer: cells whose lower face is below z_anterp (Hellsten et al. 2021, §3.5). In an urban child the parent cannot represent the canopy the child resolves (its buildings are a few cells of a mostly solid layer), and the solid-aware means of nearly solid layers are noisy;
  • the inflow band: exclude_width cells next to the child's inflow faces (exclude_sides = :inflow finds them from the sign of the child's face-mean normal velocity), so synthetic inflow turbulence (N-inflow-c) added at the child's inflow is never fed back.

Masked parent cells and faces are left bit for bit as the parent computed them. A velocity face is anterpolated when it lies strictly inside the covered region and both its parent cells are in the mask.

Refluxing with flux registers

The parent cells just outside the nest were advanced with the coarse flux through the interface, the child with the fine fluxes. The composite integral (uncovered parent cells plus all child cells) drifts by the difference unless it is put back (Berger & Colella 1989):

\[δΦ = Φ_\text{coarse} - \frac{1}{r_t r_{t'}}\sum_\text{fine}Φ, \qquad φ_\text{outside} \mathrel{+}= \pm\frac{δΦ\,A_p}{W_p V_p}.\]

The registers (NestFluxRegisters) hold $Φ = ∫F\,dt$ on the interface planes only (both grids). Scalars the cores transport (θ, a core qv, tracers updated inside body_force!) are booked by a hook that NestedRun chains in front of each domain's body_force!: the cores call it inside every stage, after the tendencies and before the predictor, when the halos, the velocities and the SGS coefficients are exactly those the stage used. The booked face flux is the one face kernel AdvectionSchemes.scalar_face_flux (shared with boundary_scalar_flux, #327), the sum of

  • the advective flux $u_n ϕ_f$ (the face reconstruction of the core's advection; :centered for θ and qv),
  • the molecular flux $-ν\,∂ϕ/∂n$ when the simulation is viscous,
  • the SGS flux $-s\,\tfrac12(K_L+K_R)\,∂ϕ/∂n$ with the coefficients of the closure's sgs_scalar_face_diffusivity, the face form of its add_sgs_scalar! (θ's diffusivity for θ, the tracer diffusivity for qv). A closure with a tensor scalar flux has no two-point face form and is refused,

times the stage weight of the core (1 for step!, $\tfrac16, \tfrac16, \tfrac23$ for the three Euler stages of step_rk3!, which is what their Shu–Osher combination amounts to) and $W$ for a sound-proof pair. Rejected (rolled-back) steps discard their bookings. A tracer that is not a simulation prognostic is refluxed when its owner declares its transport, tracer_fluxes = (:co2 => NestScalarFlux(scheme = :upwind),); qv is declared the same way where it is a tracer of the field set (a moist sound-proof nest) rather than a core prognostic.

Tracers moved by a separate transport. A transport that advects its tracers outside the core's stages — after the dynamics, with its own scheme, velocity and time step, like the Berlin case's forward-Euler WENO-Z5 slot — is invisible to the hook: booking its scheme at the core's stage state books the wrong flux. Such a tracer is declared with NestScalarFlux(applied_by = :transport) and the transport books exactly what it applies with book_nest_flux!(sim, name, ϕ, dt; scheme, diffusivity), called with the state it is about to update (a no-op for a simulation outside a two-way run). The domain's core then wraps the dynamics and the transport and declares the weights of its dynamics (nest_stage_weights). Measured (test/nest_twoway.jl, 100 steps, forward-Euler WENO-Z5 slot after step!): composite $∫$tracer drift 1.2e-14 per step booked by the transport, 2.9e-7 booked at the core stage (the pre-#327 way: right scheme, wrong velocity), 2.5e-6 not booked; a qv tracer of an anelastic pair, W-weighted slot, 20 steps: $∫ρ̄q_v$ drift 2.9e-15.

Volume-consistent refluxing. Each grid conserves volume, but their time-integrated volume fluxes through one face patch differ: the child's boundary velocity is interpolated in time over the parent step (and its stages read it at their own times), the parent's is its stage velocity. Refluxing the flux of $θ$ itself books $θ$ times that volume mismatch: for $θ ≈ 300$ K this heated and cooled the cells around the nest by up to kelvins per step and drove a 4 m/s circulation within ten minutes of the convective test below. The feedback therefore refluxes the flux of $ϕ - ϕ_\text{ref}$, $ϕ_\text{ref}$ the parent's mean of $ϕ$ over the covered cells, with the volume fluxes booked in one more set of registers. Both velocity fields are divergence-free, so the volume mismatches of all patches sum to zero over the closed interface: the total correction, and the exact conservation of the composite integral, do not depend on $ϕ_\text{ref}$; only the spurious redistribution $θ\,δV$ between patches is gone.

What conservation needs. A sponge (NestSponge) is a source and breaks it. A child face that is a wall or periodic must coincide with the parent's own wall or periodic seam: a child lid inside the parent blocks the child but not the parent, the parent's flux through that plane has no fine counterpart, and the run warns. The anelastic core's SGS flux of θ is in the $W$-weighted flux form (ONE-j, #460; see Sound-proof cores) and conserves $∫ρ̄θ$; its molecular term $ν∇²θ$ is unweighted and does not. The registers book the $W$-weighted diffusive flux, exact for the lateral planes.

Secondary circulation

Hellsten et al. (2021, §4.1, Fig. 8) found that two-way nests of a convective boundary layer develop a secondary circulation: a mean updraft of 0.4–0.9 m/s over the child, subsidence around it. They exclude differential surface heating (the surface flux is prescribed in both domains) and attribute it to "a slightly different divergence of the vertical heat flux between the region occupied by the child domain and the remaining parent domain due to different grid spacing". Anterpolation is exactly what makes the parent feel that difference, so this is a property of two-way nesting, not a defect of one scheme.

Our two-way CBL nests show a mean descent over the child instead (−0.36 m/s in the suite's single run, −0.42 m/s in the 4 h benchmark). #327 (M5) investigated whether that is a bug or a sampling artefact, with reduced runs (parent at 100 m, 2 h, the 2nd hour averaged, 4–6 seeds per configuration, paired one-way/two-way runs from the same initial noise; ≈ 70 min of runs in total):

(Means ± standard errors over the seeds; the one-way child mean, which the parent never sees, averages to zero within its error in every configuration, e.g. −0.002 ± 0.061 m/s in the suite case.) Standalone periodic CBLs of the suite case, which hold the same heat at every resolution (column integrals equal to 2e-4), are cooler below 800 m at the child's spacing than at the parent's: −0.080 K at 33 m against 100 m, the mean of seeds 1 and 2 (−0.087 K and −0.072 K; the suite runs seed 1), and −0.044 K below 500 m at 50 m against 100 m for the lapse-rate start; the finer CBL carries more of the surface heat into its entrainment zone.

What this shows:

  • the descent is not a sampling artefact: it is systematic across seeds, child sizes and child depths;
  • it is not produced by the coupling machinery: with r = 1 (the same physics on both grids) there is no circulation in any geometry, and the buffer width (0, 2, 4), refluxing (on or off) and the interface flux correction (a no-op in this case: the prolongation is flux-conserving, so the child's interface flux already is the parent's) do not change its sign. The anterpolation weighting is the volume-weighted child mean of Clark & Farley (1984) and Hellsten et al. (2021, Eq. 15);
  • it is thermally direct and has the sign of the resolution dependence: the anterpolated column takes the child's cooler lower CBL, is heavier than its surroundings, and sinks. A child top above the CBL does not remove it. This is the mechanism Hellsten et al. propose, with the sign set by how the model's CBL changes with grid spacing; in Aeolus's numerics (centred advection, Smagorinsky) a finer CBL is cooler below the inversion, and a mean descent over the child is the physical response of a two-way nest to that. The opposite sign in PALM implies its finer child was warmer; we have not run PALM's numerics to confirm this.

So the two-way coupling is conservative, stable and free of a coupling-made circulation, but the secondary circulation of a convective two-way nest is real and set-up dependent (≈ 0.05–0.2 m/s of descent here, growing with the resolution contrast and shrinking with the child's size). Two-way nesting is not validated against Hellsten et al.'s circulation; it reproduces their mechanism, not their sign. This is one reason the Berlin nest of the urban site stays one-way until its validation (#331), although two-way is the self-nesting default (ONE-f, #455). validation/nest_twoway_longrun.jl asserts that the suite run's circulation is thermally direct and that the standalone CBL is cooler at the child's spacing.

Mixed cores

A Boussinesq domain and an anelastic one may be nested either way round, one-way or two-way (NEST-p2, #482; NestCoreConversion). The two cores conserve different things: the Boussinesq core the volume flux ($\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u} = 0$, its mass $ρ_0 V$ with the constant $ρ_0 = p_{\rm ref}/(R_d θ_0)$), the anelastic core the mass flux ($\nabla\cdot(\bar ρ\mathbf{u}) = 0$). Across the interface the conserved quantity is the mass flux, and a scalar's flux is that mass flux times the (intensive) scalar. The nest therefore measures each side with its own density $W$ ($ρ_0$ or $\bar ρ(z)$), so that an intensive scalar is prolongated and restricted as a mass-weighted mean and a constant stays that constant, and converts the velocity. A covered parent layer $K$ of mass $W_{p,K}\,Δz_K$ with child mass $M_{c,K} = \sum W_c Δz_c$ sends the child

\[\sum_{c \in K} W_c\, u_c\, Δz_c = W_{p,K}\, u_K\, Δz_K ,\]

the parent velocity scaled by $r_K = W_{p,K} Δz_K / M_{c,K}$ before the conservative prolongation ($r_f = W_{p,f}/W_{c,f}$ at a horizontal face for $w$), and takes back $u_K = \sum W_c u_c Δz_c / (W_{p,K} Δz_K)$ in the anterpolation and the interface flux correction (the inverse scaling around the feedback; a face the feedback leaves alone keeps its value bit for bit). The flux registers of both sides book mass fluxes (the Boussinesq side's weighted by $ρ_0$), so the refluxing conserves the composite $\sum W θ V$.

Measured (test/model_nesting.jl; a 3.2 km deep parent, 16³ at Δ = 200 m, with a 1.6 km deep child at $r = 2$, where $\bar ρ/ρ_0$ differs from 1 by up to 12 % (anelastic parent) and 14 % (anelastic child)): the mass flux the boundary data carry through the west plane matches the parent's per face patch to $7\times10^{-16}$ (relative); two-way, the composite $\sum Wθ V$ changes by $1.0\times10^{-14}$ and $8.0\times10^{-15}$ over six windows and the interface mass-flux imbalance after the correction is 0. An anelastic child steps with the Euler step (the anelastic SSP-RK3 step takes no open faces).

Current limits

  • Refluxing needs a core with known stage weights in both domains: step!, step_rk3!, or a user core that declares them (nest_stage_weights). The IMEX cores (implicit θ diffusion) are refused with an ArgumentError and run two-way with TwoWayFeedback(reflux = false).
  • Two-way feedback needs parent and child of one core family (two Boussinesq sims, or two sound-proof sims of one system sharing one reference profile). A mixed pair has no consistent measure and is refused by every two-way entry point (NestedRun, anterpolate!, restrict_state_to_parent!); it nests one-way.
  • Two-way feedback needs a conservation box on every axis (#328): at least two parent faces inside the child must be child faces; a child without one nests one-way. In a NestHierarchy the children of a domain feed back after each of its steps (post order, the domain re-projected once after all of them); see nest hierarchies, non-aligned and stretched children.
  • With a buffer zone the re-projection is not idempotent: the anterpolated faces next to the buffer leave the parent divergent, and the projection moves them (and the interface faces: the ledger's :mass_after_projection). The next step's child boundary data take the projected parent.
