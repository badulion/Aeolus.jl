Sub-grid supersaturation in activation and droplet growth

Phase 45 (issue #13) — module TurbulentMicrophysics.

The sub-grid cloud PDF says how a cell's saturation state is distributed. This page covers what that distribution does to the droplets: which aerosol particles activate (Phase 42, AerosolCloud), and how the droplets then grow by condensation (Phase 41, Superdroplet). Both are delegated. The CCN spectrum is AerosolCloud.ccn_fraction, and growth is Superdroplet.grow_droplet through condensation_growth!. This module only supplies the distribution of supersaturation that they see.

Activation: the PDF-mean of the CCN spectrum

Phase 42 (Abdul-Razzak & Ghan 2000) computes one peak supersaturation $S_\max$ per cell from the mean updraught and activates every particle whose critical supersaturation is below it. For a log-normal mode with median critical supersaturation $S_m$ that fraction is

\[f_\mathrm{CCN}(S) = \tfrac12\,\mathrm{erfc}\!\left( \frac{2\ln(S_m/S)}{3\sqrt2\,\ln\sigma_g}\right),\]

which is ccn_fraction. arg_activation evaluates this same function at $S_\max$.

With unresolved turbulence, sub-volumes reach $S_\max + S'$, with $S'$ distributed with standard deviation $\sigma_S$ (the SubgridCloud.σ_S of issue #12, or given directly) and any of the issue #12 shapes. Each sub-volume activates its own particles, so

\[\langle f\rangle = \int f_\mathrm{CCN}(S)\,P(S)\,dS = \int \varphi(u)\,P\bigl(S > s_c(u)\bigr)\,du,\]

where $u = \ln(r_d/r_g)/\ln\sigma_g$ is the standardised dry radius and $s_c(u) = S_m e^{-3u\ln\sigma_g/2}$. The two forms are the same 2-D Gaussian probability integrated in opposite orders. sgs_ccn_fraction uses whichever has the smoother integrand: the radius form when the activation threshold is wider than one standard deviation in $u$, $\sigma_c/(1.5\ln\sigma_g\max(\bar S,\sigma_c)) \ge 1$, and the supersaturation form otherwise. With 2001 trapezoid nodes the result agrees with an independent Simpson integral to $1.6\times10^{-11}$.

Jensen: the sign follows the curvature

$f_\mathrm{CCN}$ is a sigmoid in $\ln S$. In $S$ it is convex on the steep low-$S$ flank, where few particles have activated, and concave once most of the mode has. Expanding about the mean,

\[\langle N\rangle - N(\bar S) = \tfrac12 N''(\bar S)\,\sigma_S^2 + O(\sigma_S^4),\]

so sub-grid fluctuations add droplets where the spectrum is convex and remove them where it is concave. For the two-mode test population (300 cm⁻³ at 40 nm and 50 cm⁻³ at 150 nm, $\kappa = 0.6$, 283 K), the measured ratio $\Delta N/(\tfrac12N''\sigma^2)$ at $\sigma = 0.05\bar S$ is 0.999, 1.013, 1.003, 1.003, 0.999, 1.002 and 1.007 for $\bar S$ = 0.01, 0.02, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.4 and 0.8 %. The first two values are in the convex regime and the rest are concave. At $w = 0.5$ m s⁻¹ ($S_\max = 0.21\,\%$, concave) $N_d$ falls from 182.3 to 181.5, 179.1 and 171.4 cm⁻³ for $\sigma_S$ = 0.025, 0.05 and 0.1 %.

$\sigma_S = 0$ returns arg_activation bit for bit.

Limitations. The fluctuation is superimposed on $S_\max$. It does not feed back on the competition for vapour that sets $S_\max$ (ARG's $\eta$), and with $w \le 0$ nothing activates, as in Phase 42. The common alternative integrates over a sub-grid updraught PDF (Ghan et al. 1997; Morrison & Gettelman 2008). This scheme fluctuates the supersaturation directly, as diagnosed from the scalar variances.

Growth: a random walk in $r^2$

Diffusional growth obeys $r\,dr/dt = G(S - a/r + b/r^3)$. At a single $S$ every droplet gains the same $r^2$, so the radius spectrum narrows. This is the Phase 41 result, and the reason condensation alone cannot make rain. In a turbulent cloud each droplet takes its own path through the supersaturation field and gains $2G\int S\,dt$ along it (Cooper 1989). Here droplet $i$ sees $\bar S + \sigma_S\xi_i(t)$, where $\xi_i$ is a unit Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process with correlation time $\tau$, updated exactly, with $\tau = \infty$ freezing each droplet in its sub-volume. For an initially monodisperse population this gives

\[\mathrm{Var}(r^2) = (2G)^2\,2\sigma_S^2\tau\left[t - \tau\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right)\right]\]

(supersaturation_integral_variance; Paoli & Shariff 2009; Sardina et al. 2015). The width of this variance grows as $\sigma_S t$ while $t \ll \tau$, and as $\sqrt{2\sigma_S^2\tau t}$ without bound for $t \gg \tau$.

Measured with 4000 superdroplets (sampling error 2.2 %), the ratio of $\mathrm{Var}(r^2)$ to the analytic value is 0.991 (τ = 5 s) and 0.992 (τ = ∞).

sgs_condensation_step! is Phase 41's two-way-coupled condensation_step! with a per-droplet $S$. Vapour removal and latent heating go through apply_condensate_tendency! using the total condensed mass, so the cell's total water is conserved to round-off for any $\sigma_S$. The fluctuations only decide which droplets receive the condensate. The step is sub-stepped to at most $\tau/10$, which puts the error in $\mathrm{Var}\int S\,dt$ at $(h/\tau)^2/12 \approx 0.08\,\%$. With $\sigma_S = 0$ it is condensation_step! bit for bit. Stepping a grid cell by cell (index = k) advances only that cell's droplets' fluctuations, so each $\xi_i$ ages once per time step however many cells there are; fl.σ_S is one value and should be set to each cell's $\sigma_S$ before its call.

Starting from a 5 μm population with $\varepsilon_0 = 0.115$ in a cell at 12 % supersaturation (1000 superdroplets, τ = 10 s, 25 s), the relative dispersion $\varepsilon = \sigma_r/\bar r$ depends on $\sigma_S$ as follows:

$\varepsilon$0.02320.02400.02670.03580.0603

In every run $|\Delta W/W| \le 5.4\times10^{-16}$, and the mean radius differs by less than 0.5 % between runs. After 100 s the mean supersaturation has been consumed. The $\sigma_S = 0$ spectrum has stopped narrowing, with $\varepsilon$ = 0.0233, while the $\sigma_S = 0.2\,\%$ spectrum has broadened to 0.0714.

Entrainment

At a cloud edge the entrained air is warm and dry, so $\overline{q_t'\theta_{li}'} < 0$. In the Mellor mapping $\sigma_s^2 = a_q^2\sigma_{q}^2 - 2a_qb\,\overline{q_t'\theta_{li}'} + b^2\sigma_\theta^2$ the covariance term then adds to the variance. Surface thermals are moist and warm, and partly cancel it. With $\sigma_q = 3\times10^{-5}$ and $\sigma_\theta = 0.03$ K the correlations $\rho = -0.8, 0, +0.8$ give $\sigma_S = 0.63, 0.50, 0.32\,\%$ and $\varepsilon$ = 0.092, 0.073, 0.049. Entrainment broadens the spectrum the most, and total water is conserved in each case.

Caveat on $\sigma_S$ inside cloud. SubgridCloud.σ_S is the clear-air spread, $\sigma_s/(a\,q_{sl})$. Inside a cloud, droplets absorb supersaturation fluctuations on the phase-relaxation time $\tau_\mathrm{phase}$ (a few seconds), so the sustained in-cloud $S'$ is smaller, and its correlation time is at most $\min(\tau_\mathrm{eddy}, \tau_\mathrm{phase})$. The clear-air value is appropriate where droplets meet freshly entrained, unmixed air. Deeper inside a cloud it is an upper bound.

A bug this found

Before the Phase 41 fix in this change, a pure-water droplet (no solute) in any subsaturation evaporated to the 10⁻¹² m radius floor in a single step: a 10 μm droplet at $S = -0.1\,\%$ over 0.5 s came out at 1e-12 m instead of 9.995 μm. Without a Raoult term, the Kelvin term makes the backward-Euler residual positive at the floor, and that alone was taken to mean "no root". In the stochastic benchmark droplets briefly see $S < 0$, and $\mathrm{Var}(r^2)$ came out 61 times the analytic value. The code was wrong, not the expectation. It now brackets the root from the residual's minimum, and a regression test is in superdroplet.jl.
