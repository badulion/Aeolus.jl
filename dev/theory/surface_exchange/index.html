Surface exchange and Monin–Obukhov similarity

Every surface model — soil, canopy, urban facet, snow, ice, ocean, sub-grid tile — ends in the same question: given the skin state and the air at the lowest model level, what are the momentum, heat and moisture fluxes between them? This page develops the answer implemented once, in src/SurfaceExchange.jl, which the surface models delegate to.

1. Similarity and sign convention

In the surface layer the fluxes are nearly constant with height and set the scales

\[u_*^2 = -\overline{u'w'},\qquad \overline{w'\theta'} = -u_*\theta_*,\qquad \overline{w'q'} = -u_* q_* .\]

Fluxes are positive upward: a surface warmer than the air has $\theta_* < 0$ and a sensible heat flux $H = -\rho c_p u_*\theta_* > 0$. Net radiation $R_n$ is positive into the surface and the substrate flux $G$ positive downward, so the energy balance is $R_n = H + LE + G$.

Monin–Obukhov theory (1954) states that the dimensionless gradients are universal functions of $\zeta = z/L$, with the Obukhov length

\[L = \frac{u_*^2\,\theta_v}{\kappa\, g\, \theta_{v*}} = -\frac{u_*^3\,\theta_v}{\kappa\, g\,\overline{w'\theta_v'}}, \qquad \theta_{v*} = \theta_*(1+\varepsilon' q) + \varepsilon'\theta q_*,\]

$\varepsilon' = R_v/R_d - 1 \approx 0.608$. Buoyancy is carried by the virtual potential temperature, so evaporation alone can destabilise the surface layer. $\zeta > 0$ is stable, $\zeta < 0$ unstable.

\[\phi_m(\zeta) = \frac{\kappa z}{u_*}\frac{\partial u}{\partial z},\qquad \phi_h(\zeta) = \frac{\kappa z}{\theta_*}\frac{\partial \theta}{\partial z}.\]

Integrating from the roughness length $z_0$ to $z$ with $\psi(\zeta) = \int_0^\zeta [1-\phi(x)]/x\,dx$ gives the profiles

\[U(z) = \frac{u_*}{\kappa}\Big[\ln\frac{z}{z_{0m}} - \psi_m\!\Big(\frac{z}{L}\Big) + \psi_m\!\Big(\frac{z_{0m}}{L}\Big)\Big] \equiv \frac{u_*}{\kappa}\,\Phi_m ,\]

and likewise $\theta_a - \theta_s = (\theta_*/\kappa)\,\Phi_h$ with $\psi_h$ and $z_{0h}$, $q_a - q_s = (q_*/\kappa)\,\Phi_q$ with $z_{0q}$. Separate roughness lengths for momentum, heat and moisture matter: over land $z_{0h}$ is typically an order of magnitude below $z_{0m}$.

2. Stability functions

The default family, BusingerDyerBeljaarsHoltslag, joins two standard forms at $\zeta = 0$.

Unstable — Businger et al. (1971) as recast by Dyer (1974) for $\kappa = 0.40$:

\[\phi_m = (1-16\zeta)^{-1/4},\qquad \phi_h = (1-16\zeta)^{-1/2},\]

integrated exactly by Paulson (1970), with $x = (1-16\zeta)^{1/4}$:

\[\psi_m = 2\ln\frac{1+x}{2} + \ln\frac{1+x^2}{2} - 2\arctan x + \frac{\pi}{2},\qquad \psi_h = 2\ln\frac{1+x^2}{2}.\]

Stable — Beljaars & Holtslag (1991), with $a = 1$, $b = 2/3$, $c = 5$, $d = 0.35$:

\[\psi_m = -\Big[a\zeta + b\Big(\zeta - \frac{c}{d}\Big)e^{-d\zeta} + \frac{bc}{d}\Big],\qquad \psi_h = -\Big[\Big(1 + \frac{2a\zeta}{3}\Big)^{3/2} + b\Big(\zeta - \frac{c}{d}\Big)e^{-d\zeta} + \frac{bc}{d} - 1\Big].\]

Unlike the log-linear $\phi = 1 + 5\zeta$, these do not produce a critical Richardson number: $\phi_m$ grows only linearly for large $\zeta$, so a very stable surface layer keeps a small but finite exchange instead of decoupling. The corresponding $\phi$ follow from $\phi = 1 - \zeta\,d\psi/d\zeta$.

The tests check these values, $\psi(0) = 0$, $\phi(0) = 1$, continuity across $\zeta = 0$, and $d\psi/d\zeta = (1-\phi)/\zeta$ numerically (max error 1.3 × 10⁻¹¹ over $-5 \le \zeta \le 10$).

Other families (e.g. the COARE convective form of Grachev & Fairall) subtype AbstractStabilityFunctions and add methods to psi_m, psi_h; the solver needs nothing else.

3. Solving for the Obukhov length

Given $U$, $\theta_a$, $q_a$ at the forcing level and $\theta_s$, $q_s$ at the skin, $u_*$, $\theta_*$, $q_*$ are explicit functions of $\zeta$; substituting them into the definition of $L$ leaves one scalar equation

\[F(\zeta) = \zeta - \frac{z_m\,\kappa\, g\,\theta_{v*}(\zeta)}{u_*(\zeta)^2\,\theta_{va}} = 0 .\]

solve_surface_layer brackets the root in $[\zeta_{min}, 0]$ (unstable) or $[0, \zeta_{max}]$ (stable) — the sign of $F(0)$ tells which — and applies the Illinois variant of regula falsi. The textbook fixed-point iteration $L_{n+1} = L(u_*(L_n), \theta_*(L_n))$ is not used: it oscillates or diverges in strongly stable conditions. A root beyond the bracket is clamped to its end and reported with converged = false. For the dry case with $z_m = z_h$ the equation is equivalent to the classical

\[Ri_B = \frac{g z (\theta_{va} - \theta_{vs})}{\theta_{va} U^2} = \zeta\,\frac{\Phi_h}{\Phi_m^2},\]

which the tests verify to 8 × 10⁻¹⁶.

Calm winds. As $U \to 0$ over a heated surface $u_* \to 0$ and $L \to 0$; physically, convective eddies keep mixing. The solver uses $U_e = \max(\sqrt{U^2 + w_g^2}, U_{min})$ with an optional gustiness $w_g$ (Beljaars 1995 suggests $w_g = 1.2\,w_*$) and a floor $U_{min} = 0.1$ m s⁻¹.

Transfer coefficients. Referred to $U_e$,

\[C_D = \frac{\kappa^2}{\Phi_m^2},\quad C_H = \frac{\kappa^2}{\Phi_m\Phi_h},\quad C_E = \frac{\kappa^2}{\Phi_m\Phi_q},\]

so $\tau = \rho C_D U_e^2$, $H = \rho c_p C_H U_e(\theta_s - \theta_a)$, $E = \rho C_E U_e (q_s - q_a)$. In the neutral limit $C_D = [\kappa/\ln(z/z_0)]^2$, which the solver reproduces to 2.7 × 10⁻¹⁶.

4. From a surface model to fluxes

turbulent_fluxes(s, forcing) works for any AbstractSurface. The air temperature is referred to the surface along the dry adiabat, $\theta_a = T_a + \Gamma_d z_{ref}$, $\theta_s = T_s$; density and $c_{pm}$ come from MoistThermodynamics. The skin humidity uses the "β" moisture availability,

\[q_s = q_a + \beta\,[q_{sat}(T_s, p) - q_a] \;\Longrightarrow\; E = \beta\,\rho\, C_E U_e\,[q_{sat}(T_s) - q_a],\]

so a dry surface ($\beta = 0$, the default) has zero moisture flux — setting $q_s = 0$ instead would turn it into a vapour sink — and open water has $\beta = 1$. Models with stomatal or soil resistances, dew physics or ice skins override surface_specific_humidity and surface_latent_heat.
