Grid nesting: conservative staggered prolongation

A nested child grid refines a box of a parent grid by integer ratios $(r_x, r_y, r_z)$ (each $\ge 1$, odd, even or mixed, e.g. the Berlin $(2, 2, 4)$). Every operator between the two grids lives in Aeolus.Nesting (API); this page derives the three on which everything else rests — prolongation, restriction and refluxing — and quotes the numbers they are tested at (test/era2_nesting.jl).

Geometry

The child must align to parent cell boundaries: the child origin sits on a parent face, $N_\text{child} = r\,n$ for an integer number $n$ of covered parent cells, and $\Delta_\text{child} = \Delta_\text{parent}/r$ in each direction. NestedGrid checks all three and refuses a misaligned child with an error that names the direction. On this geometry child face $q\,r + 1$ (counted from the nest's low face) coincides with parent face $q$, for every $q = 0, \dots, n$.

Prolongation: the primitive-function construction

On a finite-volume grid a cell-centred value is a cell average $\bar f_I = \frac{1}{\Delta}\int_{x_{I-1/2}}^{x_{I+1/2}} f\,dx$. A child value must be an average over the child cell, and the $r$ child averages inside a parent cell must average back to the parent value — otherwise prolongation creates or destroys the conserved quantity.

Both requirements are met by interpolating the primitive function (Colella & Woodward 1984, §2),

\[F(x) = \int_{x_{s-1/2}}^{x} f\,dx', \qquad F(x_{s-1/2+q}) = \Delta \sum_{q'=1}^{q} \bar f_{s-1+q'} ,\]

which the parent averages give exactly at the parent faces. For a stencil of $p$ parent cells, a degree-$p$ polynomial $P$ through the $p+1$ face values of $F$ is built, and the average over child cell $[a, b]$ is the difference quotient

\[\bar f^{\,c}_{[a,b]} = \frac{P(b) - P(a)}{b - a}.\]

Because $P = F$ at the parent faces, the $r$ child averages of one parent cell telescope to $(F(x_{I+1/2}) - F(x_{I-1/2}))/\Delta = \bar f_I$: conservation is structural, independent of $p$ and of $r$, not a property of the accuracy. $P'$ reproduces polynomials of degree $p-1$, so the reconstruction is formally order $p$. In 3-D the weights are a tensor product of 1-D weights, precomputed once per (direction, rule, order) into a plan cached on the NestedGrid.

Staggering: nodal in the normal direction, conservative across it

A C-grid velocity is not a cell average in every direction. $u$ on a FaceX is a point value in x (it sits on the face) and a face-area average in y and z (it is the volume flux through the face divided by its area). The prolongation therefore uses, per direction,

locationxyz
CellCenter (θ, qv, tracers)conservativeconservativeconservative
FaceX (u)nodalconservativeconservative
FaceY (v)conservativenodalconservative
FaceZ (w)conservativeconservativenodal

where nodal is $p$-point Lagrange interpolation of the face values. Two exact properties follow.

  • Coincident faces. The Lagrange basis evaluated at a node is exactly 0 or 1 in floating point, so a child face lying on a parent face takes the parent value in the normal direction without round-off. With refinement only along the normal (e.g. $(3, 1, 1)$ for $u$) the coincident child faces equal the parent faces bit for bit; this is tested for $r = 3$ and $r = 5$.
  • Flux preservation. On a coincident face the tangential rule is conservative, so the $r_t \times r_{t'}$ child faces covering a parent face carry, summed, exactly the parent volume flux. The parent and the child agree on the mass entering the nest before any correction.

A purely conservative (cell-average) treatment of $u$ would get the tangential flux sums right but smear the normal position; a purely nodal one would get the point values right but not the fluxes. The mixed rule is what makes a staggered nest both accurate and conservative (the same split underlies the interactive nesting of Clark & Farley 1984 and the PALM nest of Hellsten et al. 2021).

Restriction

The child → parent operator is the exact adjoint in the conservative directions: a volume-weighted mean of the $r_x r_y r_z$ child cells of each covered parent cell for cell-centred fields, and an area-weighted mean of the $r_t r_{t'}$ child faces on each aligned parent face for velocities. With prolongation it satisfies

\[\mathcal R\,\mathcal P = \mathcal I\]

on the covered parent cells and faces, for any $r$ and $p$.

Refluxing

In a two-way nest the parent cells just outside the child were advanced with the coarse flux through the interface, the child with the fine fluxes. The composite integral (uncovered parent cells plus all child cells) then drifts by the difference. With time-integrated flux registers $\Phi = \int F\,dt$ on both grids, the Berger & Colella (1989) correction books the mismatch into the outside parent cell,

\[\delta\Phi = \Phi_\text{coarse} - \frac{1}{r_t r_{t'}}\sum_\text{fine}\Phi, \qquad \bar f_\text{outside} \mathrel{+}= \pm\frac{\delta\Phi\,A_p}{V_p},\]

($+$ on the nest's low face, $-$ on the high face), after which the composite integral changes only by what crossed the parent's own boundary. reflux! does this for one scalar and reflux_scalars! for every scalar of a NestInterpState field set.

Field sets

The operators above are per-field. A NestInterpState names the set they act on: $u, v, w$, $θ$, $q_v$ when the simulations are moist, and any extra cell-centred scalar passed as scalars = (:co2 => (child_field, parent_field), …). Prolongation, the halo write, the sponge, restriction, the composite integral and refluxing iterate over the set through one code path per member, so a tracer initialised equal to $θ$ stays bit-identical to $θ$ through all of them (tested).

Stretched parent, reference-mass measure, solid-aware restriction

The operators above were written for a uniform parent and a volume average. Three generalisations (N-nest-b, #256) make them usable for the Berlin nest, whose parent is z-stretched, whose sound-proof variant weights by the reference density, and whose child contains buildings.

A z-stretched parent

NestedGrid accepts a StretchedGrid parent (child stays a uniform RegularGrid). The child bottom and top must lie on parent faces, and every covered parent layer must hold exactly $r_z$ child layers: the overlap sits in a uniform part of the parent, which may stretch freely above it. The face heights and layer thicknesses come from StretchedGrids.zfaces / cell_thickness (one source of truth).

The primitive-function construction carries over unchanged once it is written in a coordinate instead of in "parent cell numbers": the primitive $F(z_j) = \sum_{q \le j} \bar f_q\,\Delta z_q$ is known exactly at the actual parent faces $z_j$, a degree-$p$ Lagrange polynomial on those (non-uniform) nodes interpolates it, and a child value is the difference quotient across the child cell. The weight of parent cell $q$ for the child cell $[a, b]$ is

\[w_q = \frac{\Delta z_q}{b - a} \sum_{j \ge q} \big(L_j(b) - L_j(a)\big),\]

which reduces to the uniform weights for $\Delta z_q \equiv 1$. Child faces inside the overlap are generated from the parent faces, so a child face lying on a parent face is that face bitwise, the basis is exactly 0/1 there, and $\mathcal R \mathcal P = \mathcal I$ holds to round-off. Nodal ($w$) weights are Lagrange weights on the actual face heights. On a uniform RegularGrid parent the integer plans of the previous sections are used, bit for bit.

The reference-mass measure

For the sound-proof systems (MassWeightedSimulation: anelastic, with $W = \bar\rho$; pseudo-incompressible, $W = \bar\rho\bar\theta$) the conserved quantity is $\int W\phi\,dV$, not $\int\phi\,dV$. The same construction in the mass coordinate $\mu(z) = \int W\,dz$ gives

  • restriction $\phi_P = \sum W_c \phi_c \Delta V_c / \sum W_c \Delta V_c$ for scalars and $u_P = \sum W_c u_c A_c / \sum W_c A_c$ for the horizontal-normal faces (the parent face carries the children's summed mass flux); on $w$ faces $W$ is common to all children and cancels;
  • prolongation conserving $\int W\phi\,dV$ exactly and keeping a constant constant ($F$ is linear in $\mu$).

nest_measure(nest, parent_sim, child_sim) builds it from the sims' constraint_weights, after asserting that parent and child share one ReferenceState profile: nest_reference_mismatch compares the parent's $\bar\rho\,\Delta z_p$ with the sum over its $r_z$ children. One smooth profile sampled on both grids differs only by the midpoint rule, $(\Delta z_p^2 - \Delta z_c^2)/(24 H^2)$ (measured $8.64\times10^{-6}$ against a predicted $8.63\times10^{-6}$ for 125 m layers, $H = 8.4$ km); a child surface 1 K warmer gives $3.5\times10^{-3}$ and is refused (default tolerance $10^{-4}$). With $W$ constant the measure is the volume measure and the operators are the uniform ones bit for bit.

Two parent cell masses exist. The coordinate gives a covered parent layer the summed mass of its children (what makes prolongation and restriction exact); the parent simulation conserves its own $W_p \Delta z_p$. Uncovered parent cells — the ones the composite integral counts and refluxing corrects — use the parent's own mass, so the composite $\int W\phi\,dV$ is exactly what the two simulations conserve. (The first implementation used the summed mass there too and refluxing left a $2.9\times10^{-12}$ drift: the midpoint-rule difference times the interface flux. It is now $3.6\times10^{-15}$.)

The sound-proof core has no $q_v$ field, so a moist anelastic nest carries $q_v$ as a tracer of the field set (scalars = (:qv => (child_qv, parent_qv),)); it is transferred in the mass measure like every other member.

Solid-aware restriction

A child containing IBM buildings must not hand the zero velocity inside a building to the parent (Hellsten et al. 2021, §3.5). With solid (the child's SolidMask or a Boolean solid array, such as the Berlin case's) restriction averages fluid child cells only, and fluid child faces only (both neighbouring cells fluid). A parent cell or face with no fluid child keeps its value. The preserved quantity is the fluid-weighted integral $\sum_\text{fluid} W\phi\,\Delta V$. N-nest-e (#263) builds its canopy-restricted anterpolation on this primitive.
