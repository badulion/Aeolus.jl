Clouds and microphysics: overview

The clouds-and-microphysics work (Phases 39–45) replaced the Kessler / two-moment scaffold with schemes that represent cloud microstructure: a shared thermodynamic contract, a P3 bulk scheme, a superdroplet reference, aerosol activation, ice nucleation, fog and visibility, and sub-grid supersaturation. This page shows how the pieces fit together, gives the theory of the three phases that have no page of their own (39, 41, 42), and collects the measured validation. The detailed pages are listed at the end.

The contract: one thermodynamics for every scheme

MoistThermodynamics plays the role for clouds and microphysics that RadiationCore plays for radiation: every scheme takes temperature, saturation, latent heat and buoyancy from it, so two schemes cannot disagree about whether a parcel is saturated.

Latent heat and saturation are consistent by construction. The latent heat follows Kirchhoff's relation,

\[L_v(T) = L_{v0} + (c_{pv} - c_l)(T - T_0),\]

and the saturation vapour pressure is the analytic integral of the Clausius–Clapeyron equation $d\ln e_s/dT = L_v/(R_v T^2)$ with that same $L_v(T)$:

\[\ln\frac{e_s(T)}{e_0} = \frac{L_{v0} - (c_{pv}-c_l)T_0}{R_v} \left(\frac{1}{T_0} - \frac{1}{T}\right) + \frac{c_{pv}-c_l}{R_v}\ln\frac{T}{T_0},\]

and the same over ice with $L_s$ and $c_i$. A fitted formula (Tetens, Bolton) is accurate near 273 K and drifts in the cold upper troposphere, which is where ice microphysics needs it. The residual of Clausius–Clapeyron is 5.2e-12 (liquid) and 4.2e-12 (ice); the liquid–ice gap peaks at 27.00 Pa at 261.5 K. Extrapolated 100 K to the boiling point with nothing fitted, $e_s$ reaches 99,754 Pa, 1.55 % low: the constant-heat-capacity assumption, recorded rather than tuned.

Two saturation adjustments, for two different questions (#82):

  • saturation_adjustment is a diagnosis: given the prognostic $(θ_{li}, q_t)$, which partition into vapour and condensate is consistent with them? It conserves $θ_{li}$ and is what a scheme that carries $θ_{li}$ needs (TurbulentMicrophysics, CAPE / CIN).
  • isobaric_saturation_adjustment is a process: condensation at constant pressure, which conserves the moist enthalpy referenced to liquid water (moist_enthalpy), $h = (c_{pd} q_d + c_l q_t)\,T + L_v(T)\,q_v - L_f(T)\,q_i$, so that condensing $Δq$ warms the parcel by exactly $c_{pm}ΔT = L_v(T_0)\,Δq$. P3, the fog adjustment and the fog column use it.

Using the diagnosis as a process overstates the latent heating by 1.2 % at 900 hPa and 6.4 % at 600 hPa; most of that gap comes from the Exner exponent $R_m/c_{pm}$ taken at the current partition. Moist static energy is the invariant of neither: it misses 24 % of $L\,Δq$ in an isobaric condensation.

Köhler theory lives here too. Three modules (AerosolOptics, Superdroplet, AerosolCloud) each carried their own; they had drifted far enough (a surface tension of 0.0728 against 0.0720 N m⁻¹ at 298 K, 1.2 % in critical supersaturation) to disagree about whether a particle activates. The κ-Köhler form of Petters & Kreidenweis is now the one implementation, and the three agree to 1.6e-12.

Superdroplets (Phase 41)

Superdroplet follows Shima et al. (2009). Each superdroplet stands for $ξ$ identical real droplets. In a cell with $N_s$ superdroplets, $\lfloor N_s/2 \rfloor$ random pairs are tested per step instead of all $N_s(N_s-1)/2$; the pair probability is scaled up by the ratio of the two counts. In a successful collision with $ξ_j ≥ ξ_k$ each of the $ξ_k$ droplets of $k$ absorbs one droplet of $j$ and $ξ_j$ drops by $ξ_k$ ("all or nothing"), so mass is conserved exactly. Diffusional growth is integrated implicitly in $r^2$ with Kelvin and Raoult terms. Against Golovin's analytic solution for the additive kernel the $L^1$ error is 0.037 at 1200 s, 0.080 at 2400 s, 0.118 at 3600 s (2¹⁵ superdroplets), mass is conserved to 5e-15, and the error falls with $N_s$ — a wrong multiplicity rule would converge to a wrong answer instead.

Aerosol activation (Phase 42)

AerosolCloud uses Abdul-Razzak & Ghan (2000): the peak supersaturation of a rising parcel from a closed-form balance between the supersaturation source $αw$ and the condensation sink of the activating modes, and the activated fraction of each log-normal mode from its critical supersaturation. An explicit adiabatic parcel model is the reference: a particle activates when its critical supersaturation lies below the parcel's $S_{max}$, because crossing the Köhler barrier is irreversible for an ascending parcel (testing whether the droplet has grown past $r_c$ at the instant of $S_{max}$ reported zero activation in every case — the first bug this module's tests caught). Over w = 0.1–5 m s⁻¹ and Na = 100–1000 cm⁻³ ARG under-predicts ``S{max}`` by 8–24 % and the activated fraction agrees with the parcel to 1–25 % (mean 7 %), the accuracy the literature claims for it.
