Prognostic SGS TKE (Deardorff–Moeng) and Reynolds stress

Phase 56 (P56-b, #182). The 1.5-order closure of Deardorff (1980) carries the subgrid-scale turbulent kinetic energy $e$ as a prognostic variable; the eddy viscosity follows from it, not from the resolved strain alone. Aeolus implements it with the coefficients of Moeng (1984) as TKEClosure in the module Aeolus.PrognosticSGS (API), an AbstractSGSClosure of the closure contract.

The model

\[\frac{∂e}{∂t} + \mathbf u·∇e = P_s + P_b − ε + ∇·(2ν_t∇e),\]

\[ν_t = c_k\, l\sqrt e, \qquad κ_t = \Big(1 + \frac{2l}{Δ}\Big)ν_t, \qquad ε = c_ε\frac{e^{3/2}}{l}, \qquad c_ε = 0.19 + 0.51\,\frac{l}{Δ},\]

\[l = \min\Big(Δ,\; 0.76\,\frac{\sqrt e}{N},\; κ\,d_{wall}\Big)\]

with $c_k = 0.1$, $Δ = (Δx\,Δy\,Δz)^{1/3}$, the stability limit only for $N^2 > 0$, the wall cap $κ d_{wall}$ ($κ = 0.4$, $d_{wall}$ from IBM.wall_distance) when a wall distance is given, and $c_ε$ multiplied by 3.9 at the first level above a surface (Moeng 1984; IBM.first_level_cells). $N^2$ is the one of the stability contract, Operators.buoyancy_frequency_squared! (P58-a, #170), with the virtual potential temperature when the case is moist. Given a condensate field (qc, with the pressure p; #239) it is the $N^2$ of cloudy air: condensate loading, and in saturated cells the saturated $N^2$ of a moist-adiabatic parcel (Durran & Klemp 1982). That is Deardorff's (1980) stratocumulus buoyancy, so a moist-neutral fog or cloud layer has $l = Δ$ instead of the stable-layer $0.76\sqrt e/N$ (Turbulence stability).

Two limits fix the constants' meaning:

  • Neutral equilibrium ($P_s = ε$, $l = Δ$): $e = (c_k/c_ε)Δ^2 S^2$, so $ν_t = (C_sΔ)^2 S$ with $C_s = c_k^{3/4}c_ε^{-1/4} = 0.1944$ — a Smagorinsky closure with $C_s = 0.194$, about 14 % above the 0.17 of the static default.
  • Critical Richardson number. In stable shear, with $l = 0.76\sqrt e/N$ below $Δ$, $P_s + P_b = ε$ divided by $c_k l\sqrt e$ reads $S^2 − (1 + 2x)N^2 = (0.19 + 0.51x)N^2/(c_k 0.76^2)$ with $x = l/Δ$: it is linear in $x$, so the equilibrium is explicit, and it has a root $x > 0$ only for $Ri = N^2/S^2 < Ri_c = 1/(1 + 0.19/(c_k\,0.76^2)) = 0.233$ (tke_critical_richardson). Above it the equilibrium TKE is zero: in equilibrium the closure switches itself off without a stability function. In a transient $ν_t$ stays positive while $e$ decays.

No stability function on top. The $0.76\sqrt e/N$ length is the closure's own stability response, so no TurbulenceStability factor $f_m(Ri)$ is applied to it, and the StabilityCorrected wrapper of P55-e (#187), which multiplies a Smagorinsky-type $ν_t$ by such a factor, must not wrap a TKEClosure.

No horizontal–vertical anisotropy. The closure has one filter width $Δ = (ΔxΔyΔz)^{1/3}$ and one $ν_t$, $κ_t$ in every direction, as in Deardorff (1980) and PALM; the $κ_h ≠ κ_v$ length scales $Δ_h = \sqrt{ΔxΔy}$, $Δ_v = Δz$ of StabilityCorrected are by design not part of it. On the Berlin grid ($Δx/Δz = 2.5$, $ℓ_h = 1.842$, $ℓ_v = 0.2947$) the neutral equilibrium of TKEClosure therefore mixes $1/ℓ_v = 3.39×$ more vertically and $ℓ_h = 1.84×$ less horizontally than a StabilityCorrected Smagorinsky closure of the same $C_s = 0.194$ ($(0.1944/C_s)^2$ more again for a smaller $C_s$: 4.44× and 1.41× less for 0.17, 12.8× and 2.05× more for the Berlin cfg.C_s = 0.1; tke_equilibrium, $S = 0.1$ s⁻¹, $N = 0$). The rotated scalar tensor of IsentropicDiffusion is not offered for a TKEClosure either; both are possible follow-ups.

Discretization

Coefficients (sgs_coefficients!, pure) are cell-local: $l$, $ν_t$ and $κ_t$ from the committed $e$ and the current $N^2$. tke_length_scale also returns $s = \sqrt e/l$, the rate every sink is written with; it stays finite as $e → 0$ in stable air ($s → N/0.76$), where $l → 0$.

Shear production — the dual of the momentum operator. The SGS momentum tendency of the contract is the Laplacian flux form $∂_j(ν_t ∂_j u_i)$ of add_eddy_viscosity_u! (and _v!, _w!): normal fluxes with the cell $ν_t$, cross fluxes on the cell edges with the average of the four surrounding cells (weights 0.25). Its kinetic-energy sink $Σ \mathbf u·\mathbf T_{sgs} V$ is rearranged, flux by flux, into a sum over cells: every flux $f_L = ν_L δu_L/Δ$ contributes $−u_p(±f_L)/Δ$ at each velocity point $p$ it enters, and that is returned to the cells of $ν_L$ with the same weights. dual_strain! builds the velocity factor $D$, so that $P_s = ν_t D$ and

\[\sum_c P_s V = −\sum \mathbf u·\mathbf T_{sgs}(\mathbf u, ν_t)\, V\]

exactly, for any $ν_t$. In the interior each flux contributes $(δu/Δ)^2$, so $D ≥ 0$ (for $u = Sz$, $D = S^2$); the shares of halo cells are folded onto the interior cells they copy (periodic image, or mirror at a wall), which is exact because a halo $ν_t$ equals its image. Each cell computes its own $D$ (KA-l, #304): it gathers the shares destined for it, and those of the halo cells it is the image of, from the velocity points around it, so the kernel runs on any number of threads with the same bits. $D$ is built from the face and edge differences of the momentum kernels, not from Operators._velocity_gradient_at: that tensor's off-diagonal entries are centred over two cells, and their square is not the dual of the edge-flux operator.

Shear production at wall-modelled walls (#232). Where a wall model replaces the stencil's flux through an immersed wall (WallModels.remove_stencil_wall_flux! takes $ν_{t,e}(q_N − q_F)/Δ_d^2$ out of the tendency of a wall face $F$, $N$ the IBM-zeroed neighbour, and apply_immersed_wall_stress! applies $τ = a Δ_d q_F$), the dual above would still book the removed stencil share $ν_{t,e} q_F(q_F − q_N)/Δ_d^2$ as production — energy the resolved flow never lost — and none of the work of the wall stress. With the walls attached (attach_immersed_walls!) that share is taken out of $D$ at the four cells around the edge, as dual_strain! put it there, and the wall stress times the resolved shear across the wall, $τ\,q_F/Δ_d = a q_F^2$ per unit volume ($a$ from WallModels.immersed_wall_drag!, the one evaluation of the law), is added, half to each cell the face separates:

\[\sum_c P_s V = −\sum \mathbf u·\big(\mathbf T_{sgs} − \mathbf T_{sgs}^{wall}\big) V + \sum_{\text{wall faces}} a\, q_F^2\, ΔV ,\]

the kinetic energy the SGS operator the step applies and the wall stress take out of the resolved flow. Booking all of the wall stress's work as production is the constant-stress log layer: its shear production integrated from $z_0$ to the first face, $∫ τ_w\,∂U/∂n\,dn = τ_w U$, is the work of the wall stress at the face velocity. The test (test/tke_facades.jl (removed)) checks the budget to 0 relative error, the removed share to 4.6e-15, the wall work against the energy apply_immersed_wall_stress! removes to 4.7e-8 ($δt = 10^{-6}$), and, for uniform flow along a wall, $Σ P_s V/A = u_*^3 \ln(h/z_0)/κ$ to round-off.

Buoyancy production — the dual of the κ_t operator. $P_b = −κ_t N^2$. On the uniform grid the $N^2$ of buoyancy_frequency_squared! is the average of the two face gradients around the cell, which is how the three-argument scalar kernel averages $κ_t$ to the faces, so $Σ P_b V = (g/θ_0) Σ z\,T_θ V$: the potential energy the SGS heat flux moves. A prescribed surface heat flux (θ_flux_bottom, θ_flux_top of the Boussinesq core) sits on the boundary face and contributes $(g/θ_0)F/2$ to the adjacent cell, as the interior faces do.

Transport. $−∇·(\mathbf u e)$ with the flux-limited AdvectionSchemes.advect_positive! (WENO-Z5 when the halo is ≥ 3, else the limited centred flux) and $∇·(2ν_t∇e)$ with the three-argument add_eddy_diffusion_scalar! (solid-masked when a mask is given). The Berlin case transports $e$ in its :transport stage instead (transport = false).

Time integration (advance_sgs!, once per accepted step after the projection; once per accepted piece of an adaptive step). With the coefficients of $e^n$ and the new state's $N^2$:

\[e^* = e^n + Δt\,\big(T + P_s + \max(P_b, 0)\big), \qquad e^{n+1} = \frac{e^*}{1 + Δt\,(c_ε s^n + r_b^n)},\]

with $r_b = κ_t N^2/e = (1 + 2l/Δ)c_k N^2/s$ the buoyant destruction rate ($N^2 > 0$). Both sinks are linear in $e^{n+1}$ (a Patankar linearisation), so $e^{n+1} ≥ 0$ whenever $e^* ≥ 0$ and its fixed point is the exact equilibrium $P = ε$. The issue proposed $\sqrt{e^*}$ in the denominator; that form's fixed point is biased by $O(Δt)$ (the equilibrium tests to 1e-6 could not pass with it at a usable step), so the rate is lagged at $e^n$, identical in pure decay. dissipation = :exact integrates $de/dt = −c_ε e^{3/2}/l$ exactly at frozen $l$. If transport makes $e^*$ negative it is set to zero and booked (tke_statistics: clips, clipped_energy); none of the tests books a clip. $e = 0$ in solids. Because $e$ is the closure's memory, sgs_state / restore_sgs_state! make a step step_adaptive! rejects exact.

$e = 0$ is a fixed point of the equation ($ν_t = 0$ produces nothing): initialize_tke! seeds the local equilibrium of the initial flow (tke_equilibrium), with a floor e_min.

Surface value. With per-column friction velocities the first air cell holds $e_s = u_*^2/\sqrt{c_k c_ε}$, the TKE of a constant-stress layer in equilibrium ($ν_tS = u_*^2$, $ν_tS^2 = ε$); the energy this sets is booked (surface_energy). The Berlin case takes $u_*$ from its MOST surface exchange (bus.u_star).

On a stretched vertical grid (#259)

The Berlin nesting parent runs this closure on a StretchedGrid (25 m levels to 500 m, then growing by 1.08 per level to 100 m). Four things change, each tested in test/tke_stretched.jl (removed):

  • Filter width. $Δ_k = (Δx\,Δy\,Δz_k)^{1/3}$ per level, $Δz_k$ the cell thickness — the width the core's Smagorinsky uses there (Deardorff 1970). $l$, $c_ε$, $κ_t = (1 + 2l/Δ_k)ν_t$ all take the local value.
  • Shear production. The stretched Laplacian momentum operator differences the vertical fluxes of $u, v$ over the centre spacing $Δz_f$ and divides by the cell thickness $Δz_c$ ($w$ the other way round). The duality is volume-weighted, $Σ_c ν_c D_c V_c = −Σ_p u_p T_p V_p$, so each flux's share is accumulated per unit horizontal area (multiplied by the control-volume height of its velocity point), folded onto the interior as on the uniform grid, and divided by $Δz_c$ of the receiving cell. Measured: the identity holds to 2.2e-16 for random fields on a 1.08-stretched grid. For $u = Sz$, $D = S^2 (z_{k+1} − z_{k−1})/(2Δz_k)$ (to 2.7e-15): exactly $S^2$ on the uniform levels, $S^2(1 + 1.5\times10^{-3})$ on the geometric part, +1.7 % in the last uniform cell under the first stretched one.
  • Buoyancy production. $N^2$ is the difference of the two face values over the cell thickness, $(θ_{k+1} − θ_{k−1})/(2Δz_c)$, the per-cell share of the stretched κt operator's potential-energy change: $Σ(−κN^2)V$ equals ``(g/θ0)Σ z\,T_θ V$to 4.0e-15. For a linear θ it carries the same 1.5e-3 / 1.7 % factor as$D``.
  • Transport. AdvectionSchemes.advect_positive! now runs on a StretchedGrid with the centred reconstruction (the vertical flux difference over $Δz_c$), flux-limited toward first-order upwind so that $e$ stays positive; the WENO / TENO stencils assume uniform spacing and raise an ArgumentError there. A 30-step stretched CBL books no clip (the plain centred flux form needed 370).

A :uniform StretchedGrid reproduces the RegularGrid closure ($e$ to 8.5e-17 relative after 20 convective steps without transport). A solid mask is not supported on a stretched grid.

Differential Reynolds-stress closure (P56-d, #188)

ReynoldsStressClosure is the second closure in PrognosticSGS. It goes past eddy viscosity in the way Deardorff (1973) did: the six subgrid stresses $τ_{ij} = \overline{u_i'u_j'}$ (m² s⁻²) are prognostic, and the momentum flux is the stress itself, not $−2ν_tS_{ij}$:

\[\frac{Dτ_{ij}}{Dt} = P_{ij} + G_{ij} + C_{ij} + Φ_{ij} − \tfrac23 ε δ_{ij} + ∇·(2ν_t ∇τ_{ij}),\]

  • production $P_{ij} = −τ_{ik}∂_k\bar u_j − τ_{jk}∂_k\bar u_i$, exact;
  • buoyancy $G_{ij} = (g/θ_0)(δ_{i3}h_j + δ_{j3}h_i)$ with the Daly & Harlow (1970) heat flux $h_i = −c_θ τ_{ij}∂_jθ_v$;
  • Coriolis $C_{ij} = −2Ω_k(ε_{ikm}τ_{mj} + ε_{jkm}τ_{mi})$, which is trace-free; Ω = :auto takes the f-plane $(0, 0, f/2)$ that the resolved momentum uses;
  • pressure–strain, the LRR-IP model (Launder, Reece & Rodi 1975) with the buoyancy term of Gibson & Launder (1978): $Φ_{ij} = −C_1\frac{ε}{e}(τ_{ij} − \tfrac23 eδ_{ij}) − C_2(P_{ij} − \tfrac13P_{kk}δ_{ij}) − C_3(G_{ij} − \tfrac13G_{kk}δ_{ij})$, with $C_1 = 1.8$, $C_2 = 0.6$, $C_3 = 0.5$;
  • dissipation $ε = c_εe^{3/2}/l$ and the length $l$, both shared with the TKE closure by function call: tke_length_scale (with $N^2$ of θv and the wall cap) and [`tkedissipationcoefficient`](@ref Aeolus.PrognosticSGS.tkedissipationcoefficient) (Moeng's 3.9 at the first level). ``νt$for the$2νt`transport comes from [tkeeddycoefficients`](@ref Aeolus.PrognosticSGS.tkeeddy_coefficients).

Half the trace is the TKE equation. $Φ$ and $C$ are trace-free, so $\tfrac12 τ_{kk}$ obeys $De/Dt = −τ_{ik}∂_k\bar u_i + (g/θ_0)h_3 − ε + ∇·(2ν_t∇e)$. For an eddy-viscosity stress this is the P56-b equation term by term.

The Daly–Harlow coefficient. The scalar flux is the tensor diffusivity $K_{ij} = c_θτ_{ij}$ (daly_harlow_coefficient). The default, C_θ = :tke, sets $c_θ = \tfrac32κ_t/e$ with the TKE closure's $κ_t = (1 + 2l/Δ)ν_t$. An isotropic $τ = \tfrac23eI$ then diffuses scalars with exactly the P56-b $κ_t$. In Daly & Harlow's notation this is $C_θ = \tfrac32(1 + 2l/Δ)c_kc_ε$: 0.315 at $l = Δ$ and 0.099 in the stable $l → 0$ limit, inside the 0.1–0.3 range of the published constants. The value is a calibration, not a published constant. A number C_θ gives Daly & Harlow's $c_θ = C_θe/ε$.

Homogeneous-shear equilibrium. Because $ε/e = c_ε\sqrt e/l$ grows with $e$, the SGS closure has a true equilibrium, $P = ε$. Unlike the RANS asymptote, $P/ε$ does not depend on an ε equation. $P_{ij} + Φ_{ij} = \tfrac23εδ_{ij}$ with $P = ε$ gives (reynolds_stress_shear_equilibrium):

\[\frac{τ_{22}}{e} = \frac{τ_{33}}{e} = \frac23 − \frac23\frac{1 − C_2}{C_1} = 0.518519,\quad \frac{τ_{11}}{e} = \frac23 + \frac{2(1 − C_2) + \tfrac23C_2 − \tfrac23}{C_1} = 0.962963,\quad \frac{−τ_{13}}{e} = \sqrt{\frac{1 − C_2}{C_1}\frac{τ_{22}}{e}} = 0.339450 .\]

These are the anisotropies $b_{11} = 0.148$, $b_{22} = b_{33} = −0.074$, $b_{13} = −0.170$. Tavoularis & Corrsin (1981) measured a larger anisotropy in which the two cross-stream components differ (roughly $b_{11} ≈ 0.20$, $b_{22} ≈ −0.14$, $b_{33} ≈ −0.06$, $b_{12} ≈ −0.15$). The IP model's $τ_{22} = τ_{33}$ is a known deficiency. The comparison is quoted here and not asserted.

Discretization

Momentum. add_sgs_momentum! adds $−∂_jτ^{dev}_{ij}$ through the one stress operator, Operators.add_stress_divergence! (P55-b). The deviatoric diagonals stay at the cell centres; the isotropic part is a pressure. Each off-diagonal is the mean of the four cells around its edge (stress_edges!). An edge on a wall, or one that touches a solid cell, carries no stress, because the wall stress belongs to the surface exchange. The stress keyword of the cores is ignored.

Production is the dual of the momentum operator. The cell-centred velocity gradient of $P_{ij}$ (dual_velocity_gradient) is built as follows:

  • the diagonal is the face-to-face difference through the cell;
  • each off-diagonal is the mean of its edge differences over the cell's four edges in that plane, with closed edges counting zero.

Summation by parts then gives $−Σ_{faces}\mathbf u·∇·τ^{dev}V = Σ_{cells}τ^{dev}_{ik}G_{ik}V$ exactly, walls and solids included. The resolved kinetic energy that the stress removes is the SGS energy that $P$ produces; the isotropic part pairs with the discrete divergence. For a linear velocity field $G$ is the exact gradient away from closed edges.

Scalars. add_sgs_scalar! applies $K = c_θτ$ (built in sgs_coefficients!) through the one tensor operator, Operators.add_tensor_diffusion_scalar! (P55-c), for θ and qv. There is no second flux operator. sim.νt holds ``νt$and `sim.κt` holds$\operatorname{tr}K/3``, which the adaptive step's diffusion limit uses.

The commit (advance_sgs!, once per accepted step):

  1. With transport, the transport is explicit. The diagonals are advected with the flux limiter (advect_positive!), the off-diagonals with the plain scheme, and every component is diffused with $2ν_t$. Prefer WENO-Z5, which needs a halo of 3. With centred differences the off-diagonals overshoot, and in a 16³ decaying box (amplitude 0.3 m/s, 60 s) the realizability projection fired 9140 times and added 2.8 % of the SGS energy. With WENO-Z5 it did not fire.

  2. Coriolis is applied exactly, as the rotation $τ ← RτR^T$ with $R = \exp(−2Δt[Ω]_×)$ (Rodrigues; coriolis_rotation). The trace, the determinant and realizability are kept.

  3. The other sources are linear in $τ$ once $ε/e$ and $c_θ$ are lagged at $τ^n$. They are integrated by backward Euler on the 6×6 system $(I − ΔtL)τ^{n+1} = τ^*$ (reynolds_stress_step). A steady state of the update satisfies $Lτ = 0$ with $L$ evaluated at $τ$ itself, so it is exactly a root of the stress equations.

    Backward Euler inverts a growing mode once $Δtλ ≥ 1$; production faster than the sinks is such a mode. The step is therefore split into $⌈2Δtμ_∞(L)⌉$ sub-steps whenever the logarithmic norm $μ_∞(L)$, which bounds every mode's growth rate, is positive. Bug caught by the Berlin smoke check: before the split, a single 1–2 s step at a street corner (run without the wall cap) made the stresses so unrealizable that the projection added 3.96e4 m⁵ s⁻², more SGS energy than the whole domain held. After the split the same run needs 0 corrections.

  4. Realizability (Schumann 1977) is enforced next. Every cell is projected onto the positive semi-definite cone: the eigenvalues are clipped at zero (project_realizable), which gives the nearest realizable stress in the Frobenius norm. Every correction and the energy it adds are booked (reynolds_stress_statistics).

  5. With surface, the first air cell of each column is set to the LRR-IP constant-stress layer:

    • $τ_{13}, τ_{23} = −u_*^2(\cos α, \sin α)$, against the cell's wind direction $α$;
    • $e_s = u_*^2/0.3395$;
    • the normal stresses $(0.963, 0.519, 0.519)e_s$ in the wind frame.

    This is the counterpart of the TKE closure's $e_s$.

sgs_state / restore_sgs_state! make a rejected adaptive step exact. step_imex! refuses the closure through the contract trait Operators.sgs_explicit_stress, because its implicit operator can only represent an eddy viscosity.

Stabilisation. The issue allowed an optional eddy-viscosity split ($τ = τ^{RSM} − 2ν_tS + 2ν_tS$, with the last term implicit). It was not needed and is not implemented, so the momentum flux is the Reynolds stress alone, and the claim of going beyond eddy viscosity holds without qualification. Three runs were stable without it: the decaying box (200 RK3 steps), the Berlin smoke runs (12 adaptive steps, with and without the wall cap), and the 30 min flagship runs.

  4. Rotation. Over 10 periods, $τ(t) = R(2Ωt)τ_0R^T$ holds to 5.1e-14, with $R$ from the matrix exponential, independent of the Rodrigues code. $\operatorname{tr}τ$ is invariant to 3.8e-14 and $\det τ$ to 1.0e-13. RK4 of the right-hand side's $C_{ij}$ returns to $τ_0$ after one period of $2Ω$.

Validation against filtered DNS (P56-e, #192)

The TKE closure and the Reynolds-stress closure are compared a priori in time with filtered DNS, with Smagorinsky ($C_s = 0.17$) and dynamic Smagorinsky as baselines (the Lagrangian scale-dependent model, #186, is not in this branch's stack). Fixture: validation/fixtures/sgs_validation.jl (module SGSValidation; it was the sgs_decay run type from EX-d #441 to ONE-l #475); validation run: validation/sgs_validation.jl. The 96³ example and the wall-bounded surface-mounted cube (below) were run types of Aeolus.RunDrivers; they are not configurations of the model and were dropped with it (ONE-l, #475): the filtered-DNS study is the a-priori study the single-model design drops (the a-posteriori comparison is examples/dns_vs_les/), and the cube is a channel case (a periodic channel held at its bulk velocity). Their numbers below are the record of the runs made before.

Method. The P56-c DNS of decaying isotropic turbulence (2π box, spun up from a fixed seed to $t_0 = 2$; see LES filtering) is continued from $t_0$ for two turnovers $T = L/u'$ ($u'$ and the integral scale $L$ measured at $t_0$) in three ways: unchanged; with f-plane rotation $f = u'/(\mathrm{Ro}\,L)$, Ro = 0.2; and with a uniform background stratification $N = u'/(\mathrm{Fr}\,L)$, Fr = 0.3. The DNS velocity is filtered with the Gaussian of width $Δ = 4h$ and resampled on the four-times-coarser grid by Fourier truncation. The exact subfilter stress $τ_{ij} = \overline{u_iu_j} − \bar u_i\bar u_j$ and the subfilter heat flux are sampled at the coarse centres by the 2×2×2 mean, which keeps $τ$ positive semi-definite (a Fourier resample of $τ$ does not). Every LES ($Δ = 4h$, the same ν, f and N) starts from the filtered DNS at $t_0$. The TKE closure gets $e = ½τ_{kk}$ (initialize_tke!(c, τ)) and the Reynolds-stress closure gets $τ_{ij}$ (initialize_reynolds_stress!(c, τ); no cell needed a realizability correction).

Stratification in a periodic box. The simulations carry $θ = θ_0 + θ'$ with $θ'$ periodic. The background $Γz$ ($Γ = N^2θ_0/g$) enters as the body force $−Γ\bar w$ on $θ'$, through the body_force! hook of step_rk3!. The face average of θ in the w equation and the centre average of w in the θ equation are adjoint, so $K + PE$ falls at every step (asserted). The closures need the full θ for $N^2$ and for the flux $−K∇θ$, so a fixture wrapper hands them θ₀ + Γ(z − zmid) + θ′, with halos continued across the periodic boundary with the jump ``ΓLz``.

Expectations of the issue that failed at this Re_λ. The tests keep them as @test_broken, with the numbers.

  • Resolved KE within 10 % at $t_0 + 2T$ holds for both Smagorinsky baselines but not for TKE (−11.6 %) or Reynolds stress (−11.7 %). TKE: e within 25 % of ½⟨τ_kk⟩ fails too: $e$ is 2.8 times the DNS value. Both failures have the same cause, which the test asserts. The Deardorff constants ($c_k = 0.1$, and $c_ε = 0.70$ at $l = Δ$; Moeng & Wyngaard 1988) are inertial-range values, but $Δ = 4h ≈ 8η$ sits in the near-dissipation range. With the exact $e$ at $t_0$:

    • the production $c_kΔ\sqrt e\,2⟨\bar S\bar S⟩ = 0.184$ is 1.9 times the exact transfer $Π = −⟨τ_{ij}\bar S_{ij}⟩ = 0.096$;
    • the dissipation $c_εe^{3/2}/Δ = 0.073$ is 0.68 times the viscous dissipation of the subfilter scales, $2ν(⟨SS⟩ − ⟨\bar S\bar S⟩) = 0.109$.

    So $e$ grows for a turnover while the DNS's decays, and $ν_t$ follows it. The trace of the Reynolds-stress equation is the same TKE equation, so its $e$ grows the same way (0.125 at $2T$).

  • The τ₃₃/τ₁₁ of the Reynolds-stress closure correlates with the DNS, ρ > 0.5. Pointwise, this holds while the LES still follows the DNS: 0.71 at $T/8$ and 0.56 at $T/4$, against 0.17 and 0.20 for TKE. It then falls to 0.02 at $2T$. Every LES decorrelates pointwise from the DNS within a turnover: the correlation of $τ_{33}$ itself is 0.15 at $2T$. The test therefore asserts it at $T/8$ and $T/4$, and asserts the mean ratio over the whole run.

  • The Reynolds-stress heat flux is closer to the DNS than TKE's. Both are within 2×, but TKE is closer (1.35× against 1.49×). The DNS flux oscillates with the buoyancy period $2π/N = 1.9T$ and is counter-gradient for about a third of the run, reaching +1.4. No down-gradient closure follows this. The Reynolds-stress $e$ also decays more slowly (0.029 against 0.015 at $2T$), which makes its flux larger.
