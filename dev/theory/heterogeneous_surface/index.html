Heterogeneous-surface validation

The tile mosaic (Sub-grid surface tiles) and the blending height (Blending height) claim that a grid cell holding several surfaces gets its fluxes right if each surface exchanges with the cell's air on its own and the fluxes are area-averaged. This page tests that claim against the only reference that has every flux at every point: an LES in which the heterogeneity is resolved. It then compares the resolved flow with published LES of the same kind of surface.

Set-up

A dry convective boundary layer over strips of hot (310 K) and cool (300 K) ground, 50 % each, normal to $x$. Both surfaces have $z_0 = 0.1$ m and $z_{0h} = z_0/10$. The initial mixed layer is at 300 K up to 600 m with 6 K km⁻¹ above. This is close to the heterogeneous cases of Margairaz et al. (2020), as described by Wanner, Calaf & Mauder (2022). The roughness lengths are the same, and so is $f = 10^{-4}$ s⁻¹ in the windy runs. They used random patches whose temperatures have a ±5 K standard deviation about the mean; here there are two strip temperatures at ±5 K.

The skin temperatures are prescribed, and the fluxes are not. Avissar & Schmidt (1998) prescribed the heat flux. With a prescribed flux, tiled aggregation of the heat flux would be exact by construction. Here each column's flux depends on its own first-level air through Monin–Obukhov similarity, as in a coupled land surface (Patton et al. 2005).

The harness is test/support/patchy_surface.jl (the example driver examples/validation/patchy_surface/driver.jl until ONE-g, #456). It is a Boussinesq LES with Smagorinsky SGS and SSP-RK3 with one projection per stage. Every surface column is a SurfaceExchange.AbstractSurface, and its fluxes come from the contract's turbulent_fluxes. The heat flux goes into the first level, and the stress acts along the local wind. Three surface modes use that one code path:

modewhat a column holdsmeaning
:resolvedthe hot or the cool surfaceheterogeneity resolved
:tileda SurfaceMosaic of both (0.5/0.5)heterogeneity sub-grid, tiled
:parameterparameter_aggregate(mosaic): one 305 K surfaceparameter aggregation

The coupling conserves heat. The column heat content changes by the applied flux to $10^{-10}$ relative, and the flux is accumulated with the RK3 stage weights 1/6, 1/6, 2/3. Over a uniform surface the three modes agree to $8×10^{-13}$ K after 200 s.

A-priori: tiles forced by the LES's own air

The resolved domain-mean flux $\overline{w'θ'_0}$ is compared with the mosaic's flux under several choices of forcing air. 50 m grid, 3.2 × 1.6 × 1.6 km, mean over the second hour (the former examples/validation/patchy_surface_les.jl, (removed by ONE-g, #456)):

  • Patch air. Each tile is forced by the mean air over its own patches. The tiles then reproduce the resolved flux to 0.1–2.5 %, which is the uniform-surface level (−2.5 %). The mosaic therefore adds no error of its own. What remains is the one-point Monin–Obukhov closure applied to averaged air.
  • Domain-mean air. This is what one grid cell of this size would see. The error grows with λ/zi and reaches −5.8 % at λ/zi = 3.2. A patch-locked circulation makes the air over the hot strips warmer than the domain mean, and the mean air does not know this.
  • Parameter aggregation misses 22–37 %. The flux is strongly convex in the skin temperature: the cool strips sit below the mixed-layer temperature and are stably stratified, with almost no flux. A single surface at the mean temperature averages that nonlinearity away.
  • BlendedMosaic, with $l_b$ at the first level and forced by the mean air at 125 m, is within 1.2 % with wind or at λ/zi < 1. At λ/zi = 3.2 in calm conditions it is −12 %, because one Monin–Obukhov layer does not describe the mean profile under a patch-locked circulation.

The wind used for the domain-mean forcing matters more than the tiles do. In free convection the mean wind vector is ≈ 0.03 m s⁻¹. Tiles forced by it sit on the solver's $U_{min}$ floor and are +16 to +34 % off (uniform surface included). The Beljaars (1995) gustiness $1.2 w_*$ gives +8 to +21 %. Forcing with the mean speed is −1 to −6 %. This is a statement about the grid-cell wind that couples to a surface scheme, not about tiling.

The tiled run falls short of the 3.2 km strips for the same reason as the a-priori test: at λ/z_i ≳ 3 the resolved circulation moves warm air over the hot strips, and horizontally uniform tiles cannot represent that.

Comparison with published strip LES

Patton, Sullivan & Moeng (2005) ran LES coupled to a land-surface model over wet/dry strips with 2–30 km wavelength (1 < λ/zi < 18). The conference abstract of that work (NASA NTRS 20030068489) states that 4 < λ/zi < 9 "induces the strongest organized flow fields (up, wp) while heterogeneity with smaller or larger λ/z_i induces little organized motion". The paper's abstract adds that "surface fluxes in the middle of the patches are small compared to the patch edges".

Here the sweep was run at 100 m resolution, 12.8 × 1.6 × 1.6 km, mean over 1.5–3 h. $A_u$, $A_w$ are the rms of the phase-averaged first-level $u$ and of $w$ at 350 m, over $w_*$:

  • Reproduced. The near-surface circulation converges on the hot strip from both sides, with the updraft above it. It strengthens from λ/z_i ≈ 0.8 to 5.5, by 3.5×. The flux is smallest mid-patch, where the converging air is warmest and the wind weakest. The mid-patch dip deepens with λ, from 2 % to 26 %.
  • Not reproduced. The decline above λ/zi ≈ 9. Here ``Au$keeps growing to λ/z_i = 10.6, and$A_w`` at 350 m is nearly flat (0.33–0.42) rather than peaked. Possible reasons, none tested: the prescribed skin temperature (Patton et al. coupled soil moisture, which weakens the forcing where the circulation dries or cools the surface); runs of 3 h that are short against the λ/U adjustment time of the largest strips; a 1.6 km lateral domain and 100 m cells.
  • Background wind. Wanner, Calaf & Mauder (2022, Sect. 3 and Fig. 5) describe the LES of Margairaz et al. (2020): the large cellular circulations at Ug = 1 m s⁻¹ "disappear with increasing wind speed". Here a 5 m s⁻¹ geostrophic wind across the strips reduces ``Au/w_*`` from 0.76 to 0.024, a factor 30. That is below the phase-average noise of the calm uniform surface (0.045) but above that of the windy uniform surface (0.009), so a weak patch-locked signal remains.

validation/heterogeneous_surface.jl repeats the comparison on a 100 m grid (32 × 16 × 16, 1.25 h), with three seeds checked. It asserts:

  • conservation (the identity of the three code paths is in test/heterogeneous_surface.jl);
  • tiles with patch air within 3 %, and with mean air within 10 %;
  • parameter aggregation below 80 % of the resolved flux;
  • the tiled LES within 8 % of the λ = 0.8 km strips;
  • the windy mosaic and BlendedMosaic within 3 %;
  • $A_u$ at λ/z_i ≈ 3 more than 2× that at ≈ 0.9, which is itself more than 3× the uniform-surface noise;
  • convergence on the hot strip, mean ascent over it and descent over the cool strip, and a mid-patch flux below 0.95 of the edge flux;
  • the windy $A_u$ below 0.1 of the calm one.

What is not validated

  • No field observations. The patchy-terrain campaigns (LITFASS-98, LITFASS-2003) publish area-averaged fluxes for a real landscape of forest, lake, farmland and villages. Reproducing them needs the vegetated land surface (#21), which does not exist yet, and the campaign's land-use and forcing data. The comparison above is with published LES, and only qualitatively: the numbers of Patton et al. were not available to compare against.
  • Moisture: the surfaces are dry, so the scalar dissimilarity that Patton et al. report for wet/dry strips is not tested.
  • Roughness heterogeneity in a resolved LES. The effective roughness is compared with the published LES of Bou-Zeid et al. (2007) in Blending height, not with an Aeolus LES.
