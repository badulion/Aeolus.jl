LES filtering & subgrid closure

Why filter?

Real turbulent flows have energy across a huge range of scales — in the atmospheric boundary layer, from the depth of the layer (~1 km) down to the Kolmogorov microscale (~1 mm). Direct numerical simulation would need 10¹⁸ grid points to resolve them all at meteorological Reynolds numbers. That is not going to happen.

Large-eddy simulation side-steps this by filtering the equations. We separate every field into a resolved (large-scale) part and a subgrid-scale (SGS) part:

\[u_i = \bar u_i + u_i''\]

where the overbar is a spatial filter of width Δ (in practice, the grid spacing). Substituting into the Boussinesq momentum equation and filtering yields:

\[\frac{\partial \bar u_i}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}(\bar u_j \bar u_i) = -\frac{1}{\rho_0}\frac{\partial \bar p}{\partial x_i} + \bar b\,\delta_{i3} - \frac{\partial \tau_{ij}}{\partial x_j} + \nu \nabla^2 \bar u_i\]

The new term

\[\tau_{ij} = \overline{u_i u_j} - \bar u_i \bar u_j\]

is the subgrid stress tensor — the effect of unresolved scales on resolved momentum. It is not closed by the resolved variables alone; it has to be modelled.

The Boussinesq eddy-viscosity closure

The oldest and simplest closure, and the one Aeolus implements, is due to Boussinesq (1877 — a different Boussinesq than the buoyancy one):

\[\tau_{ij} - \tfrac13\,\tau_{kk}\,\delta_{ij} = -2\,\nu_t\,\bar S_{ij}\]

where the resolved rate-of-strain tensor is

\[\bar S_{ij} = \tfrac12\!\left(\frac{\partial \bar u_i}{\partial x_j} + \frac{\partial \bar u_j}{\partial x_i}\right).\]

The trace (τₖₖ) is absorbed into a modified pressure and is not needed explicitly. The eddy viscosity νₜ must be specified.

Smagorinsky-Lilly

The choice of νₜ in Aeolus is the classical Smagorinsky-Lilly form:

\[\nu_t = (C_s\,\Delta)^2\, |\bar S|, \qquad |\bar S| = \sqrt{2\,\bar S_{ij}\,\bar S_{ij}}.\]

  • Cₛ is the Smagorinsky coefficient. In free turbulence Cₛ ≈ 0.17. Near walls it is often reduced; wall-model refinements come later.
  • Δ = (Δx·Δy·Δz)^{1/3} is the geometric-mean filter width — the standard choice for anisotropic grids in this closure.

For the scalar transport equation the SGS scalar flux is closed with an analogous gradient-diffusion hypothesis:

\[\overline{u_j'\theta'} = -\frac{\nu_t}{Pr_t}\frac{\partial \bar \theta}{\partial x_j}\]

with a turbulent Prandtl number Prₜ. For dry ABL work Prₜ ≈ 1/3 is a common starting value; for engineering Prₜ ≈ 0.7 – 1 is typical.

See the Smagorinsky-Lilly page for the discrete implementation.

Explicit filters and spectra

The LES filter above is implicit: the grid and the discrete operators decide what "resolved" means. Analysis, the dynamic procedure and filtered-DNS comparisons need explicit filters and a spectrum, which Aeolus.Operators provides (Phase 54; API in Operators).

Box (top-hat) filter. box_filter! averages over $n$ cells per direction ($n$ odd). On a Fourier mode $e^{ikx}$ the three-point box acts as

\[\hat G_{\text{box}}(k) = \frac{1 + 2\cos kΔ}{3},\]

and its second moment $(n^2-1)Δ^2/12$ is what sets the test-to-grid width ratio of the dynamic model ($\sqrt{1 + 12 \cdot 2/3} = 3$ for $n = 3$, see the SGS page). It is the dynamic model's test filter: compute_dynamic_smagorinsky_νt! sums the same stencil, so the two cannot drift (the refactor is bit for bit). With a solid mask the average runs over fluid neighbours only and is renormalised by their number, so values inside buildings never leak into the filtered field; staggered_solid_mask builds the face masks (a face is solid if either cell is).

Gaussian and sharp spectral filters (periodic, FFTW). The Gaussian of width $Δ$ has $\hat G = \exp(-k^2Δ^2/24)$, the same second moment as a top hat of width $Δ$ (Pope 2000, §13.2). The sharp cut-off keeps $|\mathbf k| ≤ k_c$ and is a projection (idempotent).

Energy spectrum. isotropic_energy_spectrum Fourier transforms each velocity component on its own staggered grid and sums $\tfrac12|\hat{\mathbf u}|^2$ over shells of unit width $Δk = 2π/L$, normalised so that $\sum E Δk = \tfrac12⟨u_iu_i⟩$ exactly (Parseval; shell 0 is the mean flow). The average is over the stored face values, so the shell sum is the kinetic energy the solver carries and conserves (kinetic_energy(sim) per unit volume). A half-cell shift only changes the phase of a mode, so no interpolation is needed. Interpolating to cell centres first, as the first version did, damps mode $\mathbf k$ by $\prod_j\cos^2(k_jΔ_j/2)$, which is 1/2 along an axis at $k_c/2$ and 0 at $k_c$. That is a filter, not a spectrum of the model state.

Initial fields. isotropic_random_field! draws random phases and projects each mode so that the discrete C-grid divergence vanishes. Face amplitudes $a_j = \hat b_j e^{-ik_jΔ_j/2}$ have divergence $2i\sum_j \hat b_j \sin(k_jΔ_j/2)/Δ_j$, so $\hat b$ is projected normal to $\sin(k_jΔ_j/2)/Δ_j$ (the continuous projection normal to $\mathbf k$ is recovered as $kΔ → 0$). Each shell is then scaled to hold exactly $E(k)Δk$: the field is divergence-free to $10^{-15}$ of its gradients, its spectrum equals the request to round-off, and so does its kinetic energy. The first version matched the request in the centre-averaged spectrum and divided the face amplitudes by $\cos(k_jΔ_j/2)$. In the solver's energy it started the CBC case 22 % (32³) and 14 % (64³) too energetic, ×2.1 in the cut-off shell (see the SGS page). A random-phase field has the right spectrum but no energy transfer. rescale_to_spectrum! supports the usual "evolve, then rescale the shells back" preparation (Rogallo 1981), which keeps the phases a run has built.

The benchmark built on these tools — decaying grid turbulence of Comte-Bellot & Corrsin (1971) — is described on the SGS page.

A-priori tests: the exact subfilter stress (Phase 56)

An a-priori test evaluates a closure on a resolved (DNS) field, without running an LES with it (Clark, Ferziger & Reynolds 1979). The DNS field is filtered explicitly, the exact subfilter stress $τ_{ij} = \overline{u_iu_j} - \bar u_i\bar u_j$ is computed, and the model stress, evaluated from $\bar u$ alone, is compared with it point by point. The tools live in Aeolus.Operators (API in Operators):

  • subfilter_stress! computes $τ_{ij}$ at cell centres under any of the filters above (passed as a callable). For a filter with a positive kernel, $τ$ is a covariance under the kernel weights, so it is positive semi-definite at every point (Vreman, Geurts & Kuerten 1994). The sharp spectral filter has a sinc kernel with negative lobes and gives no such guarantee. $τ$ is Galilean invariant for every filter that preserves constants.

  • sgs_dissipation gives $Π = -τ_{ij}\bar S_{ij}$, the transfer from resolved to subfilter scales (positive for forward scatter).

  • Model stresses: smagorinsky_stress!, eddy_viscosity_stress! for any $ν_t$, and bardina_stress!. The Bardina model (Bardina et al. 1980) is the scale-similarity model $\overline{\bar u_i\bar u_j} - \bar{\bar u}_i\bar{\bar u}_j$. It is a reference for comparisons only and not a closure.

  • apriori_metrics returns:

    • the per-component correlations of the deviatoric stresses;
    • the correlation of $Π$;
    • the ratio of mean $Π$;
    • the backscatter fraction (points with $Π < 0$);
    • the Lumley invariants $II = b_{ij}b_{ji}$, $III = b_{ij}b_{jk}b_{ki}$ of $b = τ/τ_{kk} - I/3$, and the fraction of points inside the Lumley triangle.

    dissipation_matched_cs gives the $C_s$ whose mean dissipation is the exact one, $C_s^2 = ⟨Π⟩/(Δ^2⟨|\bar S|^3⟩)$.

The filtered-DNS fixture. The reference field is decaying isotropic turbulence that Aeolus computes itself (test/fixtures/dns_decay.jl). An external database would need binary I/O and a download, so the fixture is generated in-package instead. Set-up:

  • 96³ on a 2π box (48³ before the review of #208, see below);
  • $E(k) ∝ k^4 e^{-2(k/1.5)^2}$, $u' = 1$, fixed seed;
  • $ν = 7.14·10^{-3}$ ($0.018·(48/N)^{4/3}$, which holds $k_{max}η$ fixed across $N$);
  • step_rk3! with the energy-conserving centred scheme, run to $t = 2$.

Each column is a single seed. The 256³ record starts at 8.05η, so its ≈ 8η entry is the 4h value. Earlier versions of this page and of the test said that $ρ_Π$ falls with $Re_λ$, and the test asserted 48³ > 96³ > 256³ at fixed $Δ/η$. The 256³ record broke that, and the expectation was what was wrong, not the code. It was extrapolated from two single-seed points (48³ → 96³), and the physical argument does not hold at fixed $Δ/η$. The filter sits at the same distance from the dissipation range whatever the $Re$, so only $Δ/L$ changes, and no monotone dependence is established for $Re_λ$ between 32 and 79. The generator runs had already shown this: from 64³ upward they agree within about ±0.01 at 4h (below). At 16h the filter is a third of the 48³ box. None of the 48³ / 96³ / 256³ comparisons is asserted; the table is data. On the maintainer's decision on #208 both trend checks were removed: 48³, 96³ > 256³, and 48³ > 96³, which still passes but rests on the same claim.

At $Δ ≈ 8η$ the filter sits in the near-dissipation range. There $τ$ is given by its Taylor expansion, the gradient model $(Δ^2/12)∂_k\bar u_i∂_k\bar u_j$, which correlates with the exact $τ$ at 0.97 (48³), 0.98 (96³) and 0.977 (256³). Both its $Π$ and the Smagorinsky $(C_sΔ)^2|\bar S|^3$ are cubic in the velocity gradient, which is why $ρ_Π$ is high at small $Δ/η$. The test asserts the literature band at 4h for the 96³ fixture and for the 256³ record.

The dynamic coefficient depends on the filter-width ratio. The coefficient from compute_dynamic_smagorinsky_νt! is comparable to the dissipation-matched one only when the test-to-grid width ratio describes the filters actually applied. On the DNS grid, the 3-cell box test filter (second moment $2h^2/3$) acts on top of the Gaussian ($Δ^2/12$), so $α = \sqrt{1 + 8h^2/Δ^2}$. The routine's default $α = 3$ assumes the grid is the filter. On the coarse LES grid of spacing $Δ$ (field coarse-grained by Fourier truncation) the dynamic $C_s$ is 0.116 at 4h (+24 %; 0.133, +33 %, at 48³), because the coarse second-order strain underestimates $|\bar S|$.

The example (examples/validation/apriori_sgs.jl, the same 96³ fixture) writes the full tables for 4h, 8h and 16h. The Smagorinsky $ρ(τ_{12})$ is 0.20, 0.22 and 0.24. The dissipation-matched $C_s$ is 0.094, 0.097 and 0.111. The dynamic $C_s$ on the DNS grid is +6 %, +3 % and −14 % from it, and on the LES grid +24 % and +47 % (4h, 8h).

It writes test/fixtures/dns256_apriori.toml: the DNS checks, $Re_λ$, $k_{max}η$, skewness, energy budget, the energy spectrum, and per Gaussian width 4h, 8h, 16h and 32h the per-component correlations, $ρ_Π$ and $⟨Π⟩$ ratio of the Smagorinsky, Bardina and gradient models, the matched and dynamic $C_s$ and the backscatter. Two things the record does not test. The check eps_plateau (formerly on_plateau) is $\mathrm{d}\ln ε/\mathrm{d}t < 0.25$ over the last 0.25 time units, a test on ε(t), not on the spectrum. And the spectrum has no $k^{-5/3}$ range at $Re_λ ≈ 79$: the compensated spectrum $E k^{5/3} ε^{-2/3}$ rises to a bump of 2.37 (3-shell mean, near $kη ≈ 0.16$) and falls straight into the dissipation range, so no assertion is made on the spectrum's shape.

The integral scale of the final field, $L = (π/2u'^2)\int E/k\,\mathrm{d}k$, is 0.82, so the 32h filter ($Δ = 0.785$) is at $Δ ≈ L$ and the 16h filter at $L/2$. Their numbers describe filtering at the energy-containing scale, not a subgrid model in an inertial range; only 4h and 8h are subgrid in the usual sense (Δ ≤ L/4).

The pipeline is proven at 32³ in every suite run and was validated end to end at 32³–96³. The suite's 256³ testset is a no-op when the file is absent. At 4h (≈ 7.5–7.8η) the generator gives 0.819 at $Re_λ = 21$ (32³), 0.869 at 31 (48³), 0.811 at 45 (64³) and 0.822 at 52 (96³, past the peak), and the fixture gives 0.810 at 64. From 64³ upward these agree within about ±0.01, the scatter of a single seed.

What LES buys us — and what it doesn't

Buys us: the ability to run turbulence at meteorological Reynolds numbers on machines that exist. The largest eddies (which carry most of the energy and most of the fluxes) are resolved directly; only the inertial-range and dissipation-range physics is modelled.

Doesn't buy us: correct near-wall physics without help. The resolved velocity gradients near a solid boundary are severely under-resolved unless we place many grid points inside the surface layer, which for atmospheric applications is impractical. This is why LES codes couple to wall models (MOST — Monin–Obukhov Similarity Theory in the atmosphere; equilibrium wall models in engineering). Aeolus has them in Aeolus.WallModels (Phase 57; see Wall models).
