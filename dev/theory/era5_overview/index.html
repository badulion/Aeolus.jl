Land, urban and surface exchange (Era V): overview

Era V (Phases 46–53) made the surface as detailed as the atmosphere above it: soil, vegetation, a resolved plant canopy, urban facets and buildings, snow and ice, water surfaces, and the sub-grid mixing of all of them. This page shows the one interface they share, how the pieces are composed, and the measured validation. The physics of each piece is on its own page (list at the end).

One interface, many surfaces

Every surface model is an AbstractSurface of the Era V contract, SurfaceExchange (theory). An implementer provides its skin temperature, roughness lengths $(z_{0m}, z_{0h}, z_{0q})$, broadband albedo and emissivity (the RadiationCore generics), optionally a moisture availability $β$, and a step_surface!(s, forcing, dt) that advances its own state and returns SurfaceFluxes. The contract supplies the rest once:

  • Monin–Obukhov fluxes for any implementer through turbulent_fluxes, from a bracketed solve for $ζ = z/L$ (Businger–Dyer / Paulson unstable, Beljaars–Holtslag stable by default; COARE's convective form plugs in as another AbstractStabilityFunctions).
  • One sign convention: fluxes positive upward, $R_n$ positive into the surface, $G$ positive into the substrate, so $R_n = H + LE + G$.
  • One set of constants: densities and conductivities of water, ice and air are in SurfaceConstants; latent heats, heat capacities and $g$ are read from MoistThermodynamics, not restated.

Because the interface is the same, the composites below work with any mix of surfaces, and an atmospheric model needs to know only the interface.

The skin balance as a boundary condition

Most implementers have a massless skin over a conducting column (soil, facet wall, snow, ice). They share one technique: the surface energy balance $F(T_s) = R_n - H - LE$ is linearised about the current skin temperature,

\[F(T_s) \approx F(T_s^n) + \frac{\partial F}{\partial T_s}\,(T_s - T_s^n),\]

which is exactly a Robin condition $-k\,\partial T/\partial z = h\,(T_{env} - T_s) + q$ with $h = -\partial F/\partial T_s$ (radiative plus turbulent, stability dependence included). The skin is then solved implicitly together with the column, and the returned fluxes close $R_n - H - LE = G$ to round-off (1e-10 W m⁻² or better in every implementer's tests). The same trick, applied to the radiosity longwave of all facets at once, gives the implicit facet–facet coupling of Phase 49: the longwave exchange is linear in the emission $εσT^4$, so one linear solve couples every facet's skin, and the scheme conserves energy to 7e-15 W m⁻² where a lagged coupling leaks 14.7 W m⁻².

A SurfaceMosaic is itself an AbstractSurface, so a grid cell can hold a soil tile, a snow tile and a water tile, each stepping its own balance, and return $\sum_i f_i F_i$. Flux aggregation is the point: averaging the parameters instead (one surface with mean albedo, log-mean roughness) gets the sign of $H$ wrong for a stable + unstable pair (+43.6 against −24.1 W m⁻²) and is 22–37 % low against a resolved patchy-surface LES.

In the 3-D flagship

The Berlin case runs the contract in every column: since #129 a SoilThermalColumn with hydrology under streets, building-adjacent cells and the park, slabs elsewhere, Monin–Obukhov fluxes from the first air cell and an implicit surface drag (the Berlin case). The vegetated land column, per-facet walls, building zones, snow, water surfaces and tiles are wired in by open issues of the Berlin epic (#139–#149).
