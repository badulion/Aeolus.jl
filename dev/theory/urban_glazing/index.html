Glazing and window facets

Windows are where sunshine gets into a building without first being turned into heat on an outer surface. The glazing reflects part of the incident shortwave, absorbs part in the glass, and transmits the rest straight into the room. All three fractions depend on the incidence angle: at grazing incidence clear glass is a mirror. The single glass class of the urban example had one fixed albedo and one fixed transmission. src/urban_surface/glazing.jl, part of UrbanSurface, replaces it with the first-principles optics of LBNL WINDOW and ISO 15099 and a window facet that implements the Era V surface interface.

1. One pane: Fresnel plus Beer–Lambert

A pane is a homogeneous, specular, weakly absorbing slab of thickness $d$, refractive index $n$ and solar extinction coefficient $K$. For a beam at incidence angle $θ$, Snell's law gives the refraction angle $\sin θ_t = \sin θ / n$. The Fresnel equations give the reflectivity of each interface for the two polarisations,

\[ρ_s = \Bigl(\frac{\cos θ - n\cos θ_t}{\cos θ + n\cos θ_t}\Bigr)^2,\qquad ρ_p = \Bigl(\frac{n\cos θ - \cos θ_t}{n\cos θ + \cos θ_t}\Bigr)^2 .\]

This is SurfaceOptics.fresnel_polarized_reflectance, the package's only implementation of the Fresnel equations; the water albedo uses it too. Along the refracted path the internal transmittance is $τ = \exp(-Kd/\cos θ_t)$. Summing the incoherent multiple reflections between the two faces gives, for each polarisation,

\[T = \frac{(1-ρ)^2 τ}{1 - ρ^2τ^2},\qquad R = ρ + \frac{ρ(1-ρ)^2τ^2}{1-ρ^2τ^2},\qquad A = 1 - R - T ,\]

and sunlight, which is unpolarised, takes the mean of the two (Duffie & Beckman 2013, §5.1–5.3). Each polarisation is summed separately before averaging. Using the averaged $ρ$ in the sum instead, as WINDOW's Eq. 7-23 does, under-predicts $T$ at oblique incidence: by 0.008 at the Brewster angle and 0.018 at 70° for a 4 mm pane. The two components differ most there, with $ρ_p → 0$ while $ρ_s$ does not.

Parameters. Duffie & Beckman give $n = 1.526$ and $K$ from about 4 m⁻¹ (water-white, low-iron glass) to about 32 m⁻¹ (ordinary float with a greenish edge). The defaults are these book values, not fitted ones. glass_pane_from_normal performs the inversion WINDOW uses for uncoated glass (Furler 1991). It takes a data sheet's normal-incidence $(T_0, R_0)$, solves for $ρ_0$, then $n = (1+\sqrt{ρ_0})/(1-\sqrt{ρ_0})$ and $τ = (R_0-ρ_0)/(ρ_0T_0)$. The resulting pane reproduces the data sheet exactly. Inverting the Vitro 3 mm clear row (0.83, 0.08) gives $n = 1.543$ and $K = 31.6$ m⁻¹, close to the book values.

Limitation: a grey pane. One broadband $K$ is a Beer–Lambert law for the whole solar spectrum. Real float glass absorbs mainly in the Fe²⁺ band near 1 μm, and once that band is saturated, extra thickness removes little more. The grey model therefore matches building-glass thicknesses (3–8 mm) but under-predicts the transmittance of very thick glass by up to 0.051 at 19 mm (see §6).

2. Multi-pane units

A unit of $N$ panes (outside first) separated by gas gaps is solved with the net-radiation method. Let $D_k$ and $U_k$ be the inward and outward fluxes in gap $k$, where gap 0 is outside and gap $N$ is the room, treated as black. Then

\[D_0 = 1,\quad U_N = 0,\quad D_j = T_jD_{j-1} + R_jU_j,\quad U_{j-1} = R_jD_{j-1} + T_jU_j ,\]

and pane $j$ absorbs $A_j(D_{j-1} + U_j)$. This linear system is equivalent to the recursions of ISO 15099 §6 and WINDOW Eqs. 7-15 to 7-20. It is solved per polarisation. $R + T + ΣA_j = 1$ holds to round-off. For diffuse irradiance, the optics are the cosine-weighted hemispherical average $P_{hem} = \int_0^1 P(μ)\,2μ\,dμ$ (WINDOW Eq. 7-36), computed by Gauss–Legendre quadrature in $μ = \cos θ$.

3. Heat transfer across the gaps

The fill gas has the temperature-dependent conductivity, viscosity and heat capacity of ISO 15099 Annex B (air and argon are provided). Its density is that of an ideal gas at the gap pressure. Convection in a vertical cavity follows the ISO 15099 correlation (from Wright 1996), $Nu = \max(Nu_1(Ra), 0.242(Ra/A)^{0.272})$ with $h_c = Nu\,k/d$, which reduces to pure conduction as $Ra → 0$. The ISO 15099 air conductivity at 0 °C equals the contract's still-air SurfaceConstants.k_air, and a test asserts this. Radiation between the panes is the exact grey parallel-plate exchange,

\[h_r = \frac{σ(T_1^2+T_2^2)(T_1+T_2)}{1/ε_1 + 1/ε_2 - 1},\]

with the EN 673 emissivity of uncoated glass, 0.837, as the default.

4. The pane network: U-value and SHGC

Each pane is a node at its mid-plane with heat capacity $ρcd$, taken from the :glass row of the thermal material table. A half-pane conductance $2k/d$ separates the node from each face. Adjacent nodes are coupled through a half pane, the gap coefficient $h_c + h_r$ and another half pane in series. The outer and inner boundaries are the same Robin conditions (ColumnBoundary) as the opaque facets use. Absorbed shortwave is deposited at the nodes.

Three results come from this one network:

  • U-value (glazing_u_value). The steady heat flow through the unit between outdoor and indoor air, with the gap coefficients iterated to the pane temperatures. The defaults are the EN 673 surface coefficients $h_e = 25$ and $h_i = 7.7$ W m⁻² K⁻¹.
  • Inward-flowing fractions $N_j$ (inward_flowing_fractions). The fraction of the heat absorbed in pane $j$ that reaches the room in steady state.
  • SHGC (glazing_shgc). The total solar energy transmittance $\mathrm{SHGC}(θ) = T(θ) + Σ_j N_jA_j(θ)$ (Klems 2002; ISO 15099 §7; EN 410's $g$). The default surface coefficients are EN 410's, $h_e = 23$ and $h_i = 3.6 + 4.4\,ε/0.837 = 8.0$ W m⁻² K⁻¹.

5. The window facet

WindowFacet <: AbstractSurface makes the network time-dependent. The incident shortwave on the facet plane is split into a direct beam at the current incidence angle and a diffuse part (set_solar_incidence!). The optics of §1–2 then partition it:

  • $R\,SW↓$ is reflected. surface_albedo(w) returns $R$, so net_radiation and the atmosphere see the correct reflected shortwave.
  • $A_j\,SW↓$ heats pane $j$.
  • $T\,SW↓$ enters the room. It is reported by transmitted_shortwave, the direct solar gain a building energy model deposits on its interior surfaces. The conductive, convective and long-wave gain through $h_{in}$ is reported separately by interior_heat_flux.

The outer-face balance without the shortwave, $F(T_s) = εL↓ - εσT_s^4 - H - LE$, is linearised about the current skin temperature. The pane temperatures are advanced by the θ-scheme, exactly as for an opaque UrbanFacet. The contract's $G = R_n - H - LE$ is everything the facet takes from outside: storage in the panes, the heat conducted into the room, and the transmitted shortwave. It satisfies $G\,Δt = ΔE_{panes} + (Q_{interior} + SW_{trans})\,Δt$ to round-off.

The WINDOW angular regression is a single curve for all thicknesses, whereas the physical path length grows as $1/\cos θ_t$. For 6 mm glass the model therefore falls below the regression towards grazing incidence: by 0.026 in $T$ and 0.038 in $R$ at 80°. The direction is the one the path-length argument predicts.

The published argon U-value is for a 90 % argon / 10 % air fill; the model gap is pure argon. Blending the air and argon property fits 90/10 (a crude stand-in for the ISO 15099 mixing rules, which are not implemented) raises the model U by about 0.017 W m⁻² K⁻¹, so the like-for-like model value is about 2.56 (+0.3 %) rather than 2.547.

No field observations of façade temperatures are used here. The BUBBLE / CAPITOUL comparison is on BUBBLE / CAPITOUL validation. Its walls are opaque, as in the TEB runs it is compared with, so glazing is not validated there either.
