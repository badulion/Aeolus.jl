Smagorinsky-Lilly SGS closure

Aeolus closes the filtered momentum equation with the eddy-viscosity Smagorinsky-Lilly model (see LES filtering & closure for motivation). This page walks through the discrete implementation.

The model

Local eddy viscosity is determined by the resolved strain rate:

\[\nu_t = (C_s\,\Delta)^2\, |\bar S|, \qquad |\bar S| = \sqrt{2\,\bar S_{ij}\,\bar S_{ij}}\]

where the strain-rate tensor is

\[\bar S_{ij} = \frac12\!\left(\frac{\partial \bar u_i}{\partial x_j} + \frac{\partial \bar u_j}{\partial x_i}\right)\]

and the filter width is

\[\Delta = (\Delta x\,\Delta y\,\Delta z)^{1/3}.\]

For the scalar (potential temperature) the SGS diffusivity is κ_t = ν_t / Prₜ, kept in its own field sim.κt (see Closure contract: other closures set κ_t independently).

Discretization on the C-grid

The six independent components of the symmetric tensor S_{ij} live at different places on the staggered mesh:

  • Diagonal S₁₁ = ∂u/∂x, S₂₂ = ∂v/∂y, S₃₃ = ∂w/∂z live naturally at cell centers (they are 2-point face → cell differences of the corresponding velocity components — the same stencils used by the divergence operator).
  • Off-diagonal S_{12}, S_{13}, S_{23} live at cell edges (x-y, x-z, y-z edges respectively).

Aeolus computes ν_t at cell centers. This requires interpolating the off-diagonal components to the cell center — a standard 4-point edge average.

The kernel _compute_smagorinsky_νt! reads:

S11 = (u[i+1,j,k] - u[i,j,k]) / dx           # cell center
S22 = (v[i,j+1,k] - v[i,j,k]) / dy           # cell center
S33 = (w[i,j,k+1] - w[i,j,k]) / dz           # cell center

# ∂u/∂y at cell center: average two face-to-face differences in y,
# each interpolated in x to the cell center.
du_dy = ((u[i,j+1,k] + u[i+1,j+1,k]) -
         (u[i,j-1,k] + u[i+1,j-1,k])) / (4 dy)
dv_dx = ((v[i+1,j,k] + v[i+1,j+1,k]) -
         (v[i-1,j,k] + v[i-1,j+1,k])) / (4 dx)
S12 = 0.5 * (du_dy + dv_dx)

# ... similarly for S13 and S23 ...

|S|² = 2*(S11² + S22² + S33²) + 4*(S12² + S13² + S23²)
νt[i,j,k] = (C_s * Δ)^2 * sqrt(|S|²)

Applying the SGS tendency to momentum

Once νt is known at cell centers, the SGS contribution to the momentum equation is

\[\frac{\partial u_i}{\partial t} \supset \frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}\!\left(\nu_t \frac{\partial u_i}{\partial x_j}\right).\]

Note this is the diagonal-only or eddy-viscosity flux form — we retain the term with ∂uᵢ/∂xⱼ and drop the transposed piece ∂uⱼ/∂xᵢ from the full stress divergence. For a well-resolved LES with modest νₜ variation, the omitted piece is small (and it disappears exactly for incompressible flow when νₜ is constant). PALM makes the same choice. It is the default of every core (sgs_stress = :laplacian); the full stress form is sgs_stress = :symmetric, below, and the Berlin case runs it.

For u-momentum at FaceX:

\[\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\!\left(\nu_t \frac{\partial u}{\partial x}\right)\bigg|_{i-1/2} = \frac{\nu_{t,i}\,(u_{i+1}-u_i)/\Delta x - \nu_{t,i-1}\,(u_i-u_{i-1})/\Delta x}{\Delta x}\]

with νₜ already at cell centers. The y- and z-flux terms need νₜ at x-y and x-z edges respectively — again a 4-point average.

Stress form vs Laplacian form

The SGS stress of an eddy-viscosity closure is τᵢⱼ = −2νᵢⱼSᵢⱼ, and its divergence

\[-\frac{\partial \tau_{ij}}{\partial x_j} = \frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}\left[\nu_{ij}\left(\frac{\partial u_i}{\partial x_j} + \frac{\partial u_j}{\partial x_i}\right)\right]\]

differs from the Laplacian form above by the transposed term ∂ⱼ(νᵢⱼ ∂uⱼ/∂xᵢ). For constant ν that term is ν ∂ᵢ(∇·u) = 0; where ν varies (the canopy, the surface layer, the edge of a wake) it does not vanish, and the Laplacian form then has three defects: it is not the divergence of a symmetric stress (so it does not conserve angular momentum), it gives a tendency for a solid-body rotation, and its kinetic-energy tendency is not a negative-definite dissipation. The stress form fixes all three. add_sgs_stress_divergence! (in Aeolus.Operators, src/operators/sgs_stress.jl) implements it, and add_stress_divergence!(tu, tv, tw, τ) is the generic operator for any symmetric stress — P56-d's Reynolds-stress model passes its prognostic τ there.

Placement. The stresses sit where the eddy-viscosity fluxes of the Laplacian kernels already live: τ11, τ22, τ33 at cell centres, τ12 on the xy-edges (where u-face i meets v-face j), τ13 on the xz-edges, τ23 on the yz-edges. Each stress enters the two faces it separates with opposite signs, so the operator conserves momentum exactly for any τ.

Strain. At each location the compact face difference: Sᵢᵢ is the diagonal of _velocity_gradient_at (face to face through the cell), and on the xy-edge S12 = ½((u_{i,j} − u_{i,j−1})/Δy + (v_{i,j} − v_{i−1,j})/Δx) from the four velocities adjacent to it. The cell-centred off-diagonal Sᵢⱼ of _velocity_gradient_at (two-cell centred differences, used for |S| in the closures) is not used for the fluxes: interpolated to the edges it would not commute with the discrete divergence, and the constant-ν identity below would fail at truncation order.

Edge interpolation. ν is the cell value for the diagonals and the 4-cell average onto the edge for the off-diagonals, exactly as in add_eddy_viscosity_u!, so the stress form is the Laplacian kernel plus the transposed term, and the two agree to round-off when ν is constant.

Anisotropy. ν11 = ν22 = ν12 = ν_h and ν33 = ν13 = ν23 = ν_v: νh for the horizontal pairs, νv for every pair involving z. This is the convention of CM1 (Bryan & Fritsch 2002, turbnmod), which also matches the split of horizontal and vertical mixing in Kosović (1997)'s anisotropic model. The isotropic closure is ν_h = ν_v. Through the closure contract the two come from SGSFields.νh and SGSFields.νv (aliased for every closure so far; P55-e supplies the split).

Discrete dissipation. Summation by parts on a periodic domain gives the kinetic-energy tendency exactly:

\[\sum_{\rm faces} u_i\, \frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}(2\nu_{ij}S_{ij})\, \Delta V = -\sum 2\nu_{ij} S_{ij} S_{ij}\, \Delta V \le 0,\]

with the diagonal terms summed over cells and each off-diagonal over its edges, counted for ij and ji (sgs_stress_dissipation).

IMEX split. step_imex! with sgs_stress = :symmetric treats the diagonal part ∂ⱼ(νᵢⱼ ∂uᵢ/∂xⱼ) implicitly and the transposed part explicitly. The implicit operator takes a per-direction coefficient per velocity component (ν + ν_h on the faces of the 11, 22, 12 fluxes, ν + ν_v on those of 33, 13, 23) with the explicit kernel's edge interpolation, so it is identical to the explicit part = :diagonal and stays symmetric (I − γΔt L is SPD for CG). The cross terms carry no second derivative of a component along its own direction, so they are not stiff; they vanish for constant ν on the projected stage values, which is why the split is exact in that limit. With :laplacian the implicit operator is unchanged (the co-located Phase-25 approximation). A pair (ν_h, ν_v) is also accepted as the coefficient of solve_implicit_diffusion! (νh on the x/y faces, νv on the z faces), and a closure with a separate κv gets it for the scalars.

Explicit stability of the stress form. The explicit operator L is symmetric and negative semi-definite, with −⟨u, Lu⟩ = Σ 2νᵢⱼSᵢⱼSᵢⱼ ΔV. Forward Euler is therefore stable for Δt ρ(L) ≤ 2, where ρ(L) is the largest Rayleigh quotient. To bound it, write each diagonal strain as one difference, (u_{i+1} − u_i)²/Δx² ≤ 2(u_{i+1}² + u_i²)/Δx², and split each off-diagonal pair with (a + b)² ≤ 2a² + 2b². Every face value enters two differences per direction, which gives

\[\rho(L) \le 8\max\Bigl(\nu_h^{\max}(\Delta x^{-2} + \Delta y^{-2}) + \nu_v^{\max}\Delta z^{-2},\; \nu_v^{\max}(\Delta x^{-2} + \Delta y^{-2} + \Delta z^{-2})\Bigr).\]

The first entry is the u and v rows. The second is the w row, which sees only νv. For `νh = ν_v = νthis is8ν ΣΔ⁻², twice the radius4ν ΣΔ⁻²of the 7-point Laplacian. The factor 2 is the 2 in the normal stressτᵢᵢ = −2ν∂uᵢ/∂xᵢ, and the bound is sharp. With constant ν,L = ν(∇² + ∇∇·)on the grid, and on the irrotational checkerboard modeu = ∇φboth terms give−4ν ΣΔ⁻². Power iteration measuresρ = 8ν ΣΔ⁻²` to 1e-6.

The factor applies to the unprojected operator. The cores project every predictor, uⁿ⁺¹ = P(uⁿ + Δt Luⁿ) = (I + Δt PLP)uⁿ. For solenoidal periodic u, Σ 2S:S = Σ|∇u|², so ρ(PLP) ≤ 4ν_max ΣΔ⁻², the old Laplacian bound. Power iteration confirms this for every ν field tested (table below). Even so, cfl_diagnostics and adaptive_dt use the unprojected bound with sgs_stress = :symmetric: νt_cfl = stress_form_cfl_viscosity(ν_h^max, ν_v^max, mesh) in place of νt_max in C_diffusive Δ²_harm / (ν + νt_cfl) and in the SGS-only limit. The reason is that the projected bound needs an exact, orthogonal projection of a periodic field. Several parts of the code do not have one: the immersed-boundary masking of the Berlin run, an iterative pressure solve stopped at a tolerance, wall boundaries, and the anelastic ρ-weighted projection. Only the unprojected bound holds for every velocity field. :laplacian keeps νt_cfl = νt_max, so its limits are unchanged bit for bit.

Applying the SGS tendency to a scalar

The scalar tendency is

\[\frac{\partial \theta}{\partial t} \supset \frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}\!\left(\frac{\nu_t}{Pr_t} \frac{\partial \theta}{\partial x_j}\right).\]

Because θ is cell-centered, its diffusive flux lives at faces. The kernel _add_eddy_diffusion_scalar! interpolates νₜ linearly from cell centers to each face. The same tendency, with the same coefficient, is applied to qv and to every tracer the Berlin case transports (see Every transported scalar uses κ_t below).

Tensorial scalar diffusivity

P55-c (#181) generalises the scalar flux to a diffusivity tensor, $\partial_t\phi \supset \partial_i(K_{ij}\,\partial_j\phi)$, through one operator family in Aeolus.Operators (src/operators/scalar_tensor.jl). P55-e (κh ≠ κv under stratification) and P56-d (the Daly–Harlow heat flux $K_{ij} = C\,(e/\varepsilon)\,\tau_{ij}$) reuse it.

Diagonal. add_eddy_diffusion_scalar!(tϕ, ϕ, κh, κv) puts κh on the x and y faces and κv on the z faces (each the 2-point average of the cell values), the tensor $\mathrm{diag}(\kappa_h, \kappa_h, \kappa_v)$. The isotropic three-argument form is the same kernel with κh ≡ κv, bit for bit. The contract's default add_sgs_scalar! calls it with the closure's f.κh, f.κv; before P55-c the explicit cores used f.κh alone while step_imex! already used the pair, so a closure with a separate κ_v was diffused differently by the two paths.

General symmetric tensor: the triad form. add_tensor_diffusion_scalar!(tϕ, ϕ, K) takes the six components of K at cell centres (DiffusivityTensor). The cross terms need gradients that are not co-located with the flux they enter. The discretisation follows Griffies et al. (1998). Each cell is split into eight octants. Octant $(a,b,c)$ pairs one x-face, one y-face and one z-face gradient of the cell, $g = (g_x, g_y, g_z)$. The operator is defined as the gradient of a discrete functional:

\[F[\phi] = -\tfrac12 \sum_{\text{cells}} \frac{\Delta V}{8} \sum_{\text{octants}} g^{\mathsf T} K_{\text{cell}}\, g, \qquad \Delta V\, \partial_t \phi_n = \frac{\partial F}{\partial \phi_n}.\]

In flux form, the flux through a face is the mean of $(Kg)_n$ over the eight octants that touch it. Because K is per cell, the four octant terms of a cell collapse to the average of its two cross gradients:

\[F_x^{\,i+1/2} = \tfrac12\left(K_{11}^{i} + K_{11}^{i+1}\right)\delta_x\phi + \tfrac14 \sum_{c\in\{i,i+1\}}\left[K_{12}^{c}\,(g_{y,S}+g_{y,N})_c + K_{13}^{c}\,(g_{z,B}+g_{z,T})_c\right].\]

$F_y$ and $F_z$ follow the same pattern. The tendency is $\delta_x F_x + \delta_y F_y + \delta_z F_z$. Its diagonal part is exactly the face-averaged diagonal kernel. Three properties follow from the functional, hold to round-off for any K, and do not depend on resolution:

  • conservation: $\sum \Delta V\,\partial_t\phi = 0$, because F is invariant under a constant shift of ϕ;
  • down-gradient: $\sum \Delta V\,\phi\,\partial_t\phi = 2F \le 0$ when every cell's K is positive semi-definite (Griffies et al. 1998, Sec. 3);
  • symmetry of the discrete operator.

For constant K, a plane wave $\cos(\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x)$ is an exact eigenfunction. Its rate is $K_{11}\hat k_x^2 + K_{22}\hat k_y^2 + K_{33}\hat k_z^2 + 2K_{12}s_x s_y + 2K_{13}s_x s_z + 2K_{23}s_y s_z$, where $\hat k = 2\sin(k\Delta/2)/\Delta$ and $s = \sin(k\Delta)/\Delta$: the diagonal terms see the compact second difference and the cross terms the centred one.

The bounded keyword (from the scalar boundary conditions, bounded_directions(bcs)) handles walls. A wall face's gradient is left out of the cross terms. The wall flux keeps only its diagonal part from the halo, as in the diagonal operator, which is zero for a no-flux halo. With no-flux halos the three properties hold with walls as well. Prescribed surface fluxes are added separately.

Rotated isentropic tensor. isentropic_tensor!(K, θ, κh, κv; slope_max, grad_min) builds the Redi (1982) tensor, with κh along the isentropes and κv across them. With $\mathbf n = \nabla\theta/|\nabla\theta|$:

\[K = \kappa_h(I - \mathbf n\mathbf n^{\mathsf T}) + \kappa_v\,\mathbf n\mathbf n^{\mathsf T} \;\to\; K = \kappa_h I - f\,(\kappa_h-\kappa_v)\,\mathbf n\mathbf n^{\mathsf T}.\]

Here $f\in[0,1]$ blends the rotated tensor toward the isotropic κ_h:

  • the small-slope taper (the slope clipping of Gerdes, Köberle & Willebrand 1991; see Danabasoglu & McWilliams 1995): with $S = |\nabla_h\theta|/\partial_z\theta$, $f = 1$ for $S \le S_{max}$ and $f = (S_{max}/S)^2$ above it;
  • the isotropic fallback $f = 0$ where $N^2 \propto \partial_z\theta \le 0$. This is the limit of the taper as S → ∞, so K is continuous across N² = 0. f also ramps linearly to 0 as $|\nabla\theta|$ drops below grad_min.

∇θ is the centred gradient at the cell centre. The eigenvalues are $\kappa_h, \kappa_h, \kappa_h - f(\kappa_h-\kappa_v)$, so K is positive semi-definite and the triad operator dissipates variance. Where ∇θ is uniform, the triad gradients of θ are the cell's ∇θ, which K annihilates when κv = 0. Isentropic diffusion therefore leaves θ unchanged (the "triad property"). With $\mathbf n = \hat z$, K is ``\mathrm{diag}(\kappah,\kappah,\kappav)``.

In the closure contract. sgs_scalar_tensor(c, f, sim) (default nothing) exposes a closure's full tensor. IsentropicDiffusion(inner, mesh; slope_max, grad_min, diapycnal) runs inner for the coefficients, the momentum and the memory, builds K from sim.θ in sgs_coefficients!, and applies it in add_sgs_scalar!. κv is the closure's own f.κv, or diapycnal·f.κh when diapycnal is given. IMEX: `stepimex!solves the diagonal(\nu+K_{11}, \nu+K_{22}, \nu+K_{33})implicitly, each component on the faces of its own direction (exactly thepart = :diagonalof the explicit operator). In a stable boundary layer κ_v sits there, which is the stiff term. The off-diagonal part (part = :offdiagonal) is added explicitly on θ and qv. K is frozen at the start of the step, the same lagged linearisation as ν_t. The explicit cross terms are bounded by|K_{13}| \le \sqrt{K_{11}K_{33}}, which the taper keeps at or below\kappa_h S_{max}/(1+S_{max}^2)`. They therefore do not tighten the explicit advective limit the IMEX step runs at.

Stability correction

P55-e (#187) is the one stability-corrected closure of the turbulence-closure work (the "stratification-dependent anisotropic" closure of P55 merged with the former "buoyancy-aware Smagorinsky" of P58). The constant-$C_s$ closure mixes at the same rate whatever the stratification: it keeps mixing where $Ri \gg 1/4$, damps internal gravity waves and keeps a stable boundary layer artificially turbulent. StabilityCorrected(base, mesh; scheme, length_scales) (src/operators/sgs_stability.jl) wraps any eddy-viscosity closure (StaticSmagorinsky, DynamicSmagorinsky, FunctionSGS, ML.MLSGS, …) and returns four coefficients:

\[\nu_h = \nu_{base}\,\ell_h, \qquad \nu_v = \nu_{base}\,\ell_v\,f_m(Ri), \qquad \kappa_h = \frac{\nu_{base}\,\ell_h}{Pr_t}, \qquad \kappa_v = \frac{\nu_{base}\,\ell_v\,f_h(Ri)}{Pr_t}.\]

  • $Ri = N^2/\max(|S|^2, S^2_{min})$ is the gradient Richardson number of gradient_richardson! with the strain $|S|^2 = 2S_{ij}S_{ij}$ the Smagorinsky closure sees, and $N^2$ from buoyancy_frequency_squared! (of $\theta_v$ when a humidity is known; about the core's own reference, $\theta_0$ on the Boussinesq core and $\bar\theta(z)$ on the anelastic / pseudo-incompressible cores).
  • $f_m, f_h$ are the stability functions of the TurbulenceStability contract (P58-a; theory); nothing is redefined here. The default is Brown, Derbyshire & Mason (1994) (the LEM/MONC functions: $(1 - Ri/Ri_c)^4$ with $Ri_c = 1/4$ in stable air, enhanced $\sqrt{1 - cRi}$ mixing in unstable air); LillyCutoff() is Lilly (1962) / Mason (1989). Both are 1 at $Ri = 0$, so the correction leaves a neutral flow alone, and the neutral $Pr_t$ is the base closure's.
  • Anisotropic length scales (length_scales = :anisotropic, the default). The filter width of the horizontal coefficients is $\Delta_h = \sqrt{\Delta x\,\Delta y}$ and of the vertical ones $\Delta_v = \Delta z$ (Bryan & Fritsch 2002, CM1) instead of $\Delta = (\Delta x\Delta y\Delta z)^{1/3}$. For $\nu \propto \Delta^2$ this is the factor $\ell_h = (\Delta_h/\Delta)^2$, $\ell_v = (\Delta_v/\Delta)^2$ — on the Berlin grid ($\Delta x/\Delta z = 2.5$) 1.842 and 0.2947. On cubic cells both are exactly 1.
  • Horizontal coefficients keep no stability factor. $\nu_h$ and $\kappa_h$ are the base values (with $\ell_h$): the correction targets the vertical exchange that the stratification suppresses. The horizontal part of the SGS damping of a gravity wave therefore remains (see the numbers below).
  • Dynamic bases. A coefficient from the Germano identity already sees the damping of the resolved motion by the stratification, so a second $f_m$ would count it twice. For dynamic_sgs_coefficient(base) = true the momentum gets no factor ($\nu_v = \nu_{base}\ell_v$) and only the scalar is corrected, through $Pr_t(Ri) = Pr_t f_m/f_h$: $\kappa_v = \nu_{base}\ell_v (f_h/f_m)/Pr_t$ (0 where $f_m = 0$).
  • Not every closure is wrapped. A closure with its own buoyancy term (the anisotropic minimum-dissipation model, P55-d) or its own length scale (prognostic TKE, P56-b) already accounts for the stratification; the constructor accepts eddy-viscosity closures only. This is by design, not a gap: the TKE closure also has no $\ell_h, \ell_v$ anisotropy (one $\Delta = (\Delta x\Delta y\Delta z)^{1/3}$, as in Deardorff 1980 and PALM), so on the Berlin grid its neutral equilibrium mixes $1/\ell_v = 3.39\times$ more vertically and $\ell_h = 1.84\times$ less horizontally than this wrapper around a Smagorinsky closure of the same $C_s$ (details); the rotated tensor of IsentropicDiffusion is not offered for it either.

The four fields reach every core through the contract defaults: the stress form $\partial_j(2\nu_{ij}S_{ij})$ with $\nu_h, \nu_v$ (P55-b, sgs_stress = :symmetric; the Laplacian form uses $\nu_h$ only) and the diagonal scalar operator with $\kappa_h, \kappa_v$ (P55-c), explicit or — under step_imex! — in the implicit operator. The scalar tendency keeps the base closure's order of operations, $(1/Pr_t)\,\partial_j(a_j\,\partial_j\phi)$, so with $N^2 = 0$ and $\ell_h = \ell_v = 1$ the wrapper is the base closure bit for bit. IsentropicDiffusion(StabilityCorrected(base, mesh), mesh) gives the rotated tensor the corrected $\kappa_v$ across the isentropes.

Choice of coefficients

Choice of coefficients

  • Cₛ = 0.17 for isotropic free turbulence (Lilly 1967). Reduced to ~0.1 near walls; the constant-Cₛ form is used everywhere (no near-wall damping), with the wall stress from a wall model.
  • Prₜ = 1/3 is standard for atmospheric ABL (Deardorff 1972). Engineering flows often use Prₜ ≈ 0.7 – 1.

Both are constructor parameters on BoussinesqSimulation.

Dynamic coefficient (Germano–Lilly)

compute_dynamic_smagorinsky_νt! computes $C_s$ from the resolved flow instead of prescribing it (Germano et al. 1991, Lilly 1992). The resolved stress at a coarser test filter $\widetilde{\cdot}$ (a 3 × 3 × 3 box of cell-centred values) is known exactly, and the Germano identity equates it to the difference of the model at the two filter levels:

\[L_{ij} = \widetilde{u_i u_j} - \tilde u_i \tilde u_j = C_s^2 M_{ij}, \qquad M_{ij} = 2Δ^2\left(\widetilde{|S| S_{ij}} - α^2 |\tilde S|\, \tilde S_{ij}\right),\]

with $α = \hat Δ/Δ$. The least-squares solution, averaged over each horizontal level (the homogeneous directions), is $C_s^2(z) = ⟨L^d_{ij} M_{ij}⟩ / ⟨M_{ij} M_{ij}⟩$, clipped to $[0, 0.5^2]$ (no backscatter). averaging = :local uses the pointwise ratio instead.

Filter-width ratio. The discrete box of three cells has second moment $2Δ^2/3$; combined with the implicit grid filter (a top hat of width $Δ$, second moment $Δ^2/12$) the effective test-level width is $\sqrt{Δ^2 + 12 \cdot 2Δ^2/3} = 3Δ$, so filter_ratio = 3 is the default. Lagrangian dynamic averaging (Meneveau, Lund & Cabot 1996)

The plane average of the Germano identity needs horizontally homogeneous statistics, which an urban canopy does not have. Before P54-b (#173) the plane average also ran over the solid cells of the immersed buildings. LagrangianDynamicSmagorinsky averages the identity backwards in time along fluid pathlines instead (MLC96), so the coefficient is local and needs no homogeneous direction. The averages obey

\[\mathcal I_{LM}^{n+1}(\mathbf x) = ε\,[L^d_{ij}M_{ij}]^{n+1}(\mathbf x) + (1-ε)\,\mathcal I_{LM}^{n}(\mathbf x - \mathbf u^n Δt), \qquad \mathcal I_{MM}^{n+1}(\mathbf x) = ε\,[M_{ij}M_{ij}]^{n+1}(\mathbf x) + (1-ε)\,\mathcal I_{MM}^{n}(\mathbf x - \mathbf u^n Δt),\]

\[ε = \frac{Δt/T}{1+Δt/T}, \qquad T = θ\,Δ\,(\mathcal I_{LM}\mathcal I_{MM})^{-1/8},\ θ = 1.5, \qquad C_s^2 = \mathcal I_{LM}/\mathcal I_{MM}.\]

This is the exponentially weighted pathline average of MLC96 eqs. 2.10–2.13, in the time discretisation of their eqs. 3.1–3.3. The details:

  • The upstream values come from trilinear interpolation. Periodic directions wrap, and in a bounded direction the departure point is clamped to the boundary cell.
  • $\mathcal I_{LM}$ is clipped at 0.
  • $T$ uses the upstream memories. Where $\mathcal I_{LM}$ has been clipped to 0, $T$ is evaluated with $\mathcal I_{LM} ≥ 10^{-8}\mathcal I_{MM}$, so the point can recover; with $T = ∞$ it would freeze. Where $\mathcal I_{MM} = 0$, $ε = 1$.
  • Initialisation (MLC96 §3): $\mathcal I_{MM} = M_{ij}M_{ij}$ and $\mathcal I_{LM} = C_0^2\mathcal I_{MM}$ with $C_0 = 0.16$.
  • $C_s^2$ is clipped to $[0, 0.5^2]$. No damping function is applied anywhere.

One Germano kernel. $L^d_{ij}M_{ij}$ and $M_{ij}M_{ij}$ come from _germano_identity!. That kernel also computes the plane and local averages of compute_dynamic_smagorinsky_νt!, and its test filter is the stencil of box_filter!. The refactor is bit for bit: LM, MM, $C_s^2$ and $ν_t$ on the evolved field below are == the pre-refactor values for both averagings and both filter ratios.

Contract. The memory advances only in advance_sgs!, once per accepted step, from the state after the step. The departure point uses the cell-centred velocity committed with the previous memory, $\mathbf u^n$. sgs_coefficients! is pure: it takes $C_s^2$ from the committed memory and $|S|$ from the current state. So the three RK3 substages and the CFL estimate see one coefficient, and between commits a coefficient call costs only a strain evaluation. sgs_state copies the memory, and a rejected adaptive step is rolled back bit for bit.

Solids. mask is a cell mask, e.g. IBM.SolidMask.mask. Its halos are filled from the boundary conditions. The plane / local DynamicSmagorinsky take the same mask.

  • $ν_t = 0$ and the memories are 0 in solid cells.
  • The dynamic procedure reads the velocity with the no-slip value 0 on every face that touches a solid.
  • The test filter runs over fluid neighbours only, renormalised by their number.
  • A departure point whose nearest cell is solid takes the value of the cell itself.
  • Solid corners of the trilinear stencil are dropped and the remaining weights renormalised.

Nothing stored inside an obstacle reaches a fluid coefficient: test 6 below sets every solid face to NaN and to 1e30 and gets the same bits.

Scale-dependent Lagrangian dynamic model (Bou-Zeid, Meneveau and Parlange 2005)

The scale-invariant dynamic procedure assumes that the coefficient is the same at the grid scale $Δ$ and at the test-filter scale $αΔ$. Near a wall, where the filter widths are comparable to the distance to it, the coefficient falls toward the grid scale, so the procedure underestimates $C_s$ there and the mean shear comes out too large or too small depending on the closure (Porté-Agel, Meneveau & Parlange 2000). Static Smagorinsky overshoots ($Φ_m$ up to 1.45-1.65 at 24³ with $C_s = 0.17$, P54-d), and in the half channel below LASI undershoots ($Φ_m ≈ 0.7$ in the log layer, mean $C_s$ 0.04-0.06 near the wall). The classical fix is a damping function; the closures here use none.

LagrangianDynamicSmagorinsky(mesh; scale_dependent = true) (P54-e, #186) is the scale-dependent Lagrangian model (LASD) of Bou-Zeid, Meneveau & Parlange (2005), as an option of the same type. It adds a second test filter, the 5 × 5 × 5 box. Its second moment is $2Δ^2$, so with the convention that makes the 3-cell box $α = 3$ (implicit grid filter plus the discrete box, $\sqrt{Δ^2 + 12σ^2}$) its width is $α_2 = 5$. It also adds two memories transported along the same pathlines, with the same $ε$ and $T$:

\[Q_{ij} = \widehat{u_i u_j} - \hat u_i \hat u_j, \qquad N_{ij} = 2Δ^2\left(\widehat{|S| S_{ij}} - α_2^2 |\hat S| \hat S_{ij}\right),\]

\[\mathcal I_{QN}^{n+1} = ε\,[Q^d_{ij}N_{ij}]^{n+1} + (1-ε)\,\mathcal I_{QN}^{n}(\mathbf x - \mathbf u^n Δt), \qquad \mathcal I_{NN}^{n+1} = ε\,[N_{ij}N_{ij}]^{n+1} + (1-ε)\,\mathcal I_{NN}^{n}(\mathbf x - \mathbf u^n Δt).\]

$C^2_{αΔ} = \mathcal I_{LM}/\mathcal I_{MM}$ and $C^2_{α_2Δ} = \mathcal I_{QN}/\mathcal I_{NN}$ are the coefficients at the two test-filter scales. With a power law $C^2(aΔ) = C^2_Δ a^φ$ between them,

\[φ = \frac{\ln(C^2_{α_2Δ}/C^2_{αΔ})}{\ln(α_2/α)}, \qquad β = 2^φ \in [1/8,\ 8], \qquad C^2_Δ = C^2_{αΔ}\,α^{-φ}.\]

For Bou-Zeid's filters ($α = 2$, $α_2 = 4$) this is their $β = C^2_{4Δ}/C^2_{2Δ}$ and $C^2_Δ = C^2_{2Δ}/β$. In general $β$ is the coefficient ratio per doubling of the filter width. The lower clip $β ≥ 1/8$ is Bou-Zeid's. The upper clip, 8, is ours: it is symmetric in $φ$ and keeps the diagnostic finite. The details:

  • The box is separable and summed in three passes over the whole field (x, y, then z with the identity; KA-l, #304). In a periodic direction it wraps. In a bounded direction it stays in the interior, and the points beyond are dropped (renormalised). The 3-cell first filter reads the halo cell, but at a free-slip wall that cell holds the mirrored, reversed shear, and the 5-cell box would reach it two cells from the wall. The first version did read it; the second level then had $β ≈ 1$ and a $C_s$ dip (0.066 against 0.13-0.16 around it).
  • With a solid mask the box runs over fluid cells only, and the memories are 0 in solids.
  • Where $C^2_{αΔ} = 0$ the coefficient is 0 and $β$ is stored as 1. Where only $C^2_{α_2Δ} = 0$, $β = 1/8$.
  • Initialisation: $\mathcal I_{QN} = C_0^2 \mathcal I_{NN}$, so $β = 1$.
  • beta = 1 (a fixed $β$) reproduces LASI bit for bit (test 2).
  • cadence = N: the Germano identities, which are the expensive part, are evaluated every $N$ commits. In between, the memories are only transported ($ε = 0$), and the next evaluation relaxes over the elapsed time. This is common practice in Lagrangian dynamic codes. $N = 1$ is the exact model, and the counters are part of sgs_state.

No wall damping. No damping function is applied anywhere. The near-wall coefficient comes from the scale dependence, and the wall stress from the wall model (WallModels, #169, with the P57-b matching input, #183).

Closure contract

The SGS closure contract (#167) defines the interface between an SGS closure and the dynamical cores once, in src/operators/sgs_closures.jl, so that closures that need more than the velocity — buoyancy-aware mixing, a prognostic TKE, Reynolds-stress transport, a Lagrangian-averaged dynamic coefficient — plug into every core the same way. Why sgs_coefficients! must be pure. The cores evaluate the coefficients more often than once per step: once per RK3 substage, once for the adaptive stepper's CFL limit, and again on every retried try of a rejected step. Before the contract the closure hook f(νt, u, v, w) was called at each of these, so a closure with memory would have advanced it two to four times per step and never rolled it back. Under the contract the memory changes only in advance_sgs!, which every core calls exactly once per accepted step (RK3: once with dt after the third substage, not three times), and step_adaptive! brackets each try with sgs_state / restore_sgs_state!, so after a rejection the memory is bit for bit that of a run that took the shorter step directly.

κ_t is a field. The simulations carry κt next to νt. The θ tendency is formed with it, the CFL limit is $\Delta t_{sgs} = C_{diff}\, Δ^2_{harm} / \max(ν_{t,\max}, κ_{t,\max})$ (rather than $ν_{t,\max} \max(1, 1/Pr_t)$), and the IMEX implicit operator freezes $ν + κ_t$ for the scalars. The Smagorinsky-type closures (AbstractEddyViscosityClosure: StaticSmagorinsky, DynamicSmagorinsky, ML.MLSGS, and FunctionSGS, which wraps a legacy f(νt, u, v, w)) set $κ_t = ν_t/Pr_t$; they apply it as $(1/Pr_t)\,∂_j(ν_t ∂_jθ)$ through the same kernel, the order of operations of the pre-contract code, so the default run is unchanged bit for bit (face averages of $ν_t/Pr_t$ are not bitwise $1/Pr_t$ times face averages of $ν_t$). A closure with its own $κ_t$ (a stability function, a TKE closure) gets the three-argument add_eddy_diffusion_scalar! by default.

Every transported scalar uses κ_t (P56-a, #177). The scalar flux of the closure is applied, with the same coefficients, to every scalar a core or the Berlin case transports, not only to θ:

  • step! and step_rk3! add it to qv when moisture = true (add_sgs_scalar!(tqv, qv, closure, f, sim), or eddy diffusion with qv's own diffusivity for a closure that has one — AMD, sgs_tracer_diffusivity!, #176 — the same rule as step_imex!'s implicit qv coefficient), together with the molecular ν∇²qv that θ gets; step_imex! already diffused qv implicitly with ν + κ_t. Before #177 step! advected qv but never diffused it — a code bug: a moist run's vapour saw no SGS mixing at all — and step_rk3! did not even combine qv over its substages. Dry runs are unchanged bit for bit. (The anelastic and pseudo-incompressible cores carry no qv.)
  • The Berlin case diffuses every tracer its :transport stage advects (qv, ql, the chemistry species) and the pollutant with sim.κt of the last dynamics step: one shared κ, θ's κt, under every :sgs provider including :amd (see [The Berlin case](../design/berlinnumerics.md#Numerics-slots)). The Berlin core runs with moisture = false, so qv and ql are case tracers, mixed in the :transport stage with the same shared κ, not by the core's qv path above. Under :amd this is a design choice, not a missing feature; the reasons follow (#234).

Why the Berlin tracers share one κ (#234). AMD can give every scalar its own diffusivity from its own gradient (see below); the :transport stage deliberately does not use it.

  • Physics. At high Reynolds number the subfilter eddies that mix one passive scalar mix every other one the same way: the turbulent Schmidt number is a property of the flow, not of the species, and one Sc_t (here Pr_t) for every passive scalar is the standard LES assumption. Per-scalar AMD κ makes the mixing depend on the shape of each field, which is a property of the closure's gradient model, not of the turbulence.
  • Chemistry. NO, NO₂ and O₃ are chemically linked: away from sources they sit near the photostationary state $[\mathrm{NO}][\mathrm{O_3}]/[\mathrm{NO_2}] = j_{NO_2}/k$. Mixing them with different κ separates them artificially and moves the mixture off that state, i.e. the transport itself does chemistry.
  • Water. With different κ for qv and ql, total water $q_t = q_v + q_l$ and $θ_l$ are no longer mixed as conserved variables: a cloud edge that mixes vapour faster than liquid creates or destroys supersaturation in the mixing step instead of in the microphysics.
  • Fallback. Where a scalar's gradient vanishes AMD falls back to $κ = ν_t/Pr_t = 3ν_t$. A tracer that is uniform (a clean background, ql = 0 outside clouds) or flat at a plume edge then gets the largest κ of all, three times ν_t, and over-mixes plume edges.
  • Time step. The adaptive step limits by $\max(ν_{t,\max}, κ_{t,\max})$ of θ (sgs_cfl_diagnostics); per-tracer κ, which can be larger (the fallback), is not seen by it, so an explicit tracer diffusion could run past its own stability limit.

Anisotropic minimum-dissipation and Vreman closures

P55-d (#176), src/operators/sgs_amd_vreman.jl. Two closures on the contract that use the spacing of each direction instead of one $Δ = (ΔxΔyΔz)^{1/3}$, which matters on the Berlin grid (15.6 × 15.6 × 6.25 m), and that vanish in laminar flow, where Smagorinsky's $(C_sΔ)^2|S|$ does not. Both read the one gradient stencil, _velocity_gradient_at; the θ gradient is the centred difference over two cells (exact for a linear field, like the off-diagonal velocity gradients).

AMD (AMDClosure, compute_amd_coefficients!). The anisotropic minimum-dissipation model of Rozema, Bae, Moin & Verstappen (2015) bounds the energy of the subfilter scales in a grid box with the Poincaré inequality and takes the smallest eddy viscosity that keeps it from growing. With the scalar model of Abkar, Bae & Moin (2016) and the buoyancy term of Abkar & Moin (2017):

\[ν_t = C\,\frac{\max\!\left(0,\; -(δ_k∂_k u_i)(δ_k∂_k u_j)S_{ij} + \frac{g}{θ_0}(δ_k∂_k w)(δ_k∂_k θ)\right)}{∂_l u_m\,∂_l u_m}, \qquad κ_t = C\,\frac{\max\!\left(0,\; -(δ_k∂_k u_i)(δ_k∂_k θ)\,∂_i θ\right)}{∂_l θ\,∂_l θ},\]

$δ_k = (Δx, Δy, Δz)$, summed over $k$ inside each product. The numerators are the gradient-model estimates of the subfilter shear production and of the subfilter buoyancy flux. $κ_t$ is AMD's own (not $ν_t/Pr_t$); where $∂_lθ∂_lθ = 0$ it falls back to $ν_t/Pr_t$. It is per scalar: another scalar $ϕ$ gets the same formula with its own gradient (compute_amd_scalar_diffusivity!, reached through the contract generic sgs_tracer_diffusivity!). This is how step_imex! diffuses $q_v$. The Berlin :transport stage does not use it for its tracers: they share θ's κt by design (see Why the Berlin tracers share one κ above, #234). The formula is unchanged under $ϕ → aϕ + b$. $C = 0.3$ is the modified Poincaré constant for a second-order central discretisation from Rozema et al. (2015) (their decaying-turbulence tests; 1/12 for a spectral method, 0.212 for fourth order), as restated by Gadde, Stieren & Stevens (2021, §2.3). Abkar & Moin (2017) used 1/3 on their mixed spectral / second-order grid. The buoyancy parameter is the core's: ``g/θ0$on the Boussinesq core and$g/\bar θ(z)`on the sound-proof cores ([sgsbuoyancyparameter`](@ref Aeolus.Operators.sgsbuoyancyparameter)). No separate Richardson-number correction is applied: the buoyancy term is AMD's stratification response, and a stability wrapper (P55-e, #187) is not stacked on it by default.

Quiescent stratified air (#504). The shear term of $ν_t$ is of degree 1 in the velocity gradient and vanishes with it; the buoyancy term is of degree 1 over degree 2, so as published $ν_t ∝ 1/|∇u|$ where the air is at rest and θ is stratified. A convective boundary layer started from rest ($Γ = 3$ K/km, $Δx = 40$ m, $Δz = 25$ m, $|u| ≈ 0.01$ m/s after the first step) got $ν_t = 5.7·10^6$ m²/s and an adaptive step of $2.4·10^{-4}$ s. The code therefore multiplies the buoyancy term by $\min(1, 1/Ri_∇)$, with

\[Ri_∇ = \frac{|g/θ_0|\,|∇θ|}{∂_l u_m\,∂_l u_m},\]

a gradient Richardson number ($|g/θ_0||∇θ| = |N^2|$ for a vertical θ gradient). Because $∂_l u_m ∂_l u_m ≥ (∂_z u)^2 + (∂_z v)^2$, $Ri_∇$ is at most the gradient Richardson number, and the Miles–Howard threshold of shear instability is $Ri_g = 1/4$. So the published model is evaluated unchanged (the weight is exactly 1) wherever $Ri_∇ ≤ 1$, which covers resolved shear turbulence. Where $Ri_∇ > 1$ the buoyancy contribution is $C(g/θ_0)(δ_k∂_k w)(δ_k∂_k θ)/(|g/θ_0||∇θ|) ≤ Cδ_{max}^2|∇w|$, which vanishes with the velocity, and in every regime it is at most $Cδ_{max}^2 N_∇$ with $N_∇^2 = |g/θ_0||∇θ|$. Neither Rozema et al. (2015) nor Abkar & Moin (2017) regularise the ratio; Oceananigans' AMD only guards $∂_l u_m ∂_l u_m = 0$ and leaves its buoyancy term off by default as unvalidated.

Measured (test/amd_vreman.jl): on $θ = θ_s + Γz$ with velocity noise of amplitude $ε = 10^{-2}, 10^{-4}, 10^{-6}$ m/s on the 40 × 40 × 25 m grid, max $ν_t$ is 0.611, 6.1·10⁻³, 6.1·10⁻⁵ m²/s (the published ratio: 241, 2.4·10⁴, 2.4·10⁶). On the decaying-turbulence field with $Γ$ set to a bulk Richardson number of 0.1, 0.53 % of the cells have $Ri_∇ > 1$ and $⟨2ν_tS_{ij}S_{ij}⟩$ changes by −2.4·10⁻⁵ (relative). The CBL of #504 now runs at the 5 s maximum step for 100 steps ($ν_t$ max 0.019 m²/s at step 1).

What the buoyancy term does is set by the sign of $∂w/∂z$, not by the stratification alone: on $θ = θ_s + Γz$ it is $(g/θ_0)Δz^2(∂w/∂z)Γ$, which lowers $ν_t$ where the resolved flow is compressed vertically and raises it where it is stretched. On a random velocity field with $Γ = 3$ K/km it lowered $ν_t$ in 1341 cells and raised it in 450 (of 3584). The monotone decrease with $N^2$ that the issue asked for holds for $∂w/∂z < 0$ (the test uses $u = ax + Sz$, $v = ay$, $w = -2az$).

Vreman (VremanClosure, compute_vreman_νt!). Vreman (2004): $ν_t = c\sqrt{B_β/(α_{ij}α_{ij})}$ with $α_{ij} = ∂_i u_j$, $β_{ij} = Δ_m^2 α_{mi}α_{mj}$, $B_β = β_{11}β_{22} - β_{12}^2 + β_{11}β_{33} - β_{13}^2 + β_{22}β_{33} - β_{23}^2$ and $c = 2.5C_s^2$ (0.0723 for $C_s = 0.17$); $κ_t = ν_t/Pr_t$. $B_β$ is the sum of the principal 2 × 2 minors of $β$ and is computed in the equivalent Cauchy–Binet form $Σ_{m<n} Δ_m^2Δ_n^2 |∂_m\mathbf u × ∂_n\mathbf u|^2$. That form is non-negative by construction and exactly zero for any flow that varies in one direction only. The minor form leaves round-off on a two-component shear $u(z), v(z)$, and the square root turned that into $ν_t ≈ 10^{-10}$ m²/s in the Berlin laminar check.

Prognostic TKE closure (P56-b)

PrognosticSGS.TKEClosure (#182) is the first stateful closure of the contract: the Deardorff (1980) / Moeng (1984) 1.5-order model with the SGS TKE $e$ as its memory, $ν_t = c_k l\sqrt e$ and $κ_t = (1 + 2l/Δ)ν_t$. Its shear production is the exact discrete dual of the momentum operator above, so the resolved kinetic energy the closure removes is the energy $e$ gains; its neutral equilibrium is this page's model with $C_s = 0.194$; its stability length $0.76\sqrt e/N$ gives a critical Richardson number of 0.233 (in equilibrium; $ν_t$ stays positive while $e$ decays in a transient), so no stability function is applied to it and StabilityCorrected does not wrap it — by design, not a gap. It has no $κ_h ≠ κ_v$ anisotropy either (one filter width $Δ$, as in PALM): on the Berlin grid it mixes about 3.4× more vertically and 1.84× less horizontally than StabilityCorrected Smagorinsky of equal $C_s$. Its $N^2$ is that of cloudy air when it is given the condensate (#239). In the CBC benchmark above it decays too fast (−9.4 % / −26.9 % at 32³). Everything else is on Prognostic SGS TKE.

σ-model

The σ-model of Nicoud, Baya Toda, Cabrit, Bose & Lee (2011) is a closure of the contract, SigmaModel(C_σ = 1.35), P59-b (#184). It builds the eddy viscosity from the singular values $σ_1 ≥ σ_2 ≥ σ_3 ≥ 0$ of the resolved velocity gradient $g_{ij} = ∂u_i/∂x_j$:

\[ν_t = (C_σ Δ)^2\, D_σ, \qquad D_σ = \frac{σ_3\,(σ_1 - σ_2)\,(σ_2 - σ_3)}{σ_1^2}, \qquad Δ = (Δx\,Δy\,Δz)^{1/3},\]

with $κ_t = ν_t/Pr_t$. The design goal is an operator that is zero wherever the flow cannot be turbulent. Smagorinsky's $|S|$ sees only the strain, so it is on in any laminar shear. $D_σ$ vanishes in these cases (Nicoud et al. 2011, Sec. II):

  • $σ_3 = 0$ whenever $g$ has rank ≤ 2: any two-dimensional flow, pure shear, solid-body rotation;
  • $σ_1 = σ_2$ or $σ_2 = σ_3$: isotropic expansion or contraction, axisymmetric strain.

At a no-slip wall $u, v = O(z)$ and $w = O(z^2)$, so $σ_1 = O(1)$, $σ_2 = O(z)$, $σ_3 = O(z^2)$ and $D_σ = O(z^3)$. This is the near-wall behaviour of the true SGS dissipation, and it comes without a damping function. $C_σ = 1.35$ is Nicoud et al.'s value from isotropic turbulence.

Computing the singular values. $σ_i^2$ are the eigenvalues of $G = g^Tg$. They are found in closed form, with no LAPACK call per cell, from the trigonometric solution of the characteristic cubic (Smith 1961; the form Nicoud et al. take from Hasan, Basri & Kopp 2010). The cubic is written in the deviator $G' = G - \tfrac13 \mathrm{tr}G\,I$, with $α_1 = \mathrm{tr}(G'^2)/6$ and $α_2 = \det G'/2$, so neither is formed by cancellation. $D_σ$ is a product of eigenvalue gaps, and near a double eigenvalue the cubic gives a gap only to $\sqrt ε$, because the arccos argument is near ±1. A plain implementation therefore returns $ν_t ≈ 10^{-8}(C_σΔ)^2|g|$ for axisymmetric strain instead of zero. Two steps fix this:

  1. Where the smaller gap is below 1 % of the spread, the simple eigenvalue is deflated. Its eigenvector is the largest cross product of two rows of $G − λI$, and the close pair is recomputed from the 2 × 2 compression of $G$ on the orthogonal complement, whose gap is accurate to $ε\|G\|$ (Kopp 2008).
  2. The smallest singular value is $σ_3 = |\det g|/(σ_1σ_2)$, which is exactly zero for a rank-2 $g$ rather than the square root of a round-off eigenvalue.

$σ_1 − σ_2$ is formed as $(λ_1 − λ_2)/(σ_1 + σ_2)$. Round-off can still leave $σ_3$ a few ulp above $σ_2$ when the two coincide; the operator is clipped at zero there. The gradient is _velocity_gradient_at, the one stencil of the contract, so there is no second stencil.

Limitations and planned upgrades

The current implementation is deliberately the simplest working Smagorinsky-Lilly. Known deficiencies (all fixable in later phases):

  • No wall damping function (Van Driest / Piomelli) for Smagorinsky, by design. The static closure has none; near walls it overshoots ($Φ_m$ up to 1.45-1.65 at $C_s = 0.17$, P54-d). The dynamic closures replace damping with the dynamic procedure: the scale-dependent Lagrangian model (LASD, #186, above) recovers the log law ($Φ_m ∈ [0.93, 1.15]$ in the half channel at 24³) without a damping function, and the wall stress comes from the wall model. The plane-averaged DynamicSmagorinsky is kept as an option of the same Germano kernel; it is not scale dependent. The σ-model needs none either: its $ν_t$ falls off as $z^3$ at a no-slip wall (measured slope 2.955).
  • The constant coefficient is too dissipative in transitional / stably stratified regimes. The dynamic coefficient and the σ-model, which is zero in laminar shear and in 2-D flow, are the alternatives; in stable stratification the Richardson-number reduction of Stability correction (P55-e) applies to the Smagorinsky, dynamic and MLSGS closures, on by default for those providers in the Berlin case; its validation on GABLS1 and the stratified channel (P55-f) is in Stable boundary layers.
  • The σ-model costs about 8 times the Smagorinsky kernel (6.7 against 0.85 ms on 64×64×32). It is zero only in exactly laminar (parallel, 2-D, pure-strain) flow; a laminar but weakly 3-D free stream gets a small $ν_t$ (Berlin, sponge relaxing to zero: 2.7 % of Smagorinsky's above the sponge start), and in a weakly turbulent, nearly unsheared stream it can exceed Smagorinsky's (Berlin default: 1.7 ×, both small, 2e-3 ν). It is not stability-aware by itself; it can be wrapped in StabilityCorrected like any eddy-viscosity closure, but the Berlin :sigma provider runs it unwrapped. The prognostic TKE closure (P56-b, above) has its own stability length and is not wrapped.
  • Relaminarised stable regions (P59-e, #195; see Transition → Relaminarisation). In decaying turbulence on a held laminar shear at $Ri = 1$ the collapsed state keeps Smagorinsky at max $ν_t/ν$ = 0.118 (its $(C_sΔ)^2|S|$ sees the laminar shear), the σ-model's horizontal viscosity at 0.017 ν (the residual internal waves are 3-D, so $σ_3 ≠ 0$), and the σ-model + stability correction's vertical viscosity at exactly 0 ($Ri_g > Ri_c$, $f_m = 0$ in Brown et al. 1994). Only the vertical viscosity is stability-corrected; the horizontal one is not. Coarse wall-resolved LES of the cooled open channel relaminarises at a much smaller $h/L$ than DNS, with any of the closures and without one (see the transition page): the critical stability of a resolved near-wall cycle is resolution-limited.
  • The SGS closure itself has no wall treatment: at a free-slip z-wall its flux through the wall face is zero. The wall stress — smooth (Spalding), rough (log law) or Monin–Obukhov — is supplied by the wall model, apply_wall_stress! of Aeolus.WallModels (Phase 57, #169; see Wall models); the Berlin ground uses it.
