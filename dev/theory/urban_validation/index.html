BUBBLE / CAPITOUL validation of the urban canopy

This page is the acceptance test of the Phase 49 urban canopy (#30). The pieces were validated one at a time against analytic solutions and standards: conduction (Urban facet conduction), radiosity coupling (Facet–radiosity coupling), the building zone (Building energy) and glazing (Glazing). Here all of them run together in street canyons taken from two urban field campaigns, and the results are compared with published observations:

  • CAPITOUL (Toulouse, 2004–05): the surface temperatures of roads, walls and a roof.
  • BUBBLE (Basel, June 2002): the surface energy balance.

The test is validation/urban_validation.jl. The case is built in validation/support/urban_validation_driver.jl, which labels every input with its source, or as an assumption.

The offline canyon case

Every piece of physics comes from the package:

  • Scene. A street canyon between two building rows is a Radiosity scene (build_scene, view_factors). Shading is ray-cast and view factors are exact.
  • Rotation. The scene's street axis is its +y axis. Real street azimuths are handled by rotating the sun into the scene frame.
  • Facets. Every facet is an UrbanFacet conduction column. The facets are coupled through the implicit longwave radiosity system (UrbanFacetScene).
  • Buildings. Each building row is a BuildingZone behind its own facets (scene_building_zone), solved with them in one implicit system (step_buildings!). Following TEB in the CAPITOUL runs (Pigeon et al. 2009), zones are heated to at least 19 °C and not cooled.
  • Air-side exchange. This uses the TEB closures, added to UrbanSurface for this issue:
    • Roofs: Monin–Obukhov similarity (SurfaceExchange) in the forcing wind, with TEB's roughness lengths $z_{0m} = 0.15$ m and $z_{0h} = 7.5\times10^{-4}$ m.
    • Street: Monin–Obukhov similarity in the canyon wind at $H/2$, with $z_{0m} = 0.05$ m and $z_{0h} = 2.5\times10^{-4}$ m (Masson et al. 2002; Lemonsu et al. 2004).
    • Walls: $h = 11.8 + 4.2\,U_{can}$, from rowley_convection_coefficient.
    • Canyon wind (canyon_wind_speed, Masson 2000; Lemonsu et al. 2004 eq. B1):

\[U_{can} = \frac{2}{\pi}\exp\!\left(-\frac{H}{4W}\right) \frac{\ln\big((H/3)/z_{0,town}\big)}{\ln\big((z_{ref}+H/3)/z_{0,town}\big)}\,U, \qquad z_{0,town} = H/10 .\]

  • Sampling. Temperatures are averaged over the facets of the two central rows of a canyon about 5 H long. The street there sees the sky within 0.8–1.4 % of an infinitely long canyon (canyon_sky_view_factor, asserted < 2 %).
  • Time step. One hour, with the sun at mid-hour.

Forcing — and why it is not the tower's

The campaign tower records are not public. The forcing is NASA POWER hourly data for the campaign dates:

  • Radiation: CERES SYN1deg, giving $S↓$, direct normal, diffuse and $L↓$.
  • Meteorology: MERRA-2, giving 2-m temperature and humidity, the 50-m wind and pressure.

The files are test/data/nasa_power_*_hourly.csv, with provenance headers.

The beam is applied as $(S↓ - S_{dif})/\sin h_\odot$ at the mid-hour sun. This way the scene receives exactly the hourly global irradiance; below 3° elevation the beam is counted as diffuse.

The satellite radiation is checked against the tower where the papers allow it:

  • Seven clear BUBBLE days, all hours. CERES $S↓ + L↓ = 681.5$ W m⁻². The tower value, from Hamdi & Masson 2008 Table 4 as $Q^* + S↑ + L↑$, is 680 W m⁻² (asserted within 2 %).
  • Hamdi & Schayes (2005) windows. CERES $L↓$ is 366 / 338 W m⁻² (day / night) against the tower's 360 / 340. CERES $S↓$ in the day window is 766 W m⁻² against 741 (+3.4 %).

The MERRA-2 temperature is a grid-box value without the urban heat island:

  • BUBBLE. On the seven clear days it is 2.55 K below the tower's 18-m mean (Hamdi & Masson 2008, Table 3). BUBBLE therefore adds this one constant.
  • CAPITOUL. No published tower mean was available for the three periods, so the grid value is used unchanged. The canyon air is taken equal to the forcing air, because there is no canyon-air model.

These two approximations act in opposite directions. The sensitivity of the CAPITOUL means to the air temperature is measured below.

CAPITOUL — road, wall and roof temperatures

Canyons. Three canyons were instrumented with infrared thermometers:

H and W are from Moscicki & Voogt (2007), Table 2.1. The azimuths and facing directions are from Pigeon et al. (2009), Table 3.

Constructions and optics. These are the TEB inputs of Pigeon et al. (2009), Table 2:

  • Walls: 30 cm red brick ($C = 1.58$ MJ m⁻³ K⁻¹, $k = 1.15$ W m⁻¹ K⁻¹), α = 0.25, ε = 0.92.
  • Roofs: 6 cm clay tile over 3 cm wood, α = 0.15, ε = 0.90.
  • Road: 5 cm asphalt, 20 cm aggregate and 1 m gravel/soil, α = 0.08, ε = 0.95.

The deep-road temperature and the initial temperature are the mean air temperature of each 46-day window. This is an assumption.

Periods. There are three independent runs. Each has 14 days of spin-up, then the 32-day periods of Moscicki & Voogt (2007):

  • 15 Jul–15 Aug 2004
  • 15 Oct–15 Nov 2004
  • 15 Jan–15 Feb 2005

Observations. The observations are from Moscicki & Voogt (2007), Table 4.1, typed from the table text into validation/data/capitoul_surface_temperature_statistics.csv. That table gives the observed and TEB-modelled mean and standard deviation over all three periods (about 4500 half-hourly samples), with TEB forced by the tower itself. The observed series are:

  • Road: the mean of the three roads.
  • Walls: a weighted mean of the six walls.
  • Roof: a single pitched tile roof on the Alsace canyon.

The model uses equal weights for the walls. Pigeon et al. state that their weighting changes the statistics by at most 0.3 K.

Assertions. The yardstick is the error of the reference model forced by the campaign tower:

  • Means. Each mean must be within TEB's RMSE. Roads and walls pass.
  • Roof. The roof fails, at +3.93 K against 3.50 K. It is recorded with @test_broken.
  • Amplitude. Each standard deviation must be within the largest model/observation ratio that TEB itself shows (1.29, roads). The measured ratios are 1.28, 1.11 and 1.20.
  • Ordering. The observed ordering of the variability, roof > roads > walls, is reproduced.

Orientation (no per-wall observations are published, so these are physics checks):

  • Alsace (north–south). The east and west walls have the same mean (difference 0.00 K). In the summer composite the east wall peaks at 10–11 UTC and the west wall at 14–15 UTC.
  • Pomme and Rémusat. The equator-facing walls are warmer than the pole-facing ones: SSW − NNE = +0.62 K and SSE − NNW = +0.74 K.

The match of the road and wall means is partly luck. The means move by +0.82 K (road), +0.85 K (walls) and +0.76 K (roof) for every +1 K of forcing air temperature. The grid-box temperature lacks the urban heat island: in Basel, on the seven clear summer days, it was 2.55 K below the tower's 18-m mean (that difference mixes the heat island with any reanalysis bias; the two cannot be separated here). There is also no canyon-air model. The Hamdi & Masson (2008) Table 3 observations show canyon air about 1 K warmer than the air above the roofs by day. An uncorrected cold forcing and the missing canyon warming both bias the same way: towards colder surfaces. The agreement to 0.1 K therefore cannot be read as a skill of 0.1 K. What the comparison does establish:

  • the mean is within the reference model's per-sample error;
  • the variability is within the reference model's amplitude error;
  • the orientation behaviour is physical.

The roof is too warm, and why. The TEB input roof (6 cm tile over 3 cm wood) sits directly on the building zone. Its U-value is 2.9 W m⁻² K⁻¹ with the ISO 6946 surface resistances. Over the summer nights (22–04 UTC) the modelled roof stays at the air temperature (19.95 °C against 19.89 °C), fed by 15.8 W m⁻² of heat conducted up from the rooms. The observed roof "typically dropped below 15 °C at night and above 40 °C during mid-afternoon" in summer (Moscicki & Voogt 2007, §4.1.3). It is a pitched tile roof, whereas TEB (and this case) assume a flat one; what lies under it is not described in the sources used here. TEB with the same inputs also runs warm (+1.40 K), and an independent SUM analysis in the same paper suggests that the observed roof is not unusually cold.

A flat, thin roof straight onto a heated zone is the likely cause. Replacing it with some other construction (e.g. a ventilated roof space) would be a guess that no paper here supports, so it was not done.

BUBBLE — the surface energy balance of Basel-Sperrstrasse

Geometry and materials (Hamdi & Schayes 2005, Tables 1 and 3):

  • Buildings 14.6 m, street 11.23 m (H/W = 1.30), roofs 15 m wide (modelled 14.97 m; roof plan fraction 0.571).
  • Two street directions, 67° and 157°, averaged with equal weights.
  • Albedo 0.14 / 0.14 / 0.08 and emissivity 0.90 / 0.90 / 0.94 for wall / roof / road.
  • Thermal diffusivity and heat capacity 6.7×10⁻⁷ m² s⁻¹ and 1.4 MJ m⁻³ K⁻¹ for walls and roofs, 2.8×10⁻⁷ and 1.8 for the road.

The thicknesses are not published. The case uses 0.30 m for walls and roofs and 0.50 m for the road. These are at least 2.2 diurnal damping depths, so the diurnal surface response does not depend on them.

Fluxes. The fluxes are those of one repeat unit of the canyon array per unit plan area:

  • $Q^*$, $Q_H$ and $ΔQ_S$ are the area-weighted facet $R_n$, $H$ and $G$. $G$ includes the heat conducted into the rooms.
  • $S↑ = S↓ - Σ A\,S_{abs}/A_{plan}$ and $L↑ = L↓ - Σ A\,L_{net}/A_{plan}$.

Observations, in validation/data/bubble_sperrstrasse_energy_balance.csv:

  • Hamdi & Masson (2008), Table 4. Means over the clear days 17, 18, 19, 22, 23, 29 and 30 June 2002, with TEB's skill at the same site. The paper does not define its day/night split, so only its all-hours means are compared with. Its day (D) and night (N) RMSEs are, however, borrowed as the error scale for the Hamdi & Schayes windows below.
  • Hamdi & Schayes (2005), Table 2. Clear days 17, 18, 23, 26 and 30 June. Day is 08–16 UTC and night is 22–03 UTC.

Storage hysteresis. Hamdi & Masson (2008) observe two things: the storage flux rises faster than $Q_H$ in the morning and peaks earlier, and it turns negative 1–2 h before $Q^*$. In the model's clear-day composite:

  • $ΔQ_S$ peaks at 09 UTC, $Q^*$ at 11 UTC and $Q_H$ at 12 UTC;
  • $ΔQ_S$ turns negative at 16 UTC and $Q^*$ at 18 UTC.

At night the storage release exceeds the radiative loss ($ΔQ_S/Q^* = 1.58$, observed 1.30), and it keeps $Q_H$ positive, as observed. All of these are asserted.

What fails, and the estimates that go with it. These are estimates, not fits:

  • Daytime $L↑$ (+39 W m⁻² against TEB's 15). The modelled canopy is radiometrically too warm by day. The source area is 16 % vegetation, which is not modelled. At the air temperature of the window (28.7 °C) that vegetation would emit about 469 W m⁻² instead of the urban 560, which lowers $L↑$ by about 15 W m⁻². The satellite $S↓$ in the window is also 3.4 % above the tower's.
  • $S↑$ (−4.3 against ±4). The canyon array reflects 9.2 % of $S↓$. The vegetation at the package's park-class albedo (0.205) would add about 6 W m⁻².
  • Turbulent flux. Without vegetation there is no $Q_E$, so the model's $Q_H$ is compared with the observed $Q_H + Q_E$ (155), using the RMSE of $Q_H$ (49) as the tolerance. Against the observed $Q_H$ alone (112) the model is +36.6 W m⁻², which is also inside 49 but is a real sensible-heat excess: the energy the missing vegetation would evaporate goes into $Q_H$ instead. At night the model's $Q_H/Q^* = -0.58$ against an observed $Q_H/Q^* = -0.21$, i.e. about 46 against 17 W m⁻² upward; only its sign is asserted.

Sensitivity to the temperature correction. With separate day and night offsets instead of one (+1.91 / +4.90 K, which assumes that "day" in Hamdi & Masson Table 3 means sun up):

  • night $Q_H/Q^* = -0.28$ and $ΔQ_S/Q^* = 1.28$, closer to the observed −0.21 and 1.30;
  • all-hours $L↑$ 482.6 and $Q^*$ 169.3 W m⁻²;
  • daytime $L↑$ 557.7 W m⁻².

The single documented offset is what the test uses.

What was not validated

  • Time series. The campaign time series are not public. Hence there is no per-sample RMSE, bias by hour, or scatter against observations, only period statistics. The model's RMSE against observations is unknown.
  • Per-season and per-wall observations. Moscicki & Voogt give these only as figures, and figures were not digitised. The seasonal statements in their text (TEB's winter road bias −2.53 K, the summer road over-estimate at noon) were not tested.
  • The CAPITOUL tower forcing, the canyon air temperature, and the anthropogenic heat flux against Pigeon et al. (2009). The last was compared with the building energy inventory in #29.
  • Vegetation and latent heat (BUBBLE's $Q_E$).
  • Windows. The walls here are opaque brick, as in TEB.
  • Hand-held and airborne infrared data. The hand-held infrared samples in Qu (2011, Tables 6.3–6.5) and the airborne infrared images (Lagouarde et al. 2010; Hénon et al.) are single-instant brightness temperatures of individual sunlit and shaded patches. The canyon model's facet averages are not comparable with them.
  • Other papers. Lemonsu et al. (2004), Marseille, is not BUBBLE or CAPITOUL and was not used. Rotach et al. (2005) and Christen & Vogt (2004) were not accessible, so nothing is quoted from them.

Wrong expectations found while writing the test

  • The hand value of the canyon-wind check. I wrote 0.28158 for $\frac{2}{π}e^{-1/4}\ln(10/3)/\ln(25/3)$; the correct value is 0.28154. This was an arithmetic slip in the expectation, not the code.
  • $S↑$. It passed TEB's tolerance by 0.2 W m⁻² with the first forcing split, which gave the scene 1.2 % more shortwave than the hourly global irradiance. After that was corrected, it fails by 0.3 W m⁻². The assertion was kept and is recorded as broken.
