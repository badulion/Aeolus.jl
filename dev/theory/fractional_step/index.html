Fractional-step projection

The Boussinesq equations couple velocity and pressure through the incompressibility constraint. Because there is no evolution equation for p (it is a Lagrange multiplier), we cannot advance u and p by a straightforward explicit step. The classical remedy is the fractional-step / pressure-projection method of Chorin (1968) and Temam (1969).

The idea

Split each time step into two sub-problems:

  1. Predictor. Advance the momentum equation ignoring the pressure gradient. The result u* is an intermediate velocity that is generally not divergence-free.
  2. Corrector. Find a pressure-like field φ whose gradient projects u* back onto the space of divergence-free fields.

Concretely, one step of size Δt is:

\[\frac{u^* - u^n}{\Delta t} = F(u^n) \tag{1}\]

\[\frac{u^{n+1} - u^*}{\Delta t} = -\frac{1}{\rho_0}\nabla \phi \tag{2}\]

with F(u) collecting advection, viscous, SGS, buoyancy, Coriolis, and any body forces — everything except the pressure gradient. Taking divergence of (2) and applying incompressibility ∇·uⁿ⁺¹ = 0:

\[\nabla^2 \phi = \frac{\rho_0}{\Delta t}\,\nabla\!\cdot\! u^*. \tag{3}\]

Solve (3) for φ, then apply (2) to get uⁿ⁺¹. The physical pressure after the step is p = φ/Δt.

The Aeolus recipe

In code (Simulations.step!), one Euler-projection step reads:

1.  fill_halos!(u, v, w, θ)
2.  zero!(tu, tv, tw, tθ)
3.  momentum_advect!(tu, tv, tw, u, v, w)
    advect_centered!(tθ, u, v, w, θ)
4.  if ν > 0
        tu += ν ∇²u; tv += ν ∇²v; tw += ν ∇²w; tθ += ν ∇²θ
5.  if C_s > 0
        compute_smagorinsky_νt!(νt, u, v, w)
        add_eddy_viscosity_[u,v,w]!(t_, _, νt)
        add_eddy_diffusion_scalar!(tθ, θ, νt, Pr_t)
6.  if θ₀ > 0
        add_buoyancy!(tw, θ, θ₀, g)
7.  if f ≠ 0
        add_coriolis!(tu, tv, u, v, f)
8.  u* = u + Δt · tu     (in place)
    v* = v + Δt · tv
    w* = w + Δt · tw
9.  fill_halos!(u*, v*, w*)
    div_buf = ∇·u*
10. solve!(φ, div_buf, solver)      # solves ∇²φ = div_buf
11. u   = u* - ∂x φ                 (staggered gradient at faces)
    v   = v* - ∂y φ
    w   = w* - ∂z φ
12. θ  = θ + Δt · tθ
    p  = φ / Δt
    t += Δt

Steps 1–7 build the tendency F(uⁿ); steps 8–9 form u*; steps 10–11 project. All buffers are pre-allocated in the constructor — no heap traffic in the hot loop.

Why the projection is exact on the discrete grid

As shown on the staggered grid page, the discrete divergence of the discrete gradient equals the standard 7-point Laplacian. If the Poisson solve in step (10) uses that same discrete Laplacian (as our FFT solver does — see Discrete Poisson), then:

\[\nabla \!\cdot\! u^{n+1} = \nabla\!\cdot\! u^* - \nabla\!\cdot\!\nabla\phi = \nabla\!\cdot\! u^* - \nabla^2\phi = 0.\]

The residual divergence is zero to machine precision, not to some truncation order. The max_divergence diagnostic in our TGV and convection examples sits at 10⁻¹⁵–10⁻¹⁶ throughout the run, confirming this.

Order of accuracy

The Chorin-style splitting used above is:

  • First-order in time (Euler predictor).
  • Second-order in space (from the finite-difference stencils).

There is a well-known splitting error on the pressure of O(Δt) that biases the pressure boundary layer but does not degrade the velocity accuracy globally. For higher-order timestepping we can apply the projection at each stage of a Runge-Kutta scheme — this is projected RK3, planned for a later step.

Splitting error and its remedies

The Chorin scheme applies ∇p only after the predictor. This is equivalent to imposing a boundary condition ∂φ/∂n = 0 on φ (for periodic BCs this is trivial). For walls, ∂φ/∂n = 0 is inconsistent with ∂p/∂n ≠ 0 at solid boundaries, giving the well-known "pressure boundary layer". The incremental projection and rotational projection schemes of Timmermans, Minev & Van De Vosse and Guermond et al. fix this by projecting pⁿ⁺¹ - pⁿ instead of pⁿ⁺¹. Aeolus is periodic-only for now, so this refinement is deferred to Phase 1b when walls arrive.

Prescribed-flux faces (open boundaries)

A nested child, or any domain driven by outside data, prescribes the velocity on every face (N-open-b, #255; OpenBoundary). The normal component is written into the boundary-face slot of u* before the divergence is formed, and φ obeys the homogeneous Neumann condition ∂φ/∂n = 0 (Kim & Moin 1985). Then

\[u^{n+1}\cdot n = u^*\cdot n - Δt\,\frac{∂φ}{∂n} = g^{n+1}\cdot n ,\]

so the projection leaves the prescribed face value exactly unchanged (the code asserts that ∂φ/∂n is exactly zero at the west and south faces, whose face slots lie inside the interior index range). This is not an approximation: with $u^* = u(t+Δt) + Δt∇p$ in the interior and the face value $g$ on the boundary, the discrete Neumann problem is solved by $φ = Δt\,p$ — the face's missing $Δt\,∂p/∂n$ is exactly the term the mirror halo drops from the boundary cell's Laplacian.

Compatibility and the mass-flux correction

With every normal velocity prescribed, the Neumann problem is solvable only if $\sum ∇·u^*\,ΔV = \oint u·n\,dA = 0$ (compatibility). Interpolated or analytic data never balance to round-off, and the solvers would leave the uniform residual $∇·u = Q/V$ in every cell. update_open_boundaries! therefore applies, once per projected (sub)step and before the projection, the only boundary mass correction in the code base (correct_boundary_mass_flux!; Hellsten et al. 2021, §3.3, Eq. 2):

\[Q = \sum_{\text{open faces}} W\,u·n\,A, \qquad δ = \frac{Q}{\sum W A}, \qquad u·n \leftarrow u·n - δ ,\]

with $W = 1$ for the Boussinesq core and the constraint weight ($ρ̄$ anelastic, $ρ̄θ̄$ pseudo-incompressible) for the sound-proof cores, taken exactly as the projection's divergence takes it ($W_c(k)$ on lateral faces, $W_f$ on the top). correction = :outflow_only adjusts only the outflow faces. Measured (test/open_boundaries.jl, 16 × 12 × 8 box, $Q = 4.8 × 10^4$ m³ s⁻¹ of net outflow): without the correction $\max|∇·u| = Q/V = 2.5 × 10^{-4}$ s⁻¹ in every core, to 1e-12; with it $\max|∇·u| < 10^{-12}$ s⁻¹ in step!, step_rk3!, step_imex! (ARK2, ROS2) and step_adaptive!, and $\max|(1/W)∇·(Wu)| < 10^{-9}$ s⁻¹ in the anelastic and pseudo-incompressible cores; the boundary faces equal the corrected data bit for bit.

Where the boundary data enter a step

The buffers belong to the state: the halo fill at the start of a (sub)step reads them, so they must hold the boundary values of the (sub)step's start state. update_open_boundaries!(sim, t, dt) is called after the predictor with the time t the projected velocity belongs to — t + Δt for an Euler step; t + Δt, t + 2Δt and t + 3Δt/2 for the three Shu–Osher substages of step_rk3!, whose buffers are then combined with the fields' own weights (3/4, 1/4 and 1/3, 2/3); the stage times of ARK2 and ROS2. step_adaptive! restores the buffers with the fields when it rejects a step.

Implicit diffusion: the intermediate-velocity condition

With an implicit viscous term (step_imex!) the diffusion solve needs boundary values for the intermediate velocity $u^* = u^{n+1} + ∇φ$, $φ ≈ τ p$ ($τ$ the stage's projection step). Imposing the data $g$ itself on the tangential components leaves, after the projection, a viscous boundary layer of amplitude $τ\,∂p/∂s$ — a first-order splitting error that shows up as order reduction. Kim & Moin's (1985) remedy is the condition $u^*_s = g_s + τ\,∂p/∂s$, with the latest pressure; open_intermediate_velocity! applies it around each ARK2 stage (the projection itself sees $g$). Measured on the Dirichlet-everywhere Taylor–Green window ($ν Δt/h^2 = 0.2$, $N = 16, 32, 64$): with $g$ alone the observed order was 1.60 and 1.65, the time error growing with $ν Δt/h^2$ (at 0.4 the $N = 32$ error was 2.2 times the spatial one); with the Kim & Moin condition it is 2.005 and 2.006 and independent of $ν Δt/h^2$ up to 0.4. The explicit drivers need no such condition (their tendencies use the step-start halos): step_rk3! measures 2.035 and 2.022.

Radiation outflow

Structures generated inside a nested child — building wakes, plumes, eddies — reach the outflow face, where the parent's data do not contain them; a Dirichlet face then reflects them (N-open-c, #260). The alternative is a radiation condition on the outflow points,

\[\frac{∂φ}{∂t} + c\,\frac{∂φ}{∂n} = 0 ,\]

selected per face by open_boundary_conditions(…; outflow = …): :dirichlet (the data everywhere, the nested default), :convective or :orlanski. A point is an outflow point when the outward normal velocity of its boundary face is positive at the start of the step; inflow points keep the Dirichlet data. prepare_open_boundaries! fixes the phase speed once per step:

  • :convective — $c = \max(u·n, 0)$ (Pauley, Moin & Reynolds 1990);
  • :orlanski — for the tangential velocities and the scalars, the speed diagnosed from the interior layer next to the face at this and the previous step start, $c = -(∂φ/∂t)/(∂φ/∂n)$ (Orlanski 1976), clipped to $0 ≤ c ≤ Δn/Δt$ (Miller & Thorpe 1981); the normal velocity uses the convective speed (below).

Every halo layer $b$ of every field (u, v, w and each scalar with boundary data) is then advanced, inside update_open_boundaries!, by the explicit second-order upstream update

\[φ_b \leftarrow φ_b^0 - r\,\frac{3φ_b^0 - 4φ_{b-1}^0 + φ_{b-2}^0}{2}, \qquad r = \min\!\left(\frac{c\,Δt}{Δn},\,1\right),\]

in values at the start of the (sub)stage (the buffers, and the two interior layers recorded by mark_open_boundary_stage! after the stage's halo fill). Two details matter:

  • Stage-start values. At the update the velocity is already the predicted $u^*$, which differs from the projected velocity by $Δt∇φ$. Radiating from $u^*$ put that pressure-gradient error into the outflow; radiating from the stage-start values makes the boundary update an explicit stage that SSP-RK3 combines with its own weights. A first version that read the halos before the stage's fill picked up stale corner values (a uniform stream through the pseudo-incompressible core with SGS on drifted, |w| = 6.5e-4 m/s after 100 steps); the layers are now recorded after the fill.
  • Second-order upstream. With the centred interior schemes the first-order upstream difference reflected a scalar pulse (σ = 4Δx) with a residual $∫θ'^2/∫θ'^2_0 = 1.9 × 10^{-4}$; the second-order one, $7.9 × 10^{-6}$.

The normal velocity is not a radiating wave. In a projected core continuity ties $u·n$ at the face to the interior, and the pressure responds at once to the boundary value. An Orlanski speed diagnosed from $u·n$ and fed back through the pressure drifts: in the outgoing-vortex test the diagnosed speeds of u and w settled at 0.79 U and 1.23 U (the diagnostic evaluated on a run with an accurate boundary gives 0.96 U for both) and R reached 0.15. With the convective speed for $u·n$ and Orlanski for the rest, R = 4.9e-3. Both radiating modes therefore advance the normal velocity convectively. The mass-flux correction of the previous section balances the radiated normal velocities exactly as it balances data.

The vortex is carried by a uniform stream between free-slip walls (a 2-D x–z flow), whose images make its far field decay as $\exp(-π|x|/L_z)$; the truth is the same flow in a periodic domain three times longer. A single vortex in a periodic row would instead carry the uniform jump $±Γ/(2L)$ of a vortex sheet, which no uniform parent matches. The Poiseuille reference is the discrete steady state, the parabola shifted by $U_{max}Δz^2/H^2$: the no-slip halo is linear ($u_0 = -u_1$), and the parabola itself differs from the scheme's steady solution by exactly that constant.

The advective flux through the boundary

boundary_scalar_flux(sim, ϕ; scheme) (in AdvectionSchemes) returns the flux $\oint ϕ\,u·n\,dA$ out of each face, formed from the face velocity and the face value of $ϕ$ that the scheme reconstructs (:centered, :upwind, WENO-Z5/7, TENO5/8, WENO-JS5). The flux-form schemes telescope, so

\[\int ϕ\,dV\Big|^{n+1} - \int ϕ\,dV\Big|^{n} = -Δt \oint ϕ\,u·n\,dA\]

per forward-Euler step to round-off: measured $|·|/\int|ϕ|dV$ = 2.6e-18 (centred), 1.5e-18 (WENO-Z5), 4.0e-18 (TENO5) and between 5.5e-18 and 1.8e-16 through step! (the last depends on the summation order of the run). It is the only boundary-flux function of the code base: the nesting and Berlin budgets (N-nest-d, N-nest-e, N-open-d, N-berlin-nest) use it. The inflow faces of a scalar carry Dirichlet data in all $H$ halo layers, so the wide upwind stencils read data, not extrapolation; open_boundary_conditions(…; halo = required_halo(scheme)) and boundary_scalar_flux refuse a mesh whose halo along an open axis is shallower than the scheme needs.

Near the open faces (N-open-d)

Opening a face breaks four assumptions a closed, periodic box makes.

The sponge target. The cores' Rayleigh sponge adds $-α(z)(u - u_{target})$ to the momentum. Below a lid the target is zero; below an open top it must be the state the boundary data impose (the parent's, in a nest; Hellsten et al. 2021 relax toward the parent), or the layer is a momentum sink of $α(z)\,U$ against the inflow. Every core passes sim.sponge_target (the constructor keyword sponge_target, e.g. sponge_target(data, mesh)) to the one damping kernel; a sponge below an open top without a target is an error. Measured: a geostrophic stream $U_g = 5$ m s⁻¹ through an open box with an open top stays $U_g$ to 0.0 over 100 steps of step!, step_rk3!, step_imex! (ARK2) and the anelastic core; with the zero target it loses 5.97 m s⁻¹.

Neighbours. Code that reached across the domain edge with a periodic wrap (mod1(i ± 1, N)) reads the neighbour through neighbor_index(mesh, bcs, i, axis; face, outside): the wrap across a periodic axis (bit for bit the old code), the mirror (zero-gradient) cell or no neighbour across any other face, and for a staggered field the boundary-face slot $N+1$, which holds an open face's prescribed normal velocity. The immersed-wall model, the wall distance, the a-priori stress centring and the Berlin couplings use it.

Solid points. A building the box edge cuts touches the open face. Its boundary points (mark_open_boundary_solids!, the faces the direct-forcing IBM zeroes) take the solid value, zero velocity, after the data are written, and the mass-flux correction neither counts nor adjusts them, so $δ = Q/A_{fluid}$. Measured with a cube at the open east face under west inflow: $\max|∇·u|$ = 1.5e-16, $δ = Q/A_{fluid}$ to 1e-12.

Budget terms. boundary_scalar_flux also returns, per level, the diffusive flux $-K ∂ϕ/∂n\,A$ through every open scalar face, with the kernels' face diffusivity $K = ν + \tfrac12(κ_{in} + κ_{out})$ (keyword diffusivity), and the part of the advective flux the mass correction carries, $-δ\,W ϕ_{face} A$ on every face it adjusted (keyword correction_levels). Then a whole step! with centred advection, $ν$ and Smagorinsky diffusion through open faces satisfies

\[\int θ\,dV\Big|^{n+1} - \int θ\,dV\Big|^{n} = -Δt \oint \bigl(θ\,u·n - K\,∂_n θ\bigr)\,dA\]

to 1.2e-13 (relative). For a +1 K inflow anomaly in a uniform stream the flux into the box is $U ΔT A$ for the upwind and WENO-Z5 face values, $U ΔT A/2$ for the centred one (the mean of the halo and the first cell), plus $ν ΔT A/Δx$ of diffusion across the jump, each to 1e-12.

Alternative: pressure-Poisson-per-substage

For RK3 (or higher-order Runge-Kutta), the divergence-free constraint must be enforced at each Runge-Kutta stage, not just once per step. Each stage becomes a mini-projection. Cost: 3× the Poisson solves per time step. Benefit: 3rd-order in time on u, and no splitting-error accumulation. This is what Oceananigans.jl and NCAR-LES do.
