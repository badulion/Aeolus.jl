Large-scale forcing

An LES domain of a few kilometres does not contain the synoptic pressure field, the large-scale advection or the surface that drives a boundary-layer case. They are prescribed. Aeolus.LargeScaleForcing (Phase 58, #171) is the one place where that forcing lives. The GABLS and CASES-99 cases are built on it, Phase 67 will fill it from reanalysis, and the Berlin flagship uses it for its background wind. Before #171 there were two copies of the geostrophic forcing, and they disagreed: the Berlin case nudged the mean wind, while the patchy-surface driver applied a pressure gradient.

Forcing quantities

Each forcing quantity $F$ is one of:

  • a number;
  • a height profile, ForcingTable(z, values);
  • a height-time table, ForcingTable(z, t, values);
  • a function (z, t) -> value.

A table is interpolated linearly in $z$ and in $t$. Outside the tabulated range it is held at its end values, as the GABLS and DICE forcing files are used, and it is never extrapolated.

A forcing is applied over a whole step $[t, t+Δt]$, so the tendency used is the step mean

\[\bar F(z) = \frac{1}{Δt}\int_t^{t+Δt} F(z, t')\,dt' .\]

For a table this mean is exact, because $F$ is piecewise linear in $t$ and the antiderivative is evaluated segment by segment. A forward step therefore adds $Δt\,\bar F$, and the steps sum to $\int F\,dt$ to round-off whatever the step size and wherever the table nodes fall. A function is averaged with Simpson's rule. A number is its own mean.

All forcings act on the cell-centre heights $z_k$, read from the mesh: $z_0 + (k - \tfrac12)Δz$ on a RegularGrid, the stored centres and cell thicknesses on a StretchedGrid (#248). On the C-grid these are the heights of $u$, $v$ and the scalars.

Geostrophic pressure gradient

When the large-scale pressure field is in geostrophic balance with the wind $(U_g, V_g)$, the horizontal momentum equations on an f-plane are

\[\frac{\partial u}{\partial t} = \dots + f v - f V_g, \qquad \frac{\partial v}{\partial t} = \dots - f u + f U_g .\]

The core already adds the Coriolis terms $+fv$ and $-fu$. GeostrophicPressureGradient(U_g, V_g) adds the pressure-gradient terms $-f V_g$ and $+f U_g$ (Holton & Hakim 2013, §3.2). In this form the geostrophic wind is a pressure gradient, not a target state. Two consequences follow, and both are tested.

Inertial oscillation (Blackadar 1957). For frictionless, horizontally uniform flow, the ageostrophic wind $W = (u - U_g) + i(v - V_g)$ obeys $dW/dt = -ifW$. It turns clockwise in the northern hemisphere at the rate $f$, returns after the period $2π/f$, and the speed peaks at $|U_g| + |W_0|$. At night the surface friction decouples from the residual layer, the daytime subgeostrophic wind is left as an ageostrophic departure, and that departure oscillates. This is the mechanism of the nocturnal low-level jet.

Profile nudging

ProfileNudging(targets, τ; weight) relaxes the plane mean of any of $u$, $v$, $θ$, $q_v$ toward a reference (#248):

\[\left.\frac{\partial φ}{\partial t}\right|_n = -\,w(z)\,\frac{\langle φ\rangle_{xy}(z) - φ_{ref}(z, t)}{τ} .\]

The tendency is one number per level, added to every point of it, so the fluctuations $φ - \langle φ\rangle$ are not damped. The reference is any forcing quantity and enters as its step mean. This is the nudging of DALES (Heus et al. 2010, Eq. 92), of the PALM HOPE runs (Heinze et al. 2017, Eq. 4, τ = 6 h for θ and q) and of MicroHH (van Heerwaarden et al. 2017). The weight $w(z)$ is nothing ($w ≡ 1$), a NudgingWeight(z_n; factor, depth) (zero below $z_0 = \mathrm{factor}\cdot z_n$, one above $z_0 + \mathrm{depth}$, a $\sin^2$ ramp between) or any callable z -> w. $z_n$ may be a callable on the state, e.g. a diagnosed boundary-layer depth, so that only the free troposphere is nudged. apply_forcing! returns the volume integrals $\int (∂φ/∂t)_n\,dV$ it added (also tendency_integrals(F)), which a ledger books.

GeostrophicNudging(U_g, V_g, τ) is ProfileNudging((u = U_g, v = V_g), τ): a constructor, not a second implementation. It is the Berlin case's behaviour before #171. As a body-force hook of 100 SSP-RK3 or forward-Euler steps of a rotating, sheared, turbulent box it reproduces the former add_geostrophic_wind! bit for bit (== on $u, v, w, θ, p$; test/large_scale_forcing.jl, which until #317 ran the same oracle inside 100 Berlin smoke steps and also compared every tracer; the tracer check is redundant, because the forcing writes only $u$ and $v$ and the state a tracer is transported by is compared bit for bit); the plane mean and the subtraction are computed in the same order as before.

Nudging is not a pressure gradient. It has no Coriolis partner, so it damps the inertial oscillation on the time scale $τ$ and pulls the nocturnal jet back toward $U_g$.

Large-scale subsidence

A large-scale vertical velocity $w_s(z, t)$ (negative downward) advects the resolved fields. Subsidence(w_s; fields = (:θ, :qv), form = :mean_gradient) adds (#248)

\[\left.\frac{\partial φ}{\partial t}\right|_s = -\,w_s(z, t)\, \frac{\langle φ\rangle_{k+1} - \langle φ\rangle_{k-1}}{z_{k+1} - z_{k-1}}\]

uniformly per level: the centred gradient of the plane mean, as DALES (Heus et al. 2010, Eq. 91) and MicroHH do. It moves the mean profile and leaves the fluctuations alone, and it is exact for a linear profile on any grid, stretched included. form = :local (Heinze et al. 2017, Eq. 3) uses each column's own gradient, first-order upwind with respect to $w_s$. On the bottom and top levels the gradient is one-sided toward the interior. Subsidence caps the growth of a convective boundary layer: it lowers the inversion at $w_s(z_i)$ while entrainment raises it.

The centred form needs a time scheme that is stable for centred advection. SSP-RK3 is ($|w_s|Δt/Δz ≤ \sqrt 3$); forward Euler is weakly unstable for it, so with Euler use :local or a small Courant number. qv is subsided by default only when the simulation carries moisture; a field named explicitly that the simulation lacks is an error. Unlike the advective tendencies, subsidence depends on the state and does not conserve $\int φ\,dV$. apply_forcing! returns the volume integrals it added, and a ledger books them: with SSP-RK3 the three stages are booked with the weights 1/6, 1/6, 2/3.

GABLS2

The GABLS2 case applies $w_s = -0.005\,z/1000$ m s⁻¹ below 1 km (−0.005 above) from 40 h local time (16 LT 23 October), from the CLUBB participant set-up (examples/sbl/data/gabls2_case.csv; since CFG-d #359 the [forcing.subsidence] table of examples/sbl/gabls2.toml). That config applies it to θ (--set forcing.subsidence={} for the sensitivity run without it), and the SBL driver books it in its θ ledger. In the reduced test (16 × 16 × 40, Δx = 150 m, Δz = 40 m), the residual-layer inversion (the largest $∂⟨θ⟩/∂z$ above 300 m) at 06 LT 24 October is 851.7 m with subsidence and 1038.7 m without (−187.0 m), 0.74 Δz above a passive surface descending from the 18 LT inversion (856.0 m, 1039.7 m and 0.80 Δz before the halo fix of the former sbl_surface! in CFG-d, #359; on the model, ONE-g #456, 0.88 Δz). The θ ledger closes to 8.1 × 10⁻¹³ of the column content.

Advective tendencies

AdvectiveTendencies(z, t, ∂θ/∂t, ∂q/∂t, ∂u/∂t, ∂v/∂t) adds the prescribed large-scale advection uniformly over each level, as the GABLS2 and DICE protocols specify it (Svensson et al. 2011). Each entry may be absent. The humidity tendency goes to the simulation's tqv, or to the field passed as tq. A humidity tendency with no field to receive it is an error, not a silent no-op. Subsidence depends on the model state and is Subsidence (below).

Test: a turbulent closed box, with periodic sides, impermeable walls and no surface flux, so flux-form advection and diffusion conserve $\int θ\,dV$. The $θ$ table varies with height and is piecewise linear in time, with a kink at 250 s that falls inside a step and an end at 600 s that is passed after 602 s. The change of $\int θ\,dV$ matches $\iint (∂θ/∂t)_{adv}\,dV\,dt$ (trapezoid rule on the table nodes) to a relative 9.9 × 10⁻¹³.

Surface-temperature series

SurfaceTemperatureSeries(t, T_s) is a prescribed skin temperature. GABLS1, for example, uses 265 K cooling at 0.25 K/h. It is read with surface_temperature(series, t), a method of the SurfaceExchange generic. It is not a surface model. It is the boundary value handed to a wall law (P57-a) or a surface step.

Radiosonde soundings

Sounding holds the launches of one station. read_sounding(path) reads one from the CSV that AeolusPrep.jl's aeolusprep fetch-sounding writes (its igra_sounding_csv), and sounding_profiles(s; z_top) converts it to θ, $q_v$, u, v and p ForcingTables on heights above the launch site and launch times in seconds from an epoch (sounding_time). These tables are the form that initial states, nudging targets and boundary data take (#247, epic #246).

  • Heights come from hypsometric integration of the virtual temperature, $z_{k+1} = z_k + (R_d/g_0)\,\bar T_v\,\ln(p_k/p_{k+1})$ (Wallace & Hobbs 2006, eq. 3.29). $T_v$ comes from MoistThermodynamics.virtual_temperature. The standard gravity $g_0$ defines the geopotential metre, so $z$ plus the station elevation can be compared directly with the reported geopotential height, which is used only as a check.
  • θ is $T/Π(p)$ with the dry exner_function.
  • $q_v$ is the specific humidity whose vapour pressure is $e_s(T_d)$ (specific_humidity_from_relative_humidity at RH = 1).
  • Interpolation. The default height grid is the union of every launch's levels, so each launch's piecewise-linear profile is held exactly. forcing_value is linear in time between launches.

Measured on the six Lindenberg launches of 29/30 June 2019: the computed heights of the standard levels below 3 km match IGRA's geopotential heights to RMS 0.8–4.3 m. The bound is 4.7–4.9 m, set by the whole-hPa surface pressure of the GTS message. θ equals $T(p_0/p)^{R_d/c_{pd}}$ to $10^{-12}$. The table returns each launch's θ, $q_v$, p, u and v exactly at its own levels.

The one entry point

apply_forcing!(sim, forcing, t, Δt) (also accepts a tuple of forcings) is called from the body_force! hook of step!, for example

step!(sim, dt; body_force! = (s, Δt) -> apply_forcing!(s, forcing, s.t, Δt))

It is duck-typed on mesh, u, v, tu, tv, tθ and f_coriolis (and qv, tqv for humidity), so it works with every dynamical core, the anelastic ones included. For the state-dependent forcings (ProfileNudging, Subsidence) it returns the tendency volume integrals; for the others, and for a tuple, it returns sim, and tendency_integrals(tuple, :θ) sums the integrals of the members.

Free-atmosphere top: the damping layer and the fixed-gradient top

A deep domain (the 3 km parent of the nesting epic, #246) ends in the free atmosphere, not at a lid. Two things are needed there (N-parent-b, #254): a layer that absorbs the gravity waves the boundary layer radiates upward, and a θ condition at the top face that keeps the stratification of the air above.

Damping toward a reference

ReferenceDamping(field, profile, target) adds

\[\left.\frac{\partial φ}{\partial t}\right|_{d} = -λ(z)\,\bigl(φ - φ_{ref}\bigr)\]

to θ, qv or a velocity component, with the rate $λ(z)$ of a DampingProfile (the sin² ramp of sponge_profile(α_max, z_start, z_top), Klemp & Lilly 1978, or the Nordström ramp of the fringe). It is an AbstractLargeScaleForcing, applied with apply_forcing! from the body-force hook, so the core's time integrator treats it like every other tendency. The cores' own sponge damps the velocity only; θ and qv are damped here and nowhere else.

The target is a number, a level vector (one value per interior level, e.g. the initial $\bar θ(z)$), a Field or 3-D array (the parent state a nest is damped toward, N-open-d), a ForcingTable or $(z, t)$ function (evaluated at the stage time), or :mean. With :mean only the deviations from the slab mean are damped and the mean profile evolves freely (DALES, Heus et al. 2010, Eqs. 40–41): every level's integral is zero to round-off. The arithmetic is the one kernel FiniteVolume._add_damping! that add_sponge! (whose f_ref takes the same targets) and the fringe region use, on a RegularGrid and a StretchedGrid alike; a constant level vector gives the number target's tendency bit for bit. After each call damping_tendency(F) returns the tendency per level and its volume integral, the term a heat ledger books.

Exact relaxation. A uniform θ anomaly with no flow, integrated by SSP-RK3 with the forcing in every stage, decays per level by exactly $R = 1 - a + a^2/2 - a^3/6$ ($a = λΔt$) per step: after 100 steps of 10 s the anomaly matches $A R^{100}$ to 1.8 × 10⁻¹² K at every level, and the levels below z_start keep their tendency at zero bit for bit.

Gravity-wave absorption (validation/deep_top.jl, test 3). A 2-D packet with $N = 0.01$ s⁻¹, $λ_x = λ_z = 1$ km ($ω = N/\sqrt2$, $c_{gz} = 0.56$ m s⁻¹, initialised from a C-grid stream function so its discrete divergence is zero) propagates upward into a sin² layer of $α_{max} = 10^{-3}$ s⁻¹ ($0.1N$). The reflected energy fraction is the time-integrated energy at $m > 0$ over that at $m < 0$ in the vertical spectrum of $w$ below the layer (3–5.5 km, Hann window):

layer$u, v, w$ damped$+ θ$ damped
none (rigid lid)0.985—
1 $λ_z$ deep0.17450.0338
2 $λ_z$ deep0.03300.0036

Velocity-only damping leaves the buoyancy half of the wave energy undamped, so at this weak rate the layer absorbs much less; damping θ as well (PALM, WRF §4.4.3, MicroHH) brings the reflection below 5 % from one vertical wavelength. The advantage is a weak-damping one: in a scan of $α_{max}$ (not asserted) the θ damping helps up to $α_{max} ≈ 2 × 10^{-3}$ s⁻¹ and, for a 1 $λ_z$ layer, reflects slightly more above it (0.0157 against 0.0049 at $α_{max} = 10^{-2}$), where the onset of the damping itself begins to reflect (Klemp & Lilly 1978); the noise floor of the measurement is about 0.003.

The fixed-gradient top

FluxBC(Γ) as the top face of a scalar's boundary conditions (FieldBoundaryConditions(:periodic, :periodic; bottom = :noflux, top = FluxBC(Γ))) holds $∂θ/∂z = Γ$ at the top, usually the initial lapse rate (PALM; DALES, Heus et al. 2010, Eq. 39). fill_halos! sets

\[θ_{N+h} = θ_{N+1-h} + Γ\,\bigl(z_{N+h} - z_{N+1-h}\bigr), \qquad h = 1, …, H_z,\]

with the cell-centre heights (on a StretchedGrid the first gap is the face spacing $Δz_f$ across the top face), so a linear profile of slope Γ is continued exactly into every halo layer and a diffusion with diffusivity $κ$ carries exactly $κΓ$ (per unit area) into the domain. The condition is affine in θ: its homogeneous part is FluxBC(0) (the mirror), which is what homogeneous_bcs returns, so the implicit diffusion of step_imex! moves $κΓ$ to the right-hand side as it does for a moving wall. qv and the SGS TKE share θ's boundary conditions but not θ's prescribed gradient (they are filled with homogeneous_bcs(θ_bcs)).

Measured (validation/deep_top.jl, test 4):

  • $Δ∫θ\,dz = κΓΔt$ per step to 4.4 × 10⁻¹¹ (relative) with step!, and to 1.4 × 10⁻¹² with ARK2 at 6.8 times the forward-Euler diffusion limit;
  • a linear profile of slope Γ (with the surface flux $-κΓ$ below) is steady to 0 K over 100 steps;
  • against the analytic solution of $θ_t = κθ_{zz}$, $θ_z(0) = 0$, $θ_z(L) = Γ$, $θ = Γ(z^2/2L + κt/L) + a\cos(πz/L)e^{-κπ^2t/L^2}$, the maximum error falls by 3.98 and 4.00 under $Δz$ halving on a RegularGrid (9.6 × 10⁻⁵ → 6.0 × 10⁻⁶ from 16 to 64 levels), and by 3.55 and 3.78 on a geometric StretchedGrid refined as a smooth map (ratio $1.1^{16/N_z}$), still approaching 4 from below.

Budget. With flow, buoyancy, a surface flux $F_s$, the gradient top and θ damping toward the initial profile, every step! changes $∫ρc_pθ\,dV$ by $ρc_pΔt\,(D + κΓA + F_sA)$ ($D$ the damping integral) to 5.4 × 10⁻¹² relative (test 5).

In the Berlin case

The 200 m child's θ top is cfg.θ_top = :initial_gradient (FluxBC(3 × 10⁻³ K/m), the initial lapse rate above the 100 m inversion) instead of the closed lid; the dynamics books the heat it carries as (:dynamics, :top_flux) in the air-heat ledger. θ is not damped in the child: its sponge starts at 150 m, so a constant-target damping would clamp the top 50 m (#238, approach B), and in the nested run the child top comes from the parent. The θ damping layer runs in the parent (N-parent-e). See The Berlin case.

Users

  • Berlin case: the :geostrophic coupling, with mode = :pressure_gradient by default and mode = :nudging as an option (a ProfileNudging since #248). See The Berlin case. θ/qv nudging and subsidence belong to the deep parent run of the nesting epic (#246, N-parent-e), not to the nested Berlin child: the child takes its top and lateral θ and q from the parent, and nudging it as well would apply the large scale twice.
  • GABLS2 (examples/sbl/gabls2.toml): Subsidence from 40 h.

The Berlin case and the sponge

The cores' Rayleigh sponge (top 25 % of the domain, $α_{max} = 0.05$ s⁻¹) relaxes $u, v$ toward zero. Nudging hid this, because it pulls the mean back toward $U_g$ at every level. A pressure gradient does not. The sponge is then a momentum sink of $α⟨u⟩$, and the forcing $fU_g = 10^{-4}$ m s⁻² cannot balance it. In the 24 h run with the sponge still targeting zero, the domain-mean wind collapsed to 0.12 m/s, and the top level fell to 0.003 m/s.

So in the pressure-gradient mode the coupling retargets the sponge at $(U_g, V_g)$. This is option sponge_to_geostrophic, and it is on by default. The coupling adds $+α(z)U_g$ using the core's own sponge profile (add_sponge! on a zero field with f_ref = U_g). The sum is then $-α(z)(u - U_g)$, the usual sponge of a pressure-gradient-forced LES. The nudging mode is left exactly as it was.

24 h flagship run (alexanderplatz, 06–06 h local, $U_g = 1$ m/s, 32 levels of 6.25 m, sponge above 150 m). The values are domain means, building cells included:

With the pressure gradient, the mean wind crosses the isobars toward low pressure ($v̄ > 0$), as the Ekman balance requires. Under nudging there is no pressure gradient to oppose Coriolis, so the mean wind turns the wrong way ($v̄ < 0$).

No low-level jet forms in this domain. In every mode the night profile increases monotonically up to the sponge. The domain is 200 m deep, its top 50 m are sponge, and the city canopy keeps the whole column coupled to the surface. That leaves no decoupled residual layer in which an inertial oscillation could run. To resolve the jet, the domain would need to be deeper than the nocturnal boundary layer.
