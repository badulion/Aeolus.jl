Nested boundary conditions: one-way parent → child data

A nested child is a limited-area LES whose lateral faces and top are not walls and not periodic: the flow enters and leaves through them, and what enters is what the parent says. This page describes how the parent's solution becomes the child's boundary condition (N-nest-d, #261), with the pieces it is built from:

piecewherepage
open faces, the boundary-data contract, the mass-flux correctionOpenBoundary, AbstractBoundaryData, correct_boundary_mass_flux! (N-open-b, #255)fractional step
the projection with Neumann facesCosineFFTPoissonSolver from axis_poisson_bc (N-open-a, #251)Poisson
parent → child prolongation of the field setinterpolate_parent_state! (N-nest-a, #250)nesting
time blend over the parent step, subcyclingNestedRun, boundary_time (N-nest-c, #257)nested time coupling
the parent as boundary dataNestBoundaryData, nest_bcs (this page)

Nothing here is new physics or new numerics: NestBoundaryData is the glue that feeds the existing prolongation and time blend into the existing open-boundary buffers, and it is the Aeolus-parent implementer of the same contract a mesoscale parent (#58) and reanalysis driving (#57) will implement.

What the child's faces carry

The child is built with nest_bcs(child_mesh, x, y; bottom, top, moisture, scalars, outflow, halo). Every :nest face is an OpenBoundary (outflow = :convective or :orlanski makes its outflow points radiate instead of taking the parent's value, halo is checked against the advection scheme's stencil, #329); the Berlin child has open sides and top and a wall at the ground, a child inside its parent (not touching the ground) has an open bottom too. On an open face (PALM's Dirichlet child boundary, Hellsten et al. 2021 §3.2):

  • the normal velocity is prescribed on the boundary face and on the $H$ halo layers beyond it. On a west, south or bottom face the boundary face is the child's interior face index $H+1$; on an east, north or top face it is the first halo index $H+N+1$;
  • the tangential velocities and every scalar of the field set (θ, qv, extra tracers) get Dirichlet halos: the parent's value at the halo cell;
  • the pressure correction gets a Neumann (mirror) halo, $∂φ/∂n = 0$, so the projection $u^{n+1}·n = u^*·n − Δt\,∂φ/∂n$ leaves the prescribed normal velocity unchanged (Kim & Moin 1985). The child's default solver is therefore the all-Neumann CosineFFTPoissonSolver. Before N-nest-d the child kept a periodic FFT projection that never saw the parent's flux, and a Taylor–Green child needed the normal velocity re-imposed after every stage plus a sponge to stay stable; it now runs 1000 adaptive parent steps without either.

The boundary values

At time $t$ of the current parent step $[t_p, t_p+Δt_p]$ every buffer receives

\[B(t) = (1-τ)\,\mathcal P φ_p(t_p) + τ\,\mathcal P φ_p(t_p+Δt_p), \qquad τ = \frac{t - t_p}{Δt_p},\]

at the buffer's own storage points, for every member $φ$ of the field set. $\mathcal P$ is the conservative staggered prolongation of the nesting page: nodal (Lagrange) along a velocity component's own direction, conservative cell-average reconstruction across it and for scalars. Each dynamical core asks for the data itself, once per projected stage, at that stage's end time (update_open_boundaries!): $t_0+Δt$ for step!, $t_0+γΔt$ and $t_0+Δt$ for ARK2, and for SSP-RK3 $t_0+Δt,\ t_0+2Δt,\ t_0+\tfrac32Δt$ with the buffers combined with the fields' Shu–Osher weights, which places $B$ at the stage times $(0, 1, \tfrac12)$ exactly because $B$ is linear in $t$ (nested time coupling). The run refreshes the buffers once more at the start of each parent step, where a two-way parent may have changed.

Mass

With every face's normal velocity prescribed, the Neumann problem $∇^2φ = ∇·u^*$ is solvable only if the net boundary flux vanishes; otherwise the solver leaves a uniform divergence $Q/V$. The single global correction of N-open-b subtracts $δ = Q/\sum A$ from the outward normal velocity of every open fluid face (Hellsten et al. 2021, §3.3, Eq. 2). It is not reimplemented for nesting.

For a nest the correction has almost nothing to do. The child's faces lie on parent faces. Along the face normal the prolongation is nodal, so it reproduces the parent's face value at a coincident face; across the face it is conservative, so the $r_1 r_2$ child faces covering a parent face carry exactly the parent's flux. Summed over the closed boundary this is the parent's $\int ∇·u\,dV$ over the covered box, zero for a projected parent, and the time blend of two zero-flux states has zero flux. Measured for a random divergence-free parent: $|Q|/(UA) \le 5.7\times10^{-17}$ before the correction, $|δ|/U \le 1.4\times10^{-17}$. Hellsten et al. (2021) report $δ$ three to four orders of magnitude below $U$ for PALM's (non-conservative) interpolation; here it is round-off, and $δ$ does real work only where the child's geometry differs from the parent's.

Faces inside solids

NestedRun(...; solid = mask) (or apply_nest_boundary!(...; solid)) passes the child's solid mask, and the run marks the open faces with the one open-boundary path, mark_open_boundary_solids! (N-open-d #262). A boundary point is solid when an interior cell it borders is solid (the cell next to the face for the normal velocity and the scalars, either of the two cells for a staggered tangential component). Every velocity buffer — the normal velocity on the face and the layers beyond it, and the tangential velocities — is zero there, and the point is flagged non-fluid in its OpenPlane, so the mass-flux correction neither counts nor adjusts it. (Until #327 the nest had a second implementation, NestBoundaryData.solid, that zeroed only the normal velocity; it is retired, and nest_boundary_data no longer takes a solid keyword.) A building cut by the east face of a test child (16 solid faces of 128, 40 tangential solid points) keeps $u·n = 0.0$ and zero tangential velocity exactly, and $δ = Q / A_{\rm fluid}$ holds bit for bit. The parent does not see the building, so the solid faces create a genuine imbalance ($Q = -2.97\times10^4$ m³/s, $δ = -0.063$ m/s, 2 % of $U$) that the correction spreads over the fluid faces.

Sponge

A NestSponge (relaxation zone, Davies 1976) is optional and off by default: Kadasch et al. (2021, §2.2–2.3) and Hellsten et al. (2021) use none at the child boundary. When given, it relaxes every member of the field set toward the parent blended to the end of the child substep, on the open faces only (nest_open_sides). The velocities are relaxed on the predicted velocity of every stage, in the core's post_predictor! hook, before the pressure solve; the scalars after the substep (#329). The prolongated parent velocity is flux-consistent on parent cells, not divergence-free on child cells, so the former relaxation after the step left the child divergent: $\max\lvert∇·u\rvert = 1.6$ s⁻¹ for a random parent (width 3, $α_{\max} = 20$ s⁻¹), against $2.8\times10^{-14}$ s⁻¹ now, with step! and step_rk3!. Its effect on an outgoing anomaly is in the next section.

Storage and cost of the boundary data

Without a sponge the run reads the prolongated parent only at the child's open-boundary points, so its three field sets (the parent at both ends of the parent step and their blend) are halo-only (storage = :shell, #329): six dense halo slabs per member plus the boundary-normal face plane of the velocities, $(N+2H)^3 - N^3$ instead of $(N+2H)^3$ points. For a $32^3$ child with $H = 3$ the three sets of u, v, w, θ hold 2144 KiB instead of 5144 KiB (42 %); for $64^3$ a shell is 24 % of the full array. The results are bit-identical. The prolongation itself loops over the six halo slabs (one kernel, _interp_box!), not over the whole child array.

Three findings:

  • The prolongation order must match the transport scheme. With the core's 2nd-order centred θ the default 3rd-order prolongation is ample. With WENO-Z5 the parent's own error is $O(Δx^5)$ and the 3rd-order interface error dominates: the child was 3.5× worse than the parent over the overlap; with order = 5 it is better (0.93).
  • Centred advection and a Dirichlet outflow. The 2nd-order centred scheme has an undamped $2Δx$ mode with group velocity $-U$. The Dirichlet outflow face excites it where the child's solution and the parent's data differ, and the Dirichlet inflow face reflects it: 3–4 % of the amplitude stays trapped in the child after the anomaly has left, above the 1 % the issue asks for (kept as a @test_broken). The sponge removes it (0.08–0.2 %), and an upwind-biased scheme (WENO-Z5, the scheme of the Berlin tracers) never creates it (0.007 %). A radiating outflow face removes it too: nest_bcs(…; outflow = :convective) (#329) lets a θ pulse that only the child carries leave with a residual $\int θ'^2 / \int θ'^2_0 = 1.2\times10^{-7}$ after two crossings, where the Dirichlet parent face keeps all of it (1.00).
  • Refinement 1 is exact for the projection step. The predictor sees the same halo values as the parent, and the Neumann solve with the parent's (end-of-step) face velocities reproduces the parent's interior pressure gradient, so the child stays on the parent to round-off. With SSP-RK3 the intermediate stages see the blended parent instead of the parent's own stage values, an $O(Δt^2)$ difference per step.

Limits

  • No Berlin coupling on master yet: the Berlin multi-domain case is N-berlin-nest (#265, PR #330).
  • Since #328 the boundary data of a NestHierarchy flow down level by level through the same contract (a grandchild's data come from the child's state at the child's substep ends), and a stretched child's open faces use the per-level face areas of its mesh in the mass-flux correction; see nest hierarchies, non-aligned and stretched children. A one-way child with Dirichlet outflow faces keeps a reflected residual after a sharp feature has left it (measured there); a radiation outflow (outflow = :convective) does not.

Resolved since N-nest-d:

  • Outflow and halo (#329). nest_bcs passes outflow (:dirichlet, the default, :convective, :orlanski or per side) and the advection halo to open_boundary_conditions (N-open-c, #260). A vertically periodic open box used to skip the initialisation of its radiating faces, which silently stayed Dirichlet; fixed with it.
  • Two-way feedback is N-nest-e (#263): per-face flux correction and refluxing on the open faces only, Clark–Farley anterpolation with a buffer zone, re-projection of the parent; see the two-way nesting page.
  • Prolongation limiter (#329). The 3rd- and 5th-order prolongation overshoots at a sharp front ($[-0.25, 1.25]$ at order 3, $[-0.30, 1.30]$ at order 5 for a 0/1 tracer). A conservative limiter scales the children of each parent cell about the parent value: :positive (Zhang & Shu 2010; the default for qv and the tracers of the field set) keeps them non-negative, :monotone (Barth & Jespersen
    1. inside the parent neighbourhood's range; the children's mean stays
    the parent value. θ and the velocities are not limited by default.
  • One windowed boundary-data type (#329). NestBoundaryData is the generic two-snapshot WindowedBoundaryData with the prolongated field set as the snapshot; a mesoscale parent (#58) implements snapshot_values and reuses the time blend.
  • Externally driven children refresh their halos with the public fill_nest_halos! (#329).
