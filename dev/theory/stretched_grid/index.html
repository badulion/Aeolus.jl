Stretched vertical grid

BoussinesqSimulation runs on a StretchedGrid as well as on a uniform RegularGrid (#151). The grid is uniform in x and y and has an arbitrary face distribution z_f[k] in z. Its first user is the parent domain of the Berlin nesting epic (#246). The parent is 6 × 6 × 3 km, with Δz = 25 m up to 500 m so that the ~400 m deep child lies in its uniform part, and stretched above that.

Metrics

All vertical metrics come from the grid, through cell_thickness and center_spacing:

quantitysymbolwhere it is used
cell thickness z_f[k+1] − z_f[k]$Δz_c[k]$height of a u, v, θ, p control volume; the face → centre difference (divergence)
centre spacing z_c[k] − z_c[k−1]$Δz_f[k]$height of a w control volume; the centre → face gradient

Operators

Every operator of the core is the finite-volume form on these control volumes. On a :uniform StretchedGrid each one reduces algebraically to its RegularGrid stencil. The test suite checks that the two meshes give the same 20-step run to 1e-12.

  • Projection. The pressure equation is $DGφ = D\mathbf{u}^*$, with the grid's own divergence $D$ (metric $Δz_c$) and gradient $G$ (metric $Δz_f$):

    \[\frac{δ_x^2φ}{Δx^2} + \frac{δ_y^2φ}{Δy^2} + \frac{1}{Δz_c[k]}\left[\frac{φ_{k+1}-φ_k}{Δz_f[k+1]} - \frac{φ_k-φ_{k-1}}{Δz_f[k]}\right] = (D\mathbf u^*)_k .\]

    Because x and y are uniform, the operator is diagonalised horizontally by the real FFT (periodic sides) or the DCT-II (Neumann sides), using the exact eigenvalues of the 3-point stencil. What is left is one tridiagonal system per wavenumber, solved by the Thomas algorithm (Schumann & Sweet 1988, J. Comput. Phys. 75, 123). This is StretchedPoissonSolver, the default solver on a stretched grid. It is direct, so the projected velocity is solenoidal to round-off: max|∇·u| = 4e-17 s⁻¹ on the 32 × 32 × 53 parent-like grid.

    The same operator is available to the multigrid solvers: MultigridPoissonSolver(g::StretchedGrid) and MGPreconditionedCG(g::StretchedGrid) multiply row k by $s_k = Δz_c[k]/Δ$, which gives the symmetric variable-coefficient form coeff_x = coeff_y = s_k, coeff_z = Δ/Δz_f[k], rhs_scale = s_k on a uniform grid of spacing Δ. The two solvers agree to 1e-8 relative. Multigrid is not the default, because the hierarchy coarsens only when all three dimensions halve, and 53 levels do not halve. Measured on the parent levels with a tolerance of 1e-12: the V-cycle stalls at a relative residual of 1.9e-6 after 500 cycles (32 × 32 × 53), and MG-CG needs 64 iterations and 5.5 s at 64 × 64 × 53, against 1.2 ms for the direct solve.

  • Momentum advection. This is the divergence form on the staggered control volumes. The transported velocity is the arithmetic mean at the control-volume face. The transporting velocity is the volume flux through that face. For a w volume the side faces span half of the u cell below and half of the one above, so the transporting u there is $(Δz_c[k-1]u_{k-1} + Δz_c[k]u_k)/(Δz_c[k-1]+Δz_c[k])$. With these fluxes, the volume-flux divergence of every momentum control volume is a weighted mean of the divergences of the pressure cells it overlaps. The operator is therefore skew-symmetric in the volume-weighted inner product, and conserves kinetic energy for a discretely solenoidal field (Morinishi et al. 1998, J. Comput. Phys. 143, 90; Vasilyev 2000, J. Comput. Phys. 157, 746). Measured: $|dE/dt|/E < 10^{-12}$ on the parent grid.

  • Scalar advection and diffusion are in flux form, with flux differences divided by $Δz_c$ and gradients taken over $Δz_f$. So $Σϕ\,ΔV$ changes only through the boundary fluxes, and $Σϕ^2ΔV$ is invariant under a solenoidal flow.

  • Molecular viscosity on w uses the w control volume: height $Δz_f[k]$, with neighbours $Δz_c[k-1]$ and $Δz_c[k]$ away. Applying the centre metrics to w instead is a first-order error. The manufactured-solution test caught it. The same fix applies to the terrain-following Laplacian of a z-face field.

  • Smagorinsky. $ν_t = (C_sΔ_k)^2|S|$ with the local Deardorff (1970) width $Δ_k = (ΔxΔyΔz_c[k])^{1/3}$. The strain uses the one strain stencil (Operators._strain_at): $∂w/∂z$ across the cell, and $∂u/∂z$, $∂v/∂z$ across the two neighbouring centres. On the parent grid, the 100 m cells get $(100/25)^{2/3} = 2.52$ times the $ν_t$ of the 25 m cells for the same shear.

  • Buoyancy interpolates $θ$ (and $q_v$) to the faces with thickness weights, so a horizontally uniform stratification at rest stays at rest (|w| < 1e-12 after 20 steps).

  • Time step. The vertical CFL number of a cell is $|w|/Δz_c[k]$, so the thin near-surface cells set the limit, not the mean spacing. The diffusive and stress-form limits use the thinnest cell.

Accuracy

Two manufactured solutions are used. In both, the geometric map holds $r^N$ fixed, so the grid refines smoothly, and the stretching ratio is 1.08 on the coarsest grid.

  • Poisson. $φ = \cos(2πx/L)\cos(πz/H)$ converges at orders 1.93 and 1.97 with periodic sides. $φ = \cos(πx/L)\cos(πz/H)$ converges at 1.90 and 1.95 with Neumann sides.
  • Navier–Stokes. A forced two-mode cellular flow between free-slip walls is run through the full projected RK3 step to $t = 0.1$. It converges at orders 2.01 and 1.99 (N = 16, 32, 64).

Cost

One step! on one thread, with Smagorinsky, buoyancy and no-slip bottom:

grids/stepPoisson solve
192 × 192 × 53, stretched (parent)0.225 s0.066 s
192 × 192 × 120, uniform 25 m0.526 s0.197 s

Not covered

The following paths stay RegularGrid-only:

  • the SGS stress form (sgs_stress = :symmetric) and implicit diffusion (step_imex!), which raise an ArgumentError that names the mesh;
  • the dynamic Smagorinsky closures, whose constructors take a RegularGrid.

The prognostic TKE closure of PrognosticSGS runs on a stretched grid since #259 (the SGS closure of the Berlin nesting parent; see the SGS TKE theory page), and with it the positive flux-limited scalar advection advect_positive! with the centred reconstruction. The other Era VI closures, which have their own stencils, have not been tested on a stretched grid. Non-periodic lateral velocity boundaries are not solenoidal on either mesh; that is the open-boundary work of #251.
