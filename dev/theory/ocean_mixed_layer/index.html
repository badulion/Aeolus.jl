Water surfaces: mixed layer, cool skin and warm layer

A lake or a coastal sea is the one Era V surface whose temperature a model cannot simply prescribe: the heat it exchanges with the air is stored in, and redistributed through, metres to tens of metres of water. src/OceanMixedLayer.jl makes that temperature prognostic with three nested pieces, each at the scale on which it acts:

scaleprocesscomponent
1–100 mmixed layer and thermoclineWaterColumn, KrausTurnerMixing
0.1–10 mdiurnal warm layerFairallWarmLayer, ZengBeljaarsWarmLayer
≲ 1 mmconductive cool skincool_skin

The skin temperature seen by the atmosphere is

\[T_s = T_1 + \Delta T_{warm}(\Delta z_1/2) - \Delta T_{cool},\]

the top layer of the column, plus the warm layer's warming between the surface and the top layer's centre, minus the cool skin. Air–sea fluxes are not computed here: they come from the Era V contract (SurfaceExchange.turbulent_fluxes) or, through the flux keyword of step_surface!, from a dedicated air–sea algorithm (AirSeaFlux, #39). Whatever the flux function returns is exactly what the column absorbs.

1. The column

Layers of thickness $\Delta z_k$ carry temperatures $T_k$ at a constant salinity $S$. One step:

  1. Heating. The non-solar flux $Q_{ns} = G - SW_{net}$ enters the top layer; the net shortwave is absorbed layer by layer from a sum-of-exponentials profile $I(z)/I_0 = \sum_i a_i e^{-z/\zeta_i}$; what reaches the bottom is absorbed by a lake bed or leaves through deep water.
  2. Convective adjustment. Any group of layers with denser water above lighter is mixed. Density comes from the equation of state, so the adjustment knows about the 3.98 °C maximum of fresh water.
  3. Entrainment. A bulk (Kraus–Turner / Niiler–Kraus) energy budget deepens the mixed layer from the surface.
  4. Skin corrections — warm layer and cool skin, which hold no heat of their own.

Heat is conserved exactly: over a step the column's heat content changes by $(G - SW_{bottom} - F_{bottom})\,\Delta t$, to round-off (measured: ≤ 2.3 × 10⁻¹⁴ of the stored heat over three coupled days).

Equation of state

The one-atmosphere International Equation of State of Seawater (EOS-80),

\[\rho(t,S) = \rho_w(t) + A(t)\,S + B(t)\,S^{3/2} + C\,S^2 ,\]

covers fresh and sea water alike; $\alpha = -\rho^{-1}\partial\rho/\partial T$ and $\beta = \rho^{-1}\partial\rho/\partial S$ are its analytic derivatives. It reproduces the published check values to 5 × 10⁻⁶ kg m⁻³ and puts the fresh-water maximum at 3.982 °C. For lakes this is the whole story of autumn overturn: surface cooling drives convection until the lake reaches 3.98 °C throughout, after which colder water is lighter and stays at the top — the inverse stratification under which lakes freeze from the surface.

Shortwave penetration

Paulson & Simpson (1977) fitted two exponentials to Jerlov's water types,

The warm-layer schemes use Soloviev's three-band profile with fractions (0.28, 0.27, 0.45): e-folding depths (0.014, 0.357, 12.82) m in COARE and (0.014, 0.357, 16.7) m in Zeng & Beljaars (2005). About 60 % of the net shortwave is absorbed in the top 3 m — which is why a calm sunny day can warm the surface by a kelvin while the water a few metres down barely moves.

Mixed-layer energetics

Mixing a stably stratified column raises its potential energy. The bulk model supplies, per step,

\[E = \rho_0\, m\, u_{*w}^3\, \Delta t\; e^{-h/h_d} \;+\; n\,\Delta PE_{conv},\]

the wind work ($u_{*w} = \sqrt{\tau/\rho_0}$) plus a fraction $n$ of the potential energy released by convective overturning, and the mixed layer deepens until the potential-energy cost of mixing equals $E$. The base layer is entrained partially (a fraction found by bisection), so the depth is not quantised to the grid.

Two analytic limits check the energetics:

  • Kato–Phillips (wind into a constant $N^2$, no heat flux): mixing to depth $h$ costs $\rho_0 N^2 h^3/12$, so $h = (12\,m\,u_{*w}^3 t / N^2)^{1/3}$. Measured after a day: −0.57 % at $\Delta z$ = 0.2 m and −0.017 % at 0.1 m.
  • Free convection (surface buoyancy loss $B_0$ into $N^2$, no wind): the budget gives $h^2 = 2(1 + 2n)\,B_0 t/N^2$ — encroachment for $n = 0$, and the classical entrainment-flux ratio 0.2 for $n = 0.2$. Measured after two days: −0.04 % and +0.14 %.

The defaults $m = 0.5$, $n = 0.2$ lie in the range of the bulk-model literature (Niiler & Kraus 1977) and are not tuned. Not represented: shear instability at the mixed-layer base (currents are not carried), background diffusion below the mixed layer, and ice (below freezing the column hands over to IceThermodynamics, #37).

2. The cool skin

The top millimetre of water loses heat by conduction only, so it is cooler than the water beneath by (Saunders 1967; Fairall et al. 1996a)

\[\Delta T_{cool} = \frac{\delta}{k_w}\,\big(Q_{out} - f_s(\delta)\,SW_{net}\big), \qquad \delta = \frac{\lambda\,\nu_w}{u_{*w}},\quad \lambda = 6\Big[1 + \Big(\frac{16\,g\,c_w\rho_w\nu_w^3 B}{k_w^2 u_{*w}^4}\Big)^{3/4}\Big]^{-1/3},\]

with $Q_{out}$ the non-solar loss (net longwave + sensible + latent), $f_s(\delta) = 0.065 + 11\delta - (6.6\times10^{-5}/\delta)(1 - e^{-\delta/8\times10^{-4}})$ the shortwave absorbed within the skin, and the buoyancy flux $B = \alpha q_c + \beta S\, c_w LE/L_v$ including the salt left behind by evaporation. It is typically 0.2–0.4 K. On the COARE 3.6 ship test data the implementation reproduces the reference code to 9 × 10⁻⁵ K.

3. The diurnal warm layer

Under weak wind the absorbed sunshine builds a stable layer a few metres deep; the skin can be a kelvin or more warmer than the water at 5 m by early afternoon. Two schemes are provided.

Fairall et al. (1996b), as in COARE. From the first moment in a day the absorbed heat exceeds 50 W m⁻², the heat $Q_{ac} = \int q\,dt$ and momentum $I_{ac} = \int \tau\,dt$ put into the layer are accumulated. A layer with linear temperature and current profiles at critical Richardson number 0.65 then has

\[D = I_{ac}\sqrt{\frac{2 Ri_c c_w}{\alpha g \rho_w Q_{ac}}},\qquad \Delta T = \frac{2 Q_{ac}}{\rho_w c_w D}.\]

It needs a daily reset (at local midnight). On the COARE 3.6 test data it reproduces the reference code to 4 × 10⁻⁷ K (peak warming 0.436 K).

Zeng & Beljaars (2005), as in the IFS: a prognostic equation for $\Delta T = T_{-\delta} - T_{-d}$ over a fixed $d = 3$ m with the profile $\Delta T[1 - (z/d)^\nu]$, $\nu = 0.3$,

\[\frac{\partial \Delta T}{\partial t} = \frac{Q + R_s - R(-d)}{d\rho_w c_w \nu/(\nu+1)} - \frac{(\nu+1)\kappa u_{*w}}{d\,\phi_t(d/L)}\,\Delta T ,\]

with the log-linear water-side $\phi_t$. Its neutral steady state is $\Delta T = (Q + R_s - R(-d))/(\rho_w c_w \nu\kappa u_{*w})$. Being prognostic it needs no reset and lets a residual layer survive a calm night.

Which to use. Only when the column's top layer is thicker than the warm layer (a slab of several metres). A column with top layers of ≲ 0.5 m resolves the warm layer itself — on a clear day it gives a 1.25 K diurnal skin range at 1.5 m s⁻¹ wind and 0.32 K at 10 m s⁻¹ — and adding a sub-grid warm layer would count it twice.

Against observations. On the COARE ship data (days 19–25, trade winds 4–11 m s⁻¹) the observed difference between a sea snake at 5 cm and the thermosalinograph at 5.3 m never exceeds 0.14 K. Both schemes put the largest warming on the calmest day, as observed, but both overestimate it (Fairall 0.33 K, Zeng–Beljaars 0.85 K on that day; day-time RMS differences 0.09 K and 0.25 K). Takaya et al. (2010) refined the Zeng–Beljaars scheme for this regime (Langmuir mixing, a modified stable $\phi_t$); those refinements are not implemented.
