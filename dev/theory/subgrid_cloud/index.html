Sub-grid cloud and supersaturation PDF

Phase 45 — module TurbulentMicrophysics.

An LES cell of tens of metres is not uniformly saturated. Unresolved fluctuations of total water $q_t$ and liquid-ice potential temperature $\theta_{li}$ spread it over a distribution of saturation states, so the cell can be partly cloudy. The all-or-nothing saturation adjustment of the cell mean (Phase 39, saturation_adjustment) cannot represent this: it clouds the whole cell or none of it. Because the condensate $\max(s, 0)$ is convex in the saturation state, Jensen's inequality also says the mean-state adjustment underestimates the condensate of a variable cell.

The saturation deficit

Following Sommeria & Deardorff (1977) and Mellor (1977), define the liquid-water temperature $T_l$ — the temperature with all condensate evaporated — and the extended condensate

\[s = a\,\bigl(q_t - q_s(T_l)\bigr), \qquad a = \frac{1}{1 + \Gamma\,\partial q_s/\partial T},\]

where $\Gamma = \partial T/\partial q_c$ at fixed $\theta_{li}$, $p$ and $q_t$. $s > 0$ is the condensate a sub-volume holds after adjustment; $s < 0$ is its (scaled) saturation deficit.

$\Gamma$ is usually written $L/c_p$. Here it is differentiated from the contract's own definition $\theta_{li} = (T/\Pi)\exp[-(L_v q_l + L_s q_i)/(c_{pm} T)]$, which adds the change of the moist Exner exponent $R_m/c_{pm}$ as vapour becomes condensate:

\[\Gamma = \frac{L}{c_{pm}} + T_l \ln\frac{p}{p_0}\, \frac{\partial (R_m/c_{pm})}{\partial q_c}.\]

At 290 K and 900 hPa this is 1.4 % larger than $L/c_{pm}$. The term scales with $\ln(p/p_0)$: it vanishes at 1000 hPa and reaches 4.5 % at 700 hPa and 8.8 % at 500 hPa (280 K), so it matters most for mid-level cloud.

Compare the slope $\partial q_c/\partial q_t$ at the saturation boundary (290 K, 900 hPa) with the exact adjustment's 0.322085. The textbook $a$ with bare $L/c_{pm}$ gives 0.32569. Using $\Gamma$ gives $a$ = 0.32266. Adding the $q_t$-dependence of $T_l$ in $a_q$ (below) gives 0.322084, a relative difference of 8 × 10⁻⁷.

A fluctuation $(q_t', \theta_{li}')$ gives $s' = a_q q_t' - b\,\theta_{li}'$ with $b = a\,(\partial q_s/\partial T)\,\Pi$ and $a_q = a\,(1 - \partial q_s/\partial T\;\partial T_l/\partial q_t)$, so

\[\sigma_s^2 = a_q^2\,\overline{q_t'^2} - 2 a_q b\,\overline{q_t'\theta_{li}'} + b^2\,\overline{\theta_{li}'^2}.\]

Only the fluctuations are linearised

The cell mean $\bar s$ is anchored on the exact, nonlinear adjustment: where saturation_adjustment condenses water, $\bar s$ is that condensate; elsewhere $\bar s = a(q_t - q_s(T_l)) < 0$. Consequently the $\sigma_s \to 0$ limit of the scheme is the Phase 39 adjustment bit-for-bit (not a linearisation of it), which the tests check over 15 warm, mixed-phase and cold states.

Why this anchors on the θ_li diagnosis, not the enthalpy adjustment

The contract has two saturation adjustments (issue #82). saturation_adjustment diagnoses $T$ and the partition from a prognostic $\theta_{li}$ by inverting its definition exactly. isobaric_saturation_adjustment performs condensation as a process during a step and conserves moist enthalpy. The sub-grid cloud scheme is a diagnosis. It reads the cell means $(\theta_{li}, q_t)$ and their variances, returns cloud fraction, condensate and $T$, and leaves $\theta_{li}$ and $q_t$ exactly as they were. No heat is exchanged. So its $\sigma_s \to 0$ limit has to be the $\theta_{li}$ inversion, and $\Gamma$ is taken at fixed $\theta_{li}$ for the same reason. Anchoring on the enthalpy adjustment instead would make the diagnosed $T$ disagree with the $\theta_{li}$ the model carries, by 1.2 % of the latent heating at 900 hPa and 6.4 % at 600 hPa, so reading the state would change it. A model that prognoses $T$ or $h$ instead of $\theta_{li}$ would need an enthalpy-anchored variant of this scheme. That variant does not exist yet.

Cloud fraction and condensate

With $Q = \bar s/\sigma_s$ and a Gaussian PDF,

\[C = \tfrac12\bigl[1 + \operatorname{erf}(Q/\sqrt2)\bigr], \qquad \bar q_c = \sigma_s\Bigl[Q\,C + \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-Q^2/2}\Bigr].\]

Cumulus fields are positively skewed in $s$ (Bechtold et al. 1995), so a binormal family in the form of Larson, Golaz & Cotton (2002) is also provided, with both components sharing $\sigma_c = \sigma_s\sqrt{1-\gamma}$ and the width closed by $\gamma^{3/2} = |Sk|/(|Sk| + c)$ (a choice made here so that it reduces exactly to the Gaussian at zero skewness).

Condensate is split into liquid and ice by liquid_fraction at the resulting mean temperature, which is recovered from $\theta_{li}$ — so $q_t$ and $\theta_{li}$, the conserved variables, are untouched.

Variance from the SGS closure

The LES closes SGS fluxes with the Smagorinsky-Lilly eddy viscosity $\nu_t = (C_s\Delta)^2|S|$ and a scalar diffusivity $\nu_t/\mathrm{Pr}_t$ (see Smagorinsky-Lilly SGS). The SGS scalar-variance budget in local equilibrium (Deardorff 1980; Bechtold et al. 1995; Chaboureau & Bechtold 2002) balances gradient production against dissipation over the SGS turnover time $\Delta/\sqrt e$:

\[2\,\frac{\nu_t}{\mathrm{Pr}_t}\,|\nabla\bar\phi|^2 = C_\chi\,\frac{\sqrt e}{\Delta}\,\overline{\phi'^2}.\]

Writing $\nu_t = C_k\Delta\sqrt e$, $\nu_t$ cancels:

\[\overline{\phi'^2} = c_\mathrm{var}\,\Delta^2\,|\nabla\bar\phi|^2, \qquad c_\mathrm{var} = \frac{2C_k}{C_\chi\,\mathrm{Pr}_t},\]

and likewise $\overline{\phi'\psi'} = c_\mathrm{var}\Delta^2\,\nabla\bar\phi\cdot\nabla\bar\psi$. In equilibrium the variance therefore does not depend on $C_s$; $\nu_t$ only decides whether SGS turbulence is present ($\nu_t = 0$ ⇒ zero variance ⇒ all-or-nothing adjustment). The default $c_\mathrm{var} = 1/12$ is the variance of the resolved linear profile across a cell of width $\Delta$; equilibrium_variance_coefficient gives the turbulent value for explicit $(C_k, C_\chi, \mathrm{Pr}_t)$.

A known limitation of any gradient closure: a well-mixed layer has no resolved gradient and hence no sub-grid variance, so cloud base in a mixed layer stays all-or-nothing; the partial cloud appears at the inversion, where the gradients are.
