Staggered discretization: the Arakawa C-grid

Aeolus discretizes the Boussinesq equations on a staggered Arakawa-C grid. Pressure and scalars live at cell centers; each velocity component lives at the corresponding face:

        ┌───── FaceX(u) ─────┬───── FaceX(u) ─────┐
        │                     │                     │
        │                     │                     │
   FaceY(v)   CellCenter    FaceY(v)   CellCenter   FaceY(v)
        │      p, θ           │      p, θ           │
        │                     │                     │
        └─── FaceX(u) ──────┴──── FaceX(u) ──────┘

  (2D slice; the 3rd axis has w on FaceZ)

The reason this is the right choice for incompressible LES is deep, worth spelling out.

Why staggered?

Consider a collocated layout where u, v, w, p all live at the same cell center. Discretize the pressure gradient with centered differences:

\[\left.\frac{\partial p}{\partial x}\right|_i \approx \frac{p_{i+1} - p_{i-1}}{2\Delta x}.\]

The stencil skips cell i — the pressure at the point where we're computing the momentum tendency plays no role. The result is a checker- board null mode: alternating pressures p = (+1, -1, +1, -1, …) give zero discrete gradient. Momentum cannot see them, but they exist and grow. Collocated schemes need either Rhie-Chow interpolation or artificial dissipation to kill them.

The staggered layout eliminates the problem structurally:

\[\left.\frac{\partial p}{\partial x}\right|_{i-1/2} = \frac{p_i - p_{i-1}}{\Delta x}\]

is a compact 2-point stencil that has no null mode. Discrete gradient and discrete divergence become each other's transposes:

\[G^\top = -D\]

which is the discrete analogue of the identity ⟨p, ∇·u⟩ = -⟨∇p, u⟩ that underlies the continuum incompressibility. This exact adjointness is what makes the FFT Poisson solver recover a truly divergence-free velocity to machine precision.

Coordinate convention in Aeolus

For a RegularGrid with Nx × Ny × Nz interior cells and halo width (Hx, Hy, Hz):

  • Cell (i, j, k) occupies [(i-1)Δx, iΔx] × [(j-1)Δy, jΔy] × [(k-1)Δz, kΔz].
  • Its center is at ((i-½)Δx, (j-½)Δy, (k-½)Δz).
  • Its west face (index i on FaceX) is at x = (i-1)Δx.
  • All storage arrays have shape (Nx+2Hx, Ny+2Hy, Nz+2Hz). Interior indices are Hx+1 : Hx+Nx, and similarly for the other axes.

The east face of cell Nx is identified with the west face of cell 1 under periodic wrap. So a FaceX field stores Nx values, one per cell's west face; no extra storage for the wrap-around face.

Discrete stencils

Multiple dispatch picks the correct stencil from the Location parameters. There are three families of first-derivative stencils:

Cell-center → face (gradient)

Used for pressure gradient, buoyancy flux, etc.

\[\left.\frac{\partial \phi}{\partial x}\right|_{i-1/2,j,k} = \frac{\phi_{i,j,k} - \phi_{i-1,j,k}}{\Delta x}\]

Compact, 2-point. Second-order accurate.

Aeolus method: ∂x(dϕdx::Field{FaceX}, ϕ::Field{CellCenter}).

Face → cell-center (divergence contribution)

\[\left.\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}\right|_{i,j,k} = \frac{u_{i+1,j,k} - u_{i,j,k}}{\Delta x}\]

Also 2-point. When combined with the analogous v- and w- differences we get the discrete divergence at cell centers:

\[(\nabla\cdot u)_{i,j,k} = \frac{u_{i+1}-u_i}{\Delta x} + \frac{v_{j+1}-v_j}{\Delta y} + \frac{w_{k+1}-w_k}{\Delta z}\]

Aeolus method: ∂x(divu::Field{CellCenter}, u::Field{FaceX}) — and divergence(out, u, v, w) combines them.

Cell → cell (2nd-order centered)

Kept for diagnostics on cell-centered fields; not used in the Boussinesq core:

\[\left.\frac{\partial \phi}{\partial x}\right|_{i,j,k} = \frac{\phi_{i+1,j,k} - \phi_{i-1,j,k}}{2\Delta x}\]

Aeolus method: ∂x(dϕdx::Field{CellCenter}, ϕ::Field{CellCenter}).

The discrete Laplacian

For any field at any single location the 2nd-order centered Laplacian is the same 7-point stencil:

\[(\nabla^2 \phi)_{i,j,k} = \frac{\phi_{i+1}-2\phi_i+\phi_{i-1}}{\Delta x^2} + \frac{\phi_{j+1}-2\phi_j+\phi_{j-1}}{\Delta y^2} + \frac{\phi_{k+1}-2\phi_k+\phi_{k-1}}{\Delta z^2}.\]

It appears with three different physical meanings:

  • ∇²u_i — viscous diffusion of the i-th velocity component (in the constant-ν limit).
  • ∇²θ — molecular scalar diffusion.
  • ∇²p — the discrete pressure Poisson operator.

The Aeolus kernel _laplacian! is generic over Location.

Discrete divergence of discrete gradient

A crucial identity: the divergence of the gradient (both discrete) is exactly the 7-point Laplacian. Starting from

\[G_x p := (p_i - p_{i-1})/\Delta x \text{ at west face of cell }i,\]

\[D_x u := (u_{i+1} - u_i)/\Delta x \text{ at cell }i,\]

\[D_x G_x p \;=\; \frac{(p_{i+1}-p_i)/\Delta x - (p_i-p_{i-1})/\Delta x}{\Delta x} \;=\; \frac{p_{i+1}-2p_i+p_{i-1}}{\Delta x^2}.\]

This means: if we solve ∇²φ = ∇·u* using the same discrete Laplacian, the corrected velocity u = u* - ∇φ satisfies ∇·u = 0 exactly. Not "to leading order" — exactly, to machine precision. See Fractional-step projection.
