Soil water: Richards' equation in a CLM-style column

Phase 46 gives the land surface a soil: a column of layers below the skin in which water infiltrates, redistributes, drains and is pulled up by evaporation and roots. This page documents the water-transport part implemented in src/SoilColumn.jl (issue #15); soil heat and freeze–thaw (#16) and the water table (#17) extend the same column.

1. Layers

Depth $z$ is positive downward from the soil surface. The default column is CLM5's (Lawrence et al. 2019; CLM5.0 Technical Note, Table 2.2.3): 20 hydrologically active layers whose thicknesses grow from 2 cm at the surface to 1.14 m at the base,

\[\Delta z_j = \begin{cases} 0.02\,j & j \le 4\\ 0.08 + 0.04\,(j-4) & 5 \le j \le 13\\ 0.44 + 0.10\,(j-13) & j \ge 14 \end{cases}\]

reaching 8.6 m, with the nodes at the layer centres. The older exponential CLM2–4.5 structure $z_i = 0.025[\exp(0.5(i-0.5)) - 1]$ (10 layers to 3.43 m; Oleson et al. 2004) and a uniform grid for benchmarks are also provided. Both CLM grids are tested against the published tables.

2. Hydraulic functions

The matric potential $\psi$ (m of water, negative when unsaturated) and the volumetric water content $\theta$ are linked by a retention curve, and the hydraulic conductivity $K$ falls steeply as the soil dries. Three families are implemented behind AbstractHydraulicModel:

modelretentionconductivitysource
ClappHornberger$\psi = \psi_s(\theta/\theta_s)^{-b}$$K_s(\theta/\theta_s)^{2b+3}$Clapp & Hornberger (1978); Cosby et al. (1984) pedotransfer
VanGenuchtenMualem``S_e = [1+(\alpha\psi)^n]^{-m}``
GardnerExponential$\theta - \theta_r \propto e^{\alpha\psi}$$K_s e^{\alpha\psi}$Gardner (1958) — the exact-benchmark soil

Texture tables: CLAPP_HORNBERGER_1978 (11 USDA classes) and CARSEL_PARRISH_1988 (12 classes), stored in the papers' units and converted by clapp_hornberger_texture / carsel_parrish_texture.

Air entry in van Genuchten soils. For $n < 2$ (loams, clays) the Mualem conductivity has an infinite slope at saturation: a loam loses 40 % of $K_s$ within 2 cm of saturation. Ippisch et al. (2006) show this is non-physical, and in the solver it made the Picard iteration chatter indefinitely once the surface layers reached saturation under ponding (thousands of sub-steps per 5 minutes). Following Vogel et al. (2001) the model takes an air-entry potential $\psi_e$, below which $S_e$ is rescaled by $S_c = [1+(\alpha|\psi_e|)^n]^{-m}$; carsel_parrish_texture uses $\psi_e = -2$ cm by default ($\psi_e = 0$ recovers the published curves). The retention curve moves by at most $(\theta_s-\theta_r)(1-S_c)$, at $\psi_e$: under 0.6 % of the pore space for the loams and clays, but 1.3–2.3 % for the coarse classes (sand, loamy sand, sandy loam, sandy clay loam) — a deliberate departure from the published curves in the wettest 2 cm.

3. Temperature and ice

Hydraulic conductivity is $K = k\rho g/\mu$; the intrinsic permeability $k$ does not depend on temperature, the viscosity of water does — by a factor 1.8 between 0 and 20 °C. Layer conductivities are multiplied by $\mu(T_{ref})/\mu(T)$ with $\mu(T)$ from the correlation of Kestin et al. (1978) (within 0.075 % of the CRC values at seven temperatures, 0–80 °C; the test tolerance is 0.3 %) and $T_{ref} = 20$ °C, the laboratory temperature of the texture tables. A soil at 5 °C therefore needs a wetter profile to carry the same drainage flux, which the unit-gradient test verifies.

Pore ice reduces conductivity further: the column carries θ_ice and T_soil and multiplies $K$ by ice_impedance_factor(imp, θ_ice, θ_s) of an AbstractIceImpedance ($1$ for NoIceImpedance; CLM's $10^{-6\,θ_{ice}/θ_s}$ for PowerLawIceImpedance). With ice present the retention curve applies to the total water $θ_{liq} + (ρ_{ice}/ρ_w)θ_{ice}$, so freezing does not change ψ — see soil heat and freeze–thaw, where the temperatures and ice contents come from.

4. Richards' equation and its discretisation

With sinks $s$ (root uptake), in mixed form,

\[\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial t} = \frac{\partial}{\partial z}\left[K(\psi)\left(\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial z} - 1\right)\right] - s , \qquad q = K\left(1 - \frac{\partial\psi}{\partial z}\right)\ \text{(downward flux)} .\]

Space. Finite volumes on the layers: node values $\psi_i$, fluxes at the interfaces $q_{i+1/2} = K_{i+1/2}[1 - (\psi_{i+1}-\psi_i)/(z_{i+1}-z_i)]$. The interface conductivity is by default the logarithmic mean $K_{i+1/2} = (K_{i+1}-K_i)/\ln(K_{i+1}/K_i)$, which is exact when $K$ varies exponentially with $\psi$ between nodes. On the exact Gardner benchmark (§6) it converges at second order, while the arithmetic mean of Celia et al. (1990) is first order: the steep gradients next to a prescribed-head boundary make its boundary flux $O(\Delta z)$ wrong, and that error pollutes the whole profile.

Time. Backward Euler, linearised by the modified Picard iteration of Celia et al. (1990): with $m$ the iterate,

\[\theta^{n+1,m+1} \approx \theta^{n+1,m} + C^m(\psi^{m+1}-\psi^m),\qquad C = d\theta/d\psi,\]

and $K$ frozen at $m$, each iterate is one tridiagonal solve. The mixed form is what makes the scheme mass-conservative where the $\psi$-form is not (Celia's point). Here conservation is made exact: after convergence the new water content is formed from the converged fluxes themselves,

\[\theta_i^{n+1} = \theta_i^n + \frac{\Delta t}{\Delta z_i}\left(q_{i-1/2} - q_{i+1/2} - s_i\right),\]

so the change of storage equals the boundary fluxes to round-off, for any tolerance; the tolerance only bounds the mismatch between $\theta$ and $\theta(\psi)$ (which the next step's residual then corrects). Convergence is declared, as in HYDRUS, when water contents of unsaturated layers change by less than tol (default $10^{-7}$) and potentials of saturated layers by less than tol_ψ ($10^{-4}$ m). Saturated layers store $\theta_s + S_s(\psi-\psi_e)$ with a small elastic storage $S_s = 10^{-5}$ m⁻¹, which keeps a fully saturated column well posed.

Stiffness. Near saturation the capacity $C$ collapses and the problem becomes elliptic; the Picard iteration then needs short steps. Each call of step_soil_water! is split into sub-steps that halve when the iteration fails and grow by half after fast convergence; after 8 iterations the update is under-relaxed ($\omega = 1/2$).

5. Boundary conditions

Top — AtmosphericTop. The net supply $P - E$ is imposed as a flux. If absorbing it would require a surface potential above $\psi_{pond} = 0$ the boundary switches to the head $\psi = 0$ and the excess becomes infiltration-excess runoff; if delivering the evaporative demand would require a surface potential below $\psi_{dry}$ (CLM's $-10^5$ m) it switches to that head and evaporation becomes soil-limited (stage-2 drying). HeadTop(ψ) prescribes the surface potential directly.

Bottom — AbstractSoilBottomBoundary. Free drainage $q = K_N$ (CLM's default), an impermeable base, or a prescribed head. A boundary supplies the linearisation $q = a + b\,\psi_N$ and may keep its own state, updated by finalize_bottom! after each accepted sub-step — the interface through which the water table (#17), UnconfinedAquifer, is coupled.

Coupling. step_soil_water!(col, forcing, fluxes, dt) takes the SurfaceExchange's SurfaceForcing (rain) and SurfaceFluxes (evaporation $E$) and converts kg m⁻² s⁻¹ with the contract's $\rho_w$; moisture_availability(col) is the Lee & Pielke (1992) $\beta$ of the top layer relative to field capacity (−33 kPa).

6. Validation

Exact solution (Gardner soil). For $K = K_s e^{\alpha\psi}$ and $\theta - \theta_r = (\theta_s-\theta_r)K/K_s$, Richards' equation is linear in $K$ (Srivastava & Yeh 1991; Tracy 2006). With $\zeta$ the height above the base of a column of length $L$,

\[c\,\partial_t K = \partial_\zeta^2 K + \alpha\,\partial_\zeta K ,\qquad c = \alpha(\theta_s-\theta_r)/K_s .\]

Start from $K = K_r$ everywhere, hold the base at $K_r$ and raise the top to $K_t$ at $t = 0$. The steady part is $K_r + A(1-e^{-\alpha\zeta})/(1-e^{-\alpha L})$, $A = K_t - K_r$; the substitution $K = e^{-\alpha\zeta/2}u$ turns the transient into the heat equation with decay $\alpha^2/4$, and projecting the initial deficit on $\sin(k_n\zeta)$, $k_n = n\pi/L$, gives

\[K = K_r + A\frac{1-e^{-\alpha\zeta}}{1-e^{-\alpha L}} + e^{-\alpha\zeta/2}\sum_{n\ge1} \frac{2A k_n(-1)^n e^{\alpha L/2}}{L\lambda_n}\sin(k_n\zeta)\,e^{-\lambda_n t/c}, \qquad \lambda_n = k_n^2 + \frac{\alpha^2}{4}.\]

For a 1 m column ($\alpha = 2$ m⁻¹, $K_s = 10^{-5}$ m s⁻¹, from $\psi = -5$ m, surface held at 0):

Sorptivity. With gravity off and the surface saturated, Boltzmann similarity makes the absorption exactly $I = S\sqrt t$. For the Gardner soil (constant diffusivity) $S = 2\Delta\theta\sqrt{D/\pi}$: the solver gives 0.9993 and 0.9998 of it after 2 and 6 h. For the loam, the numerical $S$ is constant to 0.1 % between 1 and 6 h and 0.7 % above Parlange's (1975) approximation, inside the rigorous bounds. (Parlange's integral must run to the boundary potential, not the air-entry value: the saturated segment $\psi_e < \psi < 0$ still transmits water. The first version of the test stopped at $\psi_e$ and disagreed by 12 %.)

Philip infiltration. Vertical ponded infiltration into the loam exceeds horizontal absorption by $A t$ with $A/K_s$ = 0.43–0.49 over 10 min–3 h, inside Philip's $[1/3, 2/3]$; the ponded rate falls to 1.006 $K_s$ after 5 days.

Steady states and conservation. A layered CLM5 column on bedrock relaxes to hydrostatic equilibrium (total-head spread 3e-10 m); free drainage under a steady input reaches the unit-gradient content $\theta_s(q/K_sf_T)^{1/(2b+3)}$ to 1e-6. Over 30 days of mixed rain, evaporation, dew and root extraction the worst per-step budget error is 1.3e-15 m, and the Celia et al. (1990) problem has a mass-balance ratio of $1 + 3\times10^{-15}$ at Δt = 1 h.

Not validated against observations. The phase target — soil moisture profiles at FLUXNET sites — needs the vegetated land column (#21) and the thermal model (#16); this issue is validated against the exact, similarity and benchmark results above only.
