Ice nucleation

Between 0 °C and about −38 °C liquid water is metastable: a supercooled droplet does not freeze on its own on any atmospheric timescale. The first ice in a mixed-phase cloud forms heterogeneously, on a solid surface that templates the ice lattice — an ice-nucleating particle (INP). INP are rare, typically one ambient aerosol particle in $10^5$–$10^6$ at −20 °C, and dominated by two classes: mineral dust (feldspars and clays) and primary biological particles (bacteria, fungal spores, pollen fragments), which are the most active INP known at temperatures warmer than −15 °C (Hoose & Möhler 2012; Kanji et al. 2017).

This page develops the theory behind src/IceNucleation.jl (Phase 43). Sections 1–4 cover the prognostic INP population (issue #5); §6–7 the immersion and deposition freezing that draws on it and its consistency with the Phase 39 mixed-phase ramp (issue #6). The coupling into the P3 ice category (issue #7) builds on the interface described in §5 and §6.4.

1. Why INP must be prognostic

Most operational freezing parameterisations are singular: they state how many INP are active at temperature $T$, independently of time. DeMott et al. (2010) write it in terms of the number of aerosol particles larger than 0.5 μm diameter,

\[N_\mathrm{INP}(T) = a\,(273.16 - T)^b\,N_{>0.5\,\mu\mathrm{m}}^{\,c(273.16-T)+d},\]

and the ice-nucleation-active-site (INAS) formulations (Niemand et al. 2012; Ullrich et al. 2017) in terms of the particle surface area $A$ and a site density $n_s(T)$:

\[N_\mathrm{INP}(T) = N\left[1 - \exp\!\left(-n_s(T)\,\frac{A}{N}\right)\right] \;\xrightarrow{\;n_s A/N \ll 1\;}\; n_s(T)\,A .\]

Both describe the cumulative number frozen by the time the parcel has cooled to $T$. Applied every time step to a diagnostic aerosol field, they re-freeze the same particles over and over and turn a one-off nucleation burst into a steady ice source. In Arctic mixed-phase stratus that is the difference between a cloud that persists for days — as observed — and one that glaciates and precipitates itself out in hours (Harrington & Olsson 2001; Morrison et al. 2012). Solomon et al. (2015) showed that realistic ice number in such clouds requires tracking INP depletion and the return of INP when ice sublimates.

The INP population is therefore split into two prognostic populations per mode:

populationmeaning
availablehas not nucleated ice; interstitial or immersed in a droplet
consumedinside an ice crystal; cannot nucleate again until released

2. Modal representation

Each INP mode is a log-normal in dry radius with fixed geometric standard deviation $\sigma_g$, carried as two moments — number $N$ and dry mass $M$ — per population. The median radius is diagnosed,

\[r_g = \left[\frac{M}{N\,\rho_p\,\tfrac{4\pi}{3}\exp(4.5\ln^2\sigma_g)}\right]^{1/3},\]

exactly as in the Phase 42 AerosolReservoir; the mode shapes and species are the Phase 37 LogNormalMode/AerosolSpecies records (:mineral_dust, :biological), converted to SI with AerosolCloud.arg_mode. The species record is the only source of the particle density: a population built from SI ARGModes whose ρ_p disagrees with its species is rejected. Size-selective sinks (sedimentation of the coarse tail, nucleation on the largest particles) therefore shrink the diagnosed $r_g$ of what remains, which is the memory a two-moment scheme can hold.

Analytic moments above a cut-off

The $k$-th moment of a log-normal is log-normal with the same $\sigma_g$ and median $r_g e^{k\ln^2\sigma_g}$, so the part above a cut-off radius $r_c$ is

\[\mathcal{M}_k(>r_c) = \frac{\mathcal{M}_k}{2}\, \mathrm{erfc}\!\left(\frac{\ln(r_c/r_g)}{\sqrt2\ln\sigma_g} - \frac{k\ln\sigma_g}{\sqrt2}\right).\]

$k = 0$ (number, DeMott's $N_{>0.5\,\mu\mathrm m}$ with $r_c = 0.25$ μm) and $k = 3$ (mass) are the Phase 42 number_above_radius/mass_above_radius; $k = 2$ (surface area, for INAS) is surface_area_above_radius, with total $A = 4\pi N r_g^2 e^{2\ln^2\sigma_g}$. The test suite checks all three against an independent 200 000-point quadrature in $\ln r$: worst relative difference $2.1\times10^{-9}$ over $\sigma_g$ = 1.4–2.8.

Size-dependent per-particle probabilities

Every sink in the module acts particle by particle with a size-dependent probability $p(r)$: stochastic freezing $1 - e^{-J(T)4\pi r^2\Delta t}$, settling out of a layer $1 - e^{-v(r)\Delta t/\Delta z}$, size-dependent scavenging. The removed fractions of number and mass are

\[\frac{\Delta N}{N} = \int p\!\left(r_g e^{\ln\sigma_g z}\right)\varphi(z)\,dz,\qquad \frac{\Delta M}{M} = \int p\!\left(r_g e^{\ln\sigma_g (z + 3\ln\sigma_g)}\right)\varphi(z)\,dz,\]

with $\varphi$ the standard normal density. Integrating each moment in its own shifted variable makes $p \equiv c$ remove exactly $c$ of every moment, so a size-independent sink leaves $r_g$ unchanged to rounding. The trapezoid rule on a Gaussian weight is spectrally accurate for smooth $p$; for the INAS curve it agrees with an independent quadrature to $3\times10^{-15}$. A discontinuous $p$ (a hard size threshold) converges only as the node spacing: 0.3 % absolute error in the frozen fraction, which is why the threshold case has its own analytic path (selection = :largest).

3. The budget

Each operator books what it moves, so two identities hold exactly:

\[N_\mathrm{avail} + N_\mathrm{cons} = N_\mathrm{init} + N_\mathrm{src} - N_\mathrm{wet} - N_\mathrm{sed}, \qquad N_\mathrm{cons} = N_\mathrm{nuc} - N_\mathrm{rel} - N_\mathrm{cons,lost},\]

and the same for mass. inp_budget_residual evaluates them; over 1500 random steps of every source and sink operator the worst relative residual is $9\times10^{-16}$.

operatormovesbudget term
inp_emit!source → available$N_\mathrm{src}$
inp_entrain!environment ↔ available (linear mixing)$N_\mathrm{src}$ (may be negative)
inp_consume!available → consumed (nucleation)$N_\mathrm{nuc}$
inp_nucleate_singular!available → consumed, increment only$N_\mathrm{nuc}$
inp_release!consumed → available (ice sublimated)$N_\mathrm{rel}$
inp_scavenge!available or consumed → removed$N_\mathrm{wet}$
inp_sediment!available → removed (settling)$N_\mathrm{sed}$

All removals use the exact exponential solution for the step, so they are positive for any $\Delta t$: one 3600 s scavenging step equals 36 steps of 100 s to $10^{-12}$.

Singular nucleation without double counting

A singular scheme's statement applies to the total population — particles that froze earlier would freeze again at this temperature — so the nucleation increment is

\[\Delta N = \min\!\left[N_\mathrm{avail},\; \max\!\left(0,\; N_\mathrm{frozen}(T;\,N_\mathrm{avail}+N_\mathrm{cons}) - N_\mathrm{cons}\right)\right].\]

The mass increment $M_\mathrm{frozen} - M_\mathrm{cons}$ is computed the same way, but it is a difference of two separately computed totals and need not describe a set of particles that exists. If earlier nucleation took the largest particles (the DeMott number form uses selection = :largest), the consumed population already holds more mass than an INAS $p(r)$ assigns to that many particles, and the difference is negative. The increment is therefore required to lie between the mass of the $\Delta N$ smallest and the $\Delta N$ largest available particles. Outside that range, $\Delta N$ moves with the mean mass of the available particles that $p$ selects, so no particle is ever moved without its mass.

Holding $T$ fixed nucleates nothing after the first call (tested to be exactly zero); cooling further nucleates only the difference; warming nucleates nothing. INP returned by inp_release! re-freeze once the parcel is again as cold as when they first froze — the ice recycling of Solomon et al. (2015).

Which particles nucleate

For a nucleated number $\Delta N$ the mass that moves with it depends on which particles froze:

  • selection = :largest — $\Delta N$ is converted to the radius $r_c$ with $N(>r_c) = \Delta N$ and the mass above $r_c$ moves. Right for every singular scheme, whose activity rises monotonically with size.
  • selection = :random — $\Delta M = M\,\Delta N/N$, $r_g$ unchanged.
  • a probability function $p(r)$ — the integrals above.

4. Sedimentation and scavenging

Dry INP settle at the Stokes speed with Cunningham slip, the regime-1 law of the droplet terminal velocity (Superdroplet.stokes_settling_velocity, shared so that there is one drag law in the package):

\[v(r) = \left(1 + 2.51\frac{\lambda}{d}\right)\frac{(\rho_p-\rho_a)\,g\,d^2}{18\mu}.\]

It reproduces the Seinfeld & Pandis (2016) Table 9.4 speeds for unit-density spheres, 3.49×10⁻³ cm s⁻¹ at 1 μm and 0.304 cm s⁻¹ at 10 μm (tabulated 3.5×10⁻³ and 0.31). Without slip the moment-weighted speeds are closed-form,

\[\langle v\rangle_N = k r_g^2 e^{2\ln^2\sigma_g},\qquad \langle v\rangle_M = k r_g^2 e^{8\ln^2\sigma_g},\]

and the quadrature matches them to $4\times10^{-15}$. Mass settles $e^{6\ln^2\sigma_g}$ times faster than number — 17.8× for $\sigma_g = 2$ — so a settling dust layer loses its mass long before its number, and its INP (which sit in the coarse tail) faster still. For a DeMott-type dust mode with $r_g = 0.4$ μm, $\sigma_g = 2$, $\langle v\rangle_N = 0.14$ mm s⁻¹ and $\langle v\rangle_M = 2.4$ mm s⁻¹.

Scavenging takes a coefficient $\Lambda$ (s⁻¹), constant or a function $\Lambda(r)$; consumed INP are removed with their ice by passing the host's ice removal rate with population = :consumed.

5. Interface for the freezing and P3 coupling

IceNucleation never touches a hydrometeor state. Freezing routines (issue #6) take an INPPopulation, temperature and supersaturation, compute ice-number and ice-mass source terms, and draw the matching INP sink through inp_consume! or inp_nucleate_singular!. The P3 coupling (issue #7) adds those rates to its ice category and removes the corresponding liquid or vapour; when ice sublimates it calls inp_release!, and when ice precipitates out of a cell it calls inp_scavenge!(…; population = :consumed). Nucleation can therefore be tested in isolation and is independent of any one bulk scheme.

6. Immersion and deposition freezing

Freezing is singular throughout: each scheme states how many of a mode's INP are frozen at $(T, S_i)$, and inp_nucleate_singular! applies it to the total (available + consumed) population so that only the increment is nucleated (§3). $\Delta t$ only converts increments into rates.

The Ullrich et al. (2017) deposition density for dust is

\[n_s(T, S_i) = \exp\!\left\{\alpha (S_i - 1)^{1/4} \cos^2[\beta(T-\gamma)]\,\frac{\mathrm{arccot}[\kappa(T-\lambda)]}{\pi}\right\} - 1,\]

$\alpha = 285.692$, $\beta = 0.017$, $\gamma = 256.692$, $\kappa = 0.080$, $\lambda = 200.745$, with $S_i$ clamped at water saturation $e_s/e_{si}$ (both from MoistThermodynamics). The $-1$ is ours: the published $\exp\{\cdot\}$ gives 1 m⁻² at $S_i = 1$; subtracting it makes deposition vanish continuously at ice saturation and changes $n_s$ by less than 10⁻⁶ wherever it exceeds 10⁶ m⁻². Meyers et al. (1992) was not used for deposition because it predicts ice from $S_i$ alone and cannot consume a prognostic INP.

Fitted ranges. No scheme is extrapolated: warmer than its range it predicts no ice (an exponential fit carried to 0 °C gives spurious ice where no data constrain it), colder it is held at the cold-edge value; at $T \ge T_\mathrm{freeze}$ every scheme is zero regardless.

6.3 Source terms

ice_nucleation!(inp, T, S_i, Δt; p, N_liq, M_liq, immersed_fraction) applies, per mode, the immersion scheme to the immersed fraction $f$ of the INP (default 1 in cloud, 0 out of cloud) and the deposition scheme to the rest. When both are INAS schemes the per-particle probabilities are combined, $p(r) = f\,p_\mathrm{imm}(r) + (1-f)\,p_\mathrm{dep}(r)$, so the consumed mass stays size-resolved; otherwise the frozen numbers are combined. The increment is split between mechanisms in proportion to their contributions. Then:

  • an immersion-frozen INP freezes one droplet of the mean droplet mass $M_\mathrm{liq}/N_\mathrm{liq}$; at most $N_\mathrm{liq}$ droplets freeze;
  • a deposition-nucleated INP makes one crystal of mass $m_{i0} = \tfrac{4\pi}{3}\,900\,(1\,\mu\mathrm{m})^3$ = 3.8×10⁻¹⁵ kg (the P3 nucleated-ice mass), drawn from vapour but never more than the excess over ice saturation, $(S_i - 1)e_{si}(T)/(R_v T)$;
  • the latent heat is $L_f(T) M_\mathrm{liq} + L_s(T) M_\mathrm{vap}$ with the Kirchhoff latent heats of MoistThermodynamics.

The result is an IceNucleationTendency of increments (all non-negative); nucleation_rates divides by $\Delta t$. The tests check, for every call, INP consumed = crystals formed (to 10⁻¹²), $M_\mathrm{ice} = M_\mathrm{liq} + M_\mathrm{vap}$ exactly, heat per kilogram = $L_f$ or $L_s$, closure of the INP budget (§3) to 10⁻¹⁴, a second call at the same $(T, S_i)$ nucleating exactly zero, and two cooling steps summing to one step straight to the colder temperature (to 10⁻¹⁰). For the reference parcel of the tests (1 cm⁻³ dust, 0.01 cm⁻³ biological, 100 cm⁻³ droplets, 0.2 g m⁻³) immersion freezing at −25 °C forms 15.6 L⁻¹ crystals on dust and 3×10⁻⁴ L⁻¹ on biological particles; at −30 °C 150 L⁻¹; deposition at 225 K and $S_i$ = 1.25 (out of cloud) forms 30 L⁻¹.

6.4 Coupling contract for P3 (issue #7)

The host passes $T$, $S_i$ (relative_humidity_ice), $p$ and its droplet number and liquid content, adds $N_\mathrm{ice,imm} + N_\mathrm{ice,dep} + N_\mathrm{ice,hom}$ and $M_\mathrm{ice}$ to its ice category, removes $N_\mathrm{liq}$, $M_\mathrm{liq}$ from cloud liquid and $M_\mathrm{vap}$ from vapour, and heats the air by $\mathrm{heat}/(\rho c_{pm})$. When its ice sublimates or melts it calls inp_release!; when ice precipitates out, inp_scavenge!(…; population = :consumed).

6.5 The P3 host side (issue #7)

p3_ice_nucleation(s, inp, Δt) is that contract for P3's single ice category. It evaluates $S_i$ with relative_humidity_ice, passes $N_\mathrm{liq} = N_c\rho$ and $M_\mathrm{liq} = q_c\rho$ ($\rho$ from air_density of the cell) and applies the increments per kg of air: droplets frozen leave $q_c, N_c$; vapour deposited leaves $q_v$; $q_i$ gains $M_\mathrm{ice}/\rho$; $N_i$ gains one particle per crystal; frozen drops enter the rime mass and volume at $\rho_\mathrm{rim,max}$ = 900 kg m⁻³ (the P3 code's treatment of frozen cloud) while vapour-nucleated crystals are unrimed; and $c_{pm}\Delta T = \mathrm{heat}/\rho$, which is exactly the P3 enthalpy form $c_{pm}\,dT = -L_v\,dq_v + L_f\,dq_i$. Only cloud droplets freeze — P3 rain has no immersion-freezing process here.

p3_inp_release!(inp, s, r, Δt) returns the consumed INP of ice that sublimated or melted: the fraction $\Delta t\,(n_\mathrm{sub} + n_\mathrm{melt})/N_i$ of each mode, using the limited rates that p3_step_with_rates applied.

The two nucleated-crystal masses are one: NUCLEATED_ICE_MASS is defined as P3_NUCLEATED_ICE_MASS ($m_{i0}$ of P3). Hallett–Mossop splinters are larger (10 μm diameter, P3_HM_SPLINTER_MASS, as in the P3 code).

7. Consistency with the mixed-phase ramp

MoistThermodynamics.liquid_fraction (Phase 39) partitions condensate between liquid and ice with a smoothstep between $T_\mathrm{icenuc}$ = 233.15 K and $T_\mathrm{freeze}$ = 273.15 K. It and heterogeneous nucleation answer different questions — $\lambda$ is an equilibrium partition for schemes without prognostic ice, while nucleation sets the number of crystals that depositional growth (Wegener–Bergeron–Findeisen, the host's business) then feeds. They must nevertheless agree about where ice is impossible and where it is compulsory. The rule is that freezing_regime is bounded by the same constants:

regime$T$$\lambda(T)$nucleation
:liquid$T \ge T_\mathrm{freeze}$1none; no INP consumed
:heterogeneous$T_\mathrm{icenuc} < T < T_\mathrm{freeze}$(0, 1)immersion + deposition on INP
:homogeneous$T \le T_\mathrm{icenuc}$0all liquid freezes (immersed INP inside it); deposition continues on the interstitial INP only

Tested: the regime and $\lambda$ agree on a 0.05 K grid from 220 to 280 K; at $T_\mathrm{freeze}$ nothing freezes even for 100 cm⁻³ of dust at $S_i$ = 1.5; at $T_\mathrm{icenuc}$ the frozen liquid fraction is exactly $1 - \lambda = 1$ and the immersed INP are consumed inside the frozen drops; a custom $T_\mathrm{icenuc}$ moves both together. Inside the ramp heterogeneous freezing never outruns the ramp's ice fraction: for 10 cm⁻³ of immersed dust the frozen liquid fraction is at most 0.075 of $1 - \lambda(T)$.

8. Validation: mixed-phase Arctic stratus

The test a mixed-phase ice scheme must pass is that an Arctic stratocumulus deck persists: observed decks keep supercooled liquid for days with 0.1–1 L⁻¹ of ice, and a scheme that makes too much ice glaciates them through the Wegener–Bergeron–Findeisen process within hours (Harrington et al. 1999; Morrison et al. 2011, 2012; Ovchinnikov et al. 2014).

Set-up and what is simplified

run_arctic_stratus(ArcticStratusColumn(...)) integrates a 1-D column, not an LES, in the set-up of the ISDAC intercomparison (Ovchinnikov et al. 2014, flight 31): the ISDAC initial $\theta_l$ and $q_t$ profiles (isdac_initial_profile; elevated mixed layer 400–825 m under an inversion), 1020 hPa at the surface, no surface fluxes (sea ice), 200 cm⁻³ droplets and the ISDAC subsidence $w = -Dz$, $D$ = 5×10⁻⁶ s⁻¹ (constant, 4.125 mm s⁻¹, above 825 m). The initial LWP is 14.4 g m⁻², against the ~14 g m⁻² reported for the case (Paukert & Hoose 2014) — a check that the profiles are right, and asserted in the tests. The case paper itself (Ovchinnikov et al. 2014) was not accessible, so treat the set-up as "ISDAC-like".

The longwave is not ISDAC's. ISDAC simulations use a full radiation code (RRTMG). Here cloud-top cooling is the idealised DYCOMS-form $F = F_0 e^{-\kappa\,\mathrm{LWP}_\downarrow} + F_1 e^{-\kappa\,\mathrm{LWP}_\uparrow}$ with $F_0$ = 72 W m⁻², $F_1$ = 15 W m⁻², $\kappa$ = 170 m² kg⁻¹; the provenance of these three values is unverified and they are a modelling choice, not a case specification.

Simplified, and why it matters:

  • Turbulence is a prescribed eddy diffusivity, 30 m² s⁻¹ inside the 400–825 m mixed layer and zero across the inversion (implicit diffusion of $s_l$, the water species, the ice moments and the INP). Free-tropospheric air enters the mixed layer only through the subsidence, i.e. at the entrainment rate $w_e = D z_i$ ≈ 4 mm s⁻¹ that keeps the inversion stationary (Lilly 1968), not at the rate resolved turbulence would produce. The cloud-top cooling is therefore not balanced by entrainment warming as in the LES, and LWP grows with time even with ice (14 → 39 g m⁻² in 8 h without ice).
  • Ice initiation is prognostic — Niemand et al. (2012) immersion freezing on a dust mode (5 cm⁻³, $r_g$ = 0.4 μm, $\sigma_g$ = 2) with INP consumed on freezing, entrained with free-tropospheric air, falling with the ice number and released where ice sublimates — instead of ISDAC's prescribed ice number. The dust number is chosen so the ice is of order 0.1–1 L⁻¹: flight 31 observed ~0.35 L⁻¹, and the intercomparison prescribed 1 L⁻¹ with a 4 L⁻¹ sensitivity run. The tests' 0.25–4 L⁻¹ window is a band around those numbers, not a range published with the case.
  • Microphysics is the full P3 of this package: process rates with Hallett–Mossop (inactive: the cloud is at −12 to −15 °C, as in ISDAC and M-PACE), saturation adjustment (the contract's isobaric, moist-enthalpy-conserving one since issue #82; the column mixes $s_l$, which is not the same invariant), and ice sedimentation with P3's mass- and number-weighted fall speeds.

Water (column + precipitation − subsidence source) and INP (column + surface deposit − subsidence source) budgets close to 10⁻¹². A test that every P3 ice particle has a consumed INP (Hallett–Mossop being inactive) caught a bug during development: the column carried INP per m³ at a fixed transport density while P3 converts with the cell's current density, so each nucleated INP made $\rho_\mathrm{cell}/\rho$ crystals (up to 10⁻⁴ too many). INP are now rescaled to the cell density around each nucleation call.

With realistic INP the deck is mixed-phase and persistent; ice lowers LWP monotonically with ice number, as in the ISDAC LES; a 100-fold increase of INP glaciates it in about two hours, and the glaciation time shortens monotonically as INP rise. The IWP of the base case (0.36 g m⁻² at 0.48 L⁻¹) is, as far as the author recalls the intercomparison, below the several g m⁻² the ISDAC LES produced at 1 L⁻¹ — the ice number here is half that, and bulk (single-moment-shape) ice PSDs were found by Ovchinnikov et al. to underpredict IWP by a factor of two — but the IWP comparison is not a verified match and the tests do not claim one.
