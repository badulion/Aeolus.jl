Sub-grid surface tiles

A grid cell of tens to hundreds of metres is rarely one surface. An urban cell is roof, street, park and water; a rural one is field, wood and pond. src/SurfaceTiles.jl represents such a cell as a mosaic of tiles (Avissar & Pielke 1989; Koster & Suarez 1992): each tile is an ordinary Era V surface model — any AbstractSurface — with an area fraction $f_i$, $\sum_i f_i = 1$.

1. Flux aggregation

Every tile keeps its own prognostic state and solves its own energy balance

\[R_{n,i} = H_i + LE_i + G_i\]

against the grid-cell forcing, through the SurfaceExchange contract (its own skin temperature, humidity, roughness and Monin–Obukhov stability). The atmosphere sees the area-weighted flux

\[\overline{F} = \sum_i f_i\, F_i, \qquad F \in \{R_n, H, LE, G, \tau, E\}.\]

Because every component is weighted identically, the tile balances sum to the cell balance $\overline{R_n} = \overline{H} + \overline{LE} + \overline{G}$ exactly, and if tile $i$ stores $G_i\,Δt$ per unit area the cell stores $\overline{G}\,Δt$. Nothing is conserved "on average": the fraction-weighted sum is the conservation statement. The test suite measures it at a relative error of $1.7\times10^{-13}$ over a 24 h diurnal cycle of three slab tiles.

The alternative, parameter aggregation, averages the surface parameters ($\bar T_s$, $\bar α$, $\bar z_0$, $\bar β$) and computes one flux $F(\bar p)$. The two agree only where $F$ is linear in the parameters that differ between tiles (Mahrt 1987); the difference $\sum f_i F(p_i) - F(\bar p)$ is the aggregation error that tiling removes. parameter_aggregate builds the parameter-averaged surface so the two can be compared directly.

2. Exact radiative aggregates

For a uniform downwelling field the reflected shortwave and the absorbed longwave are linear in the albedo and emissivity, so area weighting is exact:

\[\bar α = \sum_i f_i α_i, \qquad \bar ε = \sum_i f_i ε_i .\]

Emission is not linear in temperature. The effective radiative temperature is defined by the emitted longwave,

\[\bar ε\, σ\, T_{rad}^4 = \sum_i f_i\, ε_i\, σ\, T_i^4 ,\]

so a radiation scheme that sees a single surface $(\bar α, \bar ε, T_{rad})$ gets $R_n = \sum f_i R_{n,i}$ identically. Because $T^4$ is convex, $T_{rad} \ge \sum f_i T_i$: two black tiles at 280 K and 320 K radiate like one at 302.0 K, not 300 K. A mosaic reports $T_{rad}$ as its surface_temperature.

These optical rules are the same area weighting as the Era III optical mosaic SurfaceOptics.TiledSurface; the difference is that a SurfaceMosaic asks each tile for its (broadband) albedo rather than holding spectral optics itself.

The neutral roughness case has a closed form, $τ_i = ρ\,[κU/\ln(z/z_{0,i})]^2$, which the mosaic reproduces to $10^{-12}$. The stress it produces corresponds to an effective roughness of 0.42 m, more than ten times the logarithmic mean 0.032 m — the result Mason (1988) formalised with the blending height (see Blending height). In the stable/unstable case a cell whose average skin is colder than the air still sends heat upward, because the warm tile's unstable exchange is far more efficient than the cool tile's stable one. Mahrt (1987) showed that when stability varies within a grid area the grid-averaged exchange coefficient is a complicated function of the grid-averaged stability; a sign reversal of $H$ is the extreme case of that nonlinearity. The wet/dry case is the land-surface situation Avissar & Pielke (1989) introduced the mosaic for: the dry half has no evaporative cooling, runs hot, loses more longwave (lower $R_n$) and converts the rest into sensible heat, so the cell's Bowen ratio is 0.52 against 0.007 for the uniform half-wet surface.

4. Class maps

The urban example stores a surface class per LES cell (street, roof, park, water, wall). That is the one-tile-per-cell limit of a mosaic. class_fractions and tiles_from_class_map coarse-grain such a map: each coarse cell gets one tile per class that occurs, with its area fraction. Because cells of one class under one forcing evolve identically, the coarse mosaic reproduces the block mean of the fine-resolution per-cell computation — measured at $5\times10^{-16}$ relative over six hours for an 8×8 five-class map in 4×4 blocks.

5. What is not here

A plain SurfaceMosaic forces all tiles with the grid-cell forcing (or a forcing per tile supplied by the caller). The blending-height formulation, in which tiles are forced at the height where their internal boundary layers merge, is BlendedMosaic; see Blending height. Changing tile fractions (set_mosaic_fractions!) leaves tile states untouched; moving heat and water between tiles when a fraction shrinks is left to the caller.
