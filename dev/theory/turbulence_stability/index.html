Stability functions and the gradient Richardson number

Phase 58 (#170) puts every Richardson-number-dependent piece of turbulence physics behind one contract, TurbulenceStability. Before it the repository had two stability families that did not share code: the Monin–Obukhov $φ/ψ$ functions of SurfaceExchange and a private _fstab(Ri) in the radiation-fog column. It also had two private mean-profile $N^2$ helpers (in TimeIntegration and Anelastic) and no pointwise $N^2$ or $Ri$ field. The contract is pure and is included before Operators, so the LES closure can call it.

Local first-order closure

A local closure writes the eddy viscosity and diffusivity as

\[K_m = l^2 |S|\, f_m(Ri), \qquad K_h = \frac{l^2 |S|}{Pr_n}\, f_h(Ri), \qquad Ri = \frac{N^2}{S^2}, \qquad N^2 = \frac{g}{θ_0}\frac{∂θ_v}{∂z},\]

with $l$ a mixing length ($C_sΔ$ in an LES, $κz$ near a wall), $|S|$ the shear, and $Pr_n$ the neutral turbulent Prandtl number. In the contract both factors are normalised to $f(0) = 1$, and $Pr_n$ is returned separately by neutral_prandtl, so different families can be compared factor for factor. Every implementer has

  • $f(0) = 1$,
  • $f ≥ 0$,
  • $f$ non-increasing for $Ri > 0$,
  • $f ≥ 1$ for $Ri < 0$.

The test suite asserts all four on $Ri ∈ [-5, 10] ∪ \{10^2, 10^4\}$ for every implementer.

  • Lilly (1962) obtained his correction from the equilibrium TKE budget $K_m S^2 - K_h N^2 = ε$ with $ε ∝ K^3/l^4$, which gives $K = l^2 (S^2 - N^2/Ri_c)^{1/2}$. Turbulence switches off at the critical Richardson number. Mason (1989) uses the same expression on the unstable side.
  • Brown, Derbyshire & Mason (1994) are the functions of the Met Office LEM and of MONC, with the constants of that code.
  • ShortTail is the radiation-fog column's function, moved into the contract unchanged (bit for bit). Its $Ri^{-2}$ tail lets turbulence collapse in a strong inversion, which radiation fog needs.
  • Louis (1979) has a long tail, $f_m ∝ Ri^{-1/2}$, with no critical value. On the unstable side Louis's coefficient depends on the roughness length. Away from the surface no roughness length exists, so the free-atmosphere limit (coefficient → 0) is used.

Consistency with Monin–Obukhov similarity

In the surface layer, similarity theory gives the gradients

\[\frac{∂u}{∂z} = \frac{u_*}{κz}φ_m(ζ), \qquad \frac{∂θ}{∂z} = \frac{θ_*}{κz}φ_h(ζ), \qquad ζ = \frac{z}{L}, \qquad L = \frac{u_*^2 θ_0}{κ g θ_*}.\]

Dividing $(g/θ_0)∂θ/∂z$ by $(∂u/∂z)^2$ gives the gradient Richardson number as a function of $ζ$:

\[Ri = \frac{g}{θ_0}\frac{θ_* φ_h/(κz)}{u_*^2 φ_m^2/(κz)^2} = \frac{κ z g θ_*}{θ_0 u_*^2}\,\frac{φ_h}{φ_m^2} = ζ\,\frac{φ_h(ζ)}{φ_m(ζ)^2}.\]

Next, ask what $f_m$ and $f_h$ make the local closure with $l = κz$ reproduce the surface fluxes $u_*^2$ and $u_*θ_*$:

\[K_m\frac{∂u}{∂z} = (κz)^2\Big(\frac{∂u}{∂z}\Big)^2 f_m = u_*^2 φ_m^2 f_m = u_*^2 \;\Longrightarrow\; f_m = φ_m^{-2},\]

\[K_h\frac{∂θ}{∂z} = (κz)^2 \frac{∂u}{∂z}\frac{∂θ}{∂z} f_h = u_*θ_* φ_m φ_h f_h = u_*θ_* \;\Longrightarrow\; f_h = (φ_m φ_h)^{-1}.\]

MOSTConsistent(sf) evaluates these at the $ζ$ that solves $Ri = ζφ_h/φ_m^2$. It finds that $ζ$ with the Illinois solver of solve_surface_layer, on $[ζ_{\min}, 0]$ or $[0, ζ_{\max}]$.

  • Log-linear functions, $φ = 1 + αζ$. Here $Ri = ζ/(1+αζ) → 1/α$ as $ζ → ∞$. That is a critical Richardson number: 0.2 for $α = 5$, and $Ri(ζ = 10^4) = 0.199996$. Beyond it no $ζ$ exists and $f = 0$.
  • Beljaars–Holtslag functions. $φ_h$ grows faster than $φ_m$, so $Ri$ is unbounded and there is no critical value: $Ri(ζ = 100) = 8.07$.
  • Unstable side, Businger–Dyer with $γ_m = γ_h$. $φ_h = φ_m^2$, so $Ri = ζ$ exactly.

Round trip (test 2). The test builds the analytic profiles $u(z)$, $θ(z)$ for $ζ ∈ [-2, 10]$ (49 values) with BDBH and with log-linear $α = 5$. It differentiates them at 10 m with a fourth-order stencil and forms $K_m = (κz)^2 S f_m$. The result reproduces $u_*^2/S$ to a maximum relative error of $5.4 × 10^{-11}$, and $K_h θ_z$ reproduces $u_*θ_*$ to $2.0 × 10^{-10}$. The requirement is $10^{-6}$.

N² and Ri on the grid

buoyancy_frequency_squared(θ_below, θ_above, Δz, g, θ_ref) is the one $N^2$ of a θ difference, $(g/θ_{\rm ref})Δθ/Δz$. Without $θ_{\rm ref}$ it is the logarithmic form $gΔ\ln θ/Δz$, which is exact for the constant-$N^2$ reference profile of the anelastic core. Three callers use it:

  • the pointwise field (Operators.buoyancy_frequency_squared!);
  • the CFL buoyancy limit of TimeIntegration, applied to plane means;
  • the reference $N^2$ of Anelastic.

Both mean-profile results are bit for bit the same as before the delegation (test 6).

The pointwise fields live at cell centres.

  • $N^2_k$ is the difference of the buoyancy that add_buoyancy! applies at the cell's two w faces. θ and $q_v$ are interpolated to the faces as there, with $θ_v = θ(1 + 0.61 q_v)$. On a uniform grid this is the centred difference $(g/θ_0)(θ_{k+1} - θ_{k-1})/(2Δz)$.
  • Cloudy air (#239). Given a condensate field $q_c$ (cloud or fog water) and the pressure, the buoyancy variable is $θ_ρ = θ(1 + 0.61 q_v - q_c)$ (condensate loading) and, in a saturated cell ($q_c > 0$), $N^2$ is that of a saturated parcel lifted on the moist adiabat, MoistThermodynamics.saturated_buoyancy_frequency_squared (see below).
  • gradient_richardson! divides by $|S|^2 = 2S_{ij}S_{ij}$ from the strain stencil of the Smagorinsky closure. For a pure vertical shear, $|S|^2 = (∂u/∂z)^2$.
  • $S^2$ is floored at RI_SHEAR2_FLOOR = $10^{-10}$ s⁻², a shear of $10^{-5}$ s⁻¹, so $Ri$ stays finite in shear-free cells.

Measured accuracy (test 4).

  • Linear profiles $u = Sz$, $θ = θ_0 + Γz$ on a 16³ grid: $Ri_g$ equals $gΓ/(θ_0 S^2)$ to a relative $2.3 × 10^{-13}$.
  • The shear layer $u = U\tanh(z/δ)$ with constant $N^2$: the discrete $Ri$ converges to $J\cosh^4(z/δ)$ with orders 1.92 and 1.98 ($N_z$ = 32 → 64 → 128, over $|z| ≤ 2δ$).

Saturated air

Without the condensate, a moist-adiabatic cloud or fog layer — neutral for the saturated parcels that make up its turbulence — has the $θ_v$ gradient of a stable layer: about $1.4 × 10^{-4}$ s⁻² for the 1 km column of test 7, enough for StabilityCorrected (Brown et al.) to shut the vertical mixing off at a shear of 0.02 s⁻¹ ($Ri = 0.36$) and for the Deardorff length scale $0.76\sqrt e/N$ to fall to 6 m at $e = 0.01$ m² s⁻². Deardorff (1980) formulated the closure for stratocumulus with the saturated buoyancy.

The saturated $N^2$ linearises the $θ_ρ$ of a parcel displaced on the (reversible) moist adiabat against the environment:

\[N^2 = \frac{g}{θ_0}\Bigl[(1 + 0.61 q_v - q_c) \Bigl(\frac{∂θ}{∂z} - \frac{Γ_d - Γ_m}{Π}\Bigr) + θ\Bigl(1.61\Bigl(\frac{∂q_v}{∂z} - \frac{dq_s}{dz}\Big|_{\rm ad}\Bigr) - \frac{∂q_w}{∂z}\Bigr)\Bigr].\]

$(Γ_d - Γ_m)/Π$ is the parcel's θ gradient ($T = θΠ$), so the pressure enters only pointwise; θ, $q_v$ and $q_w = q_v + q_c$ are differenced between the faces as for the dry $N^2$. In a saturated environment the expression is the saturated Brunt–Väisälä frequency of Durran & Klemp (1982, eq. 36) to $O(q)$. At a cloud edge it differs on purpose: Durran & Klemp assume a saturated environment and so drop the virtual effect of the humidity jump. A first version used their form. In the seeded Berlin smoke state it gave the fog-top level $⟨N^2⟩ = +3.1 × 10^{-3}$ s⁻², while $θ_v$ gives $-2.2 × 10^{-3}$. A saturated parcel lifted into drier air of the same θ is buoyant: its vapour outweighs its water.

Measured (test 7).

  • A saturated moist-adiabatic column with constant total water gives $|N^2| = 1.3 × 10^{-9}$, $3.1 × 10^{-10}$ and $7.8 × 10^{-11}$ s⁻² at $Δz$ = 50, 25 and 12.5 m (second order). The $θ_v$ form gives $1.4 × 10^{-4}$.
  • In a saturated environment the result matches eq. 36 of Durran & Klemp, written out by hand, to 0.9 %. It matches a parcel lifted 1 m by $8.5 × 10^{-9}$ s⁻², including at a cloud top.
  • It is within 1.4 %, 3.1 % and 1.3 % of the fog column's parcel $N^2$ (RadiationFog.moist_stability, an enthalpy-conserving finite displacement) on saturated layers of 8, 5 and 3 K km⁻¹.
  • Without condensate it reproduces the dry and the $q_v$ forms bit for bit.

Rain is not loaded into the closures' $N^2$ in the Berlin case: there $q_c$ is the cloud water $q_l$, which also marks a cell as saturated.

Maximum sustainable heat flux (P58-e)

Monin–Obukhov similarity, read with the wind held fixed, limits how much heat a stable surface layer can carry downward (Van de Wiel et al. 2012). With the wind $U$ at height $z$ over roughness $z_{0m}$,

\[u_* = \frac{κU}{Φ_m(ζ)}, \qquad Φ_m(ζ) = \ln\frac{z}{z_{0m}} - ψ_m(ζ) + ψ_m\!\left(ζ\frac{z_{0m}}{z}\right), \qquad ζ = \frac{z}{L} = \frac{zκgQ}{u_*^3θ_{ref}},\]

for the downward kinematic buoyancy flux $Q = -\overline{w'θ_v'} > 0$. Eliminating $u_*$ gives the flux as a function of the stability alone:

\[Q(ζ) = \frac{κ^2U^3θ_{ref}}{g z}\,\frac{ζ}{Φ_m(ζ)^3}.\]

$Q = 0$ at neutral ($ζ = 0$). As $ζ$ grows, $Q$ rises, because a stronger temperature contrast is mixed. $Q$ then falls, because the stratification suppresses $u_*$ faster than the contrast grows. The maximum is the maximum sustainable heat flux (MSHF). A surface that must lose more heat than this by turbulence, for example a skin under strong radiative cooling with little heat from below, has no turbulent equilibrium at that wind. Its surface layer collapses.

Log-linear closed form. For $φ_m = 1 + αζ$ the integrated function is $ψ_m = -αζ$, so $Φ_m = Λ + α(z - z_{0m})ζ/z$ with $Λ = \ln(z/z_{0m})$. In terms of $u_*$ (use $κU/u_* = Φ_m$):

\[Q(u_*) = \frac{θ_{ref}}{α g (z - z_{0m}) κ}\left(κU\,u_*^2 - Λ\,u_*^3\right).\]

This is a cubic in $u_*$. Setting $dQ/du_* = 2κUu_* - 3Λu_*^2 = 0$ gives the maximum at

\[u_* = \frac{2κU}{3Λ}, \qquad Q_{max} = \frac{4}{27}\,\frac{κ^2U^3θ_{ref}}{α\,g\,(z - z_{0m})\,Λ^2}, \qquad ζ = \frac{Λz}{2α(z - z_{0m})}.\]

At the maximum $Φ_m = 3Λ/2$. For $z_{0m} ≪ z$ this is the form of Van de Wiel et al. (2012), with $z$ in place of $z - z_{0m}$. Here $ψ_m(z_{0m}/L)$ is kept exactly. Note that $Q_{max} ∝ U^3$: read the other way round, $U_{min} = (Q/Q_{max}(1))^{1/3}$ is the minimum wind for sustainable turbulence of the paper's title.

Other families. max_sustainable_heat_flux maximises $ζ/Φ_m(ζ)^3$ numerically. A 48-point scan in $\ln ζ$ over $[10^{-6}, 10^3]$ brackets the maximum, then golden-section search narrows it to $|Δ\ln ζ| ≤ 10^{-12}$. This takes about 120 evaluations of $ψ_m$, about 3 µs. For BDBH (Beljaars–Holtslag stable branch) the curve is unimodal with an interior maximum. Its MSHF lies between those of log-linear $α = 5$ and $α = 1$, because $φ_m$ starts like $1 + 5ζ$ and tends to $1 + ζ$.

The heat roughness $z_{0h}$ does not enter. With the wind held fixed, the flux–stability relation is the momentum half of MOST. Every solve_surface_layer solution with effective wind $U$ lies on the curve, so its buoyancy flux $u_*θ_{v*}$ never exceeds $Q_{max}$.

Measured (test/collapse.jl (removed)).

  • Log-linear $α = 5$. The numerical maximum agrees with the closed form to $5 × 10^{-16}$ (requirement $10^{-8}$) at four $(U, z, z_{0m})$. The numerical argmax $u_*$ matches $2κU/(3Λ)$ to $7 × 10^{-9}$.
  • Solver consistency. Over $ΔT ∈ [0, 20]$ K, $U ∈ [0.5, 10]$ m/s (41 × 39 points, $z = 10$ m, $z_{0m} = 0.1$ m), the largest ratio $u_*θ_{v*}/Q_{max}$ is 0.99999976 for BDBH and 0.9999995 for log-linear. The surface flux, as a function of $ΔT$, comes up to the bound and stops there.

Flux-prescribed wall law. flux_friction_velocity solves $u_* = κU/Φ_m(ζ)$ for a prescribed flux. It takes the weakly stable root between neutral and the MSHF point, which is the root continuously connected to the neutral state. When $Q > Q_{max}$ it returns 0. WallModels.MOSTFluxWallLaw is this relation as a wall law. It is the wall of half_channel_case(surface_heat_flux = Q), and it exerts no stress in a column whose wind is too weak for the flux.

The collapse regime in LES

The flux-cooled half channel is built from three pieces:

  • the P54-d case, 16 × 16 × 32 cells on $2πH × 2πH × H$, with $z_0/H = 10^{-4}$ and $-∂p/∂x = u_*^2/H$;
  • the wall law above;
  • StabilityCorrected(StaticSmagorinsky(0.1); length_scales = :isotropic) (P55-e).

The anisotropic default length scales give $Δ_h = 12.6Δz$ on this grid. With them even the neutral run relaminarises (resolved TKE falls from 0.18 to 0.0035 $u_*^2$ over 20 $H/u_*$), so the collapse runs use the isotropic scales.

Each run starts from a neutral run spun up for 10 $H/u_*$, and the cooling is switched on at that point. The case has two MSHFs, both taken for the neutral state:

  • $Q_{wall}$ at the wall model's height $Δz/2 = H/64$, where $U_0 = 12.63\,u_*$;
  • $Q_{bulk}$ at $z = H$, the channel-scale criterion of Donda et al. (2015), who normalise by the half height.

So a single $H_{max}$ cannot separate the regimes of the issue in this case. The test asserts each threshold at the MSHF it belongs to:

  • $H = 0.5H_{max}^{bulk}$: TKE ≥ 30 % of neutral. Measured: 83 %.
  • $H = 2H_{max}^{wall}$:
    • γ < 0.2 on all 11 levels of the lowest third;
    • lowest-third TKE 2.8 % of neutral at 20 $H/u_*$;
    • whole-domain TKE 3.6 % at 40 $H/u_*$.

The whole-domain TKE at 20 $H/u_*$ is 13 %, not below 5 %. The upper two thirds, cut off from the wall shear, decay on their own turnover time. So the issue's "< 5 % at 20 turnovers" was the wrong time for the whole domain, and the test checks it at 40.

MSHF in the Berlin case

The :surface_exchange coupling evaluates the MSHF of every column at every surface step: one maximisation for the column's wind, forcing height, $z_{0m}$ and $θ_{va}$, through SurfaceExchange.surface_mshf_ratio. It writes the fields H_max_sfc and mshf_ratio. Its report gives the mean ratio of downward buoyancy flux to MSHF over the stable columns of each class (street, roof, park), and the share of those columns beyond the MSHF point: $z_{ref}/L$ past the peak of the flux curve, on the very stable branch where a stronger temperature contrast carries less heat (output mshf_beyond). A small ratio alone does not mean a column is far from collapse: a collapsed column also has a small ratio, on the other side of the peak.

Its smoke check asserts $|H| ≤ H_{max}(1 + 10^{-8})$ for every column and every surface step, on the Monin–Obukhov fluxes of the skin at the start of the step. The fluxes a soil column returns are its linear extrapolation in $T_{skin}$ to the step's mean skin temperature. Near the maximum of the concave $H(T_{skin})$ this extrapolation overshoots by $O(ΔT^2)$: up to 3.1 % in the smoke run, on shaded street columns with $U ≈ 0.6$ m/s and $H_{max} ≈ 1$ W m⁻². Those fluxes are checked against 10 % in the 12-step smoke run, and the measured worst case is reported.

That bound does not hold over a full day. In the 06:00→03:00 flagship run the delivered ratio reached 402 in the first half hour and 2684 by the evening. Instrumenting the worst columns showed why: soil columns at near-calm wind ($U ≈ 0.1$–0.3 m/s), where $H_{max} ∝ U^3$ is only 0.004–0.07 W m⁻². At the start of the step MOST gives an upward buoyancy flux (ratio −15 to −72, evaporation). The linearised step then delivers dew ($LE ≈ −10$ W m⁻²) and $H ≈ −1.5$ W m⁻², a small downward buoyancy flux that is many times the tiny $H_{max}$. The excess is a few W m⁻² in absolute terms. It is a property of the soil skin's linearisation in $T_{skin}$ and $q_s$, not of MOST, whose own fluxes stay at or below $H_{max}$ (worst 1.000) all day. The diagnostic exposes it; it does not fix it.

The boundary-layer height

boundary_layer_height(z, profile; method) is the only $z_i$ diagnostic in the package (#247). It is applied in the same way to radiosonde profiles (LargeScaleForcing.sounding_profiles) and to LES output: the CBL harness (tke_cbl, test/fixtures/tke_cbl.jl; the configuration examples/cbl_tke/) delegates to it, and so do the diagnostics of the nesting epic (#246). It offers four methods, following the review of Seibert et al. (2000):

  • :parcel, the parcel method (Holzworth 1964). $z_i$ is the first height at which $θ_v(z) = θ_v(z_1) + \text{excess}$, linearly interpolated between levels. On a profile that is stable from the ground it returns $z_1$.
  • :bulk_richardson, the bulk Richardson number from the lowest level, $Ri_b(z) = (g/θ_{\rm ref})\,Δθ_v\,Δz / (Δu^2 + Δv^2 + b u_*^2)$, with $Ri_c = 0.25$ and $b = 100$ (Vogelezang & Holtslag 1996). It is evaluated as gradient_richardson(N², S²) of the layer $[z_1, z]$, so the shear floor of the previous section also covers a calm profile.
  • :max_gradient, the midpoint of the layer with the largest $∂θ/∂z$.
  • :min_flux, the height of the minimum of the total heat flux $\overline{w'θ'}$ (Sullivan et al. 1998).

Measured.

  • On a mixed layer with a 3 K jump at $h$ = 800 m, sampled every 25 m, every profile method returns $h$ to within one level.
  • On a linear stable profile :parcel returns the first level.
  • On the 24³ CBL of the TKE harness (1 h), :min_flux reproduces the former local zf[argmin(flux)] bit for bit (900 m), and :parcel on the mean θ gives 847 m (−5.9 %).
  • At Lindenberg at 12 UTC on 29 June 2019 (released 10:45 UTC), :parcel and :bulk_richardson both give 1171 m and :max_gradient gives 1149 m. Beyrich & Leps (2012) give a monthly-mean 12 UTC mixing height of 1200–1400 m for early summer at Lindenberg.
  • The 00/06 UTC launches show surface-based inversions 79–329 m deep. The GTS significant levels stored in IGRA do not resolve a nocturnal boundary layer, so the test compares this inversion depth, not a night $z_i$.

In the Berlin case

The turbulence_diag addon writes the plane-mean $N^2$, $Ri_g$ and $f_m$ profiles at every output record. See The Berlin case, which also gives the measured values in the 60 m capping inversion of the smoke case.
