Facet temperatures coupled to radiosity

The Era III radiosity solver (src/Radiosity.jl) resolves every roof, wall strip and street patch of a city block. The Era I urban example still averaged its solution back onto one surface temperature per grid column. A column with a street, the lower half of one facade and the upper half of another therefore had one temperature for all of them. UrbanFacetScene (src/UrbanSurface.jl) removes that approximation. Facet $i$ of the radiosity scene is also facet $i$ of the surface energy balance. It has its own conduction column (Urban facet conduction) and its own skin temperature, and it exchanges longwave with every other facet directly.

1. Longwave exchange is linear in the emission

For grey Lambertian facets with view factors $F_{ij}$, sky-view factors $F_{sky,i}$ and sky irradiance $L_{sky}$, the incident longwave $Q$ and the radiosity $B = E + (1-\varepsilon)Q$ satisfy $Q = FB + F_{sky}L_{sky}$. Eliminating $B$,

\[Q = \bigl(I - F\,\mathrm{diag}(1-\varepsilon)\bigr)^{-1}\bigl(F E + F_{sky} L_{sky}\bigr),\]

so the net longwave a facet absorbs, $N = \mathrm{diag}(\varepsilon)Q - E$, is exactly linear in the emission $E_j = \varepsilon_j\sigma T_j^4$:

\[N_i = \sum_j J_{ij} E_j + j^{sky}_i L_{sky},\qquad J = \mathrm{diag}(\varepsilon)\bigl(I - F\,\mathrm{diag}(1-\varepsilon)\bigr)^{-1}F - I .\]

Radiosity.longwave_response returns $J$ and $j^{sky}$. They depend on geometry and emissivity only, so they are computed once and reused until the emissivities change. $J$ inherits two properties from the view factors:

  • Reciprocity of total exchange areas (Hottel & Sarofim 1967): $A_i J_{ij}\varepsilon_j = A_j J_{ji}\varepsilon_i$ for $i \ne j$.
  • Closure: $\sum_i A_i J_{ij} = -A_j\,f^{esc}_j$, where $f^{esc}_j$ is the fraction of $j$'s emission that escapes to the sky. In an enclosure it is zero: extra emission by one facet is absorbed, in full, by the others.

2. The implicit coupled step

The only nonlinearity in the exchange is $T^4$. Linearising it about the current skin temperatures, $E_j \approx E_j^n + 4\varepsilon_j\sigma (T_j^n)^3 x_j$, the energy balance of facet $i$ becomes

\[R_{n,i} - H_i - LE_i \approx F_{0,i} + F'_{ii}\,x_i + \sum_{j\ne i} G_{ij}\,x_j, \qquad G_{ij} = J_{ij}\,4\varepsilon_j\sigma (T_j^n)^3 .\]

Here $x$ are the θ-weighted skin increments of the step. $F'_{ii}$ combines the radiative $G_{ii}$ with the finite-difference turbulent derivatives, exactly as for an isolated facet. The diagonal part is the Robin condition that closes each facet column. The off-diagonal part couples all the columns. Each column step is affine in an extra flux $c_i$ imposed on its outer face, $x_i = a_i + b_i c_i$, so two trial steps per facet give $a$ and $b$. The coupled increments then solve one linear system,

\[\bigl(I - \mathrm{diag}(b)\,G_{\text{off}}\bigr)\,x = a ,\]

and the columns are advanced with $c_i = \sum_{j\ne i}G_{ij}x_j$. The fluxes returned are those applied: per facet, $R_n - H - LE = G$, and the stored heat changes by $(G - Q_\text{interior})\Delta t$ to round-off. The longwave actually applied, lw_net, equals the radiosity solution for the applied emission lw_emission. Shortwave does not depend on temperature and comes from Radiosity.solve_shortwave.

Albedo and emissivity have one source: the scene's UrbanMaterials. They are copied into the facets at construction and again at every step.

Solving it at city scale (#138)

The system has $n$ unknowns ($n + m$ with building zones, below), and $G$ is dense: every facet sees every other. Assembling it and LU-factorising it costs $O(n^3)$ per step. That is nothing for the 336-facet canyon of §4, but it does not scale to a city. The Berlin (Alexanderplatz) scene has 5810 facets with wall_subdivisions = 1 and 7524 with 2. There the dense step took 4.5 s and 9.3 s on one BLAS thread: half in the LU of this system and half in the LU inside solve_longwave.

The default solver = :iterative needs neither factorisation.

  • The longwave at the start of the step is exactly linear in the emission, so it is one product with the response the scene already holds, $N = J E + j^{sky} L_{sky}$ (Radiosity.solve_longwave(scene, vf, response)).
  • The coupled system is solved matrix-free by restarted GMRES (Saad & Schultz 1986). Each iteration is one product $G v = J\,(\partial E/\partial T \circ v)$, so it costs $O(n^2)$ and needs $O(n)$ extra memory. $G$ itself is never formed.
  • Building zones (below) are eliminated first. Given the exchange $c$, each zone's balance is linear in its own unknown, so the GMRES system stays in the facet increments alone.

The system has a unit diagonal and is strongly diagonally dominant. $b_i$ is the skin warming per W m⁻² of imposed flux, and $\sum_{j\ne i}|G_{ij}|$ is the longwave conductance to the other facets. In the canyon $\max_i b_i \sum_{j\ne i}|G_{ij}| = 0.084$ at 900 s, and GMRES converges to a relative residual of 10⁻¹² in 6–7 iterations.

The one $O(n^3)$ operation left is longwave_response. It depends only on geometry and emissivity, so it is paid once, and again only if the emissivities change. It takes 9 s at 5810 facets and 18 s at 7524, on one thread. The response $J$ is the memory floor: 8n² bytes, 258 MB and 432 MB.

solver = :direct keeps the dense LU as the reference.

What remains of the step is the per-facet trial column steps, 0.18 s and 0.23 s, which scale as $O(n)$. With 8 BLAS threads the 5810-facet step takes 0.22 s (GMRES) against 0.89 s (dense).

3. Why a lagged coupling is not enough

The simple alternative steps each facet on its own against the incident longwave frozen at the start of the step (coupling = :lagged, which is kept only as a reference). A facet that warms during the step emits more, and that extra emission leaves it. But the other facets never receive it, because their incident longwave was frozen. The exchange then creates or destroys energy in proportion to $\Delta t$ and the temperature change. The last two rows are the capability this adds. After a 12-hour night in the 3-D canyon, the lowest wall strips (sky-view factor 0.083) are at 291.24 K and the top strips (0.402) are at 289.61 K. A single column temperature cannot hold that difference. A step of the 336-facet scene costs 6–10 ms (machine load varies), almost all of it in the trial column steps. The coupled solve takes 0.09 ms with GMRES and 0.9 ms with the dense LU.

Steady-state tests use θ = 1. A steady state does not depend on θ. But at $\Delta t \gg \Delta z^2/\kappa$, Crank–Nicolson's amplification factor for the stiffest cell modes tends to −1, so those modes ring for hundreds of steps. With θ = ½ the plate test was still 1·10⁻⁶ K off after 400 steps.

Validation against observations is on BUBBLE / CAPITOUL validation. The air state near each facet is an input here. The canyon air temperature is not modelled. The canyon wind and the wall convection coefficient can come from the TEB closures canyon_wind_speed and rowley_convection_coefficient (#30).

Buildings behind the facets (#29). The implicit step is built from three pieces: trial column steps, one coupled solve, and the final column steps. A facet can also be bound to an interior node, the air of the building zone behind it. A third trial step then measures the column's response to that node's temperature, and the zones' air temperatures join the coupled solve. The GMRES path eliminates them exactly, as in §2. BuildingEnergy.step_buildings! uses this; see Anthropogenic heat from the building energy model. Without nodes the system is the one above, and every number in §4 is unchanged.
