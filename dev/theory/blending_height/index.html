Blending height

A tiled grid cell (Sub-grid surface tiles) still has to decide which air each tile exchanges with. Over one patch, the flow near the ground adjusts to that patch inside an internal boundary layer (IBL) that deepens with fetch. Higher up, the IBLs of neighbouring patches merge. Above the blending height $l_b$ the flow is horizontally uniform and has one Monin–Obukhov profile, which carries the area-averaged flux (Wieringa 1986; Mason 1988; Claussen 1991). The blending-height formulation in src/SurfaceTiles.jl has three parts:

  1. an effective roughness at $l_b$;
  2. closures for $l_b$ and IBL growth laws;
  3. BlendedMosaic, whose tiles are forced with the state at $l_b$.

1. Effective roughness

Below $l_b$ each tile has its own neutral log profile. At $l_b$ the wind $U_b$ is common to all tiles. Tile $i$ therefore feels the stress $ρ[κU_b/\ln(l_b/z_{0,i})]^2$. The cell stress equals that of one surface of roughness $z_{0,eff}$ if

\[\frac{1}{\ln^2(l_b/z_{0,eff})} = \sum_i \frac{f_i}{\ln^2(l_b/z_{0,i})} .\]

Because $1/\ln^2$ is convex, $z_{0,eff}$ lies above the logarithmic mean $\exp\overline{\ln z_0}$ and below $\max z_{0,i}$. It rises as $l_b$ falls, so smaller patches make a rougher cell. As $l_b → ∞$ it tends to the log average of Taylor (1987), and does so only logarithmically:

\[\ln z_{0,eff} = \overline{\ln z_0} + \tfrac32\,\frac{\mathrm{Var}(\ln z_0)}{\ln(l_b/z_0)} + O(\ln^{-2}),\]

which follows from expanding $(a - x_i)^{-2}$ for $a = \ln l_b$ large. The tests measure the ratio of the actual gap to this leading term at 0.917, 0.949, 0.971 and 0.984 for $l_b = 10^2, 10^4, 10^8, 10^{16}$ m ($z_0$ = 0.01 / 0.1 m).

For heat and moisture the same construction is applied to the neutral transfer coefficient $κ^2/[\ln(l_b/z_{0m})\ln(l_b/z_{0h})]$. This gives effective_scalar_roughness, which is exact when every tile has the same surface-to-$l_b$ scalar difference.

Mason's closure asks where $(u_*/U(l_b))^2$ equals the aspect ratio $l_b/(2L)$. It gives $l_b ≈ L/100$. Bou-Zeid's closure follows diffusion-analogy IBLs over about twice the patch length and gives heights ten times larger. Claussen's closure is Bou-Zeid's without the $-1$.

Note on the Claussen closure. Claussen (1991) was not consulted directly. Bou-Zeid et al. (2007, Sect. 6) state that Claussen's $z_{0,e}$ equation differs from theirs only by the $-1$, which fixes the linear-in-$\ln$ form used here. Kramm & Dlugi (2009, Eq. 5.5), the source of $C_b ≅ 1.75$, print the logarithm squared instead, $(l_b/L)\ln^2(l_b/z_0) ≅ C_b κ$; read literally, that gives a Mason-like $l_b$ (50 m instead of 265 m for case S25R75 below). The constant should be treated as uncertain.

The IBL growth laws are the neutral short-fetch formulae compiled by Savelyev & Taylor (2005, Table I):

  • ElliottIBL: Elliott (1958), $δ/z_{0D} = (0.75 - 0.03\ln(z_{0D}/z_{0U}))(x/z_{0D})^{0.8}$.
  • PowerLawIBL: Wood (1982), $δ/z_{0r} = 0.28(x/z_{0r})^{0.8}$ with $z_{0r} = \max(z_{0U}, z_{0D})$.
  • DiffusionIBL: the Panofsky & Dutton (1984) diffusion analogy, $1.25κx/z_{0D} = (δ/z_{0D})(\ln(δ/z_{0D}) - 1) + 1$. It is solved by Newton's method in $\ln δ$ and has the growth rate $dδ/dx = 1.25κ/\ln(δ/z_{0D})$.

3. Tiles forced at the blending height

blended_forcing(mosaic, forcing, l_b) finds the wind $U_b$, potential temperature $θ_b$ and humidity $q_b$ at $l_b$ that satisfy three conditions together:

  • each tile exchanges with $(U_b, θ_b, q_b)$ through its own surface layer of depth $l_b$, using the tile's own turbulent_fluxes, so its own $z_0$, skin state and stability;
  • the aggregate $\sum f_i F_i$ defines $u_*$, $θ_*$ and $q_*$ and an Obukhov length;
  • one Monin–Obukhov layer between $l_b$ and the forcing height $z$ carries that flux: $U(z) - U_b = (u_*/κ)[\ln(z/l_b) - ψ_m(z/L) + ψ_m(l_b/L)]$, and likewise for $θ$ and $q$ with $ψ_h$.

The three residuals are solved by a damped Newton iteration with a finite-difference Jacobian. BlendedMosaic wraps a SurfaceMosaic with a closure for $l_b$. It is an AbstractSurface: each step_surface! first solves for the blended state and then steps every tile with it. Its roughness_lengths are the blending-height effective lengths.

Exact limits (tested).

  • With $ψ ≡ 0$ and a common surface-to-$l_b$ difference, the blended cell reproduces one surface of $z_{0m,eff}$, $z_{0h,eff}$, $z_{0q,eff}$ forced at $z$. Measured: $5.4\times10^{-11}$.
  • A single tile split at $l_b$ gives the same kinematic fluxes as the tile forced at $z$. Measured: $3.8\times10^{-11}$ with $ψ ≡ 0$.
  • For $l_b ≥ z$ there is no blending, and blended_forcing returns the plain forcing.
  • As $l_b → z^-$ the result tends continuously to the plain mosaic.

Where the split is not exact. The SurfaceExchange solve refers buoyancy to $θ_v$ at its forcing level, which is $l_b$ for a tile and $z$ for the direct solve. That $O(Δθ/θ)$ ambiguity in $L$ leaves a single-tile split error, and stability amplifies it:

casesingle-tile split error
unstable and near-neutral (BDBH)$5.5\times10^{-4}$
strongly stable (skin 285 K, air 293 K)$2.5\times10^{-2}$

Fixing $θ_{v,ref}$ to a constant in both solves, as a temporary experiment, reduced these errors to $3\times10^{-11}$ and $10^{-13}$. The ambiguity is therefore the whole of the difference. Separately, the default β hook forms $q_s$ from $q_a$ at the forcing level, so a tile with $0 < β < 1$ evaporates differently when it is forced at $l_b$. A tile with a resistance formulation does not have this problem.

Conservation. A BlendedMosaic inherits the conservation of the mosaic. Over a 24 h cycle of two slab tiles with 288 steps, all 288 blended solves converged. $\sum f_i C_i ΔT_i$ matched $\sum \overline{G}\,Δt$ to $4.1\times10^{-14}$.
