Sea spray and the full COARE algorithm

Phase 52, issue #40. This page covers three additions to AirSeaFlux: the spray-mediated heat fluxes of Andreas et al. (2015), the complete COARE algorithm (bulk fluxes with cool skin, warm layer and rain), and the coupling of COARE fluxes to the prognostic OceanMixedLayer water column. The page ends with what these were validated against. The bulk algorithm itself (roughness, stability, gustiness) is described in Air–sea fluxes and wave-dependent roughness. The cool skin and warm layer are described in Water surfaces (mixed layer).

1. Spray-mediated heat transfer

Heat and moisture leave the sea by two routes (Andreas et al. 2008, 2015):

  • The interfacial route is controlled by molecular diffusion at the interface. It is the route every bulk algorithm computes.
  • The spray route runs through droplets torn from wave crests. It becomes significant, meaning at least 10 % of the interfacial flux, above about 10–12 m s⁻¹.

A spume droplet cools to its equilibrium temperature $T_{eq}$ within seconds, and the heat it gives up comes from the ocean. It evaporates more slowly, on the time scale $τ_r$, and the latent heat for that evaporation comes from the air. The droplet falls back into the sea after a residence time

\[τ_f = \frac{H_{1/3}}{2\,u_f(r_0)},\]

where $u_f$ is its terminal speed. During that flight its radius relaxes towards the equilibrium radius:

\[r(t) = r_{eq} + (r_0 - r_{eq})\, e^{-t/τ_r}.\]

The fast algorithm represents all droplet sizes by two bellwether droplets: 50 µm for the latent flux and 100 µm for the sensible flux. Their wind functions $V_L$ and $V_S$ were fitted to about 4000 eddy-covariance flux measurements:

\[H_{L,sp} = ρ_w L_v \left[1 - \left(\frac{r(τ_{f,50})}{50\,\mu m}\right)^3\right] V_L(u_*), \qquad H_{s,sp} = ρ_w c_w (Θ_s - T_{eq,100})\, V_S(u_*),\]

\[V_L = 2.08\times10^{-7} u_*^{2.39},\qquad V_S = 5.02\times10^{-6} u_*^{2.54} \quad (\text{constant below } u_* ≈ 0.14\ \text{m s}^{-1}).\]

$H_{s,sp}$ already includes the cooling of the air by droplet evaporation, the $-(α-γ)Q_L$ term of the full model. The total fluxes that leave the droplet evaporation layer are the interfacial fluxes plus the spray fluxes. As in Andreas et al., the spray does not feed back on the stability.

Microphysics comes from what the package already has. Andreas used his own approximation formulas (Andreas 2005); those are not reproduced here. Instead:

  • $T_{eq}$ is the saline wet-bulb temperature: $k_a(T_a - T) = L_v D_v [a_w e_s(T)/(R_vT) - RH\, e_s(T_a)/(R_vT_a)]$.
    • The water activity $a_w$ includes solute and curvature and comes from the κ-Köhler curve in MoistThermodynamics, with the sea-salt κ of Superdroplet.
    • $k_a$ and $D_v$ come from Superdroplet.
  • $r_{eq}$ is the stable κ-Köhler equilibrium radius from MoistThermodynamics.kohler_equilibrium_radius.
  • $τ_r$ follows Andreas's (1990) definition: the time at which $(r - r_{eq})/(r_0 - r_{eq}) = 1/e$. It is found by integrating $r\,dr/dt = (RH - 1 - s_{eq}(r))/(F_k + F_d)$ with RK4, using Superdroplet's $F_k$ and $F_d$.
  • $u_f$ is the Beard terminal velocity from Superdroplet.
  • $ρ_w$ is the EOS-80 seawater density from OceanMixedLayer.

Wave height. $H_{1/3}$ comes from a SeaState when one is given. Without one, a fully developed sea is assumed (Taylor & Yelland 2001: $H_s = 0.0248\,U_{10N}^2$). Andreas et al. did not use that relation: they used measured wave heights where available, and otherwise the depth-dependent coastal fit of Andreas & Wang (2007). That fit is not implemented, because its coefficients could not be obtained. The latent spray flux is sensitive to this choice (see §4).

Drag. Andreas et al. tuned $V_L$ and $V_S$ against the $u_*$ of their own drag relation (Andreas et al. 2012), which andreas_friction_velocity provides. coare_fluxes drives the spray with the COARE $u_*$ instead. That $u_*$ is 1–8 % larger between 5 and 25 m s⁻¹ (11 % at 30 m s⁻¹), which raises the spray fluxes by roughly 2.5 times as much, since $V_L, V_S ∝ u_*^{2.4–2.5}$.

2. Rain heat flux

Rain falls at roughly the wet-bulb temperature (Gosnell et al. 1995; Fairall et al. 1996b, eq. 23):

\[H_{rain} = R\,c_w\,α\left[(T_s - T_a) + (q_s - q_a)\frac{L_v}{c_p}\right],\qquad α = \left[1 + \frac{L_v}{c_p}\frac{dq_{sat}}{dT}\frac{D_v}{κ_T}\right]^{-1}.\]

Here $R$ is the rain mass flux and $κ_T$ the thermal diffusivity of air. The COARE reference codes evaluate $dq/dT$ with the air's actual humidity. The wet-bulb derivation calls for $q_{sat}$, which is what is used here. On the COARE 3.6 test data (same c_w) this makes the flux 11–22 % smaller than the reference.

3. The full COARE algorithm

coare_fluxes is COARE as the reference codes run it. Every piece is delegated to its owner:

  • Interface temperature: $T_{skin} = T_{sea} + ΔT_{warm} - ΔT_{cool}$. $T_{sea}$ is the bulk water temperature at the sensor depth.
  • Seawater humidity: $q_s = (1 - 0.02S/34)\,q_{sat}(T_{skin})$, from OceanMixedLayer.salinity_humidity_factor.
  • Interfacial fluxes: air_sea_fluxes.
  • Cool skin: OceanMixedLayer.cool_skin, iterated with the fluxes until $ΔT_{cool}$ changes by less than $10^{-6}$ K. The cool skin sees only the interfacial fluxes, because spray heat leaves with the droplets.
  • Rain and spray are added to the fluxes.

coare_warm_layer_series is the warm-layer time loop of coare36vnWarm_et:

  • The Fairall warm layer is stepped with the current net shortwave and the previous record's heat loss and stress.
  • It starts on the first local morning and is reset at local midnight.
  • The warming between the surface and the sensor depth is passed to coare_fluxes.

4. Coupling to the water column

OceanMixedLayer.WaterColumn takes a flux hook. Given the column's skin temperature (top layer + warm layer − cool skin), coare_flux_hook:

  • computes COARE fluxes with wave-dependent roughness, and stores that roughness in the column so a tile aggregator sees it;
  • adds spray and rain heat;
  • returns $G = R_n - H - LE - H_{rain}$, which the column absorbs exactly. The column's heat budget therefore stays closed.

One limitation: the column computes its own cool skin from the H and LE it is given, so with spray the skin also "sees" the spray heat. coare_fluxes keeps the two apart.

Spray vs Andreas et al. (2015). The comparison values were digitised from Figs. 9–11 of the extended abstract (AMS 21st Symp. on Boundary Layers and Turbulence, 2B.3), the conference version of the QJRMS paper; the figure numbers refer to that document. They are the authors' model curves, not data.

  • Sensible spray flux (independent of wave height): within 8.7 % at all 14 points above 50 W m⁻². The model is systematically about 8 % high, partly from the fresh-water c_w.
  • Latent spray flux: needs $H_{1/3}$, which the figures do not give. With one $H_{1/3}$ (7.3 m at 25 m s⁻¹) calibrated at RH = 90 %, the other six RH points agree to within 23 W m⁻². With a fully developed sea (15.5 m), the latent flux is 1.76 times Fig. 11.
  • Sign change of the latent spray flux: at RH = 98.24 %, against "about 98.1 %" in the paper. Here a_w comes from the sea-salt κ = 1.10.
  • Near saturation (RH > 99 %) the exponential radius law with the e-folding $τ_r$ breaks down, because $r_{eq}$ grows to millimetres: $H_{L,sp}$ returns to 0 at RH = 100 % (−12 W m⁻² at 99.5 %), while Fig. 11 stays near −20 W m⁻². Supersaturated air is treated as RH = 100 %.
  • Crossover winds, where spray equals the interfacial flux, with COARE interfacial fluxes: 21.0 m s⁻¹ for sensible heat and 27.75 m s⁻¹ for latent heat, against the paper's ≈ 19 and ≈ 26.
