Resolved plant canopy: leaf area, drag, radiation and leaf temperature

A resolved canopy is vegetation that occupies several model layers, so that the LES sees the leaves as a distributed momentum sink rather than as a roughness length. This page covers what src/PlantCanopy.jl implements for Phase 48: the vertical distribution of leaf area, the drag it exerts, treated implicitly so it is stable at any time step, and the attenuation of shortwave and longwave radiation through it (section 4), the temperature of the leaves themselves from their energy balance (section 5), and the within-canopy wind and air temperature profiles that result, compared with a wind tunnel, an orchard and a forest tower (section 6).

1. Leaf-area density

The leaf-area density $a(z)$ [m² m⁻³] is one-sided leaf area per unit volume; its column integral is the leaf-area index, $\mathrm{LAI} = \int_0^h a\,dz$. Three profiles are provided, each normalised to its LAI by construction:

Profile$a(z)$Use
UniformLAD(LAI, h; z_base)$\mathrm{LAI}/(h - z_{base})$ on the crownanalytic theory, idealised cases
LalicMihailovicLAD(LAI, h; z_m)$L_m r^n e^{n(1-r)}$, $r = \frac{h-z_m}{h-z}$, $n = 6$ below $z_m$, $1/2$ aboveforests (Lalic & Mihailovic 2004)
BetaLAD(LAI, h; p, q)$\frac{\mathrm{LAI}}{h}\frac{x^{p-1}(1-x)^{q-1}}{B(p,q)}$, $x = z/h$flexible crown shapes (Markkanen et al. 2003)
TabulatedLAD(z, a; LAI)linear between measured nodes, optionally rescaled to a quoted LAImeasured profiles (section 6)

For Lalic–Mihailovic the peak $L_m = a(z_m)$ is solved for so that the profile integrates to the given LAI.

On a model grid the LAD of a layer is its layer mean $a_k = Δz_k^{-1}\int_{z_k}^{z_{k+1}} a\,dz$ (layer_leaf_area_density), not a point sample. With the urban example's $Δz = 6.25$ m and a 20 m canopy the top layer spans 18.75–25 m; centre sampling puts no leaves there and loses 6 % of the LAI, the layer integral loses none. The integrals use Gauss–Legendre quadrature graded towards the ends of each smooth piece, which keeps the $(1-x)^{q-1}$ end of a beta profile with non-integer $q$ at round-off accuracy.

The leaf area above a height, $Λ(z) = \int_z^h a\,dz'$ (cumulative_leaf_area), is the optical-depth coordinate for canopy radiative transfer.

2. Canopy drag, and why it must be implicit

Form drag on the leaves is (Shaw & Schumann 1992)

\[\frac{\partial U_i}{\partial t} = -C_d\,a\,|U|\,U_i ,\]

with $C_d ≈ 0.15$–$0.3$. Taken alone, the speed obeys $d|U|/dt = -C_d a |U|^2$ and the direction is fixed, so the exact solution over a step $Δt$ from $U^*$ is

\[U^{n+1} = \frac{U^*}{1 + Δt\,C_d\,a\,|U^*|}.\]

This is also backward Euler with the drag coefficient $C_d a |U|$ lagged, but here the lagging costs nothing: for the pure drag equation the factor is exact at any $Δt$. It lies in $(0, 1]$, so the step is unconditionally stable and can never reverse the flow.

Explicit Euler, $U^{n+1} = U^n (1 - Δt\,C_d a |U^n|)$, reverses the flow once $Δt\,C_d a|U| > 1$ and amplifies it once that exceeds 2. For a dense forest ($a ≈ 1$ m⁻¹, $C_d = 0.2$) in a 5 m s⁻¹ wind the limit is $Δt = 2$ s — the urban example's step. The old explicit drag of the urban example shipped disabled after an instability at that step was reported, but in the urban example's own configuration $Δt\,C_d\,\mathrm{LAI}\,|U| ≤ 0.05$ and re-running it for #22 did not reproduce the instability; its cause is undiagnosed. The implicit form removes the stiffness limit either way.

In the LES

apply_canopy_drag!(sim, drag, Δt) runs in the post_predictor! hook, on the predicted velocity $u^*$ before the pressure projection. The three velocity components live on different faces of the Arakawa-C grid. At each face the LAD is the mean of the two adjacent cells, the speed $|U|$ includes the other two components averaged from their four neighbouring faces, and all factors are computed before any is applied. The call returns the momentum removed, $\sum (U^{n+1} - U^*)ΔV/Δt$, which closes the momentum budget exactly.

3. In-canopy wind: the exponential profile

In a deep uniform canopy with a constant mixing length $l$ the steady mean momentum balance

\[\frac{d}{dz}\left(l^2 \left|\frac{dU}{dz}\right|\frac{dU}{dz}\right) = C_d\,a\,U^2\]

has the solution $U = U_h e^{γ(z-h)}$ with $γ = (C_d a / 2l^2)^{1/3}$ (Inoue 1963). Harman & Finnigan (2007) express the same profile through $β = u_*/U_h$ and the canopy-drag length $L_c = 1/(C_d a)$: the mixing length is $l = 2β^3 L_c$ and $U = U_h e^{β(z-h)/l}$, so $γ = β/l$. Substituting shows that the stress at the canopy top $l^2 (dU/dz)^2 = β^2 U_h^2$, i.e. the friction velocity comes back as $β U_h$.

solve_canopy_wind_column solves the balance above on a 1-D column, with $K = l^2|∂U/∂z| + ν$, the wind prescribed at the column top, a free-slip or no-slip ground, and the implicit drag folded into a tridiagonal pseudo-time step. At convergence the discrete stress at the top equals the drag on the leaves plus the ground stress.

Measured against the analytic profile ($h = 20$ m, LAI = 10, $C_d = 0.25$, $β = 0.3$, so $l = 0.432$ m and $γh = 13.9$), compared down to $U = e^{-5} U_h$:

Layersmax rel. error of $U(z)$$u_*/U_h$

What this does not yet establish: agreement with forest-tower or wind-tunnel observations. That is the job of the canopy-profile validation issue (#25), once the radiation and leaf energy balance are in place.

4. Radiation in the canopy

The optical depth of a canopy is its cumulative leaf area above a height, $Λ(z) = \int_z^h a\,dz'$ (cumulative_leaf_area). How much of a beam unit leaf area intercepts depends on leaf orientation through Ross's projection function $G(μ)$; RossGoudriaanLeaves(χ_L) uses Goudriaan's fit $G = φ_1 + φ_2 μ$ (valid for $-0.4 ≤ χ_L ≤ 0.6$; $χ_L = 0$ is the spherical distribution with $G = 1/2$), and HorizontalLeaves() has $G = μ$ exactly.

Beer–Lambert. The unscattered beam from a sun at zenith cosine $μ$ reaches depth $Λ$ with $e^{-KΛ}$, $K = G(μ)/μ$, and that is also the sunlit fraction of the leaves there. For black leaves the exact diffuse transmittance is the hemispheric integral $T_d = 2\int_0^1 e^{-GΛ/μ} μ\,dμ$ (canopy_diffuse_transmittance; $2E_3(Λ/2)$ for spherical leaves).

Two-stream (Sellers 1985). With leaf reflectance $α$, transmittance $τ_l$ and $ω = α + τ_l$,

\[-\bar μ \frac{dI^\uparrow}{dΛ} + [1-(1-β)ω]I^\uparrow - ωβ I^\downarrow = ω\bar μ K β_0 e^{-KΛ},\qquad \bar μ \frac{dI^\downarrow}{dΛ} + [1-(1-β)ω]I^\downarrow - ωβ I^\uparrow = ω\bar μ K (1-β_0) e^{-KΛ},\]

with $\bar μ = \int_0^1 μ/G\,dμ$ and the up-scatter fractions $β$, $β_0$ of the CLM5.0 Technical Note (§2.3.1). Dividing by $\bar μ$ puts this in exactly the Meador–Weaver form solved by the radiation stack's TwoStream module, with optical depth $Λ$, beam cosine $1/K$ and

\[γ_1 = \frac{1-(1-β)ω}{\bar μ},\quad γ_2 = \frac{ωβ}{\bar μ},\quad γ_3 = β_0,\quad γ_4 = 1-β_0 .\]

canopy_shortwave therefore does not re-implement the two-stream: it supplies these coefficients as a TwoStreamVariant and calls layer_reflectance_transmittance_sw and adding_solver! (which gained a F_dif_top keyword for the sky diffuse entering at the canopy top). The ground enters as the lower boundary albedo. Because the equations depend on height only through $Λ$, the solution does not depend on how the leaf area is split into layers.

Sunlit and shaded leaves. A layer between $Λ_t$ and $Λ_b$ holds $(e^{-KΛ_t} - e^{-KΛ_b})/K$ of sunlit leaf area. Sunlit leaves absorb the fraction $1-ω$ of the beam they intercept; the diffuse absorption of the layer (sky diffuse plus scattered beam) is shared in proportion to leaf area (de Pury & Farquhar 1997).

Longwave. canopy_longwave treats each layer as a slab with leaf reflectance $1 - ε_{leaf}$, takes $R$ and $T$ from the same two-stream layer formulae, emits $(1-R-T)σT_{leaf}^4$ in each direction (Kirchhoff) and solves the coupled layer–ground system exactly.

canopy_shortwave_field! applies the column solution to each grid column of a resolved LES canopy (independent-column approximation) and returns the absorbed shortwave per cell in W m⁻³.

Two limitations are worth stating with numbers.

Angular closure. The two-stream extinguishes diffuse light at $1/\bar μ$ per unit leaf area, which for spherical leaves is 1. The exact black-leaf diffuse transmittance is larger: at $Λ = 1$ it is 0.443 against the two-stream's 0.368, and at $Λ = 3$ 0.114 against 0.050. Spherical-leaf canopy albedo. For spherical leaves with $σ = 0.15$ (PAR) over a deep canopy the two-stream albedo is 0.0454, 0.0338, 0.0254 at $μ = 0.3, 0.6, 1$; the widely used approximation $ρ_{cb} = 1 - e^{-2ρ_h K/(1+K)}$ gives 0.0495, 0.0362, 0.0267, i.e. the two differ by 5–8 %.

5. Leaf energy balance and leaf temperature

Leaves are not at air temperature. A sunlit leaf absorbs several hundred W m⁻² and can only lose it by emission, by conduction through its boundary layer, and by transpiration; the balance sets how far it sits above the air. Per unit (one-sided) leaf area:

\[C\,\frac{dT_\ell}{dt} = R_{abs} - ε_e σ T_\ell^4 - c_p g_H (T_\ell - T_a) - λ E, \qquad E = \frac{g_s g_v}{g_s + g_v}\,\frac{e_s(T_\ell) - e_a}{p}.\]

$R_{abs}$ is the absorbed short- and longwave radiation, $ε_e$ the emission coefficient ($2ε$ for a flat leaf, which emits from both sides), $c_p$ the molar heat capacity of air, $g_H$ the boundary-layer conductance to heat of both leaf sides and $g_v$ that to water vapour of the sides with stomata, $g_s$ the stomatal conductance, all in mol m⁻² s⁻¹ (Campbell & Norman 1998, ch. 14). $e_s$ and $λ$ come from MoistThermodynamics, the transpiration and the coupled photosynthesis–stomata solution from Photosynthesis: with CoupledLeafPhysiology the stomata, the biochemistry and the energy balance all run at the same leaf temperature and see the same boundary layer.

Boundary layer. CampbellNormanBoundaryLayer uses Campbell & Norman's flat-plate conductances per leaf side, forced $g_{Ha} = 0.135\sqrt{u/d}$, $g_{va} = 0.147\sqrt{u/d}$ (× 1.4 for outdoor turbulence, $d = 0.72 ×$ leaf width), and free convection $g_{Ha} = 0.05\,(ΔT/d)^{1/4}$, taking the larger. These coefficients are the laminar flat-plate correlations ($\overline{Nu} = 0.664\,Re^{1/2}Pr^{1/3}$ forced, $0.54\,Ra^{1/4}$ free; Incropera et al. 2007) evaluated for air at 20 °C: the test re-derives them from tabulated air properties to within 1.2 % (forced) and 2.8 % (free). CLMLeafBoundaryLayer is CLM5's $C_v\sqrt{U/d}$ (delegated to Photosynthesis). For a 4 cm leaf in 1 m s⁻¹ wind the two give 1.89 and 2.08 mol m⁻² s⁻¹ for heat.

Solution. The equation is scalar in $T_\ell$; it is solved on a bracket with the Illinois method of SurfaceExchange. With a heat capacity $C$ and a step $Δt$ it is a backward-Euler step, stable at any $Δt$; $Δt → ∞$ is the diagnostic (steady) balance. For a small departure the leaf relaxes with $τ = C/[c_p(g_H + g_r) + λ g_w s/p]$, about 14 s for $C = 1.3$ kJ m⁻² K⁻¹ at 1 m s⁻¹, so over an LES time step of seconds a leaf is close to, but not at, equilibrium.

Penman–Monteith limit. Linearising $σT^4$ (radiative conductance $g_r = 4ε_eσT_a^3/c_p$) and $e_s$ (slope $s$) gives

\[T_\ell - T_a = \frac{R_{ni} - λ g_w D/p}{c_p (g_H + g_r) + λ g_w s/p}, \qquad R_{ni} = R_{abs} - ε_eσT_a^4,\]

which is leaf_penman_monteith. The exact balance approaches it quadratically: at $R_{ni} = 300$ W m⁻², $D = 1$ kPa the difference is 0.028 K in 2.24 K, and it drops by 4.00 ± 0.01 per halving of the forcing.

In the canopy. canopy_leaf_energy_balance solves a sunlit and a shaded leaf class in every layer. The shortwave is canopy_shortwave's sunlit/shaded absorption (any number of bands), the PAR band is turned into photons for photosynthesis, and the longwave is canopy_longwave with each layer at the leaf-area-weighted $T^4$ of its two classes. Because the leaves exchange longwave radiation with each other, the layers are coupled; an outer iteration holds each layer's absorbed longwave fixed while the classes solve their own balances with their own emission $ε_e = 2(1 - R_k - T_k)/ΔΛ_k$ (the two-stream layer emissivity, $2ε_{leaf}$ for thin layers of spherical leaves), and repeats until no temperature moves by more than 1e-9 K (about 20 iterations). The sensible heat and transpiration of each layer, divided by its depth, are the heat (W m⁻³) and vapour (kg m⁻³ s⁻¹) sources for the air.

Comparison with observations

Leuzinger & Körner (2007, Table 1) measured canopy leaf temperatures with a thermal camera from the Swiss Canopy Crane (35 m deciduous forest, 47.5° N) on 18 July 2004, 11:30 true local time, clear sky, ample soil water, 25.0 °C, VPD 19.7 hPa. The broad-leaved species were on average 1.7 (Prunus), 2.4 (Quercus), 4.5 (Carpinus), 4.6 (Fagus) and 4.8 K (Tilia) above the air, with crown maxima 6.4–12.0 K above it. The paper does not report the radiation components or the wind, so the model forcing is an assumption: the solar geometry of the date and time (cos θ = 0.888), 780 + 120 W m⁻² direct + diffuse, LAI 5 (the paper's own assumption), 45 mm leaves, CLM5 broadleaf-deciduous Medlyn stomata and a canopy-top wind of 0.5–2 m s⁻¹. The leaf temperature seen from above (layers weighted by their visible leaf area) is then 3.4, 2.6 and 1.9 K above the air for $U_h$ = 0.5, 1 and 2 m s⁻¹, inside the observed range of species means, and the warmest sunlit leaves are 8.7, 8.2 and 7.5 K above it, inside the observed range of crown maxima. At 4 m s⁻¹ the view mean falls to 1.4 K, below the range. This is a check of magnitude, not a validation: the wind and radiation were not measured with the temperatures, and the ranges span several species.

Ansari & Loomis (1959), as summarised by Leuzinger & Körner, found sunlit thin leaves 6–10 K above the air in still air and 3–5 K in moderate wind (5 m s⁻¹). For a horizontal 5 cm leaf under a 1000 W m⁻² overhead sun (absorptance 0.5, assumed), VPD 2 kPa and $g_s = 0.2$–$0.4$ mol m⁻² s⁻¹, the model gives 9.1–7.7 K in still air (free convection only), inside the first range. At 5 m s⁻¹ it gives 1.05 K, below the second range. The boundary-layer conductance at 5 m s⁻¹ is 4.5 mol m⁻² s⁻¹, and 3–5 K would need either a much smaller conductance or far less transpiration than assumed. The test does not assert this second figure.

Limitations

  • The Campbell & Norman coefficients are for 20 °C and 101.3 kPa; their weak dependence on temperature and pressure is neglected.
  • Free and forced convection are not combined; the larger one is used.
  • Dew and intercepted water on leaves are not modelled; a negative $E$ goes through the stomatal path only. Wet leaves belong to the land column (#21).
  • In a prognostic step each class keeps its own previous temperature. When the sun moves, the leaves that change class carry their old class's temperature, which is not tracked. The column's energy is conserved exactly only while the class leaf areas do not change.
  • Air temperature, humidity and wind are inputs per layer; the feedback of the leaf sources on the in-canopy air is the LES's job, or, in one dimension, that of the scalar column of section 6.

6. Within-canopy profiles and their validation

The 1-D column

The steady mean wind of a horizontally homogeneous canopy is solved by solve_canopy_wind_column (section 3) with the mixing length canopy_mixing_length:

\[l(z) = \min(l_c,\ κz)\quad (z ≤ h),\qquad l(z) = l_c + κ(z - h)\quad (z > h), \qquad l_c = 2β^3 \frac{h}{C_d\,\mathrm{LAI}},\ β = 0.3,\]

the Harman & Finnigan (2007) canopy length, limited by its wall value $κz$ near the ground and growing at the surface-layer rate above the canopy. The wall limit matters: with $l = l_c$ down to a no-slip floor the first wind-tunnel comparison below put 13 % of the stress ($τ_g = 0.13u_*^2$) on the floor (observed: none; 0.007 with the limit) and under-predicted the lower-canopy wind by 0.11 $U_h$; with the limit the error is 0.02 $U_h$.

The zero-plane displacement is the centroid of momentum absorption, $d = \sum z\,C_d a U^2 Δz / (\sum C_d a U^2 Δz + τ_g)$ (canopy_displacement_height; Thom 1971, Jackson 1981), the quantity tower studies infer from within-canopy momentum-flux profiles.

A scalar $c$ (potential temperature, humidity) with leaf sources $S$ obeys, in steady state, $d/dz(K\,dc/dz) + S = 0$ (solve_canopy_scalar_column). In one dimension the flux follows from the sources alone, $F_{f+1} = F_f + S_kΔz_k$, so the discrete budget is exact; the profile is then integrated down from the top value. For heat the diffusivity is that of Harman & Finnigan (2008), as in the multilayer canopy of Bonan et al. (2018): $K_c = (l_c u_*/Sc)\,e^{(z-h)β/l_c}$ in the canopy, Sc = 0.5 (harman_finnigan_scalar_diffusivity). The local $l^2|∂U/∂z|$ of the wind solution is not used for scalars: in a sheltered trunk space it is much smaller ($K/Sc$ = 0.022 against 0.145 m² s⁻¹ at 4 m in the Hyytiälä case with $u_*$ = 0.4 m s⁻¹), because the turbulence there is imported from the crown, not made by local shear; with it, a 20 W m⁻² forest-floor heat flux produced a 13 K near-ground excess.

The air-temperature profile is coupled to the leaves by iterating canopy_leaf_energy_balance (section 5) and the scalar column until the air temperature the leaves see is the one their heat produces (6-10 iterations to 10⁻⁷ K).

Observations used

No source tabulates its profiles; all values were read from the vector graphics of the published PDFs (path coordinates, calibrated on the tick marks; reading error below 0.5 % of the axis), except the CHATS plant-area density, which is a bitmap (±0.02 in $a h$). Each dataset has a check value its own normalisation fixes ($U/U_h = 1$ at $h$: read 1.000; the Hyytiälä needle-area profile integrates to 7.02, text 7). Leaf-out lowers the crown wind at every level and raises $d$ in both the observations and the model. The temperature comparison is semi-quantitative: the paper gives class medians without the radiation and wind that produced them, so the forcing (clear-sky midsummer noon, 600 + 120 W m⁻², $u_*$ = 0.4 m s⁻¹; clear night, sky 90 W m⁻² below $σT^4$, $u_*$ = 0.1 m s⁻¹; no forest-floor heat flux) is assumed. The sign and the order of magnitude are reproduced; the daytime anomaly is 4.6 times too large and the nocturnal one 30 % too small (neutral closure in a stable night).

What first-order closure cannot do

  • Trunk-space wind. Under a dense crown the observed wind has a secondary maximum (CHATS with leaves: 0.27 $U_h$ at 0.15 $h$ above 0.14 $U_h$ at 0.3 $h$; Hyytiälä: a nearly uniform 1.2 $u_*$), carried by sweeps from above and by pressure gradients (Shaw 1977; Shaw & Schumann 1992). A down-gradient K model is monotone there and gives about half the Hyytiälä trunk-space wind. Both are recorded as @test_broken.
  • Leafless shear layer. Without leaves the observed CHATS inflection point is inside the crown at 6 m with $L_s = 0.72h$; the model's maximum shear stays at the canopy top with $L_s = 1.32h$ (@test_broken). Leaf-out shortens $L_s$ in both.
  • Counter-gradient scalar transport (Denmead & Bradley 1985) is likewise outside K-theory; the Harman–Finnigan diffusivity keeps the trunk space ventilated, which is why it is used for scalars.
  • Stability. The closure is neutral. The Harman–Finnigan stability dependence of β and Sc is not included.
  • Momentum absorption near the crown top is under-predicted (wind tunnel stress, Hyytiälä $u_*/U_h$ = 0.26), so $U/u_*$ in and above a dense crown is too large by about a quarter.

A three-dimensional LES with ResolvedCanopyDrag resolves the sweeps that the column misses; its validation against these profiles is not part of this phase.
