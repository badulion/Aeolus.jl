Wall models

An LES of the atmospheric surface layer or of a high-Reynolds-number channel cannot resolve the near-wall eddies: the first grid point sits in the logarithmic layer, where the resolved velocity gradient is far too weak to carry the wall stress through the sub-grid viscosity. A wall model supplies that stress from the resolved velocity at the first point (Piomelli & Balaras 2002). In Aeolus every modelled wall stress comes from one module, Aeolus.WallModels (Phase 57, #169); the channel harness, the stable-boundary-layer cases and the Berlin ground call it rather than writing their own.

Equilibrium laws

An equilibrium law relates the wall-parallel speed $U$ at the distance $h$ from the wall to the friction velocity $u_τ = \sqrt{τ_w/ρ}$, assuming the layer between the wall and $h$ is in equilibrium (no pressure gradient, no advection). The wall stress is $τ_w/ρ = u_τ^2$ (wall_stress, kinematic) and acts against the velocity, $τ_{w,i} = τ_w u_i/U$.

Smooth wall — Spalding (1961). One formula covers the viscous sublayer, the buffer layer and the log layer:

\[y^+ = u^+ + e^{-κB}\left[e^{κu^+} - 1 - κu^+ - \frac{(κu^+)^2}{2} - \frac{(κu^+)^3}{6}\right], \qquad u^+ = U/u_τ,\quad y^+ = h u_τ/ν .\]

It tends to $u^+ = y^+$ as $y^+ → 0$ and to the log law $u^+ = \ln(y^+)/κ + B$ as $y^+ → ∞$. Given $U$, $h$ and $ν$, friction_velocity solves $u^+ y^+(u^+) = Uh/ν$ by Newton's method, safeguarded by the bracket $0 < u^+ ≤ \sqrt{Uh/ν}$ (the left side is convex and increasing, so the iteration converges monotonically once it is above the root). Measured: the round trip $y^+ → u^+ → y^+$ over $y^+ ∈ [10^{-2}, 10^6]$ (200 points) is exact to 8.7e-15, and $u_τ$ is recovered to 9.3e-16. The defaults are κ = 0.41, B = 5.2; LEE_MOSER_2015 has the constants of the $Re_τ ≈ 5200$ channel DNS, κ = 0.384, B = 4.27.

Spalding's formula is not the log law at moderate $y^+$: truncating the exponential after the cubic leaves it above the log law by $δu^+ ≈ [e^{-κB}(1 + x + x^2/2 + x^3/6) - u^+]/(dy^+/du^+)$, $x = κu^+$. This is 2.5e-3 at $y^+ = 10^4$, 7.2e-4 at $10^5$ and 1.4e-4 at $10^6$ (measured, equal to the estimate to 0.1 %). The issue's original expectation, 1e-3 at $y^+ = 10^4$, was wrong for this formula.

In the viscous sublayer ($h^+ < 1$) the law is the linear stress $τ_w = νU/h$, the stress CutCell.wall_shear_stress computes from a probe at the same distance; at the probes of a flat cut-cell floor ($h^+ ≤ 0.40$) the two agree to 9.5e-6.

Rough wall, neutral — RoughLogLaw(z0). $u_τ = κU/\ln(h/z_0)$, independent of ν.

Rough wall, stratified — MOSTWallLaw(sf, z0m, L). Monin–Obukhov similarity for momentum at a held Obukhov length $L$:

\[u_τ = \frac{κ\,\max(U, U_{min})}{\ln(h/z_{0m}) - ψ_m(h/L) + ψ_m(z_{0m}/L)} .\]

Nothing here is restated: κ is SurfaceExchange.von_karman, and the denominator is the stability-corrected log term of SurfaceExchange.solve_surface_layer with the stability functions' psi_m, so at the solver's ζ the law returns the solver's $u_*$ bit for bit (tested at ζ = ±0.5), and with $L = ±∞$ it is the rough log law (1e-12). A surface model that returns only $u_*$ hands its stability over through matching_obukhov_length, the $L$ for which the law gives back that $u_*$ (the root of the monotone $Φ_m(ζ) = κU/u_*$, by the solver's Illinois iteration).

The operator

apply_wall_stress!(sim, wm, Δt) applies the stress of a law to the first velocity level next to the z-walls of a RegularGrid, as a post_predictor! hook. The speed of a column is taken at the centre of its wall cell ($h = Δz/2$); a face shared by two columns gets the mean of the two columns' stresses, a column contributing only at its own wall level, so a level mask can describe an immersed floor (the roof-aware Berlin ground). Three formulations:

formulationstressreference
:local$τ_w(U)\,u_i/U$Schumann (1975), Grötzbach (1987)
:schumann$⟨τ_w⟩\,u_i/⟨U⟩$, $⟨τ_w⟩ = τ_w(⟨U⟩)$Schumann (1975)
:moeng``⟨τw⟩[⟨U⟩(ui - ⟨ui⟩) + U⟨ui⟩]/(⟨U⟩\,⟨\mathbf u⟩

Each is affine in the face velocity, $τ_{w,i} = a u_i + b$, and is applied implicitly,

\[u_i ← \frac{u_i - Δt\,b/Δz}{1 + Δt\,a/Δz},\]

so the stress is evaluated with the end-of-step velocity. The explicit form is unstable for $Δt\,a/Δz > 2$; this one decays monotonically and never changes sign for any Δt (tested at 100× the explicit limit for the rough and the Spalding law). The momentum it removes is exactly $Σ τ_{w,i} ΔA Δt$ (measured ≤ 1.3e-15 relative), and nothing else changes: w, the other levels and the halo and finite-volume code are untouched, so on a free-slip ImpermeableWall the wall model is the only momentum flux through the wall face.

Immersed-boundary walls

A direct-forcing immersed boundary (Aeolus.IBM) zeroes every velocity face of a solid cell. A fluid face whose tangential neighbour is such a face then sees, through the viscous and eddy-viscosity stencils, the flux $(ν + ν_{t,e})(0 - q)/Δ$: an unresolved no-slip wall a distance $Δ$ away, with no log law. Tessicini et al. (2002) and Chester, Meneveau & Parlange (2007) replace it with the stress of a wall law at the first fluid point; Aeolus does the same in its equilibrium form (src/wall_models/immersed.jl, P57-d, #179).

immersed_walls(sim, sm, law) finds every (face, wall) pair of the mask:

facewallstangential partner interpolated to the face
u±yw (4 faces)
v±xw (4 faces)
w±xv (4 faces)
w±yu (4 faces)

The ±z walls of u and v (ground, roofs) are the z-walls of apply_wall_stress!. The law (any AbstractWallLaw, or a function of the solid cell behind the wall) is evaluated at $h = Δ_d/2$: the solid cell's face is the wall, as for the roofs of the z-wall operator, and the face centre is half a cell from it. Two hooks act on every step:

  1. remove_stencil_wall_flux! (body_force!): after the tendencies are formed, the stencil's own wall flux $(ν + ν_{t,e})(q_N - q_F)/Δ_d^2$ ($ν_{t,e}$ the mean of the four cell-centre $ν_t$ around the edge) is subtracted, from the same start-of-step velocity and $ν_t$ the stencil used.
  2. apply_immersed_wall_stress! (post_predictor!, after the IBM's direct forcing): $q ← q/(1 + Δt \sum_{walls} a/Δ_d)$, $a = τ_w(U)/U$, implicit as for the z-walls; a corner face (two walls) gets the sum.

The correction of a step is therefore the modelled stress minus the stencil stress, and the momentum the step removes at the walls is exactly $Σ τ_w ΔA Δt$ (measured to 1e-14 for u, v and the w faces of an L-shaped corner, test/era6_wall_models_immersed.jl). With the IMEX stepper the diffusion is implicit; the explicit removal is then first-order in time.

Rotated channel. A channel at $Re_τ = 1000$ whose walls in $y$ are an IBM mask reaches the bulk velocity of the same channel with z-walls and the z-wall operator to round-off (3e-15), $U_b^+ = 19.18$ at 16 cells across $2δ$ (first face at $h^+ = 62.5$), 4.0 % below Lee & Moser (2015), 19.98. With the wall model off — the stencil's no-slip flux, as the Berlin facades had before #179 — it is 13.86. The eddy viscosity there is the parabolic $ν_t = κ u_τ d(1 - d/δ)$ (the exact profile is then the log law), not an LES. The reference itself falls below Lee & Moser as the grid is refined ($U_b^+$ = 19.38, 19.18, 18.86, 18.58, 18.10 for 12, 16, 24, 32, 48 cells): the log-layer mismatch of a wall model matched at the first point (Kawai & Larsson 2012), which matching away from the wall (P57-b) addresses.

Cut-cell walls

On a cut-cell geometry (Aeolus.CutCell) the ghost-cell stencils already carry a probe off the true surface along its normal. wall_shear_stress(u, v, w, stencils, geom, law; ν) — a method of CutCell.wall_shear_stress — evaluates the law there: the tangential speed at the image point, at the probe distance $h$. law = nothing is the resolved ν-linear stress $ν u_t/h$. wall_slip_velocity turns the modelled stress into the slip wall value $u_s = u_t - τ_w h/ν_{eff}$ that makes the linear ghost-cell reconstruction (reconstruct_wall_velocity!) carry it; in the viscous sublayer it is zero (no-slip).

On a channel inclined at 30° ($Re_τ = 1000$, $Δ = δ/15$) the plane-mean modelled stress is 0.990 $δ\,dp/dx$ with the image point two cells out (probe_factor = 2, $h^+ = 133$); the ν-linear stress is 0.127 of it. At the default probe_factor = 1 the trilinear interpolation of the concave log profile at one cell gives 0.931: for wall modelling the probe should be built at least two cells out.

Wall-bounded benchmark cases

src/wall_models/channel_cases.jl (P54-d, #174) is the shared harness every Era VI closure is validated on. It adds no wall physics: the wall stress is apply_wall_stress! above, the SGS model is whatever AbstractSGSClosure is passed, and the diagnostics are Operators' phi_m_profile, total_stress_profile and streamwise_spectrum.

half_channel_case — the neutral ABL of Porté-Agel, Meneveau & Parlange (2000) and Bou-Zeid, Meneveau & Parlange (2005): a $2πH × 2πH × H$ box, periodic in x and y, driven by the mean pressure gradient $-∂p/∂x/ρ = u_*^2/H$ (a body force on $u$), free-slip top, RoughLogLaw(z0) wall stress at the bottom ($z_0/H = 10^{-4}$), inviscid, no Coriolis and no wall-damping function. In statistically steady state the plane-mean momentum budget is

\[τ_{res}(z) + τ_{sgs}(z) = u_*^2\Big(1 - \frac{z}{H}\Big), \qquad τ_{wall} = u_*^2 ,\]

and in the surface layer $Φ_m = (κz/u_*)∂⟨U⟩/∂z = 1$.

channel_case — the smooth two-wall channel at $Re_τ = u_τδ/ν$ ($2πδ × πδ × 2δ$, SpaldingLaw at both walls, forcing $u_τ^2/δ$), for the log-layer-mismatch study (P57-b) and the stratified and relaminarising channels (P55-f, P59-e).

Both return a WallBoundedCase; run_channel! advances it with SSP-RK3 (step_rk3! now takes step!'s body_force!/post_predictor! hooks) and returns time averages. advance_channel! records the stress the wall operator applied in each step, with the SSP-RK3 weights 1/6, 1/6, 2/3 of its three substages, so the plane-mean momentum budget closes per step: $L_z Δ⟨u⟩ = (G L_z - τ_b - τ_t)Δt$ to 1e-11 relative (RK3, Euler, one and two walls, local and Schumann).

The staggered-consistent Φ_m differences the plane-mean speed of two cell centres and places the result on the w-face between them, so for the exact log law it is $(z/Δz)\ln[(z + Δz/2)/(z - Δz/2)] = 1 + (Δz/z)^2/12 + (Δz/z)^4/80 + …$ — ln 3 = 1.099 at the first face; the test checks this to 1e-12 and the observed order (2.042, 2.010, 2.003). The stress profile is formed with the stencils the solver integrates (the centred flux product of momentum_advect!, the edge viscosity of add_eddy_viscosity_u!), so its divergence equals the plane-mean tendency to round-off (1e-12). E11 is Parseval-normalised ($Σ E_{11} Δk_1 = ⟨u'^2⟩$ to 1e-12).

The hooks P54-d reserved for later issues (documented, refusing non-default values) are all implemented now: filter (P57-b), surface_heat_flux (P58-e, below). channel_case's bottom_bc = :noslip and θ_walls are implemented by P55-f (#191): a wall-resolved channel with NoSlipWalls and isothermal θ walls, molecular Prandtl number Pr, buoyancy reference θ₀/g_accel and the stress-form switch sgs_stress; see Stable boundary layers. P59-e (#195) reuses it for the relaminarisation study and adds channel_case(surface_heat_flux = Q) on the resolved wall: the flux-cooled open channel (walls = :bottom) of Nieuwstadt (2005), with the prescribed bottom θ flux of the core and molecular Pr; see Transition → Relaminarisation. The half channel is inviscid and keeps refusing bottom_bc and θ_walls.

Surface heat flux (P58-e, #193). surface_heat_flux = Q (K m s⁻¹, upward kinematic; negative cools) turns the half channel into the flux-cooled channel of the stable-boundary-layer collapse studies. It switches buoyancy on ($θ_{ref}$ = 300 K by default), adds Q to the first cell as the prescribed bottom θ flux, and makes the wall MOSTFluxWallLaw: Monin–Obukhov with the stability set by Q and the wall's own $u_τ$. That law gives no stress where $-Q$ exceeds the maximum sustainable heat flux of the local wind (SurfaceExchange.flux_friction_velocity). The initial wind is the neutral log law, so neutral and cooled runs start from the same state. θ is conserved to the prescribed flux: $ΔΣθ\,dV = Q\,A\,Δt$ to $10^{-10}$. The collapse regime is in Stability functions → Maximum sustainable heat flux.

The cases are idealised validation set-ups and are not coupled to the Berlin run (a stated exception, #174): the wall physics they exercise is apply_wall_stress!, which is already the default Berlin ground stress, and their diagnostics live in Operators (infrastructure, exempt).

Log-layer mismatch: matching height and input filtering (P57-b)

An equilibrium law fed with the velocity of the wall-adjacent cell gives a wall stress that is consistent with that cell, but the LES velocity in the first one or two cells is itself wrong: the eddies there are not resolved and the SGS model is at its least accurate. The result is the log-layer mismatch (LLM): the mean profile has a kink next to the wall and is shifted against the log law above it, which shows as Φm ≠ 1 at the first faces and, in a pressure-driven channel, as an error in the bulk velocity. Two remedies, both in `src/wallmodels/matching.jl(MatchingInput), change only *what goes into the law*; the stress is still applied at the wall face byapplywallstress!(itswindandheight` keywords):

  • Matching height off the first cell (Kawai & Larsson 2012). The velocity is sampled at $h_{wm}$ (h_wm in metres, linearly interpolated between cell centres, or h_wm_cells = n, the n-th cell centre at $(n-½)Δz$) and the law is evaluated there: $τ_w = τ_w(U(h_{wm}), h_{wm})$, applied along the first-cell velocity, $τ_{w,i} = τ_w\,u_i/U_1$.
  • Input filtering. In time, the exponential filter of Yang, Park & Moin (2017), $\tilde u ← \tilde u + ε(u - \tilde u)$, $ε = 1 - e^{-Δt/T_f}$, advanced once per accepted step (so all Runge-Kutta substages see the same input); the weight is the exact solution of $T_f \dot{\tilde u} = u - \tilde u$ for a held input, so a step is followed by $1 - e^{-t/T_f}$ whatever the Δt (measured 1.1e-16 over 60 random steps). Along the wall, the 3 × 3 box of Operators.box_filter! (n = (3, 3, 1), the dynamic model's test filter; second moment $2Δ^2/3$), the discrete form of the 2Δ input filter of Bou-Zeid, Meneveau & Parlange (2005). It preserves the plane mean (1e-12) and has the exact transfer function $(1 + 2\cos kΔ)/3$ per direction (1e-13).

Input wind, corrected. Before P57-b, apply_wall_stress! with a wind used the coefficient $τ_w(U_{in})/U_{in}$, i.e. a stress of magnitude $τ_w(U_{in})\,U_1/U_{in}$; now it is $τ_w(U_{in})/U_1$, so the magnitude is the law's at the input wind, as documented. The #169 test encoded the old formula; both were wrong. Nothing used wind before this phase.

Non-equilibrium wall model: the thin-boundary-layer equations (P57-c)

An equilibrium law assumes the stress is constant between the wall and the matching point, so the wall stress follows the matching-point speed and nothing else. The non-equilibrium model of Balaras, Benocci & Piomelli (1996) and Wang & Moin (2002) instead integrates the thin-boundary-layer equations (TBLE) on an embedded wall-normal grid under every wall column,

\[\frac{∂u_i}{∂t} + \mathrm{conv}_i = -\frac{∂p}{∂x_i} + \frac{∂}{∂y}\left[(ν + ν_{t,wm})\frac{∂u_i}{∂y}\right], \qquad u_i(0) = 0,\quad u_i(h_{wm}) = u_i^{LES}(h_{wm}),\]

with the eddy viscosity of Cabot & Moin (2000)

\[ν_{t,wm} = κ_{wm}\,y\,u_τ\left(1 - e^{-y^+/A^+}\right)^2, \qquad A^+ = 17,\]

($κ_{wm}u_τ(y + z_0)$ over a rough wall). TBLEWallModel is an AbstractWallModel: the upper boundary condition is the velocity sampled by P57-b's MatchingInput (matching height, time and wall-parallel filters), and the stress is applied by the same implicit face loop as the equilibrium WallModel. The eddy viscosity is a generic (AbstractTBLEEddyViscosity, tble_eddy_viscosity) so P58 can add buoyancy by a subtype; the test file adds a channel mixing length that way.

terms selects the equation:

termsequation
:equilibrium_ode$∂_y[(ν + ν_t)∂_y u_i] = 0$, solved directly (the ODE of the equilibrium models)
:pressure_gradientunsteady, with $∂p/∂x_i$ (the mean forcing plus the resolved $p$ at the matching cell)
:fullplus $u∂_x u_i + v∂_y u_i + w∂_y u_i$ from the neighbouring columns (Park & Moin 2014): first-order upwind, $w$ from continuity

Discretisation. 30 nodes with geometric stretching (ratio 1.2; the first node at $8.5·10^{-4}h$), finite volumes, backward Euler (any Δt) and one tridiagonal solve per component. The flux of a segment is $G(u_{j+1} - u_j)$ with the conductance $G = [∫dy/(ν + ν_t)]^{-1}$ integrated exactly (4-point Gauss–Legendre), so the steady unforced solution is the quadrature of the equilibrium ODE at the nodes whatever the grid, and with $ν_t ≡ 0$ the scheme is exact for quadratic profiles. The wall stress is the first segment's flux carried to the wall with the wall value of $∂τ/∂y = ∂p/∂x$. $ν_t$ depends on the column's own $u_τ$: each step is iterated (secant on $u_τ = |τ_w(u_τ)|^{1/2}$, each iterate a full implicit solve).

Coupling. In every substage apply_wall_stress! applies the columns' current stress $\boldsymbol τ_w$, split as $τ_{w,i} = a u_i + b_i$ with the implicit part $a = \max(\boldsymbol τ_w·\hat{\mathbf u}, 0)/U$ along the resolved first-cell velocity and the explicit rest $b_i$ (a pressure gradient can turn the stress away from the flow). update_wall_model! advances the columns once per accepted step with the end-of-step velocity and pressure. The first use primes them with the equilibrium solution.

The response test follows He & Seddighi (2013) qualitatively: after a step increase of the flow rate the wall stress first rises in a thin Stokes-like layer driven by the pressure gradient, before the core accelerates. The TBLE sees the pressure gradient and reproduces that early rise (within 1.4 %); the equilibrium law sees only the matching speed and lags it.

Expectation that proved wrong: within 1 % of Spalding at h⁺ = 30. At $h^+ = 30$ the matching point is in the buffer layer, where the van Driest-damped viscosity (A⁺ = 17) and Spalding's fit are two different profiles: −3.0 % (A⁺ = 19: −6.6 %; A⁺ = 25: −14 %). In the log layer they agree to 0.2 %. Kept as a broken test.

Dynamic κ (Wang & Moin 2002). dynamic_kappa = :total makes the TBLE eddy viscosity at $h_{wm}$ equal the LES's effective one there, (resolved + SGS stress)/(resolved mean shear), in the plane mean; :sgs matches the SGS viscosity alone (Wang & Moin's form). A first version matched the stress instead; that is degenerate: in a statistically steady flow both sides equal $τ_w + h\,∂p/∂x$ by the momentum balance whatever κ is, and κ drifted to the clamp. With the viscosity matching, the channel above gives κwm = 0.27 (averaged over 5-8 δ/uτ, not fed back), close to $0.41(1 - h/δ) = 0.26$ — the outer-layer decrease of the stress that the mixing-length viscosity lacks — and to Wang & Moin's ≈ 0.2. Fed back into :pressure_gradient, which has no resolved convection to carry the difference, it lowers the viscosity near the wall as well: Ub⁺ +6.5 % at 8 δ/uτ and +15.9 % over 16-24 δ/uτ (κwm 0.27, ⟨τw⟩ 0.99); with :full +7.4 % at 8 δ/uτ. So the default is :off (κwm = κ of the law), and `κfeedback = false` reports the dynamic value without using it.

Not modelled. No Monin–Obukhov correction in $ν_{t,wm}$ (neutral).

Dynamic roughness of partially resolved relief (P57-e)

DynamicRoughness (src/wall_models/dynamic_roughness.jl, #190) is the dynamic surface roughness model of Anderson & Meneveau (2011) for a surface whose relief is only partly resolved by the grid. The height field $h$ is split at the grid scale Δ: the resolved part $h_Δ$ exerts the surface-gradient drag

\[f_i = -C_d\,(\tilde{\mathbf u}\cdot∇h_Δ)^2\,H(\tilde{\mathbf u}\cdot∇h_Δ)\,∂_i h_Δ\]

on the first cell (pressure drag on windward slopes only), and the rest enters the wall law as a roughness length proportional to the RMS sub-filter height, $z_{0,Δ} = \sqrt{z_{0,f}^2 + α^2σ_Δ^2}$. The floor $z_{0,f}$ is the roughness of scales below the height map; on a flat surface $z_0 = z_{0,f}$ exactly. α comes from requiring that the total plane-mean drag along the mean wind (modelled stress plus form drag) is the same at Δ and at the test-filter scale $\hat Δ$:

\[\Big\langle\Big[\frac{κ}{\ln(z/z_{0,\hat Δ})}\Big]^2|\hat{\mathbf u}|\,\hat{\mathbf u}\cdot\hat{\mathbf e}\Big\rangle + R_{\hat Δ} = \Big\langle\Big[\frac{κ}{\ln(z/z_{0,Δ})}\Big]^2|\tilde{\mathbf u}|\,\tilde{\mathbf u}\cdot\hat{\mathbf e}\Big\rangle + R_Δ .\]

This is solved per plane with SurfaceExchange's Illinois iteration, the root solver also used by solve_surface_layer, PlantCanopy, Photosynthesis and AirSeaFlux. A log-spaced scan finds the first bracket on $(0, α_{max})$, where $α_{max}$ is the largest α that keeps every $z_0$ below the matching height. If the residual has no sign change, or vanishes identically (relief the grid cannot see), the solver keeps α_fallback and flags the solve unconverged.

Sub-filter statistics. subfilter_height builds them from a height map that is r times finer than the grid, using its cell moments $m_1 = \bar h$ and $m_2 = \overline{h^2}$:

  • $σ_Δ^2 = m_2 - 2h_Δ m_1 + h_Δ^2$, where $h_Δ$ defaults to $m_1$. The Berlin case passes the IBM staircase instead.
  • At the test scale, $σ_{\hat Δ}^2 = \hat m_2 - 2h_{\hat Δ}\hat m_1 + h_{\hat Δ}^2$.

For the box filter, $σ_{\hat Δ}^2$ is the exact variance of the fine surface over the composite kernel (tested to 1e-12). The test filter is Operators.spectral_cutoff_filter! at $k_c = π/(2Δ)$ (ratio 2, the default on a periodic surface) or Operators.box_filter! (3 × 3, ratio 3). Neither is reimplemented.

Parameter provider. with_roughness, dynamic_wall_laws and dynamic_wall_laws! turn the per-column $z_0$ into RoughLogLaw or MOSTWallLaw matrices for a WallModel. half_channel_case(; roughness) applies the form drag as a first-cell body force (budget term τ_form) and updates α once per step.

In the Berlin case

The ground of the flagship is this operator with a MOSTWallLaw per column: z0m from the surface's roughness (the per-class table), L from the last surface step, $u_τ$ re-evaluated at the instantaneous first-air wind every step (see The Berlin case). With $L$ and the wind frozen it reproduced the drag the example used to carry ($u_*^2/U$ held between surface steps) exactly.

Since P57-b the wind the law is evaluated at is time-filtered by default (input_T_f = 10 s, of the order of the eddy time $z_{ref}/u_*$ of the 6.25 m first cell). The matching height stays at the first air cell: three cells above a street (18.75 m) lie in or above the canyon, where no log law holds; h_wm_cells = 3 over park and water columns is a smoke variant. In CaseConfig(:smoke) (1 h, last 30 min) the street wall stress has almost no sub-minute fluctuation for the filter to remove (RMS of the part faster than 60 s: 2.2e-6 m² s⁻², 0.03 % of the mean 7.8e-3), and the filter changes the ground mean $u_*$ by +0.7 % (0.05225 → 0.05260 m s⁻¹); it costs 0.07 ms per step on a 64 × 64 plane.

Since P57-c the option wall_model = :nonequilibrium of the surface_exchange coupling replaces the ground's MOSTWallLaw by a TBLEWallModel (default stays :equilibrium): a 30-point column under every ground and roof column, rough (the surface's own $z_{0m}$), driven by the same time-filtered first-air-cell wind and by the resolved pressure gradient at the first air level (one-sided next to a building), with tble_terms = :pressure_gradient. It is neutral: the Monin–Obukhov correction of the equilibrium law is not carried into the TBLE. A smoke variant runs it. Measured on the flagship (CaseConfig(:summer), 64 × 64 × 32, 100 steps after 300 steps of spin-up): the coupling's own stages cost 15.6 ms per step more (9.4 % of the 166.5 ms equilibrium step; the whole step measured 3.3 % slower on a loaded machine), so it is not proposed as the default (the issue's bar was 5 %). On the 1690 street columns the TBLE and the equilibrium stress at the same input differ in sign on 11.1 % and in sign or by more than 30 % on 77.3 % (median ratio 1.03, interquartile 0.48-2.16; mean 4.5e-3 against 3.0e-3 m² s⁻²): the resolved pressure gradient at the first air level of a street canyon (median 3.3e-3, 90th percentile 7.4e-3 m s⁻²) times the 3.1 m matching height is of the order of the stress itself, which is exactly the non-equilibrium the law cannot see.

The facades are the immersed-boundary walls above (addon wall_model_facades, #179): a RoughLogLaw with the wall (or glass) roughness of the surface exchange's class table, the stencil's no-slip flux removed. In the 1 h smoke run the facades carry 9.3 % of the surface drag; the stencil drag they replace was 33× the modelled one.

Since P57-e the option z0m = :dynamic of surface_exchange (a smoke variant, off by default) replaces the ground law's $z_{0m}$ with the dynamic roughness of the OSM height map. The sub-filter heights are the true building heights, rasterised 4× finer, about the IBM staircase $(k_{air}-1)Δz$, so they are only staircase loss. The floor is each column's table value, and α is solved every surface step.

Measured in CaseConfig(:smoke) over 30 min (900 steps):

  • α = 0.043;
  • street $z_0$ averages 0.0555 m against the table's 0.05 m (range 0.05-0.37 m), and all columns lie between 0.024 and 0.89 m, inside [1e-3, 2] m;
  • street $u_*$ rises from 0.0802 to 0.0814 m s⁻¹ (+1.5 %), ground $u_*$ from 0.0495 to 0.0523 m s⁻¹ (+5.7 %);
  • the cost is 1.1 ms per surface step on the 16 × 16 plane, 1.5 % of the run.

The table stays the default because the dynamic value's Berlin calibration has not been reviewed.
