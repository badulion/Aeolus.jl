Air–sea fluxes and wave-dependent roughness

Over land the roughness length is a property of the ground. Over water it is not: the roughness elements are the waves, which the wind itself raises, so $z_0$ depends on the friction velocity it helps determine, and on how developed the sea is. This page describes src/AirSeaFlux.jl (Phase 52, issue #39), which supplies that roughness and the COARE bulk algorithm around it. The Monin–Obukhov solve itself is the surface shared core's contract's (see Surface exchange); nothing here restates it.

1. Momentum roughness

Charnock and the smooth-flow limit

Dimensional analysis for a fully rough, wave-covered surface gives Charnock's (1955) $z_0 = \alpha u_*^2/g$. At low wind the surface is aerodynamically smooth and the viscous sublayer sets $z_0 = 0.11\nu/u_*$. Smith (1988) adds the two:

\[z_0 = \alpha\frac{u_*^2}{g} + 0.11\frac{\nu}{u_*}, \qquad R_r = \frac{z_0 u_*}{\nu}.\]

The drag coefficient $C_{D10N} = [\kappa/\ln(10/z_0)]^2$ therefore has a minimum near 3 m s⁻¹ — rising toward calm (smooth flow) and toward storms (Charnock). Because $z_0$ depends on $u_*$, even the neutral problem $u_* = \kappa U/\ln(z/z_0(u_*))$ is a fixed point; neutral_transfer_coefficients solves it by a bracketed secant, taking the lower root (for steep wave laws, $z_0 \propto u_*^{4.5}$, a second, unstable root appears where $z_0 \to z$).

Wave age and wave steepness

A wind sea at fetch or duration limit is young: short, slow, steep waves that are rougher than a mature sea at the same wind. The wave age $c_p/u_*$ (phase speed of the peak waves over friction velocity) is the usual measure; a fully developed sea has $c_p/u_* \approx 32$ (Donelan 1990), equivalently $c_p/U_{10N} \approx 1.14$–1.2.

The Oost form's equivalence of Charnock parameter and roughness length rests on the deep-water dispersion relation $g\lambda_p/2\pi = c_p^2$, which the tests check exactly. Wave information enters as a SeaState(c_p, H_s, λ_p), built from a peak period by deep_water_sea_state or, for a fully developed sea, from $H_s = 0.0248\,U_{10N}^2$, $T_p = 0.729\,U_{10N}$ (fully_developed_sea_state; Taylor & Yelland 2001).

Edson et al. (2013) found the wind-speed and wave-age COARE 3.5 forms give similar open-ocean drag because inverse wave age grows nearly linearly with wind there; the wave forms matter in coastal and fetch-limited water, lakes, and swell.

2. Scalar roughness

Heat and water vapour cross the last millimetre by molecular diffusion into surface-renewing eddies, not by form drag, so $z_{0t}$ and $z_{0q}$ are not $z_0$. Liu, Katsaros & Businger (1979) expressed them as functions of $R_r$; COARE 3.0 replaced their tables by a fit (Fairall et al. 2003, eq. 28),

\[z_{0q} = z_{0t} = \min\!\left(1.1\times10^{-4},\; 5.5\times10^{-5} R_r^{-0.6}\right),\]

and COARE 3.5 refits it ($1.6\times10^{-4}$, $5.8\times10^{-5}$, $-0.72$) to keep the Dalton number of 3.0 with the rougher 3.5 $z_0$. The Brutsaert–Garratt form $z_{0q} = z_0\exp(2 - 2.28R_r^{1/4})$ is also provided. The scalar roughness falls as the surface roughens — breaking and spray aside, a rougher sea does not transfer heat proportionally faster.

3. Convective stability functions and gustiness

In free convection ($\zeta \to -\infty$) local similarity requires $\phi \propto (-\zeta)^{-1/3}$, which the Kansas forms ($-1/4$, $-1/2$) violate. COARE blends them into a convective form (Fairall et al. 1996; Grachev & Fairall 1997):

\[\psi = (1-f)\,\psi_K + f\,\psi_C,\qquad f = \frac{\zeta^2}{1+\zeta^2}, \qquad \psi_C = \tfrac32\ln\frac{y^2+y+1}{3} - \sqrt3\arctan\frac{2y+1}{\sqrt3} + \frac{\pi}{\sqrt3},\]

$y = (1-\gamma_c\zeta)^{1/3}$, $\gamma_c = 10.15$ (momentum) and 34.15 (scalars). COAREStability implements this as a further AbstractStabilityFunctions; its Kansas and stable (Beljaars–Holtslag) parts are the contract's BusingerDyerBeljaarsHoltslag with COARE's coefficients. The analytic $\phi$ of the blend, $\phi = (1-f)\phi_K + f\phi_C - \zeta f'(\psi_C-\psi_K)$, is checked against $d\psi/d\zeta = (1-\phi)/\zeta$ to $2\times10^{-10}$.

As $U \to 0$ the mean-wind shear vanishes but boundary-layer eddies still sweep air across the surface. The gustiness $w_g = \beta\,(B z_i)^{1/3}$ (β = 1.2, $B$ the surface buoyancy flux, $z_i$ the mixed-layer depth) enters the effective wind $S = \sqrt{U^2 + w_g^2}$, keeping the fluxes finite; in stable conditions $w_g = 0.2$ m s⁻¹. Stress is $\tau = \rho u_*^2 U/S$: the gust part has no mean direction.

4. The bulk algorithm

air_sea_fluxes iterates: roughness from $u_*$ (and $U_{10N}$ or the sea state) → exact Monin–Obukhov solve (solve_surface_layer, with COAREStability and gustiness) → buoyancy flux, new gustiness and $U_{10N}$, until $u_*$ and $w_g$ change by less than 10⁻⁹. Since the stability is solved exactly inside each pass, the outer loop only converges the roughness–gustiness feedback (10–18 passes).

SeaSurface wraps this as an AbstractSurface (seawater humidity $e = 0.98\,e_{sat}$, water emissivity from SurfaceOptics) so the tile framework can aggregate open water with the other surfaces. Its skin temperature is prescribed; the ocean mixed layer, warm layer and cool skin that evolve it belong to OceanMixedLayer.
