Spectral broadening: bulk closure and validation

Era IV, Phase 45 (issue #14) — module TurbulentMicrophysics.

The previous page lets every superdroplet grow in its own supersaturation $\bar S + \sigma_S\xi_i(t)$, where $\xi_i$ is a unit Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (OU) process with correlation time $\tau$. A bulk scheme has no droplets to give a supersaturation to. This page describes what it can carry instead: the moments of the spectrum those droplets would have. It then checks the result against the superdroplet reference and against the broadening law found in DNS.

Moment equations

Diffusional growth is linear in $w = r^2$:

\[\frac{dw_i}{dt} = 2G\left(\bar S + \sigma_S\xi_i - \frac{a}{\sqrt{w_i}}\right),\]

where $G = 1/(F_k + F_d)$ and $a$ is the Kelvin coefficient. Both are taken from Phase 41 (fk_thermal, fd_vapor, kelvin_a), so the bulk scheme and the superdroplets share the same droplet physics. The Raoult term is dropped. For an activated droplet with 10⁻¹⁸ kg of NaCl it is $5.2\times10^{-6}$ at 3 μm and $1.1\times10^{-6}$ at 5 μm, two to three orders of magnitude below $\bar S$.

The bulk state (SpectralMoments) consists of the droplet number $N$, the mean $\mu = \langle w\rangle$, the variance $V = \mathrm{Var}(w)$, and the covariance $C = \mathrm{Cov}(w, \xi)$. The covariance is needed because a droplet that has been in a moist sub-volume is both larger and, for about one correlation time, still in moist air. Averaging over droplets with $\langle\xi\rangle = 0$ and $\langle\xi^2\rangle = 1$ gives

\[\begin{aligned} \dot\mu &= 2G\left(\bar S - a\langle w^{-1/2}\rangle\right),\\ \dot V &= 4G\sigma_S C - 4Ga\,\mathrm{Cov}(w, w^{-1/2}),\\ \dot C &= 2G\sigma_S - C/\tau + Ga\,\mu^{-3/2}C . \end{aligned}\]

$w$ itself carries no white noise, because $\xi$ is continuous, so there is no Itô term. Without the Kelvin term the three equations are exact and closed. For an uncorrelated start they give $V - V_0 = (2G)^2\,2\sigma_S^2\tau[t - \tau(1 - e^{-t/\tau})]$, which is the analytic result of issue #13 (supersaturation_integral_variance). The test reproduces it to $10^{-9}$.

bulk_condensation_step! integrates $C$ exactly over each sub-step, since it is linear. Within a sub-step it holds $\bar S$, $T$ and $p$ fixed, which is the same operator split the superdroplet step uses. It sub-steps to $0.2\,\tau_\mathrm{phase}$ and $\tau/10$, and passes the condensed mass $\tfrac43\pi\rho_w N\Delta\langle r^3\rangle$ to Phase 41's apply_condensate_tendency!. Total water is therefore conserved to round-off (measured $|\Delta W/W| \le 3\times10^{-15}$).

The one assumption: a gamma closure in $r^2$

$\langle r^2\rangle = \mu$ is exact. Every other radius moment ($\langle r\rangle$, $\langle r^3\rangle$, $\varepsilon$, and the Kelvin moments $\langle w^{-1/2}\rangle$) requires a shape. The scheme assumes that $w$ follows a gamma distribution with shape $k = \mu^2/V$, so that $\langle w^p\rangle = \mu^p\,\Gamma(k+p)/(\Gamma(k)k^p)$ (gamma_moment_ratio). The ratio is evaluated in log form using Stirling's series after shifting $k \ge 16$. Its accuracy is $10^{-14}$, including for narrow spectra where $k \sim 10^6$. No SpecialFunctions dependency is needed.

The gamma shape is right-skewed, with skewness $2/\sqrt k$. This fits an activated spectrum well: for a log-normal spectrum with $\varepsilon = 0.1$ the closure gives a relative dispersion 0.75–1.2 % too high. The fluctuation-generated part of the spectrum is different. It is a sum of Gaussian increments, so it is symmetric in $r^2$. When that part dominates, the closure underestimates $\varepsilon$, as shown below.

Validation case

A BroadeningCase is a fixed-mass parcel (parcel_ascent! applies dry-adiabatic cooling and hydrostatic expansion, with latent heat added by the condensation step). The case is run by run_broadening_case in two ways from the same initial droplets:

  • Superdroplet reference. This is issue #13's sgs_condensation_step!, in which every superdroplet has its own OU supersaturation and growth is Phase 41's condensation_growth!.
  • Bulk scheme. This is bulk_condensation_step! applied to spectral_moments of those droplets.

parcel_ascent! with no droplets produces $dS/dz$ equal to Phase 42's $\alpha(T, p)$ to within 0.12–0.24 %. The remaining difference is the dry-air molar mass in $\alpha$'s pressure term.

Ascending parcel: bulk vs superdroplet

The parcel starts at cloud base with 100 cm⁻³ of 5 μm droplets (log-normal, $\varepsilon_0 = 0.1$). It rises at $w = 0.5$ m s⁻¹ for 300 s with $\tau = 5$ s, and the reference uses 8000 superdroplets. The superdroplet columns are seed 1. Since #305 the superdroplet radii and fluctuations come from counter-based streams (one per droplet), so the realisations, and these numbers, changed once. The table and the seed lists below were re-measured then. The ranges quoted for the monodisperse and stationary variants are from the earlier streams.

Across seeds 1–3, the $\varepsilon$ difference is within 1.6 % for $\sigma_S \le 0.1\,\%$. That is inside the stated tolerance: three standard errors of a sample standard deviation, $3/\sqrt{2n_{sd}} = 2.4\,\%$, plus the closure error of the initial spectrum. The width $\sigma_{r^2}$, which depends on no closure, agrees within 1.6 % in every case.

At $\sigma_S = 0.2\,\%$ the bulk $\varepsilon$ is 2.9–5.1 % low for every seed. This is a systematic error, not sampling. Here the fluctuations have produced most of the variance, so the $r^2$ distribution is nearly symmetric, and the gamma closure puts too little weight in the small-$r$ tail. The error grows with the fluctuation share. In the same ascending parcel with monodisperse droplets, frozen sub-volumes ($\tau = \infty$) and 60 s, where $\varepsilon \approx 0.15$, the bulk value is 5–7 % low over four seeds. With stationary fluctuations, $\tau = 20$ s, 600 s and $\varepsilon \approx 0.165$, it is 7–9 % low. In all of these cases $\sigma_{r^2}$ still agrees to sampling error. The test suite keeps this bias as a known limitation (the test asserts a 2–6 % deficit) and does not widen the tolerance to hide it. The natural fix is to carry the third cumulant of $r^2$, which the linear fluctuation forcing leaves unchanged. That would let the closure tell the symmetric fluctuation-generated part from the skewed activated part.

Without fluctuations the spectrum narrows in both schemes, as in Phase 41, from $\varepsilon$ = 0.10 to 0.030. One exception: during the first second $\bar S$ is still below the Kelvin equilibrium $a/r \approx 0.024\,\%$ of a 5 μm droplet, and $\varepsilon$ rises by 0.06 % before narrowing starts.

Stationary fluctuations: $\sigma_{r^2} \propto t^{1/2}$

The DNS configuration has no mean updraught and statistically stationary $S'$. Sardina et al. (2015) show analytically and in DNS that under these conditions the width of the squared-radius distribution grows without bound as $t^{1/2}$. The Lanotte et al. (2009) DNS used the same $\sigma_{r^2}$ diagnostic and found that the broadening increases with the Reynolds number. That is, it increases with the scale of the fluctuations, which corresponds to larger $\sigma_S\sqrt\tau$ here. The same OU closure gives $\sigma_{r^2} \to 2G\sigma_S\sqrt{2\tau t}$ for $t \gg \tau$. The comparison is therefore the exponent. The DNS prefactors depend on their Reynolds numbers and cannot be transferred directly.

The test case uses monodisperse 10 μm droplets, $w = 0$, $\sigma_S = 0.1\,\%$, $\tau = 5$ s and 600 s, with the fit taken over $t \ge 10\tau$:

The analytic OU curve has exponent 0.514 in this window rather than 0.5 because of its $-\tau$ offset. The local exponent is $\tfrac12\,t/(t-\tau)$, which tends to $\tfrac12$. The Kelvin term adds real broadening, because small droplets grow more slowly: $d\ln\mathrm{Var}/dt = 2Ga\mu^{-3/2} \approx 2\times10^{-4}$ s⁻¹ at 10 μm. This raises the width by 3.3 % and the exponent by 0.013, and the bulk scheme and the superdroplets agree on both. In the frozen limit ($\tau = \infty$) both schemes give $p = 1.0009$. This is the $t \ll \tau$ limit of the same OU result, $\sigma_{r^2} = 2G\sigma_S t$, in which each droplet keeps its supersaturation for the whole run (cf. the stochastic model of Paoli & Shariff 2009).

Monodisperse droplets with $\sigma_S = 0$ stay monodisperse in both representations: $\sigma_{r^2}$ is at round-off level ($< 10^{-10}\mu$) and $\varepsilon < 10^{-6}$.

Worked example

The former examples/validation/spectral_broadening.jl (a parcel case, (removed by ONE-g, #456)) ran both cases, printed the tables above and wrote two SVG figures: $\varepsilon(t)$ in the ascending parcel, and $\sigma_{r^2}(t)$ on log-log axes against the OU curve and $t^{1/2}$.

Limitations

  • The fluctuation is prescribed. It does not relax on the phase-relaxation time as the droplets consume it. The in-cloud caveat on the previous page applies to both schemes equally.
  • The scheme handles activated droplets only, with no activation and no complete evaporation (bulk_condensation_step! throws if $\mu \le 0$). Activation is sgs_activation.
  • $\varepsilon$ depends on the gamma closure, and its error grows with the share of fluctuation-generated variance (see above).
  • There is no fall-speed spectrum or sub-grid precipitation fraction. Those are Phase 44 #8, deferred until P3 lands.
