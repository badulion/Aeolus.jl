Layered snowpack

A seasonal snowpack is a porous ice matrix that is, at once, a thermal insulator, a slowly flowing viscous material and a reservoir of latent heat. src/Snowpack.jl represents it the way CLM, SNOWPACK and Crocus do: a column of layers, each with ice mass $w_{ice}$, liquid mass $w_{liq}$ (kg m⁻²), thickness $Δz$ and temperature $T$, numbered from the surface down. LayeredSnowpack implements the surface shared core AbstractSurface interface, so its turbulent fluxes come from SurfaceExchange like every other surface.

1. Settling (compaction)

Mass is conserved while a layer settles, so the density follows from the fractional compaction rate

\[C_R = \frac{1}{Δz}\frac{\partial Δz}{\partial t} = -\frac{\partial \ln ρ}{\partial t} .\]

Overburden. A layer under a load creeps like a very viscous fluid, $C_R = -σ/η$. The load is the mass above plus half the layer's own, $P_s = w_i/2 + Σ_{j<i} w_j$. Two viscosity laws are provided:

lawviscosityload
Anderson (1976), CLM4$η = η_0 \exp[c_5 (T_f - T) + c_6 ρ_{ice}]$, $η_0 = 9·10^5$ kg s m⁻², $c_5 = 0.08$ K⁻¹, $c_6 = 0.023$ m³ kg⁻¹$P_s$ (kg m⁻²)
Vionnet et al. (2012), Crocus$η = f_1 f_2 η_0 (ρ/c_η) \exp[a_η (T_f - T) + b_η ρ]$, $η_0 = 7.62237·10^6$ Pa s, $a_η = 0.1$ K⁻¹, $b_η = 0.023$ m³ kg⁻¹, $c_η = 250$ kg m⁻³$σ = g P_s$ (Pa)

Both make cold and dense snow stiff: ten kelvin of cooling slows Crocus settling by $e^{-1}$, and every 100 kg m⁻³ of density by $e^{-2.3}$. In Crocus $f_1 = 1/(1 + 60\, w_{liq}/(ρ_w Δz))$ softens wet snow, and $f_2$ stiffens angular (depth-hoar) grains; $f_2$ needs the grain size, so it is a hook of the metamorphism scheme (default 1).

The Crocus law has a closed-form solution under constant load and temperature, which the tests use:

\[\frac{dρ}{dt} = K e^{-b_η ρ},\quad K = \frac{σ c_η}{f_1 f_2 η_0 e^{a_η (T_f-T)}} \;\Longrightarrow\; e^{b_η ρ(t)} = e^{b_η ρ_0} + b_η K t .\]

Destructive metamorphism. New snow also settles as its dendrites break down, independently of the load (Anderson 1976; CLM4 Eq. 7.40):

\[C_{R1} = -c_3 c_1 c_2 \exp[-c_4 (T_f - T)],\]

$c_3 = 2.777·10^{-6}$ s⁻¹, $c_4 = 0.04$ K⁻¹, $c_1 = 1$ below $ρ_{ice} = 100$ kg m⁻³ and $e^{-0.046(ρ_{ice}-100)}$ above, $c_2 = 2$ for wet snow. At the melting point it alone halves fresh snow in $\ln 2/c_3 = 2.9$ days. It is part of the Anderson law by default and can be added to the Crocus law (Vionnet2012Compaction(destructive = DestructiveMetamorphism())).

Melt thins a layer in proportion to the ice lost, at constant density — CLM's $C_{R3}$ applied exactly. No layer is compacted beyond the density of ice.

The layer ODE is integrated in $\ln Δz$ with the explicit midpoint rule, sub-stepped so no layer changes by more than 1 % per sub-step.

2. Fresh snow

New snow arrives at the density of Anderson (1976), $50 + 1.7 (T_a - T_f + 15)^{3/2}$ kg m⁻³ between $T_f - 15$ and $T_f + 2$, or of Crocus (Pahaut 1976; Vionnet et al. 2012 Eq. 1), $109 + 6 (T_a - T_f) + 26 U^{1/2}$ (at least 50), which adds wind packing. Its temperature is $\min(T_a, T_f)$; it is mixed into the top layer conserving enthalpy.

3. Heat conduction and the surface energy balance

\[C_i \frac{dT_i}{dt} = F_{i-1/2} - F_{i+1/2},\qquad F_{i+1/2} = \frac{T_i - T_{i+1}}{Δz_i/2k_i + Δz_{i+1}/2k_{i+1}},\]

with $C_i = w_{ice} c_{ice} + w_{liq} c_w$ and harmonic-mean interface conductances, solved θ-implicitly (tridiagonal; θ = 1 by default, ½ for Crank–Nicolson). The effective conductivity $k(ρ)$ is a choice of

The factor-two spread at a given density is physical — conduction depends on the bond network of the ice, not on ρ alone — and is one of the main sources of spread between snow models.

Upper boundary. The skin temperature is the temperature of the (thin, 2 cm) top layer. The surface energy balance $G = R_n - H - LE$ is evaluated by SurfaceExchange.turbulent_fluxes at $T_1$ and $T_1 + δT$ and linearised, $G(T_1) ≈ G_0 + (∂G/∂T)(T_1 - T_1^n)$; the linearisation enters the implicit matrix, so the stiff coupling of a thin top layer to radiation and turbulence is stable at hourly steps. Every returned flux is updated to the same time-weighted skin temperature, so the fluxes handed to the atmosphere satisfy $R_n = H + LE + G$ exactly and $G$ is exactly the heat that entered the pack. Latent heat is that of sublimation and the skin humidity is saturation over ice. If the pack sublimates away within a step, $E$ and $LE$ are cut to the mass actually removed and the unspent latent heat stays in $G$ and passes to the substrate, so the atmosphere never receives vapour the pack did not have.

Lower boundary. Either a fixed snow/ground interface temperature (SnowGroundTemperature) or a prescribed flux (SnowBottomFlux).

Melt. A layer left above $T_f$ melts $\min(C(T - T_f)/L_f,\, w_{ice})$ and returns to $T_f$; a surplus passes downward, and from the bottom layer into the ground.

4. Layers

Target thicknesses grow geometrically from the surface, $Δz_i = 0.02 \cdot 1.6^{i-1}$ m, up to 10 layers. Layers thinner than half their target (or lighter than 0.01 kg m⁻²) merge with their thinner neighbour, layers thicker than twice their target split, and the thinnest pair merges when there are too many. Merging conserves ice, water, depth and enthalpy exactly (the merged temperature is the heat-capacity-weighted mean).

5. Conservation

With enthalpy referred to liquid water at the melting point, $h = w_{ice}[c_{ice}(T - T_f) - L_f] + w_{liq} c_w (T - T_f)$, the SnowBudget of a pack closes

\[ΔM = \text{snowfall} + \text{rain} - \text{sublimation} - \text{runoff},\qquad ΔH = ∫G\,dt - ∫F_{base}\,dt + \text{advected} - \text{runoff enthalpy}\]

to round-off over any sequence of steps (tested over 12 days of storms, radiative cooling, melt, rain and hoar deposition: residuals $2·10^{-13}$ kg m⁻² and $1.4·10^{-7}$ J m⁻² on $4.6·10^7$ J m⁻²).

8. Liquid water: retention, refreezing, percolation

Meltwater and rain enter the top layer as liquid. InstantSnowRunoff (the default, kept so that the dry-season results above are unchanged) drains it all within the step. Two schemes keep it in the pack.

Irreducible water. Snow holds liquid against gravity up to an irreducible volumetric content $θ_r(θ_i)$, with $θ_i = w_{ice}/(ρ_{ice}Δz)$. ColeouLesaffre1998Retention is the laboratory drainage result of Coléou & Lesaffre (1998) in the form used by SNOWPACK (Wever et al. 2014, Eq. 1):

\[θ_r = \begin{cases} 0.0264 + 0.0099\,(1-θ_i)/θ_i, & θ_i ≤ 0.23\\ 0.08 - 0.1023\,(θ_i - 0.03), & 0.23 < θ_i ≤ 0.812\\ 0, & θ_i > 0.812 \end{cases}\]

(0.062 at 200 kg m⁻³, 0.050 at 300, 0.038 at 400). PoreFractionRetention(fraction = f) is $θ_r = f(1-θ_i)$: Crocus's 5 % of the pore volume (Vionnet et al. 2012, Eq. 19) or CLM's $S_r = 0.033$ (Oleson et al. 2010, Eq. 7.26). For 0.1 m of 300 kg m⁻³ snow, Coléou & Lesaffre holds 4.96 kg m⁻², Crocus 3.36 and CLM 2.22.

Refreezing. Liquid water in a layer colder than $T_f$ refreezes at constant enthalpy. With cold content $C(T_f - T)$ ($C = w_{ice}c_{ice} + w_{liq}c_w$), everything freezes if $C(T_f-T) ≥ w_{liq}L_f$ and the layer warms to $T_f - (C(T_f-T) - w_{liq}L_f)/C'$; otherwise $C(T_f-T)/L_f$ freezes and the layer ends at $T_f$. The ice fills pores: the thickness does not change (Vionnet et al. 2012, Sect. 3.10). Water that arrives in a layer is first mixed in at constant enthalpy, then refrozen. The refrozen mass is booked in SnowBudget.refreeze, so that $Δ\sum w_{liq} = \text{melt} + \text{rain} - \text{refreeze} - \text{runoff}$.

Bucket (SnowBucketPercolation, Crocus and CLM). From the top down, each layer refreezes what its cold content allows, retains $ρ_wΔzθ_r$, and passes the excess to the layer below within the step. Water leaving the bottom layer is runoff. If refrozen water would fill a layer's pores, the water that does not fit passes on; capillary barriers and ponding are not represented.

Gravity drainage (SnowGravityDrainage). This is Richards' equation without the capillary-pressure term. It is the kinematic-wave model of Colbeck (1972), used in SNTHERM (Jordan 1991) and early Crocus:

\[q = ρ_w K_s S_e^{3},\qquad S_e = \frac{θ_w-θ_r}{θ_s-θ_r},\qquad θ_s = 1-θ_i,\]

with the saturated hydraulic conductivity from the permeability of Calonne et al. (2012), $K_s = 3.0\,r_{es}^2 e^{-0.013ρ_{dry}}\,ρ_w g/μ$. The layer's optical grain radius (grain, which #35 evolves) is $r_{es}$, and $μ = 1.792·10^{-3}$ Pa s (Wever et al. 2014). Wever et al. (2014, Eq. 14) print the coefficient as 0.75; that value belongs with the optical grain diameter ($0.75\,d^2 = 3.0\,r^2$), and applied to the radius it would understate $K_s$ fourfold. The layer fluxes are upwinded explicitly. Sub-steps keep the Courant number of the kinematic wave, $dq/dθ = 3K_sS_e^2/(θ_s-θ_r)$, at 0.5 or below. No layer drains below $θ_r$ or fills beyond $θ_s$, and water arriving in a cold layer refreezes. Under a constant input $q_0$ on dry snow the wetting front is a shock. Behind it the water content is $θ_1$, set by $ρ_wK_sS_e(θ_1)^3 = q_0$, and it advances at $v = q_0/(ρ_wθ_1)$. Capillary suction and the van Genuchten retention curve of full Richards models (Wever et al. 2014) are not included, so neither are capillary barriers.
