Nest hierarchies, non-aligned and stretched children

Until #328 a nested run was one parent and one child, the child aligned with its parent's cells by an integer ratio and uniform in z. This page describes the generalisation along both lines:

  • geometry — a child whose refinement is rational (five child cells in two parent cells), whose origin and extent are off the parent faces, or whose vertical grid is stretched (NestAxis, NestedGrid(parent, child));
  • topology — a tree of domains: children, grandchildren and siblings, advanced by recursive subcycling (NestHierarchy, add_nest!, step_nested!).

Both reuse the operators of the nesting page, the time coupling of the time-coupling page, the boundary data of the boundary page and the feedback of the two-way page; nothing is forked. The tests are in test/era2_nest_hierarchy.jl; the long run is the validation case validation/era2_nest_hierarchy_longrun.jl.

Geometry: one map per axis

A NestAxis describes the parent–child pair along one axis by coordinates rather than by an integer ratio:

  • the parent and child face coordinates, storage-indexed. A child face within $10^{-9}$ of a child spacing of a parent face is replaced by that parent face's bits, so the Lagrange bases of the prolongation are exactly 0 and 1 there;
  • the common faces: parent faces inside the child that are also child faces;
  • the conservation box: the parent cells between the first and the last common face inside the child, and the child cells of the same box;
  • the overlap length $\omega(c, P)$ of every box parent cell $P$ with every child cell $c$ (compressed rows);
  • for every box parent face, the coincident child face, or the two child faces that bracket it and the linear weight between them.

NestedGrid(parent, child) builds the three axes from the meshes and checks that the child interior lies inside the parent interior, that the child is at least as fine as the parent everywhere, and that the child's storage (halos included) lies inside the parent's. nested_grid accepts rational ratios ((5//2, 2, 2)) and extents off the parent faces.

An axis that is aligned with an integer ratio and a uniform child keeps the integer plans and kernels of N-nest-a; when all three are, the nest is aligned and every operator is bit-identical to before #328. NestedGrid(parent, child; plan = :overlap) forces the general path on an aligned geometry, which is how the tests prove that the integer path is its special case.

Operators on overlap pieces

Prolongation. A child cell $c$ that straddles parent faces is integrated piecewise,

\[\bar\phi_c = \frac{1}{|c|}\sum_{P \cap c \neq \emptyset} \int_{c \cap P} \mathcal{P}'_{s(P)}(\xi)\,d\xi ,\]

where $\mathcal{P}_{s(P)}$ is the degree-$n_s$ polynomial interpolating the primitive at the faces of the stencil centred on $P$ (the coordinate rule of _cons_weights_nodes, applied once per piece; the pieces' weights are summed into one stencil row, one parent cell wider). Each stencil polynomial matches the primitive at its own cell's faces, so the child cells of every block of parent cells bounded by common faces carry the block's integral exactly: conservation is structural per block. $R\circ P = \mathrm{id}$ holds per parent cell only where every parent face is common (the aligned and the stretched-child cases); with a rational ratio it holds per block. The normal velocity at a child face is the nodal Lagrange interpolant at the face's coordinate, as before.

Restriction. A box parent cell is the overlap-weighted mean

\[\phi_P = \frac{\sum_c \omega_x\,\omega_y\,\omega_z\,\chi_c\,\phi_c} {\sum_c \omega_x\,\omega_y\,\omega_z\,\chi_c},\]

with $\chi$ the fluid mask and $\omega_z$ in the measure coordinate (the reference mass $\mu$ for a sound-proof pair), so $\sum_P V_P\phi_P = \sum_c V_c\phi_c$ over the box exactly. A parent face on a common plane takes the overlap-area mean of the child faces on it (the child's flux); a box face between child face planes (inside the box, rational ratio) the linear interpolation of the two bracketing planes' means.

Flux correction, registers, composite integrals. The interface planes are the box planes, which are common faces, so the interface flux correction and the refluxing remain exact: the fine flux of a parent face is the overlap-area mean of the child faces on it, the registers are allocated over the box and book the child faces on the box planes (interior child faces when the box is shrunk), the refluxed parent cells are those just outside the box, and the composite integral counts the parent outside the box and the child inside it. The strip between a shrunk box and the child's edge is computed by the child but owned by the parent (an extended buffer).

A stretched child

A child on a z-StretchedGrid has its own face heights in its vertical axis. The vertical measure takes the child faces from it (the mass measure maps box faces to the child's $\mu$), restriction weights are the layer overlaps, and the volume elements of the composite integral are the child's layer thicknesses. The child's open faces are OpenBoundary faces on the stretched mesh: plane coordinates from the mesh's face and centre heights, lateral face areas $\Delta y\,\Delta z_k$ in the single mass-flux correction (correct_boundary_mass_flux!); its projection is the stretched Poisson solver with Neumann sides, which _default_solver(::StretchedGrid) already picks. A radiation outflow on the bottom or top of a stretched mesh is refused (lateral faces only).

The nest tree and its time stepping

A NestHierarchy holds the domains and the links between them. Each link is a NestedRun of that parent–child pair: its boundary data, flux registers, feedback (one-way and two-way links mix freely), sponge, solid mask, ledger and statistics. One root step (step_nested!(h)) is Berger & Oliger's recursion (as in PALM, Hellsten et al. 2021 §3.1, and WRF):

advance(d, step h):     # domain d takes one step of size h (with retries)
    for each child link L of d: boundary data B(t_d) → L.s_old
    step d (register hooks of its uplink and of all its child links)
      rejected → restore d only, retry at h/2 (no descendant has moved)
    for each L: B(t_d + h) → L.s_new, open L's window [t_d, t_d + h]
    for each L: the child subcycles to t_d + h, each accepted
                substep calling advance(child, substep) — the recursion
    for each L: close the window (clock snap, two-way feedback without
                the re-projection, ledger)
    one re-projection of d if any child is two-way

Each domain uses its own CFLConstraints, SGS closure and core; the number of substeps comes from the CFL limit (re-evaluated before every substep) or from the largest refinement ratio, rounded up. Under sync = :shared_min_dt every domain takes one step of the smallest limit over the tree. The boundary data flow down level by level through the #58 contract (a grandchild's data come from the child's state at the child's substep ends, linearly blended); the feedback flows up in post order: a grandchild feeds back at the end of each of its parent's substeps, before the parent's own feedback to the root. That ordering is what makes the composite integral exact at every level — the child's integral changes only by its boundary fluxes, which the root–child registers booked, plus the grandchild's refluxing inside the child, which conserves child plus grandchild.

Siblings. Several children of one domain, each with its own ratio, geometry and feedback, must not overlap (the parent cells their interiors cover are disjoint); two two-way siblings' boxes must be at least one parent cell apart, or a cell refluxed by one would be anterpolated by the other. Overlapping siblings are refused: a priority rule needs the higher-priority sibling as boundary data of the other, a separate feature. Within a step the siblings advance in link order; they read only the parent's state at the window ends, write disjoint parent cells, and the parent is re-projected once after all their feedback, so the result does not depend on the order (bit for bit, tested).

Proper nesting. add_nest! refuses a child whose open faces' halo stencils read parent cells outside the parent's interior, unless that side of the parent is periodic (proper_nesting_violations): in a hierarchy those would be the parent's own boundary halos.

Rollback. A rejected step (non-finite state, solver failure, max_velocity; the root also divergence_tol) restores that domain only — its state, bound scalars of all its links, SGS memory, open-boundary buffers and pending register bookings — and is redone at half its size; no descendant has moved within it. Only numerical failures are rejected; a MethodError propagates. Escalation to the parent level (restoring a subtree and halving the coarser step) is not implemented: a domain that exhausts its retries raises an error, as a NestedRun child does.

NestedRun keeps its interface and its numbers: its parent step is split into the pieces the hierarchy reuses (_prepare_window!, the parent advance, _open_window!), and without a subtree the code path is the former one. Golden checksums of 20 steps of six configurations (one- and two-way, step! and step_rk3!, fixed and adaptive, moist, sponge, solid), recorded before the change, were reproduced bit for bit when the hierarchy landed (the generator and its checksums were removed later: tests compare within tolerances, not bits).

Rollback. A NaN injected into one grandchild substep (one-way grandchild) rolls back the grandchild only: the root and the child are bit-identical to a clean run, the grandchild link records one retry and one extra substep.

Limits

  • Overlapping siblings are refused (no priority rule).
  • No escalating rollback across levels (a domain that exhausts its retries raises an error).
  • Siblings advance one after the other (threading them is a separate change).
  • AdaptiveMesh keeps its own index-space proper-nesting check (block margins in its level index space), which is a different criterion from the stencil reach checked here.
  • The horizontal axes must be uniform (only z may be stretched), and a rational ratio or an extent off the parent faces in nested_grid needs a uniform parent (on a stretched parent build the child mesh and call NestedGrid(parent, child)).
  • InflowTurbulence's parent-resolved statistics need an integer horizontal ratio.
