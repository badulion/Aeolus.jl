Urban facet conduction

A city stores heat in its fabric. By day, roofs, walls and roads take up a large part of the absorbed sunshine; by night they give it back, which is a principal cause of the nocturnal urban heat island. How much they store and how late they release it depends on what they are made of and in what order the materials are stacked. A brick wall and an insulated curtain wall with the same outside temperature pass very different heat to the street at night and to the room by day. The single-slab surface of the urban example, one heat capacity per surface class, cannot tell them apart. src/UrbanSurface.jl models each facet as a multi-layer conduction column, following TEB (Masson 2000) and PALM-USM (Resler et al. 2017).

1. The equation and the grid

Across a facet, lateral gradients are small compared with the gradient through its thickness. The 1-D heat equation

\[\rho c\,\frac{\partial T}{\partial t} = \frac{\partial}{\partial z}\Bigl(k\,\frac{\partial T}{\partial z}\Bigr)\]

holds in each homogeneous layer, with $T$ and the flux $-k\,\partial T/\partial z$ continuous at material interfaces. $z$ runs from the outer face ($z = 0$) inward.

Each WallLayer is split into equal finite-volume cells, and material interfaces always lie on cell faces. Temperatures sit at cell centres. The conductance between cells $i$ and $i+1$ is that of the two half cells in series,

\[K_{i+1/2} = \Bigl(\frac{\Delta z_i}{2k_i} + \frac{\Delta z_{i+1}}{2k_{i+1}}\Bigr)^{-1},\]

which is the exact flux across an interface for a piecewise-linear profile. A steady composite wall is therefore reproduced exactly at any resolution, even with one cell per layer.

2. Boundary conditions: one Robin form for everything

Both faces take the same boundary condition. The heat flux entering the column is

\[F = h\,(T_\text{env} - T_\text{face}) + q .\]

This single form covers all the cases a facet needs:

case$h$$T_\text{env}$$q$
prescribed face temperature (deep ground)$\infty$$T$—
prescribed flux, adiabatic0—$q$
interior face ↔ indoor air (ISO 6946)$1/R_{si}$$T_\text{in}$0

The face temperature is eliminated with the half-cell conductance $K = 2k/\Delta z$. The boundary flux then becomes an affine function of the adjacent cell temperature $T_c$:

\[F = \frac{K h}{K+h}\,(T_\text{env} - T_c) + \frac{K}{K+h}\,q , \qquad T_\text{face} = \frac{K T_c + h T_\text{env} + q}{K + h}.\]

Because the column only ever sees a ColumnBoundary, it knows nothing about the atmosphere. The building energy model (Phase 50) closes the same column with its own indoor-air temperature.

3. Time stepping

The θ-scheme

\[\rho_i c_i \Delta z_i\,\frac{T_i^{n+1} - T_i^n}{\Delta t} = \theta\,R_i(T^{n+1}) + (1-\theta)\,R_i(T^n),\]

with $R_i$ the net flux into cell $i$ (including boundary fluxes), is a tridiagonal system, solved by the Thomas algorithm in $O(n)$. $\theta = 1/2$ (Crank–Nicolson) is second order and A-stable. $\theta = 1$ (backward Euler) is first order but L-stable: it damps the stiffest modes monotonically, which is preferable when $\Delta t$ is far longer than $\Delta z^2/\kappa$ of the thinnest cell, for example a 1 cm waterproofing layer stepped hourly. $\theta < 1/2$ is only conditionally stable (explicit Euler on 2 cm brick cells at $\Delta t = 600$ s diverges within 100 steps), so the column rejects it.

step_conduction! returns the θ-weighted boundary fluxes it applied. Summing the cell equations telescopes the interior conductances away, so

\[\sum_i \rho_i c_i \Delta z_i (T_i^{n+1} - T_i^n) = (F_\text{outer} + F_\text{inner})\,\Delta t\]

holds exactly. Energy is conserved to round-off, not to truncation error.

4. The outer face: surface energy balance

The skin temperature $T_s$ is the temperature of the outer face. It has no heat capacity of its own; the first cell provides that. Its energy balance

\[F(T_s) \equiv R_n(T_s) - H(T_s) - LE(T_s) = G\]

is nonlinear, through $\varepsilon\sigma T_s^4$ and through the stability dependence of $H$. It is linearised about the current skin temperature,

\[F(T) \approx F_0 + F'\,(T - T_s^n),\qquad F' = \frac{\partial R_n}{\partial T} - \frac{\partial H}{\partial T} - \frac{\partial LE}{\partial T} < 0,\]

where $F_0$ and $F'$ come from SurfaceExchange.turbulent_fluxes (Monin–Obukhov, with $F'$ by a one-sided difference so the stability dependence of $C_H$ is included). The linearised balance is exactly the Robin condition $h = -F'$, $T_\text{env} = T_s^n$, $q = F_0$: the combined radiative and turbulent surface coefficient. The column step is then implicit in the skin temperature, which stops a thin, highly conductive outer cell from oscillating, and the new skin temperature is the outer face temperature.

The fluxes returned by step_surface! are those actually applied, evaluated on the same linearisation. So $R_n - H - LE = G$ holds to round-off, and $G\,\Delta t$ minus the interior flux times $\Delta t$ is the change in stored heat. Each step is one Newton iteration on the skin balance. An inconsistent initial skin is pulled onto the balance with a quadratically falling residual (measured: 360 → 0.45 → 5·10⁻⁶ → 6·10⁻¹¹ W m⁻²).

Vertical walls fall outside Monin–Obukhov similarity. For them a facet may use a fixed convective coefficient, $H = h_c (T_s - T_\text{air})$ (h_convective). Wind-dependent wall correlations are not implemented yet; the facet–radiosity coupling takes the air state next to each facet as an input.

5. Materials and optics: one source of truth

thermal_material holds conductivity, density and specific heat only, each row cited: Bergman et al. (2011, Tables A.1, A.3) for masonry, concrete, glass, steel, plasters, insulation and soils, and Goward (1981) via Heisler & Brazel (2010, Table 2-3) for asphalt. Incropera's asphalt, $k = 0.062$ W m⁻¹ K⁻¹, is an order of magnitude below every urban compilation and would make a road an insulator. Heisler & Brazel also tabulate the thermal admittance $\mu = \sqrt{k\rho c}$: the asphalt row reproduces their 1204 J m⁻² K⁻¹ s⁻¹ᐟ² to 0.003 %.

Optical properties are not restated. An exposed material carries the name of its SurfaceOptics spectral-library entry. A facet's albedo is the white-sky broadband albedo of that spectral curve, and its emissivity is the library's broadband value. A steel roof therefore inherits the metal-roof $\varepsilon = 0.25$. Radiosity.UrbanMaterials derives its class greys from the same library, so the two cannot drift apart.
