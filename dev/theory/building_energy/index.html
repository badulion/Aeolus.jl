Building energy: indoor air and thermal mass

A heated or cooled building feeds heat into the urban canopy through its walls, windows and exhaust. How much it feeds, and when, depends on the indoor temperature. The indoor temperature in turn depends on how much heat the building's fabric has stored. A concrete office and a timber house under the same weather keep very different indoor temperatures, and they release their stored heat at different hours. src/BuildingEnergy.jl models a single thermal zone. Its capacitances are resolved: every massive element is a multi-layer conduction column, and the zone air is one prognostic node. This follows the building energy model that TEB couples to its facets (TEB-BEM; Bueno et al. 2011).

1. The network

 outdoor air / sun / sky ─┐          ┌─ glazing  U_w A_w ─┐
                          │ columns  │                    │
  envelope elements  ═════╪══════════╪══ h_j A_j ──┐      │
  (EnvelopeElement,       │          │             │      │
   FacetElement)          │          │          T_air ────┴── outdoor air
                          │          │   C_a       │      H_ve (infiltration,
  internal mass      ═════╧══════════╧══ h_j A_j ──┘            ventilation)
  (InternalMass)

The air node. The zone air has heat capacity $C_a = ρ c_p V$, with $ρ = p/(R_d T)$ and $c_p$ taken from MoistThermodynamics. It is coupled to:

  • each interior surface $j$, through the ISO 6946 combined surface coefficient $h_j = 1/R_{si}$;
  • the outdoors, through the glazing $U_w A_w$ and the air exchange (ISO 13790 §9.3)

\[H_{ve} = ρ c_p V \frac{n_{inf} + (1-η)\,n_{vent}}{3600},\]

where $n$ are air-change rates (h⁻¹) and $η$ is the heat-recovery efficiency;

  • the convective part $(1-f_r)$ of the internal gains, and any HVAC heat.

The massive elements. Each is an UrbanSurface conduction column. BuildingEnergy uses that column as it is and keeps no copy of the solver.

  • An EnvelopeElement is closed on the outside by outdoor air through $1/R_{se}$ plus an absorbed irradiance (the sol-air form), by a fixed ground temperature, or adiabatically.
  • A FacetElement is an UrbanFacet, so its outside is the full surface shared core surface energy balance.
  • An InternalMass has one or both faces in the room.

Every interior face receives the imposed flux

\[q = \frac{f_r Φ_{int} + Φ_{sol}}{\sum_j A_j},\]

which is the radiative part of the internal gains plus the transmitted solar gain, spread over the exposed area.

One node for air and radiant temperature. Surface-to-surface long-wave exchange is folded into $h_j$, as in the ISO 6946 coefficients. The node is therefore strictly a blend of the air temperature and the mean radiant temperature. The operative temperature $(T_{air} + T_{mrt})/2$ is available as a diagnostic.

2. The coupled implicit step

The air node's time constant, $C_a / \sum h_j A_j$, is a few minutes. Any useful step is longer than that, so the air balance

\[C_a \frac{T^{n+1}-T^n}{Δt} = \sum_j A_j\bigl(q - F_{in,j}(T^{n+1})\bigr) + (U_w A_w + H_{ve})(T_{out} - T^{n+1}) + (1-f_r)Φ_{int} + Φ_{hvac}\]

is solved implicitly and simultaneously with every column. Each column takes a θ-scheme step with $T^{n+1}$ as its interior environment. That step is affine in $T^{n+1}$: the new cell temperatures and both face fluxes have the form $a + b\,T^{n+1}$. This holds for a facet too. Its outer linearisation depends only on its skin temperature at the start of the step, and that value is fixed.

Each element is therefore stepped twice from the same initial state, at $T^n$ and at $T^n + 1$ K. The two solves give the base solution and its exact derivative. The air balance then reduces to one linear equation. Once it is solved, every element is set to the combination that matches the new air temperature.

The result is identical, to round-off, to stepping each column directly with the final air temperature (tested to 1e-9 K). The step is unconditionally stable and conserves the zone's heat content exactly. It costs two tridiagonal solves per column per step.

3. The ISO 13790 reference model

ISO 13790's simple hourly method (§7.2.2 and Annex C) represents a zone by five conductances and one capacitance $C_m$. The conductances link the outdoor, supply-air, air ($θ_{air}$), central surface ($θ_s$) and mass ($θ_m$) nodes, and $θ_m$ is advanced by Crank–Nicolson. iso13790_zone derives its parameters from the same construction as a BuildingZone:

  • $H_{tr,op} = \sum U_j A_j$, and $H_{tr,em} = (1/H_{tr,op} - 1/H_{tr,ms})^{-1}$;
  • $C_m = \sum κ_j A_j$ and $A_m = C_m^2/\sum A_j κ_j^2$ (§12.3.1).

Here $κ_j$ is the ISO 13786 Annex A simplified internal areal heat capacity. It is the $ρcd$ of the layers from the room side up to the smallest of three depths: half the element thickness, the first insulating layer, and 10 cm.

The 5R1C model uses the same steady conductances as the resolved zone but a single capacitance, so it is a meaningful check. It is not a substitute for data.

Means. The means agree to 0.3 % (heavy) and 2.1 % (light) of the indoor–outdoor excess. The two networks share their steady conductances; they differ only in where the gains and the glazing attach.

Amplitudes. The amplitudes differ by −29 % and +20 %. With one lumped capacitance, 5R1C under-damps the heavy box and over-damps the light one. Neither sign is a bug in either model. It is the known limit of a one-capacitance representation of distributed mass.

Peak times. The single capacitance also puts the heavy box's peak about 2 h later.

The test bounds on this comparison are 3 % of the excess for the mean, 40 % for the amplitude and 3 h for the peak. They are envelopes set after measurement, not physics expectations.

Not validated against observations. No building measurements were used. The phase target, simulated against measured energy demand for an instrumented building, needs occupancy and HVAC (issues #32 and #33). ASHRAE 140 (BESTEST) cases need its weather file and were not run.

5. Scope

This page covers issue #31 only. HVAC control and its exhaust heat (#32) are on HVAC demand and exhaust heat. Occupancy schedules and the validation against a measured building (#33), anthropogenic heat coupling to the canopy (#29, see Anthropogenic heat from the building energy model), and glazing optics (#27) are all separate issues. Q_hvac, Q_solar and FacetElement are the hooks they plug into.
