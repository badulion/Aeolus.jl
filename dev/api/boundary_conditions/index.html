Boundary conditions

Per-face boundary conditions, grouped into a FieldBoundaryConditions container. fill_halos! applies Periodic and NoFlux on every face and the two impermeable z-walls, free-slip ImpermeableWall and no-slip / moving NoSlipWall (P59-a, #172; theory in Transition and wall-bounded flow). FluxBC(Γ) is the fixed-gradient top of a cell-centred scalar (N-parent-b, #254; below). ValueBC is declared but has no halo fill. Open lateral and top faces fed by boundary data are OpenBoundary (N-open-b, #255), optionally with a convective or Orlanski radiation outflow (N-open-c, #260); see the last sections.

Every fill is a sequence of launch!es of the per-axis halo kernels (KA-f, #297): one launch per fill phase, x then y then z, bit for bit the pre-port plane copies at every thread count. The multigrid level halo shares the same kernels. The rules are in The kernel port (section KA-f).

Fields filled together are filled as a set, fill_halos!((u, v, w), bcs) or fill_halos!((u, v, w, θ), (bcs, bcs, bcs, θ_bcs)) (SC-b, #402): threaded, the whole set is two launches — the x and y phases of every field by chunks of k-planes, then the z phases by chunks of j-rows — because every x/y phase stays inside one plane and every z phase inside one column. The result is the field-by-field fill, bit for bit; below the backend's threshold and at one thread it is the field-by-field fill. A single-field fill_halos!(f, bcs) is a set of one.

BC types

Aeolus.BoundaryConditions.AbstractBC — Type
AbstractBC

Root type for per-face boundary conditions. Six BCs are grouped by face into a FieldBoundaryConditions container.

Concrete types:

  • Periodic — copy from the opposite face.
  • NoFlux — homogeneous Neumann (ghost = mirror interior).
  • ImpermeableWall — free-slip rigid wall for velocity, mirror for scalars.
  • NoSlipWall — rigid wall with a prescribed tangential velocity (no slip, or a moving wall).
  • FreeStreamBoundary — top face with zero shear and a prescribed normal (transpiration) velocity.
  • OpenBoundary — lateral or top face whose velocities and scalars come from boundary data (Dirichlet), with a Neumann φ, and optionally a convective or Orlanski radiation outflow.
  • ValueBC — Dirichlet with prescribed value.
  • FluxBC — Neumann with prescribed flux ∂/∂n = f.
Aeolus.BoundaryConditions.Periodic — Type

Periodic wrap on this face.

Aeolus.BoundaryConditions.NoFlux — Type

Zero-gradient (no-flux) reflecting boundary.

Aeolus.BoundaryConditions.ValueBC — Type
ValueBC(v)

Dirichlet boundary condition: field takes value v at the boundary. (Applied by fill_halos! for scalar cell-centered fields at z-boundaries.)

Aeolus.BoundaryConditions.FluxBC — Type
FluxBC(Γ)

Neumann boundary condition: a prescribed outward normal gradient ∂φ/∂n = Γ of a cell-centred scalar. Supported as the top (z) face of a scalar's boundary conditions (N-parent-b, #254), above a wall or a no-flux bottom: the fixed-gradient top of a free atmosphere (PALM; DALES, Heus et al. 2010, Eq. 39), with Γ = ∂θ/∂z, usually the initial lapse rate. fill_halos! sets the ghost cells to

φ(N + h) = φ(N + 1 − h) + Γ · (z_c(N + h) − z_c(N + 1 − h)),     h = 1 … Hz,

so the first ghost is φ_N + Γ·Δz_f with Δz_f the centre spacing across the top face (dz on a RegularGrid, the face spacing on a StretchedGrid), a linear profile of slope Γ is continued exactly into every halo layer, and a diffusion operator with diffusivity κ carries the flux κΓ (per unit area, into the domain for Γ > 0 and warmer air above) through the top face.

FieldBoundaryConditions(:periodic, :periodic; bottom = :noflux, top = FluxBC(Γ)). Velocity fields (FaceX/Y/Z) cannot take it. The homogeneous part (the operator an implicit solver iterates with, see homogeneous_bcs) is FluxBC(0), the mirror (no-flux) halo.

Container

Aeolus.BoundaryConditions.FieldBoundaryConditions — Type
FieldBoundaryConditions(west, east, south, north, bottom, top)
FieldBoundaryConditions(x = :periodic, y = x, z = y; bottom = z, top = z)

Six BCs, one per face. Convenience constructor FieldBoundaryConditions(:periodic) gives triply-periodic BCs; FieldBoundaryConditions(:periodic, :periodic, :wall) gives periodic in x, y and impermeable walls at z=0 and z=Lz. Recognized symbols: :periodic, :noflux, :wall (free slip) and, in z only, :noslip (a stationary NoSlipWall).

The bottom and top keywords set the two z faces separately, as a symbol or as a BC object — e.g. an open channel FieldBoundaryConditions(:periodic, :periodic; bottom = :noslip, top = :wall) or a Couette lid FieldBoundaryConditions(:periodic, :periodic; bottom = :noslip, top = NoSlipWall(U, 0)). A z-periodic face cannot be mixed with a non-periodic one, and a mixed bottom/top pair must be two walls (:wall, :noslip, NoSlipWall) or a wall below a FreeStreamBoundary or OpenBoundary top (open boxes are built with open_boundary_conditions); anything else, e.g. bottom = :noslip, top = :noflux, throws an ArgumentError rather than failing at the first halo fill.

Halo fill

Aeolus.BoundaryConditions.fill_halos! — Function
fill_halos!(K::DiffusivityTensor, bcs) -> K

Fill the halos of the six components of K with bcs (the boundary conditions of the cell-centred eddy coefficients).

fill_halos!(field, bcs)

Populate halo cells of field according to bcs (a FieldBoundaryConditions). Multi-dispatches on the field's Location so that e.g. an impermeable wall zeros w at the wall face but mirrors u, v.

The axes are filled x, then y, then z, over the whole face slab, so an edge or corner holds the value of the last axis that reaches it. Each fill phase is one launch! of a per-axis halo kernel (KA-f, #297): threaded for large faces, serial below the backend's threshold, and identical bits at every thread count. When the halo of the field is large enough to thread, the whole fill is the two launches of the set fill, fill_halos!((f,), bcs) (SC-b, #402).

fill_halos!(fields::Tuple, bcs; keys = data of each field) -> fields

Fill the halos of a set of fields together: bcs is one FieldBoundaryConditions for all of them or a tuple with one per field (keys, the per-field key of OpenBoundary data, likewise). The result is that of fill_halos!(f, bcs) on each field in turn, bit for bit; the fields must not share storage.

Threaded (SC-b, #402), the whole set is two launches: the x and y phases of every field, a chunk of k-planes per work item, then the z phases, a chunk of j-rows per work item (every x/y phase stays inside a plane and every z phase inside a column, so the axis order x, y, z of the edges and corners is kept). Below the backend's threshold (counted over the halo points of the whole set) and at one thread the fields are filled one after the other with the per-phase launches.

fill_halos!(fields::AbstractVector{<:Field}, bcs::FieldBoundaryConditions) -> fields

The set fill for a vector of fields with one set of boundary conditions (the tracers of a run): each field is its own key, the result is that of fill_halos!(f, bcs) on each field in turn, bit for bit, and threaded the whole vector is the same two launches as a tuple.

Wall conditions

Aeolus.BoundaryConditions.ImpermeableWall — Type
ImpermeableWall

Free-slip, impermeable rigid wall. Applied per-face location:

  • On w @ FaceZ at a z-wall: sets the wall-face value to zero every halo fill, and uses anti-symmetric halo extension so the wall face stays exactly zero.
  • On u, v @ FaceX/FaceY at a z-wall: mirror reflection (free-slip).
  • On cell-centered fields (p, θ) at a z-wall: mirror reflection (Neumann).

Together these are the discrete BCs consistent with CosineFFTPoissonSolver.

Aeolus.BoundaryConditions.NoSlipWall — Type
NoSlipWall(u_wall = 0, v_wall = 0)
NoSlipWall(; u_wall = 0, v_wall = 0)

Impermeable rigid wall with a prescribed tangential velocity (u_wall, v_wall) — a stationary no-slip wall when both are zero, a moving wall (Couette lid, Stokes plate) otherwise. Valid on the bottom and top (z) faces. Applied per location:

  • u @ FaceX, v @ FaceY are cell-centred in z, so the wall lies half a cell below the first interior value. The halo is filled antisymmetrically about the wall value, u_ghost(h) = 2 u_wall − u_interior(h), so the average of the first interior value and its ghost — the wall-face value seen by every stencil — is exactly u_wall, and the one-sided wall gradient (u₁ − u_ghost)/Δz = 2(u₁ − u_wall)/Δz is second-order accurate.
  • w @ FaceZ and cell-centred fields (p, θ, νₜ) are treated exactly as by ImpermeableWall: w = 0 on the wall face with an antisymmetric halo, mirror (Neumann) for scalars. The pressure problem is therefore unchanged and the CosineFFTPoissonSolver remains the exact solver.

A NoSlipWall may face an ImpermeableWall on the opposite side (an open channel, bottom = :noslip, top = :wall); see FieldBoundaryConditions.

The plane-mean wall stress ν ∂u/∂z is returned by wall_shear_stress(sim; side) (a method of the CutCell generic).

References: Ferziger & Perić (2002), Computational Methods for Fluid Dynamics, 3rd ed., §8.10 (ghost-cell no-slip walls on a staggered grid); Pope (2000), Turbulent Flows, §7.1 (laminar Couette and Poiseuille flow).

Aeolus.BoundaryConditions.is_wall — Function
is_wall(bc) -> Bool

true for the impermeable z-wall types (ImpermeableWall, NoSlipWall): the face carries w = 0 and a Neumann pressure condition. Every solver that special-cases a wall asks this instead of bc isa ImpermeableWall, so no-slip walls cannot fall back to free slip at a missed dispatch site.

Aeolus.BoundaryConditions.homogeneous_bcs — Function
homogeneous_bcs(bcs::FieldBoundaryConditions) -> FieldBoundaryConditions

bcs with every inhomogeneous face replaced by its homogeneous part: a moving NoSlipWall by a stationary one, a prescribed gradient FluxBC(Γ) by FluxBC(0) (the mirror halo), an OpenBoundary by one that writes zero boundary values (and the same zero-gradient / antisymmetric rules), a FreeStreamBoundary by one with zero transpiration. This is the homogeneous part of an affine boundary operator: implicit solvers apply the operator to search directions and increments with these, and move the boundary contribution to the right-hand side. Returns bcs itself (===) when every face is homogeneous, so the existing BC types take their usual code path. A scalar that shares θ's faces but not θ's prescribed top gradient (qv, the SGS TKE) takes passive_scalar_bcs instead, which keeps open-boundary data.

Aeolus.BoundaryConditions.passive_scalar_bcs — Function
passive_scalar_bcs(θ_bcs::FieldBoundaryConditions) -> FieldBoundaryConditions

The boundary conditions of a scalar that shares θ's faces but not θ's prescribed top gradient (qv, the SGS TKE): a FluxBC(Γ) becomes FluxBC(0) (the mirror halo), every other face is kept as it is. Unlike homogeneous_bcs it keeps the boundary data of an OpenBoundary face, so a qv named in the open faces' fields (an open-boundary or nest child, N-open-b #255 / N-nest-d #261) gets its inflow values instead of zeros. Returns θ_bcs itself (===) when no face carries a non-zero gradient.

Free-stream top (P59-d, #185)

The top face of a spatially developing boundary layer: zero shear and a prescribed transpiration velocity (theory in Transition and wall-bounded flow).

Aeolus.BoundaryConditions.FreeStreamBoundary — Type
FreeStreamBoundary(w_top::AbstractVector)

Free-stream top boundary for a spatially developing boundary layer in an x-periodic domain: zero shear stress for the tangential velocities (∂u/∂z = ∂v/∂z = 0, mirror halo — as on a free-slip wall) and a prescribed normal velocity w = w_top(x) on the top face. Cell-centred fields (p, θ, νₜ) get the mirror (Neumann) halo. This is the far-field condition of the spatial DNS of Spalart (1988) and Brandt, Schlatter & Henningson (2004): the boundary layer displaces the free stream upward at $W_∞ = U_∞ dδ^*/dx$, and a closed (w = 0) lid would instead accelerate the free stream by $Δδ^*/H$ and impose a spurious favourable pressure gradient (at $H = 40δ^*_0$ the local-similarity estimate of the Falkner–Skan wall stress is ≈ 18 % above Blasius at the outlet).

w_top is indexed by the storage x index of a FaceZ field (length Nx + 2Hx, i.e. the cell-centre x positions including halos) and is used for every y. Because the domain is periodic in x and y and the bottom is impermeable, solvability of the pressure problem needs zero net transpiration, ∑ w_top[interior] = 0; the constructor does not know the grid, so the caller guarantees it (the Blasius helper Transition.blasius_freestream returns a zero-mean profile, the outflow of the plate being returned through the fringe region).

The pressure problem is unchanged (Neumann at the top face, the CosineFFTPoissonSolver is exact): the prescribed face value enters the divergence of the top cell, and the projection leaves it untouched.

It is the special case of an AbstractOpenBoundary top face with a fixed normal velocity, an antisymmetric halo beyond the face and mirror halos for everything else: it is filled by the same face kernels as OpenBoundary (bit for bit the P59-d fill). Unlike an OpenBoundary it carries no boundary data and is not mass corrected.

Valid only as the top face, paired with a wall (:wall, :noslip, NoSlipWall) at the bottom: FieldBoundaryConditions(:periodic, :periodic; bottom = :noslip, top = FreeStreamBoundary(w)).

References: Spalart, P. R. (1988). Direct simulation of a turbulent boundary layer up to R_θ = 1410. J. Fluid Mech., 187, 61-98. Brandt, L., Schlatter, P. & Henningson, D. S. (2004). Transition in boundary layers subject to free-stream turbulence. J. Fluid Mech., 517, 167-198.

Fixed-gradient scalar top (N-parent-b, #254)

FluxBC(Γ) as the top face, above a wall or a no-flux bottom: the ghost cells continue the profile with slope Γ (φ(N+h) = φ(N+1−h) + Γ·(z_c(N+h) − z_c(N+1−h)), the cell-centre heights of a StretchedGrid or dz steps on a RegularGrid), so a diffusion with diffusivity κ carries κΓ through the top face. Its homogeneous part (homogeneous_bcs) is FluxBC(0), which the implicit diffusion of step_imex! uses. qv and the SGS TKE share θ's faces but not the gradient: they are filled with passive_scalar_bcs (FluxBC(0) on top, open-boundary data kept). Theory and measured convergence: Large-scale forcing → The fixed-gradient top.

θ_bcs = FieldBoundaryConditions(:periodic, :periodic; bottom = :noflux, top = FluxBC(3e-3))

Pressure-Poisson kind of a face (N-open-a, #251)

The boundary condition of the projection's φ that a velocity face implies (:periodic, or :neumann wherever the normal velocity is prescribed), read by the default pressure solver and by poisson_axis_bcs.

Aeolus.BoundaryConditions.axis_poisson_bc — Function
axis_poisson_bc(face::AbstractBC) -> Symbol

The boundary condition the pressure correction φ of a projection method obeys on a face whose velocity boundary condition is face:

  • :periodic for Periodic;
  • :neumann (∂φ/∂n = 0) for every face on which the normal velocity is prescribed and the projection must leave it unchanged — ImpermeableWall, NoSlipWall, NoFlux, and every AbstractOpenBoundary — FreeStreamBoundary (prescribed transpiration) and OpenBoundary, whose normal velocity comes from boundary data. Because u^{n+1}·n = u^*·n − Δt ∂φ/∂n, the face keeps its prescribed value exactly when ∂φ/∂n = 0.

A new face type that prescribes the normal velocity extends this with BoundaryConditions.axis_poisson_bc(::MyBC) = :neumann. Types with no method (e.g. ValueBC, FluxBC, which are scalar conditions and say nothing about the normal velocity) throw an ArgumentError, so a pressure solver is never silently built for the wrong problem. Simulations._default_solver and the poisson_axis_bcs(bcs) of PressureSolvers read the six faces through this function.

Open boundaries (N-open-b, #255)

Lateral and top faces whose values come from outside — a parent run, a mesoscale model, reanalysis, or an analytic field — as PALM's Dirichlet child boundary. The normal velocity is written to the boundary-face slot, tangential velocities and scalars get Dirichlet halos from the boundary data, and φ gets the mirror (Neumann) halo, so the projection leaves the prescribed face values unchanged. Once per projected (sub)step every core calls update_open_boundaries!, which refreshes the buffers from the data and applies the code base's single boundary mass-flux correction. The free-stream top above is the special case with a fixed normal-velocity profile and mirror halos. Theory: Prescribed-flux faces.

data = ProfileBoundaryData(u = 5.0, v = 2.0, θ = z -> 300 + 0.003z)
bcs  = open_boundary_conditions(mesh, data)          # open W, E, S, N and top; wall bottom
sim  = BoussinesqSimulation(mesh; bcs = bcs, θ₀ = 300.0)
step!(sim, dt)                                        # all-Neumann DCT solver chosen
open_boundary_ledger(sim)                             # (Q, residual, δ, t) of the correction

Faces and buffers

Aeolus.BoundaryConditions.AbstractOpenBoundary — Type
AbstractOpenBoundary <: AbstractBC

A face on which the normal velocity is prescribed from outside the domain and the projection must leave it unchanged (axis_poisson_bc is :neumann). Concrete types: OpenBoundary (data-driven Dirichlet faces, lateral or top) and FreeStreamBoundary (the transpiration top of a spatial boundary layer, a special case with a fixed normal-velocity profile and zero-gradient tangential halos). Both are filled by the same face kernels.

Aeolus.BoundaryConditions.OpenBoundary — Type
OpenBoundary

An open face (:west, :east, :south, :north or :top) whose values come from boundary data (PALM's Dirichlet child boundary; Hellsten et al. 2021). On every halo fill:

  • the normal velocity is written to the boundary-face slot — interior index H+1 on a west/south face, halo index H+N+1 on an east, north or top face, the slots ImpermeableWall uses for w = 0 — and the layers beyond it are copied from the buffer (or antisymmetric about the face, normal_halo = :odd);
  • tangential velocities and scalars with data get Dirichlet halos copied from the buffer (the boundary data at the halo-cell positions);
  • every other field — the pressure correction φ, p, νₜ, a scalar without data — gets the mirror (zero-gradient) halo, i.e. the Neumann condition ∂φ/∂n = 0 that keeps a prescribed normal velocity unchanged by the projection (Kim & Moin 1985).

fill_halos! only copies, so it is idempotent. The buffers are refreshed once per projected (sub)step by update_open_boundaries!, which also applies the global mass-flux correction.

Build a whole box with open_boundary_conditions. Cell-centred fields are matched to their planes by identity (sim.θ, sim.qv, bound by update_open_boundaries!); a velocity component is matched by its location.

References: Kim, J. & Moin, P. (1985), Application of a fractional-step method to incompressible Navier–Stokes equations, J. Comput. Phys. 59, 308–323. Hellsten, A. et al. (2021), A nested multi-scale system implemented in the large-eddy simulation model PALM model system 6.0, Geosci. Model Dev. 14, 3185–3214. Kadasch, E. et al. (2021), Mesoscale nesting interface of the PALM model system 6.0, Geosci. Model Dev. 14, 5435–5465.

Aeolus.BoundaryConditions.OpenPlane — Type
OpenPlane

The boundary data of one field on one open face: the storage slab the face writes (data, shaped like field.data[range, :, :] for an x face, etc.), its storage index ranges irange, jrange, krange and the physical coordinates x, y, z of those storage points, so an AbstractBoundaryData can evaluate itself on the slab.

mode is how the halo is filled:

  • tangential velocity and scalars: :value (Dirichlet: copied from data) or :mirror (zero gradient; data unused);
  • scalars only: :inflow — Dirichlet (copied from data) where the face's normal velocity points into the domain, zero gradient (the mirror) where it points out or is zero: a scalar whose outside value is known only for the air that enters (a tracer a nest parent does not carry: zero inflow, its own value out; N-berlin-nest #265);
  • the normal velocity: the boundary face is always copied from data; the layers beyond it are :value (copied) or :odd (antisymmetric about the face value).

fluid[a, b] (over the interior tangential indices of the face) marks the faces that carry flow; the mass-flux correction only adjusts and counts fluid faces. Default: all fluid.

Aeolus.BoundaryConditions.OpenBoundaryState — Type
OpenBoundaryState

What the faces of one open boundary share: the boundary data, every OpenPlane of every face (planes, the buffers handed to boundary_values!), the mass-flux correction mode, the binding of cell-centred fields to their planes, and the ledger of the last correction: Q (net outward volume, or mass, flux before the correction), residual (after), δ (the uniform normal-velocity adjustment) and, with record = true, log, one (t, Q, residual) per update.

outflow maps each open side to its outflow mode (:dirichlet, :convective, :orlanski); radiation[n] is the RadiationPlane of planes[n] on a radiating face, nothing otherwise (N-open-c, #260).

Aeolus.BoundaryConditions.open_boundary_conditions — Function
open_boundary_conditions(mesh, data; x = :open, y = :open,
                         bottom = :wall, top = :open,
                         fields = (:u, :v, :w, :θ),
                         correction = :uniform, normal_halo = :value,
                         scalar_halo = :value, record = false,
                         outflow = :dirichlet, halo = 1)
    -> FieldBoundaryConditions

A box with open faces fed by data (an AbstractBoundaryData). x and y are :open or :periodic (both faces of an axis alike); top is :open or a wall (:wall, :noslip, NoSlipWall); bottom is a wall, or — for a nest child that does not reach the ground (N-nest-d, #261) — :open with top = :open, or :periodic with top = :periodic (a child spanning its parent's periodic z extent). fields names the fields with boundary data: the three velocity components and any cell-centred scalar the simulation holds under that name (:θ, :qv). correction is the mode of correct_boundary_mass_flux! (:uniform, :outflow_only or :none). scalar_halo = :mirror gives the scalars zero-gradient halos instead of Dirichlet ones.

All open faces share one OpenBoundaryState; pass the result as bcs (and, by default, θ_bcs) of a simulation.

outflow (N-open-c, #260) is the outflow condition of the open faces: one of :dirichlet (default: every point takes the boundary data, PALM's nested child), :convective or :orlanski, or a NamedTuple per side, e.g. (east = :convective,) (unnamed sides :dirichlet). On a radiating face, a point whose outward normal velocity is positive at the start of the step is an outflow point: every field with a plane there (u, v, w and each scalar) is advanced by the radiation equation ∂φ/∂t + c ∂φ/∂n = 0 in the halo, with c = max(u·n, 0) (convective) or the Orlanski (1976) phase speed clipped to [0, Δn/Δt] (Miller & Thorpe 1981); inflow points keep the Dirichlet data. See prepare_open_boundaries! and update_open_boundaries!. In :orlanski mode the normal velocity is advanced with the convective speed (see prepare_open_boundaries! and the theory page, "Radiation outflow").

halo is the halo depth the advection scheme needs at an open face (AdvectionSchemes.required_halo(scheme): 3 for WENO-Z5 and TENO5, 4 for WENO-Z7); a mesh whose halo along an open axis is shallower throws an ArgumentError, since the scheme would then read halo layers that hold no boundary data. Inflow scalars get Dirichlet data in all H layers.

The boundary-data contract

Aeolus.BoundaryConditions.AbstractBoundaryData — Type
AbstractBoundaryData

Where an open boundary's values come from. A subtype implements

boundary_values!(planes, data, t)

which writes, for every OpenPlane p in planes (a vector over all fields and faces of one open boundary set) whose p.mode !== :mirror, the value of field p.name at time t into p.data, at the storage points with coordinates (p.x[a], p.y[b], p.z[c]). The normal velocity's boundary-face values are then made mass consistent by correct_boundary_mass_flux!; boundary_values! does not need to balance them.

Shipped: ProfileBoundaryData (horizontally uniform profiles) and FunctionBoundaryData (analytic fields). The nest boundary data of the Berlin child (N-nest-d, #261), mesoscale forcing (#58) and ERA5 (#57) plug in here.

FunctionBoundaryData(; u = f, v = f, w = f, θ = f, …)

Analytic boundary data: each keyword is a function (x, y, z, t) -> value (or a number) for the field of that name. A field with an open plane but no function throws, except w, which defaults to zero.

Aeolus.BoundaryConditions.WindowedBoundaryData — Type
WindowedBoundaryData{S}(a, b, t0, Δt) <: AbstractBoundaryData

Boundary data from two snapshots of a source, a at time t0 and b at t0 + Δt, blended linearly in time:

boundary_values!(planes, d, t):   p.data ← (1 − τ) A + τ B,   τ = window_fraction(d, t)

for every plane p (except :mirror planes), with A, B the snapshots' values of field p.name, read at the plane's storage points. Δt = 0 is steady data (a). The window is moved by assigning the fields (d.a = …; d.b = …; d.t0 = …; d.Δt = …).

A snapshot type S implements one method,

snapshot_values(s::S, name::Symbol) -> AbstractArray{T,3}

the storage-indexed values of field name on the child mesh (only the plane points are read). Linear interpolation in time is exact for data linear in time and second-order accurate otherwise, O(Δt²).

Implementers: the nest parent (NestBoundaryData, N-nest-d #261); a mesoscale parent (#58) or reanalysis forcing (#57) delivering fields at discrete times.

window_fraction(d::WindowedBoundaryData, t) -> τ

The blend weight of d.b at time t: (t − t0)/Δt, or 0 for steady data (Δt = 0).

blend_window!(dst, A, B, τ[, irange, jrange, krange]) -> dst

dst ← (1 − τ) A + τ B, the one time-blend kernel of two snapshots: over the whole array, or, with ranges, dst[a, b, c] from A[i, j, k], B[i, j, k] at the storage points irange × jrange × krange (a, b, c counting from 1, the layout of an OpenPlane's buffer).

Blend into an OpenPlane's buffer at its own storage points.

Data that superpose a part of their own on the source's values — synthetic inflow turbulence (Transition.InflowTurbulence) — declare its normal-velocity part, which a two-way nest keeps out of its parent's interface faces (#418):

Aeolus.BoundaryConditions.synthetic_normal_velocity — Method
synthetic_normal_velocity(data::AbstractBoundaryData, side::Symbol, t) -> plane or nothing

The part of the boundary-normal velocity that data writes on the open face side at time t without it being a field of its source: synthetic turbulence superposed on the source's values (Transition.InflowTurbulence). An n₁ × n₂ matrix over the face's interior cells in the order of the two tangential axes ($y, z$ on a west/east face, $x, z$ on a south/north face, $x, y$ on the bottom/top), in the sign of the grid component ($u$, $v$ or $w$); nothing (the default) when every value is source data.

A two-way nest's interface flux correction (Nesting.TwoWayFeedback) gives the parent the child's interface flux minus this part: the synthetic eddies are the child's own sub-filter turbulence, not a flux of the parent, and fed into the parent's interface faces they return in the next window's boundary data and accumulate (#418).

Update, mass-flux correction and multi-stage support

Aeolus.BoundaryConditions.update_open_boundaries! — Function
update_open_boundaries!(sim, t, dt) -> sim

Refresh the open boundaries of sim for a projected (sub)step that ends at time t (length dt): fill every buffer from the boundary data at t (boundary_values!); on a face with a :convective or :orlanski outflow, advance the outflow points of every plane instead by the radiation equation over dt (N-open-c, #260; see open_boundary_conditions); then apply correct_boundary_mass_flux! with the core's constraint weights (boundary_flux_weights). Points a solid obstacle touches (mark_open_boundary_solids!, N-open-d #262) get zero velocity after the data is written, and the correction leaves them alone.

The radiation update is the explicit second-order upstream form of ∂φ/∂t + c ∂φ/∂n = 0, applied to every halo layer b outward from the face,

φ_b ← φ_b⁰ − r (3φ_b⁰ − 4φ_{b−1}⁰ + φ_{b−2}⁰) / 2,    r = min(c Δt / Δn, 1),

where the superscript ⁰ marks values at the start of the (sub)stage (the buffer, and the interior layers recorded by mark_open_boundary_stage!) and c is the phase speed fixed by prepare_open_boundaries!. Being explicit in stage-start values, it is integrated by a multi-stage driver with the driver's own weights (SSP-RK3 for step_rk3!); r ≤ 1 (the Miller & Thorpe 1981 bound c ≤ Δn/Δt) keeps the update stable. The second-order upstream difference matters with the centred interior schemes: the first-order one reflects a scalar pulse with ∫θ'² residual 1.9e-4 against 7.9e-6 (N-open-c test, σ = 4Δx). The next fill_halos! writes the corrected face values into the predicted velocity, so the projection that follows sees a compatible problem.

Called once per projected (sub)step, after the predictor and before the projection, by every core: step! and step_rk3! (Simulations._euler_projection!, hence also step_adaptive!), step_imex! (ARK2, Rosenbrock2) and the sound-proof _step_mass_weighted!. A no-op when sim has no open face, so every closed run is bit for bit unchanged.

Aeolus.BoundaryConditions.prepare_open_boundaries! — Function
prepare_open_boundaries!(sim) -> sim

Before a step: fill the buffers at the current time sim.t if they have never been filled (a fresh simulation, or new data from set_boundary_data!), so the first tendency evaluation sees the boundary data. A no-op otherwise, and for closed boundaries.

On a face with a radiating outflow (N-open-c, #260) it also fixes, once per step, which points are outflow points (outward normal velocity of the boundary face > 0) and their phase speed c:

  • :convective — c = max(u·n, 0), the local outward normal velocity interpolated to the point;
  • :orlanski — for the tangential velocities and the scalars, c = −(∂φ/∂t)/(∂φ/∂n) from the interior layer next to the face at this and the previous step start (Orlanski 1976), clipped to [0, Δn/Δt] (Miller & Thorpe 1981); the first step, without a history, uses the convective speed. The normal velocity always uses the convective speed: in a projected core continuity ties it to the interior, so it is not a freely radiating wave, and an Orlanski speed diagnosed from it and fed back through the pressure drifts away from the advection speed (in the outgoing-vortex test the diagnosed speeds of u and w settled at 0.79 U and 1.23 U, R = 0.15; with the convective normal velocity R = 4.9e-3).

Every multi-stage driver calls it once at the start of the step, so the phase speeds stay fixed over the substages; a step that is retried at the same time keeps them.

References: Orlanski, I. (1976), A simple boundary condition for unbounded hyperbolic flows, J. Comput. Phys. 21, 251–269. Miller, M. J. & Thorpe, A. J. (1981), Radiation conditions for the lateral boundaries of limited-area numerical models, Q. J. R. Meteorol. Soc. 107, 615–628.

Aeolus.BoundaryConditions.correct_boundary_mass_flux! — Function
correct_boundary_mass_flux!(state, weights = nothing; t = state.t) -> δ

Make the prescribed normal velocities of an open boundary set compatible with the pressure problem. With every face's normal velocity prescribed, ∇²φ = ∇·u* with ∂φ/∂n = 0 is solvable only if the net boundary flux vanishes; interpolated or analytic data never satisfy that to round-off, and the solver would project out the mean and leave a uniform divergence Q/V in every cell. This is the only boundary mass correction in the code base:

  • Q = Σ W u·n A over the open fluid faces (W = 1 for the Boussinesq core; ρ̄ or ρ̄θ̄ for the sound-proof cores);
  • :uniform (default): subtract δ = Q / Σ W A from the outward normal velocity of every open face (Hellsten et al. 2021, §3.3, Eq. 2);
  • :outflow_only: subtract δ = Q / Σ_out W A from the outflow faces (u·n > 0) only, leaving the inflow untouched (all faces if there is no outflow);
  • :none: no correction (the failure mode, kept for tests).

Records Q, δ and the post-correction residual in the state (and in state.log with record = true). Returns δ.

Aeolus.BoundaryConditions.open_boundary_flux — Function
open_boundary_flux(state, weights = nothing) -> Q

Net outward volume flux Q = Σ u·n A (mass flux Σ W u·n A with weights = (Wc, Wf)) through the fluid faces of the open boundary set, from the boundary-face values in its buffers.

Aeolus.BoundaryConditions.boundary_flux_weights — Function
boundary_flux_weights(sim) -> nothing | (Wc, Wf)

The weights of the constraint ∇·(W u) = 0 a dynamical core projects onto, as halo-padded cell and face profiles: nothing (W = 1) for the Boussinesq core; the sound-proof cores extend it with their ρ̄ / ρ̄θ̄. The mass-flux correction weights lateral face fluxes with Wc[k] and the top flux with Wf[Hz+Nz+1], exactly as the projection's divergence does.

Aeolus.BoundaryConditions.assert_open_face_correction — Function
assert_open_face_correction(bcs, dφdx, dφdy) -> nothing

Check, rather than assume, that the pressure correction leaves the prescribed normal velocity of the west/south open faces (interior slots H+1, which the correction loop visits) unchanged: ∂φ/∂n must be exactly zero there (the mirror halo of φ); with dφdz also an open bottom (a nest child inside its parent, N-nest-d #261). East, north and top faces lie in the halo and are never corrected. Throws an ErrorException otherwise.

Aeolus.BoundaryConditions.open_intermediate_velocity! — Function
open_intermediate_velocity!(sim, τ) -> sim

Kim & Moin's (1985) boundary condition for the intermediate velocity of a pressure-correction step whose viscous term is implicit. The projection u = u* − ∇φ with φ ≈ τ p changes the tangential velocity next to an open face by τ ∂p/∂s, so an implicit diffusion solve for u* that imposes the end-of-step data g on the tangential halos leaves a viscous boundary layer of amplitude τ ∂p/∂s after the projection (first-order splitting error, order reduction in time). The consistent condition is u*_s = g_s + τ ∂p/∂s: this adds τ ∂p/∂s (from the latest pressure sim.p, extrapolated from the first interior layer) to the tangential-velocity buffers of every open face. The caller restores the buffers (restore_open_boundaries!) before the projection, which must see the data g itself. Normal velocities and scalars are not touched (∂φ/∂n = 0). step_imex! (ARK2) applies it around each implicit stage.

Reference: Kim, J. & Moin, P. (1985), J. Comput. Phys. 59, 308–323, §3 (boundary condition for the intermediate velocity).

Radiation outflow (N-open-c, #260)

open_boundary_conditions(mesh, data; outflow = :convective) (or :orlanski, or a NamedTuple per side) advances the outflow points of the open faces by the radiation equation instead of taking the data; the inflow points keep the data. The advective flux of a scalar through the boundary, from the scheme's own face fluxes, is boundary_scalar_flux (AdvectionSchemes). Theory: Radiation outflow.

bcs = open_boundary_conditions(mesh, data; outflow = (east = :convective,), halo = 3)
sim = BoussinesqSimulation(mesh; bcs = bcs)
step_rk3!(sim, dt)
F = boundary_scalar_flux(sim, sim.θ; scheme = :centered)   # (west, east, …, total)

RadiationPlane

Aeolus.BoundaryConditions.mark_open_boundary_stage! — Function
mark_open_boundary_stage!(sim) -> sim

Record, at the start of a projected (sub)stage, the two interior layers next to every radiating outflow face (N-open-c, #260). The radiation update of that stage (update_open_boundaries!) is explicit in these stage-start values, so a multi-stage driver integrates the boundary's semi-discrete radiation equation with its own Runge–Kutta weights. The velocity at the update is already the predicted u*, which differs from the projected velocity by Δt ∇φ; using it instead would put a pressure-gradient error into the outflow. A no-op without a radiating face. Called by Simulations._euler_projection! and Anelastic._step_mass_weighted!; step_imex! updates the boundaries before it changes the fields and needs no mark.

Aeolus.BoundaryConditions.rewind_open_boundaries! — Function
rewind_open_boundaries!(sim, snapshot) -> sim

Restore the open-boundary buffers to snapshot (from open_boundary_snapshot at the step start) when a face has a radiating outflow; a no-op otherwise. The radiation update advances a buffer from its value at the start of the stage, so a driver whose stage restarts from the step-start state (step_imex!'s ARK2 and Rosenbrock2 second stages) rewinds the buffers first. Dirichlet buffers are overwritten by the data and need no rewinding.

Near the open faces (N-open-d, #262)

The one BC-aware neighbour index (it replaces the periodic mod1 wraps of code that must also run in an open domain) and the solid points of an open face a building touches. Theory: Near the open faces.

Aeolus.BoundaryConditions.neighbor_index — Function
neighbor_index(mesh, bcs, i, axis; face = false, outside = :mirror) -> Int

The interior index (1…N along axis, 1 = x, 2 = y, 3 = z) that stands for the neighbour index i of a stencil, according to the boundary conditions bcs of that axis:

  • inside the domain (1 ≤ i ≤ N; with face = true, a field staggered along axis, 1 ≤ i ≤ N + 1, whose N + 1 is the far boundary face) i itself;
  • across a Periodic axis, the wrapped index mod1(i, N);
  • across any other face (a wall, NoFlux, an OpenBoundary): with outside = :mirror the mirror image about the face — the cell-centred zero-gradient neighbour (0 → 1, N + 1 → N), or for face = true the mirror about the boundary face (0 → 2, N + 2 → N); with outside = :none the sentinel 0 ("outside the domain: no neighbour").

This replaces every hard-coded periodic wrap (mod1(i ± 1, N)) of code that must also run in an open (nested) domain: on a periodic axis it is mod1 bit for bit, and on an open axis a stencil no longer reaches across the domain to the opposite face. With face = true the index N + 1 of an open face is the boundary-face slot of the normal velocity (storage index H + N + 1, written by the open boundary), so (u[i] + u[i+1])/2 at i = N reads the prescribed boundary value. A caller indexing the storage array adds the halo width H.

Aeolus.BoundaryConditions.mark_open_boundary_solids! — Function
mark_open_boundary_solids!(bcs, mask) -> bcs

Tell the open faces of bcs where a solid obstacle (an immersed-boundary building, mask a storage-sized Bool array, true = solid, e.g. IBM.SolidMask(...).mask) touches them. For every plane of every open face, a boundary point is solid when an interior cell it borders is solid — the cell next to the face for the normal velocity and the scalars, and either of the two cells a staggered tangential component sits between — exactly the faces the direct-forcing IBM zeroes (IBM.apply_solid_mask!). Solid points:

  • take the solid value of the velocity, zero, in every halo layer, written by update_open_boundaries! after the boundary data — the data of a building cut by the box edge is never imposed on its inside, and the projection sees an impermeable face there;
  • are excluded from the global mass-flux correction (correct_boundary_mass_flux!, N-open-b): neither counted in Q's area nor adjusted by δ, so the correction cannot push flow through a wall.

Scalars keep their data at solid points: with zero normal velocity no advective flux crosses there, and the diffusive exchange with the solid cell is the same as anywhere between air and a building. Call once after the geometry is built; the buffers are refreshed at the next update_open_boundaries!.
