Ice nucleation (Phase 43)

IceNucleation carries ice-nucleating particles (mineral dust and primary biological aerosol) as prognostic modal tracers with an available/consumed budget, and provides the log-normal number and surface-area diagnostics that heterogeneous freezing parameterisations are written in. On top of that population it implements immersion freezing (Niemand et al. 2012, Ullrich et al. 2017, DeMott et al. 2015, Tobo et al. 2013) and deposition nucleation (Ullrich et al. 2017), and returns ice number and mass source terms with the matching INP, liquid and vapour sinks and latent heat (ice_nucleation!). Those sources are coupled into the P3 ice category (p3_ice_nucleation, p3_inp_release!), and the phase is validated on a 1-D ISDAC-style mixed-phase Arctic stratus column (run_arctic_stratus). Theory: Ice nucleation.

Aeolus.IceNucleation.DEMOTT_CUTOFF_RADIUS — Constant
DEMOTT_CUTOFF_RADIUS = 0.25e-6

Dry radius (m) of the DeMott et al. (2010, 2015) aerosol cut-off: their INP parameterisations are written in terms of N_{>0.5 μm}, the number of particles with diameter above 0.5 μm, i.e. radius above 0.25 μm.

Aeolus.IceNucleation.NUCLEATED_ICE_MASS — Constant
NUCLEATED_ICE_MASS = P3_NUCLEATED_ICE_MASS ≈ 3.77e-15 kg

Mass of a crystal freshly nucleated by deposition: a 1-μm-radius ice sphere at 900 kg m⁻³, the nucleated-ice mass mi0 of the P3 scheme (Morrison & Milbrandt 2015). It is defined as P3Microphysics.P3_NUCLEATED_ICE_MASS — one source of truth, so the nucleated crystals and P3's Hallett–Mossop splinters cannot drift apart. Immersion-frozen crystals instead take the mass of the droplet that froze.

Aeolus.IceNucleation.DeMott2015 — Type
DeMott2015(; cf = 3.0)

Immersion freezing on mineral dust in terms of N_{>0.5 μm}, DeMott et al. (2015), ACP 15, 393, Eq. (2) with α = 0, β = 1.25, γ = 0.46, δ = -11.6:

\[n_\mathrm{INP} = c_f\,N_{>0.5}^{\,β}\,\exp[γ(273.16 - T) + δ],\]

n_INP in std L⁻¹ and N_{>0.5} in std cm⁻³ (273.15 K, 1013.25 hPa). The calibration factor cf = 3 is the value DeMott et al. recommend for immersion freezing (CFDC at 105 % RH_w under-counts by ≈ 3). Applied over −35 to −20 °C: DeMott et al. note both their fit and D10 are weakly constrained warmer than −20 °C. Provided mainly as an independent cross-check of Niemand2012, with which DeMott et al. report "excellent agreement" in a Saharan dust layer.

Aeolus.IceNucleation.FreezingScheme — Type
FreezingScheme

Supertype of the heterogeneous ice-nucleation parameterisations. Two families, which differ in what they need from an INP mode:

  • SiteDensityScheme — ice-nucleation active surface-site (INAS) density n_s(T[, S_i]) in m⁻²; a particle of radius r is frozen with probability 1 - exp(-n_s 4πr²) (Hoose & Möhler 2012; Niemand et al. 2012; Ullrich et al. 2017).
  • AerosolCountScheme — INP number as a function of T and the number of particles larger than 0.5 μm diameter (DeMott et al. 2010, 2015; Tobo et al. 2013).

Every scheme is evaluated only inside the temperature range it was fitted over (scheme_temperature_range): warmer than the range it predicts no ice (a fitted exponential extrapolated towards 0 °C produces spurious ice where the fit is unconstrained), colder than the range it is held at its cold-edge value. At or above T_freeze every scheme returns zero, whatever its range.

Aeolus.IceNucleation.INPPopulation — Type
INPPopulation{T}

Prognostic ice-nucleating particles in one air parcel or grid cell, organised as log-normal modes (the Phase 37/42 modal representation) with two moments per population:

  • species — the AerosolSpecies of each mode (:mineral_dust, :biological, …); freezing parameterisations dispatch on it
  • σ_g, ρ_p, κ — fixed shape parameter, dry density (kg m⁻³) and hygroscopicity of each mode
  • N_avail, M_avail — number (m⁻³) and dry mass (kg m⁻³) of INP that have not nucleated ice: interstitial or immersed in droplets, still able to freeze
  • N_cons, M_cons — INP consumed by nucleation: now inside ice crystals, unable to nucleate again until released

plus cumulative budget counters (N_src, N_nuc, N_rel, N_wet, N_sed, N_cons_lost, their mass twins, and the initial N_init, M_init) that make conservation checkable — see inp_budget.

Why available/consumed and not just a number. Singular (time- independent) freezing parameterisations — DeMott et al. (2010, 2015), Niemand et al. (2012), Tobo et al. (2013) — give the number of INP that have frozen by the time the parcel reaches temperature T. Applied to a diagnostic INP field every time step, they re-freeze the same particles and produce a steady ice source where the real atmosphere produces a one-off burst. Carrying the consumed population turns the parameterisation into the correct increment, ΔN = max(0, N_frozen(T) - N_cons), so holding T fixed nucleates nothing after the first step. That is the mechanism by which INP depletion sustains mixed-phase Arctic stratus (Solomon et al. 2015).

Modal closure. Each population's median radius is diagnosed from its number and mass with σ_g held fixed, exactly as in AerosolCloud.AerosolReservoir (Whitby & McMurry 1997). A size-selective sink (sedimentation of the coarse tail, nucleation on the largest particles first) removes more mass per particle than average, so the diagnosed median radius of what remains shrinks.

AerosolReservoir itself is not reused as the container because its two populations mean interstitial versus activated as CCN; the INP split is orthogonal (an available INP may be interstitial or immersed), and the INP budget needs the species identity that the reservoir drops.

Aeolus.IceNucleation.Niemand2012 — Type
Niemand2012()

Immersion freezing on desert dust, INAS density from AIDA cloud-chamber expansions (Niemand et al. 2012, JAS 69, 3077):

\[n_s(T) = \exp\left[-0.517\,(T - 273.15) + 8.934\right]\ \mathrm{m^{-2}},\]

valid −36 to −12 °C at or above water saturation. n_s(−20 °C) = 2.35×10⁸ m⁻². The default dust immersion scheme.

Aeolus.IceNucleation.SiteDensityScheme — Type

INAS-density (surface-area) freezing schemes; see FreezingScheme.

Aeolus.IceNucleation.Tobo2013 — Type
Tobo2013()

Immersion/condensation freezing by forest-site INP dominated by primary biological particles, Tobo et al. (2013), JGR 118, 10100 (coefficients as in DeMott et al. 2015, Eq. 2 with cf = 1):

\[n_\mathrm{INP} = N_{>0.5}^{\,α(273.16 - T) + β}\,\exp[γ(273.16 - T) + δ],\]

α = -0.074, β = 3.8, γ = 0.414, δ = -9.671, same std units as DeMott2015, measured over −34 to −9 °C. Applied here to the N_{>0.5 μm} of a :biological INP mode; the default scheme for that species.

Aeolus.IceNucleation.Ullrich2017Deposition — Type
Ullrich2017Deposition()

Deposition nucleation on desert dust below water saturation, Ullrich et al. (2017), JAS 74, 699, with their dust coefficients:

\[n_s(T, S_i) = \exp\left\{α (S_i - 1)^{1/4} \cos^2[β(T - γ)]\,\frac{\mathrm{arccot}[κ(T - λ)]}{π}\right\} - 1,\]

with α = 285.692, β = 0.017, γ = 256.692, κ = 0.080, λ = 200.745, arccot x = π/2 - arctan x, valid 206–240 K and 1 < S_i ≤ S_{i,w}(T) (water saturation). Two deliberate choices:

  • the published form is exp{…}, which gives n_s = 1 m⁻² rather than zero at ice saturation; exp{…} - 1 makes the site density vanish continuously at S_i = 1. The difference is at most 1 m⁻², i.e. a relative change below 10⁻⁶ wherever n_s > 10⁶ m⁻² and a frozen fraction below 10⁻¹¹ for a 1 μm particle.
  • S_i above water saturation is clamped to S_{i,w}(T) = e_s(T)/e_{si}(T) from MoistThermodynamics: above water saturation droplets form and the INP freeze by immersion instead.

This is the deposition scheme chosen for Phase 43. Meyers et al. (1992) is not used: it predicts ice number from S_i alone, with no reference to the INP present, and so cannot consume a prognostic INP population.

Aeolus.IceNucleation.Ullrich2017Immersion — Type
Ullrich2017Immersion()

Immersion freezing on desert dust from the 11-year AIDA data set of Ullrich et al. (2017), JAS 74, 699:

\[n_s(T) = \exp(150.577 - 0.517\,T)\ \mathrm{m^{-2}},\]

applied over −30 to −14 °C (the range tabulated for this fit by Marinou et al. 2019, ACP 19, 11315, Table 1). It has the same slope as Niemand2012 and sits a constant factor e^{0.424} = 1.53 above it.

Aeolus.IceNucleation.default_freezing_scheme — Method
default_freezing_scheme(species::AerosolSpecies) -> (immersion = …, deposition = …)
default_freezing_schemes(inp::INPPopulation) -> Vector of the above, one per mode

The freezing schemes used for a species unless the caller passes its own: mineral dust — Niemand2012 immersion and Ullrich2017Deposition deposition; biological — Tobo2013 immersion and no deposition scheme (no deposition parameterisation for biological particles is established; see the review of Kanji et al. 2017); any other species — nothing for both (not an INP).

Aeolus.IceNucleation.ice_active_site_density — Function
ice_active_site_density(scheme::SiteDensityScheme, T, S_i = Inf;
                        c = DEFAULT_CONSTANTS) -> m⁻²

INAS density n_s of an INAS scheme at temperature T (K) and, for deposition, ice saturation ratio S_i. Immersion schemes ignore S_i (they apply to INP inside droplets, at water saturation). Zero for T ≥ c.T_freeze, for T warmer than the scheme's fitted range, and — deposition only — for S_i ≤ 1. Non-decreasing with supercooling.

Aeolus.IceNucleation.ice_nucleation! — Method
ice_nucleation!(inp, T, S_i, Δt; p = STANDARD_PRESSURE,
                N_liq = 0, M_liq = 0, immersed_fraction,
                schemes = default_freezing_schemes(inp),
                m_ice0 = NUCLEATED_ICE_MASS, c = DEFAULT_CONSTANTS)
    -> IceNucleationTendency

Heterogeneous (and, at the cold end, homogeneous) ice nucleation in one parcel over a step Δt (s): consume INP from inp and return the ice number and mass sources with the matching liquid, vapour and INP sinks and the latent heat (IceNucleationTendency). The host owns the hydrometeors: it adds the ice sources to its ice category and removes the liquid and vapour; nothing here touches a hydrometeor state.

Arguments: temperature T (K); ice saturation ratio S_i = e/e_si; pressure p (Pa, for the std-concentration schemes); cloud droplet number N_liq (m⁻³) and liquid water content M_liq (kg m⁻³); immersed_fraction — the fraction of available INP inside droplets, by default 1 when liquid is present and 0 otherwise. The default treats every available INP as immersed: dust and biological INP are mostly larger than 0.25 μm and activate readily as CCN, but the smaller ones stay interstitial, so pass a smaller fraction when the host knows it. In the :homogeneous regime the same fraction decides which INP are frozen inside drops and which remain for deposition; schemes — one (immersion = …, deposition = …) pair per mode (see default_freezing_scheme); m_ice0 — mass of a deposition-nucleated crystal.

Physics, by freezing_regime:

  • :liquid (T ≥ T_freeze) — nothing happens; inp is untouched.
  • :heterogeneous — for each mode, the immersion scheme applies to the immersed fraction and the deposition scheme to the rest; the combined singular statement is applied with inp_nucleate_singular!, so INP are consumed only once and holding (T, S_i) fixed nucleates nothing after the first call. One immersion-frozen INP freezes one droplet of the mean droplet mass M_liq/N_liq (at most N_liq droplets freeze; if more INP than droplets freeze, the extra INP are inside the same ice and form no extra crystal). One deposition-nucleated INP makes one crystal of mass m_ice0, drawn from vapour but never more than the vapour in excess of ice saturation, (S_i - 1) e_si(T)/(R_v T).
  • :homogeneous (T ≤ T_icenuc) — every droplet freezes (N_ice_hom = N_liq, all of M_liq), matching liquid_fraction = 0; the immersed INP are inside the frozen drops and are moved to the consumed population (counted in N_inp but forming no crystal of their own); deposition on the interstitial INP continues as above.

Number conservation in the heterogeneous regime: N_ice_imm + N_ice_dep = N_inp whenever N_liq exceeds the immersion-frozen INP. Water conservation always: M_ice = M_liq + M_vap.

Aeolus.IceNucleation.ice_number_formed — Method

Total ice crystals formed, N_ice_imm + N_ice_dep + N_ice_hom (m⁻³).

Aeolus.IceNucleation.inas_frozen_fraction — Method
inas_frozen_fraction(n_s, A) -> ∈ [0, 1]

Singular INAS frozen fraction of particles of surface area A (m²) at site density n_s (m⁻²): the probability that at least one active site lies on the surface (Poisson),

\[f = 1 - \exp(-n_s A) \;\xrightarrow{\;n_s A \ll 1\;}\; n_s A .\]

Evaluated with expm1 so that the small-n_s A limit is exact to rounding rather than lost to cancellation.

inas_frozen_fraction(scheme, mode::ARGMode, T, S_i = Inf; c) -> ∈ [0, 1]

Number fraction of a log-normal mode frozen at (T, S_i), integrating 1 - exp(-n_s 4πr²) over the size distribution (the quadrature of inp.jl). For n_s A/N ≪ 1 it tends to n_s A/N with A the mode surface area (mode_surface_area).

Aeolus.IceNucleation.inp_budget — Method
inp_budget(inp; mode = 0) -> NamedTuple

Cumulative number and mass budget of one mode or of all modes:

  • N_total, M_total — available + consumed now
  • N_init, M_init — at construction
  • N_src, M_src — net sources (emission, entrainment)
  • N_nuc, M_nuc — moved available → consumed by nucleation
  • N_rel, M_rel — moved consumed → available by ice loss
  • N_wet, M_wet — removed by scavenging
  • N_sed, M_sed — removed by sedimentation / precipitation
  • N_cons_lost, M_cons_lost — the part of wet + sed that came from the consumed population (INP leaving inside falling ice)

The two identities that must hold to rounding are

\[N_\mathrm{avail} + N_\mathrm{cons} = N_\mathrm{init} + N_\mathrm{src} - N_\mathrm{wet} - N_\mathrm{sed}\]

\[N_\mathrm{cons} = N_\mathrm{cons,0} + N_\mathrm{nuc} - N_\mathrm{rel} - N_\mathrm{cons,lost}\]

and the same for mass; inp_budget_residual evaluates them.

Aeolus.IceNucleation.inp_budget_residual — Method
inp_budget_residual(inp; mode = 0) -> (N_total, M_total, N_cons, M_cons)

Relative residuals of the two budget identities in inp_budget (total and consumed; number and mass), each normalised by the larger of the initial and current totals. All four are rounding-level (≲ 1e-14) for any sequence of the source/sink operators in this file; anything larger is a bookkeeping bug. The consumed identity assumes the population started with nothing consumed, which is how inp_population builds it.

Aeolus.IceNucleation.inp_consume! — Method
inp_consume!(inp, i, ΔN; selection = :largest) -> ΔN_moved
inp_consume!(inp, i, p::Function) -> ΔN_moved

Nucleation sink: move INP from the available to the consumed population of mode i. Consumed INP cannot nucleate again, so repeated calls can never freeze more particles than were ever available.

Number form — a freezing scheme has computed how many INP nucleated (ΔN, m⁻³). Which particles is set by selection:

  • :largest — the coarsest particles go first: ΔN is converted to the cut-off radius that holds exactly that number (AerosolCloud.number_above_radius inverted) and the mass above that radius (AerosolCloud.mass_above_radius) moves with it. The appropriate choice for surface- or size-threshold schemes (INAS, DeMott N_{>0.5μm}), where nucleation ability rises steeply with size.
  • :random — size-independent: mass moves at the population mean, ΔM = M ΔN/N, leaving the median radius unchanged.

Probability form — p(r) is the probability that a particle of dry radius r (m) nucleates during the step, e.g. stochastic immersion freezing p(r) = 1 - exp(-J_het(T) 4πr² Δt). Number and mass moved are the log-normal integrals of p over the available population, evaluated in each moment's own standard-normal variable so that a constant p ≡ c moves exactly c N and c M.

ΔN is clipped to the available number; the return value is what was actually moved.

Aeolus.IceNucleation.inp_emit! — Method
inp_emit!(inp, i, ΔN, ΔM) -> ΔN
inp_emit!(inp, i, source::ARGMode, Δt) -> ΔN
inp_emit!(inp, i, source::LogNormalMode, Δt) -> ΔN

Source hook: add number ΔN (m⁻³) and dry mass ΔM (kg m⁻³) to the available population of mode i. The ARGMode form treats source.N as a number emission rate (m⁻³ s⁻¹) with the size distribution of source, so ΔN = N Δt and ΔM = mode_dry_mass(source) Δt; the LogNormalMode form converts from cm⁻³ s⁻¹ and μm first.

Only the two moments are added. Under the fixed-σ_g modal closure a source whose median radius differs from the resident population's shifts the diagnosed median between the two — which is the intended behaviour of a two-moment scheme; a source with a very different σ_g belongs in a separate mode.

Negative increments are rejected; use a sink operator to remove INP.

Aeolus.IceNucleation.inp_entrain! — Method
inp_entrain!(inp, N_env, M_env, f) -> ΔN

Entrainment hook for parcel and single-column models: mix a fraction f ∈ [0, 1] of environmental air, carrying available INP number N_env[i] (m⁻³) and mass M_env[i] (kg m⁻³) per mode, into the parcel. The available population relaxes linearly, X ← (1-f) X + f X_env.

Consumed INP are not diluted here: they are inside ice crystals, and the host's ice category (which is transported and entrained with its own tendencies) owns them — remove them with the ice via inp_scavenge! or inp_sediment! with population = :consumed, or return them with inp_release!.

In an LES this is resolved transport and the hook is not used. The net change (which is negative when the environment is cleaner) is booked as a source, so the budget identities still close.

Aeolus.IceNucleation.inp_frozen_fraction — Method
inp_frozen_fraction(inp, i, p; population = :total) -> (fN, fM)

Number and mass fractions of mode i for which the per-particle probability p(r) holds, without changing the population. With the default :total population this is what a singular parameterisation says has frozen by now, regardless of when.

Aeolus.IceNucleation.inp_mass — Method

Aeolus.IceNucleation.inp_nucleate_singular! — Method
inp_nucleate_singular!(inp, i, N_frozen) -> ΔN
inp_nucleate_singular!(inp, i, p::Function) -> ΔN

Apply a singular (time-independent, deterministic) freezing parameterisation without double counting.

Singular schemes — DeMott et al. (2010, 2015), Niemand et al. (2012), Ullrich et al. (2017), Tobo et al. (2013) — state how many of the parcel's INP are frozen at the current temperature: N_frozen(T), or a per-particle frozen indicator p(r) = 1 - exp(-n_s(T) 4πr²). The population that statement applies to is the total (available + consumed), because particles that froze earlier would freeze again at this temperature. The nucleation increment is therefore

\[ΔN = \min\!\left[N_\mathrm{avail},\ \max(0,\ N_\mathrm{frozen} - N_\mathrm{cons})\right]\]

and likewise for mass with the p-weighted mass fraction. When earlier nucleation used a different selection (e.g. :largest via the number form) that mass difference need not be realisable — it can even be negative — so it must lie between the mass of the ΔN smallest and the ΔN largest available particles; otherwise ΔN moves with the mean mass of the available particles that p selects. At constant temperature a second call returns exactly zero; cooling further nucleates only the difference; warming nucleates nothing (it does not un-freeze anything — melting is the host's business, see inp_release!).

The number form uses :largest selection for the mass, matching the monotone size dependence of every singular scheme above.

Aeolus.IceNucleation.inp_population — Method
inp_population(modes::AbstractVector{<:LogNormalMode}) -> INPPopulation
inp_population(modes::AbstractVector{<:ARGMode}, species) -> INPPopulation

Initialise an INP population from Phase 37 optical modes (which carry their species; units are converted with AerosolCloud.arg_mode) or from SI activation modes plus a species per mode (an AerosolSpecies, a symbol, or a vector of either). Everything starts available.

The species is the single source of the particle density: an ARGMode whose ρ_p disagrees with 1e3 * species.ρ is rejected (note that arg_mode's default ρ_p is sulphate's). Hygroscopicity κ is taken from the mode, since coating and ageing legitimately change it.

dust = lognormal_mode(:mineral_dust, 1.0, 0.4, 2.0)    # 1 cm⁻³, r_g 0.4 μm
bio  = lognormal_mode(:biological, 0.01, 1.0, 1.6)
inp  = inp_population([dust, bio])
Aeolus.IceNucleation.inp_release! — Method
inp_release!(inp, f; mode = 0) -> ΔN

Return a fraction f ∈ [0, 1] of the consumed INP to the available population — the host calls this when that fraction of the ice that carries them sublimates or melts completely without precipitating.

Whether a released INP should be able to nucleate again is a physical question (pre-activation: particles that have held ice often nucleate at warmer temperatures the second time; Kanji et al. 2017, §5). This operator makes them available again; with a singular scheme applied through inp_nucleate_singular! they will re-freeze once the parcel is again as cold as when they first froze — which is the ice recycling Solomon et al. (2015) found necessary to sustain observed Arctic ice number.

Aeolus.IceNucleation.inp_scavenge! — Method
inp_scavenge!(inp, Λ, Δt; mode = 0, population = :available) -> ΔN

Wet scavenging with scavenging coefficient Λ (s⁻¹) over a step Δt: each particle survives with probability exp(-Λ Δt) — the exact solution of dN/dt = -Λ N, so the operator is unconditionally positive for any Δt.

Λ may be a number (size-independent: number and mass decay together and the median radius is unchanged) or a function Λ(r) of dry radius in m (e.g. below-cloud impaction scavenging, which has the Greenfield-gap minimum near 0.1–1 μm; Seinfeld & Pandis 2016, ch. 20). population = :consumed removes INP that are inside ice with a precipitation removal rate; :both applies the same Λ to both.

Aeolus.IceNucleation.inp_sediment! — Method
inp_sediment!(inp, Δz, Δt; mode = 0, micro = DEFAULT_MICRO) -> ΔN

Gravitational sedimentation of available INP out of a well-mixed layer of depth Δz (m) over Δt (s), with no inflow from above (a transport- free sink; in a column model the host passes the outflow down as the next layer's inp_emit! source). A particle of radius r survives with probability exp(-v(r)Δt/Δz) — exact for a well-mixed box and positive for any Δt — so the coarse tail is lost first, number and mass are removed with different effective speeds, and the diagnosed median radius of what remains shrinks.

Consumed INP fall with their ice crystal, not at the dry-particle speed; remove them with inp_scavenge!(…; population = :consumed) using the host's ice sedimentation rate.

Aeolus.IceNucleation.inp_settling_velocity — Method
inp_settling_velocity(inp, i; moment = 0, population = :available,
                      micro = DEFAULT_MICRO) -> m s⁻¹

Moment-weighted gravitational settling speed of mode i: moment = 0 gives the number-weighted mean ⟨v⟩_N (what sets the number flux), moment = 3 the mass-weighted ⟨v⟩_M. The single- particle speed is Superdroplet.stokes_settling_velocity (Stokes drag with Cunningham slip) at the mode's dry density.

Without slip v = k r², and the log-normal moments give the closed forms

\[⟨v⟩_N = k r_g^2 e^{2\ln^2σ_g}, \qquad ⟨v⟩_M = k r_g^2 e^{8\ln^2σ_g},\]

so for σ_g = 2 mass settles e^{6 ln²2} = 18 times faster than number — the reason dust plumes coarsen at their base and fine-mode dust travels intercontinental distances.

Aeolus.IceNucleation.p3_ice_nucleation — Method
p3_ice_nucleation(s::P3ParcelState, inp::INPPopulation, Δt;
                  pp = P3_DEFAULT_PROCESS,
                  schemes = default_freezing_schemes(inp),
                  immersed_fraction = nothing)
    -> (P3ParcelState, IceNucleationTendency)

Heterogeneous (and, below T_icenuc, homogeneous) freezing of one P3 cell over Δt: calls ice_nucleation! with the cell's temperature, ice saturation ratio (MoistThermodynamics.relative_humidity_ice), pressure and cloud droplets (N_liq = N_c ρ, M_liq = q_c ρ, with ρ = MoistThermodynamics.air_density of the cell), consuming INP in inp, and applies the returned increments to the P3 variables:

P3 variablechange
q_c, N_c- M_liq/ρ, - N_liq/ρ (droplets frozen)
q_v- M_vap/ρ (vapour deposited on new crystals)
q_i+ M_ice/ρ (= (M_liq + M_vap)/ρ exactly)
N_i+ (N_ice_imm + N_ice_dep + N_ice_hom)/ρ
q_rim, B_rim+ M_liq/ρ, + M_liq/(ρ ρ_rim_max) (frozen drops are rime at 900 kg m⁻³)
T+ heat/(ρ c_pm), c_pm = heat_capacity_moist of the cell

so total water q_v + q_c + q_r + q_i is unchanged to rounding, every crystal formed is one P3 ice particle, and the heating is the c_pm dT = -L_v dq_v + L_f dq_i of p3_tendencies. Crystals nucleated from vapour have mass NUCLEATED_ICE_MASS, which is P3_NUCLEATED_ICE_MASS.

Only cloud droplets freeze: P3 rain has no immersion-freezing process here (the Bigg rain freezing of the P3 code is not implemented), and rain is untouched even at T ≤ T_icenuc.

Note that the next p3_step applies P3's mean-size limiter (mean diameter ≥ 2 μm): a population made only of deposition-nucleated 1-μm crystals is below it and has its number reduced there, as in the P3 code — this function itself conserves number exactly.

Aeolus.IceNucleation.p3_inp_release! — Method
p3_inp_release!(inp::INPPopulation, s::P3ParcelState, r, Δt) -> ΔN

Return to the available population the consumed INP of the P3 ice that sublimated or melted during a step: the limited rates r (from p3_step_with_rates) remove Δt (n_sub + n_melt) of the N_i crystals of the start-of-step state s, and the same fraction f = min(1, Δt (n_sub + n_melt) / N_i) of each mode's consumed INP is released (inp_release!) — each crystal carries its INP, and a crystal that has gone leaves its INP behind (Solomon et al. 2015 call this INP recycling). Returns the number released (m⁻³).

Aeolus.IceNucleation.run_arctic_stratus — Function
run_arctic_stratus(cfg = ArcticStratusColumn(); record_profiles = false) -> NamedTuple

Integrate the 1-D mixed-phase stratus column described by cfg (ArcticStratusColumn; set-up and simplifications in the source header of src/icenuc/stratus_column.jl and the theory page). Each step of dt:

  1. longwave heating from the current LWP profile, then implicit mixing of sl, the water species, ice moments and INP with the prescribed K(z); T recovered from sl;
  2. P3 source terms per level (p3_step_with_rates, with Hallett–Mossop and the saturation adjustment); INP of sublimated or melted ice released (p3_inp_release!);
  3. ice nucleation per level (p3_ice_nucleation) on the level's prognostic INP;
  4. sedimentation: ice with p3_sediment! at the P3 mass- and number-weighted speeds (p3_ice_sedimentation_velocities), the consumed INP falling with ice number; rain with p3_sediment_rain!.

Returns a NamedTuple with time series (every output_every s) t (h), LWP, IWP (g m⁻²), Ni_cloud (mean ice number in the liquid layer, L⁻¹), Ni_max (L⁻¹), precip_ice (accumulated surface ice, kg m⁻²), plus z, final states, the INP populations and the budget totals inp_total0, inp_total, inp_surface, inp_subsidence (m⁻²; column available+consumed INP initially and at the end, consumed INP that reached the surface, and the net INP brought in by subsidence) and water0, water, precip, water_subsidence (kg m⁻²), so that inp_total = inp_total0 + inp_subsidence - inp_surface and water = water0 + water_subsidence - precip close the budgets.
