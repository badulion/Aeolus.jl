Aerosol-cloud interaction (Phase 42)

AerosolCloud diagnoses the cloud droplet number $N_d$ from the aerosol population instead of prescribing it, so the two aerosol indirect effects are computed. Log-normal dry modes with κ-Köhler hygroscopicity give each particle its critical supersaturation; the updraught sets the supersaturation the parcel reaches. Two routes from there to $N_d$ and $r_{\mathrm{eff}}$ are provided and compared in the tests: the Abdul-Razzak & Ghan (2000) algebraic parameterisation (the workhorse) and an explicit bin-resolved adiabatic parcel (the reference). The resulting droplet number feeds the Twomey (optical depth, albedo) and Albrecht (autoconversion) effects. The CCN spectrum used by TurbulentMicrophysics, ccn_fraction / ccn_spectrum, is defined here.

Aeolus.AerosolCloud — Module
AerosolCloud

Aerosol-cloud interaction — Phase 42.

The two aerosol indirect effects, computed rather than prescribed.

WHY THIS MODULE EXISTS

A bulk microphysics scheme that carries a fixed cloud droplet number concentration cannot represent an aerosol indirect effect at all: the entire signal — Twomey brightening, Albrecht lifetime lengthening — is carried by the dependence of N_d on the aerosol population and on the dynamics that lift it. So N_d here is a diagnosed quantity, obtained from the aerosol size distribution, its hygroscopicity, and the updraught velocity, through activation physics.

THE CHAIN

log-normal dry modes (N, r_g, σ_g, κ)
          │
          ├─ κ-Köhler  ──→  s_crit(r_d, κ),  r_crit(r_d, κ)
          │                        │
updraught w ──→ α w  ─────────────┤
          │                        │
T, p ──→ α, γ, G  ────────────────┤
                                   ↓
          ┌────────────────────────┴───────────────────────┐
          │                                                │
 Abdul-Razzak & Ghan (2000)                    explicit adiabatic parcel
 algebraic S_max, erfc cutoff                  dS/dt = αw − γ dχ/dt
          │                                     bin-resolved r_j(t)
          └────────────────────────┬───────────────────────┘
                                   ↓
                              N_d,  r_eff
                                   │
                    ┌──────────────┴──────────────┐
               Twomey (τ, albedo)          Albrecht (autoconversion)

The parcel model is the reference. The parameterisation is the workhorse. Both are here, and the test suite measures the gap between them instead of asserting it.

WHAT IS AND IS NOT RE-DERIVED HERE

Nothing thermodynamic is re-derived: saturation vapour pressure, latent heat, air density and heat capacity all come from MoistThermodynamics (Phase 39). Nothing hygroscopic is re-derived either: the κ-Köhler equilibrium saturation ratio is AerosolOptics.kohler_saturation (Phase 37), and the Kelvin coefficient is scaled from the exported AerosolOptics.KELVIN_A_298 so there is exactly one value of 2σ_w M_w / (R T ρ_w) in the package. The one analytic expression that is written out again is ∂S/∂r of the Köhler curve — a derivative has to be written somewhere — and the test suite checks it against a finite difference of kohler_saturation to guard the duplication.

THE THREE COEFFICIENTS THAT MATTER

α = g M_w L / (c_p R T²) − g M_a / (R T)          [m⁻¹]
γ = R T / (e_s M_w) + M_w L² / (c_p p M_a T)      [m³ kg⁻¹]
G = [ ρ_w R T/(e_s D_v M_w) + L ρ_w/(K_a T)·(L M_w/(R T) − 1) ]⁻¹  [m² s⁻¹]

α is adiabatic cooling expressed as a supersaturation source, γ converts condensed mass into a supersaturation sink, and G is the condensational growth coefficient in r dr/dt = G(S − S_eq). Note that γ as written multiplies the tendency of the liquid water content (kg m⁻³), not the mixing ratio — the mixing-ratio form is γ ρ_a. That factor of ρ_a is a classic and silent source of ~20 % errors in S_max, so it is stated explicitly and checked in the tests.

Aeolus.AerosolCloud.KAPPA_MIN — Constant

Hygroscopicity below which a particle is treated as non-CCN-active.

AerosolOptics.wet_radius returns the dry radius unchanged for κ ≤ 1e-8; the same threshold is used here, so a black-carbon mode (κ = 0) neither takes up water nor activates in either module.

Aeolus.AerosolCloud.RHO_WATER — Constant

Density of liquid water, kg m⁻³. Matches the value inside AerosolOptics.

Aeolus.AerosolCloud.ARGMode — Type
ARGMode{T}

One log-normal aerosol mode in the units activation physics is written in — SI throughout, unlike AerosolOptics.LogNormalMode, which uses the aerosol-measurement conventions of cm⁻³ and μm:

  • N — number concentration, m⁻³
  • r_g — geometric median dry radius, m
  • σ_g — geometric standard deviation (> 1)
  • κ — hygroscopicity parameter
  • ρ_p — dry particle density, kg m⁻³

The unit change is deliberate and explicit rather than hidden inside the routines: mixing cm⁻³ with m⁻³ inside an η coefficient produces a 10⁶-fold error in the competition term and a silently wrong S_max.

Aeolus.AerosolCloud.ActivationCoefficients — Type
ActivationCoefficients{T}

Everything the activation physics needs about the ambient state, evaluated once so that the parcel integrator and the parameterisation provably use identical numbers.

  • T_K, p — temperature (K) and pressure (Pa)
  • e_s — saturation vapour pressure over liquid, Pa
  • L, c_p — latent heat (J kg⁻¹) and moist heat capacity (J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹)
  • ρ_a — air density, kg m⁻³
  • A — Kelvin coefficient, m
  • α — supersaturation source, m⁻¹
  • γ — supersaturation sink, m³ kg⁻¹
  • G — growth coefficient, m² s⁻¹
Aeolus.AerosolCloud.ActivationResult — Type
ActivationResult{T}

Outcome of an activation calculation, from the parameterisation or (in its own struct) from the parcel model.

  • S_max — maximum supersaturation reached (fractional, not %)
  • S_m — per-mode critical supersaturation at the median radius
  • fraction — activated number fraction per mode
  • N_act — activated number concentration per mode, m⁻³
  • N_act_total — summed activated number, m⁻³
  • r_cut — per-mode dry radius at which s_crit = S_max, m
  • coeffs — the coefficients used
Aeolus.AerosolCloud.AerosolReservoir — Type
AerosolReservoir{T}

Prognostic aerosol state for a grid cell: a two-moment (number + dry mass) representation of each mode, split into an interstitial population — unactivated, still free — and an activated population, the solute currently dissolved inside cloud droplets.

Fields are per mode: σ_g, κ, ρ_p (fixed shape parameters) and N_int, M_int, N_act, M_act (prognostic, m⁻³ and kg m⁻³).

Why two moments and not a spectrum. Number and mass are what an LES can afford to advect. The median radius of each population is derived from them,

\[r_g = \left[\frac{M}{N ρ_p (4π/3)\exp(4.5\ln^2σ_g)}\right]^{1/3}\]

with σ_g held fixed — the standard modal closure (Whitby & McMurry 1997; the MAM and M7 schemes). It is an approximation, and it is the right one here, because it is exactly what makes aerosol processing representable: coalescence removes number at fixed mass, r_g grows, and the enlarged particles are returned to the interstitial population on evaporation. A one-moment scheme cannot express that at all.

Aeolus.AerosolCloud.ParcelResult — Type
ParcelResult{T}

Result of an explicit parcel integration.

  • S_max, t_smax, z_smax — peak supersaturation and where it occurs
  • N_act, fraction, N_act_total — activated number using the dynamic criterion r > r_crit evaluated at the time of S_max
  • N_act_end, N_act_total_end — the same criterion at the end of the run
  • N_crit, N_crit_total — the equilibrium criterion s_crit < S_max, i.e. what ARG's erfc cutoff would give from this parcel's own S_max. The gap between N_crit and N_act is the kinetic limitation: particles whose barrier S_max cleared but which had no time to grow past it.
  • lwc, nsteps, and optional t/z/S/lwc_trace time series
Aeolus.AerosolCloud.ParcelSettings — Type
ParcelSettings

Numerics of the bin-resolved adiabatic parcel.

  • nbins — size bins per mode (log-spaced in dry radius)
  • n_sigma — half-width of the binned range in units of ln σ_g
  • dt_max — ceiling on the time step, s
  • ds_step — target supersaturation change per step from the αw term; the main accuracy control
  • t_max, z_max — integration limits
  • s_init — initial supersaturation (0 = exactly saturated, the conventional cloud-base start)
  • stop_fraction — stop once S has fallen to this fraction of its peak
  • max_steps — hard iteration cap
  • store_trace— keep the t, z, S, LWC time series

Defaults resolve S_max to better than 0.5 % and the activated fraction to better than ~1 % (self-convergence under bin and step refinement is checked in the test suite).

Aeolus.AerosolCloud.activate! — Method
activate!(res, S_max; T_K = 283.15) -> N_newly_activated

Move the interstitial particles whose critical supersaturation is below S_max into the activated population, mode by mode.

The cut-off dry radius follows from inverting s_crit = sqrt(4A³/27κr³):

\[r_\mathrm{cut} = \left(\frac{4A^3}{27 κ S_\max^2}\right)^{1/3}\]

and the transferred number and mass are the log-normal integrals above it, number_above_radius and mass_above_radius. Mass moves with a coarser cut-off than number (by 3lnσ/√2 in the erfc argument), so activating 30 % of the particles typically removes 70-90 % of the aerosol mass — which is why the interstitial mode left behind has a markedly smaller median radius.

Number and dry mass are conserved exactly; nothing is created or destroyed, only relabelled.

Modes with κ ≤ KAPPA_MIN never activate.

Aeolus.AerosolCloud.activation_susceptibility — Method
activation_susceptibility(modes, w, T_K, p; δ = 0.05, kwargs...)
    -> dln N_d / dln N_a

The activation susceptibility: the fractional response of cloud droplet number to a fractional change in total aerosol number, with the size distribution shape, updraught and thermodynamic state held fixed. Evaluated by a centred logarithmic finite difference through arg_activation, scaling every mode by 1 ± δ.

This is the quantity that controls how much of an aerosol perturbation survives into a radiative forcing, and it is emphatically not 1. Values are typically 0.6-0.9 in clean air and fall towards 0.2-0.4 in polluted air, because adding particles increases the competition for vapour, lowers S_max, and so lowers the fraction that activates. In the extreme updraught-limited regime it approaches zero: no matter how much aerosol is added, the vapour supply fixes the droplet number.

The corresponding albedo response is ΔA = albedo_susceptibility(A) × (dlnN_d/dlnN_a) × ΔlnN_a.

Aeolus.AerosolCloud.albedo_susceptibility — Method
albedo_susceptibility(A) -> dA/dlnN_d

Twomey's (1991) susceptibility of cloud albedo to droplet number at fixed liquid water path:

\[\frac{dA}{d\ln N_d} = \frac{A(1-A)}{3}\]

The factor 1/3 is dlnτ/dlnN_d, from τ ∝ r_eff^{-1} ∝ N_d^{1/3} at fixed LWP; A(1−A) is dA/dlnτ for the two-stream albedo above. It peaks at 1/12 = 0.083 for A = 0.5.

Aeolus.AerosolCloud.albrecht_lifetime_factor — Method
albrecht_lifetime_factor(N_d, N_d_ref; b = 1.79) -> factor ≥ 1

Cloud-lifetime lengthening implied by the autoconversion suppression, (N_d/N_d_ref)^{b}, on the argument that the lifetime of a non-precipitating cloud scales inversely with its rain-production rate at fixed water supply.

This is Albrecht's (1989) mechanism and the factor is deliberately crude: a real lifetime also responds to entrainment, drizzle-driven decoupling and mesoscale organisation, several of which act in the opposite direction (the cloud-lifetime term in AR6 is much smaller than this scaling alone implies, and the sign of the liquid-water-path adjustment is still contested). It is exported as a diagnostic of the microphysical suppression, not as a forcing.

Aeolus.AerosolCloud.arg_activation — Method
arg_activation(modes, w, T_K, p; q, constants) -> ActivationResult
arg_activation(modes, w, coeffs)              -> ActivationResult

Abdul-Razzak & Ghan (2000) multi-mode activation parameterisation.

Given the updraught w (m s⁻¹) and the ambient state, it returns the maximum supersaturation and the activated fraction of each log-normal mode, without integrating anything.

The algebra. Define the dimensionless updraught and competition parameters

\[ζ = \frac{2A}{3}\sqrt{\frac{α w}{G}}, \qquad η_i = \frac{(α w / G)^{3/2}}{2π ρ_w γ N_i}\]

ζ measures curvature against the kinetic growth forced by the updraught; η_i measures how fast mode i can remove vapour. Then (ARG 2000, eq. 6)

\[\frac{1}{S_\max^2} = \sum_i \frac{1}{S_{mi}^2}\left[ f_i\left(\frac{ζ}{η_i}\right)^{3/2} + g_i\left(\frac{S_{mi}^2}{η_i + 3ζ}\right)^{3/4}\right]\]

with f_i = 0.5 exp(2.5 ln²σ_i) and g_i = 1 + 0.25 ln σ_i — empirical shape factors fitted by ARG to their own parcel solutions. Finally each mode's activated fraction follows from the log-normal integral above the dry radius whose critical supersaturation equals S_max:

\[\frac{N_{a,i}}{N_i} = \frac12\,\mathrm{erfc}\!\left( \frac{2\ln(S_{mi}/S_\max)}{3\sqrt2 \ln σ_i}\right)\]

Limits and known behaviour. Modes with κ ≤ KAPPA_MIN have S_mi = ∞; they contribute nothing to the sum and activate nothing, which is the correct treatment of insoluble aerosol in this framework (it ignores the weak Kelvin-only activation of very large hydrophobic particles). With w ≤ 0 there is no source of supersaturation and the result is identically zero. With no CCN-active aerosol at all the denominator vanishes and S_max = Inf is returned — physically the parcel would supersaturate until homogeneous nucleation, which is outside this parameterisation's scope.

ARG report ~10-20 % accuracy in N_act against parcel models over the range of updraughts and aerosol loadings encountered in warm clouds; the test suite measures it rather than assuming it.

Aeolus.AerosolCloud.autoconversion_rate — Method
autoconversion_rate(q_c, N_d; A = 1350, a = 2.47, b = 1.79) -> kg kg⁻¹ s⁻¹

Warm-rain autoconversion following Khairoutdinov & Kogan (2000):

\[\left.\frac{∂q_r}{∂t}\right|_\mathrm{auto} = A\,q_c^{2.47}\,N_d^{-1.79}\]

with the cloud water mixing ratio q_c in kg kg⁻¹ and the droplet number N_d in cm⁻³. The exponents were fitted to explicit bin-microphysics LES of marine stratocumulus and are the standard choice in LES; Kessler-type schemes instead use a threshold and no N_d dependence at all, and therefore have no second indirect effect.

The N_d^{-1.79} is the heart of the Albrecht mechanism: collision efficiency depends steeply on droplet size, so spreading the same liquid water over more, smaller droplets suppresses rain formation far faster than linearly.

Aeolus.AerosolCloud.autoconversion_suppression — Method
autoconversion_suppression(N_d, N_d_ref; b = 1.79) -> factor ≤ 1

Ratio of autoconversion rates at two droplet numbers and the same liquid water, (N_d/N_d_ref)^{-b}. This is the second indirect (Albrecht) effect expressed as a single number: doubling the droplet number suppresses autoconversion to 2^{-1.79} = 0.289 of its former rate, a 71 % reduction.

N_d may be in any consistent units, since only the ratio enters.

Aeolus.AerosolCloud.ccn_fraction — Method
ccn_fraction(m::ARGMode, S; T_K = 298.15) -> fraction in [0, 1]

Fraction of the particles of mode m that are activated at the (fractional) supersaturation S, i.e. whose dilute κ-Köhler critical supersaturation is below S:

\[\frac{N_\mathrm{CCN}(S)}{N} = \tfrac12\,\mathrm{erfc}\!\left( \frac{2\ln(S_m/S)}{3\sqrt2\ln σ_g}\right),\]

with S_m = mode_critical_supersaturation(m, T_K). This is the CCN spectrum of a log-normal mode (Abdul-Razzak et al. 1998, eq. 13) and the expression arg_activation evaluates at S_max, so ccn_fraction(m, r.S_max; T_K) reproduces r.fraction bit for bit.

Returns 0 for S ≤ 0 and for non-CCN-active modes (κ ≤ KAPPA_MIN).

Aeolus.AerosolCloud.ccn_spectrum — Method
ccn_spectrum(modes, S; T_K = 298.15) -> m⁻³

CCN number concentration N_CCN(S) = Σ_i N_i ccn_fraction(m_i, S) of a multi-mode population at supersaturation S. Accepts ARGModes or AerosolOptics modes.

Aeolus.AerosolCloud.cloud_albedo — Method
cloud_albedo(τ; g = 0.85) -> A

Two-stream albedo of a conservatively scattering cloud over a black surface,

\[A = \frac{(1-g)τ}{2 + (1-g)τ}\]

i.e. Twomey's (1991) formula, with (1−g)τ the similarity-scaled optical depth. It is 0 at τ = 0 and → 1 as τ → ∞, with the steepest logarithmic response near A = 0.5. That is exactly why the Twomey effect is largest for optically intermediate clouds and nearly absent for very thin or very thick ones.

g = 0.85 is the standard visible asymmetry parameter for liquid cloud and matches the value TwoStream.MieCloudOptics uses in the solar bands.

Droplet number from a precomputed result.

Aeolus.AerosolCloud.cloud_droplet_number — Method
cloud_droplet_number(modes, w, T_K, p; kwargs...) -> m⁻³

Cloud droplet number concentration diagnosed from the aerosol population and the updraught via arg_activation. This is the quantity that makes an indirect effect an effect rather than a prescribed number.

Aeolus.AerosolCloud.cloud_optical_depth_lwp — Method
cloud_optical_depth_lwp(lwp, r_eff) -> τ

Geometric-optics cloud optical depth,

\[τ = \frac{3\,\mathrm{LWP}}{2 ρ_w r_\mathrm{eff}}\]

with LWP in kg m⁻² and r_eff in m. This is the same relation TwoStream.compute_optics! uses (there written as τ = 1.5·LWP/r_eff with LWP in g m⁻² and r_eff in μm, where the unit factors cancel), so the two phases agree by construction: 100 g m⁻² at 10 μm gives τ = 15.

Named _lwp to avoid colliding with the exported TwoStream.cloud_optical_depth.

Aeolus.AerosolCloud.cloud_water_path — Method
cloud_water_path(lwc, h) -> kg m⁻²

Liquid water path of a slab of depth h (m) with uniform water content lwc (kg m⁻³). Named to avoid colliding with TwoStream.liquid_water_path, which integrates a full AtmosphericColumn and returns g m⁻².

Aeolus.AerosolCloud.critical_radius — Method
critical_radius(r_dry, κ; T_K = 298.15) -> m

Wet radius at the Köhler peak, r_crit = sqrt(3 κ r_d³ / A), in the dilute limit. A 100 nm sulphate particle has r_crit ≈ 1.3 μm: the droplet must grow by more than an order of magnitude in radius — three in volume — before it is free of the barrier.

For κ ≤ KAPPA_MIN the curve has no interior maximum and r_dry is returned.

Aeolus.AerosolCloud.critical_supersaturation — Method
critical_supersaturation(r_dry, κ; T_K = 298.15) -> s_crit

Critical (peak) supersaturation of the Köhler curve, in the dilute limit where r_crit ≫ r_dry:

\[s_\mathrm{crit} = \sqrt{\frac{4A^3}{27\,κ\,r_d^3}}\]

obtained by maximising s ≈ A/r − κ r_d³/r³. It falls as r_d^{-3/2} and as κ^{-1/2}: big particles and soluble particles activate first, which is the whole reason a polluted cloud has more but smaller droplets than a clean one.

For κ ≤ KAPPA_MIN the solute term vanishes and the curve is pure Kelvin, monotonically decreasing from its value at r = r_d; the peak then sits at the dry radius and s_crit = exp(A/r_d) − 1. That limit is returned instead, which is continuous with the exact numerical result from kohler_critical.

Accuracy of the dilute form: the neglected term is O((r_d/r_c)^3), so the error is well under 0.1 % for accumulation-mode particles and rises to a few per cent for r_d ≲ 20 nm. Use kohler_critical when that matters.

Aeolus.AerosolCloud.droplet_effective_radius — Method
droplet_effective_radius(lwc, N_d; k = 0.8) -> m

Radiatively effective droplet radius r_eff = ⟨r³⟩/⟨r²⟩, obtained from the mean-volume radius through the spectral-shape factor k = (r_v/r_eff)³:

\[r_\mathrm{eff} = \left(\frac{3χ}{4π ρ_w N_d k}\right)^{1/3}\]

Martin et al. (1994) measured k ≈ 0.80 ± 0.07 in continental stratocumulus and k ≈ 0.67 ± 0.07 in maritime — maritime spectra are broader, so the effective radius sits further above the volume radius. k = 1 would be a monodisperse cloud.

This is the radius TwoStream.mie_cloud_optics (Phase 33) consumes, and τ = 3·LWP/(2 ρ_w r_eff) is the link between the two — see cloud_optical_depth_lwp. Note Phase 33 works in μm and g m⁻²; this function returns metres.

Aeolus.AerosolCloud.droplet_number_from_effective_radius — Method
droplet_number_from_effective_radius(lwc, r_eff; k = 0.8) -> m⁻³

Inverse of droplet_effective_radius — the droplet number a retrieval of r_eff and LWC implies.

Aeolus.AerosolCloud.droplet_volume_radius — Method
droplet_volume_radius(lwc, N_d) -> m

Mean-volume droplet radius, r_v = (3 χ / (4π ρ_w N_d))^{1/3}, for a liquid water content χ (kg m⁻³) shared among N_d droplets (m⁻³).

At fixed χ it is exactly ∝ N_d^{-1/3} — the Twomey scaling, before any spectral-shape correction.

Aeolus.AerosolCloud.equilibrium_radius — Method
equilibrium_radius(r_dry, s, κ; T_K = 298.15) -> m

Wet radius of the stable equilibrium at ambient supersaturation s, i.e. the smaller root of s_eq(r) = s. Returns NaN if s exceeds the critical supersaturation, because no equilibrium exists then — the particle is activated and grows without bound.

Bisection between r_dry and the (numerically exact) critical radius, on which branch s_eq is strictly increasing.

For κ ≤ KAPPA_MIN the dry radius is returned, matching AerosolOptics.wet_radius: a hydrophobic particle takes up no water.

Aeolus.AerosolCloud.growth_coefficient — Method
growth_coefficient(T_K, p; constants, r = Inf, α_c = 1.0, α_T = 1.0) -> m² s⁻¹

Condensational growth coefficient G in

\[r\,\frac{dr}{dt} = G\,(S - S_\mathrm{eq})\]

combining vapour diffusion to the droplet and conduction of latent heat away from it (Pruppacher & Klett eq. 13-28):

\[G^{-1} = \frac{ρ_w R T}{e_s D_v M_w} + \frac{L ρ_w}{K_a T}\left(\frac{L M_w}{R T} - 1\right)\]

G ≈ 1 × 10⁻¹⁰ m² s⁻¹ = 100 μm² s⁻¹ near cloud base, with the two terms of comparable size — neglecting either is a factor-of-two error.

Kinetic (non-continuum) corrections. When a finite droplet radius r is supplied, D_v and K_a are reduced by the Fuchs-Sutugin type factors

\[D_v' = \frac{D_v}{1 + \frac{D_v}{α_c r}\sqrt{2π M_w/(RT)}},\qquad K_a' = \frac{K_a}{1 + \frac{K_a}{α_T r ρ_a c_p}\sqrt{2π M_a/(RT)}}\]

which matter below ~1 μm and therefore during activation. They are off by default (r = Inf) because the Abdul-Razzak & Ghan parameterisation is derived in the continuum limit, and mixing the two would confuse parameterisation error with kinetic-limitation physics in the validation. Supply r (and, realistically, α_c ≈ 0.06-1.0) to turn them on.

Aeolus.AerosolCloud.kohler_critical — Method
kohler_critical(r_dry, κ; T_K = 298.15) -> (r_crit = …, s_crit = …)

Critical radius and critical supersaturation found by numerically maximising AerosolOptics.kohler_saturation, with no dilute approximation.

A golden-section search on ln r is used: the Köhler curve is unimodal on (r_d, ∞) for every κ > 0, so the search is globally convergent without a derivative.

This is the reference against which the closed forms critical_supersaturation and critical_radius are checked, and it is what defines "activated" in the parcel model.

Aeolus.AerosolCloud.kohler_equilibrium_supersaturation — Method
kohler_equilibrium_supersaturation(r_wet, r_dry, κ; T_K = 298.15) -> s

Equilibrium supersaturation s = S − 1 over a solution droplet of wet radius r_wet grown on a dry particle of radius r_dry (both in metres), in the κ-Köhler representation.

This delegates to AerosolOptics.kohler_saturation — there is one κ-Köhler implementation in the package and this is not it. The only differences are the unit (metres here, μm there) and the offset by one, both of which exist because activation physics is written in terms of a small supersaturation rather than a saturation ratio near unity.

The curve rises from s = −1 at r_wet → r_dry⁺, peaks at critical_radius, and decays back to zero from above. Below the peak an equilibrium is stable (a perturbed droplet is restored); above it the droplet grows without bound and is said to have activated.

Aeolus.AerosolCloud.kohler_slope — Method
kohler_slope(r_wet, r_dry, κ; T_K = 298.15) -> ∂S/∂r   [m⁻¹]

Analytic radial derivative of the κ-Köhler saturation ratio.

With u = r³, a = r_d³, b = a(1−κ) and f = (u−a)/(u−b):

\[\frac{∂S}{∂r} = e^{A/r}\left[ 3r^2 \frac{κ a}{(u-b)^2} - f\,\frac{A}{r^2} \right]\]

This is the one place where the κ-Köhler expression is differentiated rather than called, because the exponential integrator used by the parcel model needs the local relaxation rate λ = 2G ∂s_eq/∂(r²) to step the haze population stably. The test suite verifies it against a centred finite difference of AerosolOptics.kohler_saturation.

The sign is the branch indicator: > 0 on the stable (haze) branch, < 0 beyond the critical radius.

Aeolus.AerosolCloud.kohler_stable — Method
kohler_stable(r_wet, r_dry, κ; T_K = 298.15) -> Bool

true when the droplet sits on the stable (rising) branch of the Köhler curve, i.e. ∂S/∂r > 0. Equivalent to r_wet < r_crit, but evaluated from the exact slope rather than the dilute critical radius.

Aeolus.AerosolCloud.mass_above_radius — Method
mass_above_radius(m, r_cut) -> kg m⁻³

Dry mass above a cut-off radius. The mass distribution of a log-normal is itself log-normal with the same σ_g and a median shifted to r_g exp(3 ln²σ_g), so the cut-off simply moves by 3 lnσ_g/√2 in the erfc argument. For σ_g = 2 that shift is 1.47 — i.e. the mass sits about 1.5 standard deviations coarser than the number, which is why a small activated number fraction can still remove most of the aerosol mass.

Aeolus.AerosolCloud.mode_critical_supersaturation — Function
mode_critical_supersaturation(m, T_K) -> s_m

Critical supersaturation of a particle at the mode's median dry radius. This is Abdul-Razzak & Ghan's S_mi, and their algebra is entirely written in terms of it and σ_g.

Aeolus.AerosolCloud.mode_dry_mass — Method

Dry mass concentration of a mode, kg m⁻³: M = N ρ (4π/3) r_g³ exp(4.5 ln²σ_g).

Aeolus.AerosolCloud.number_above_radius — Method
number_above_radius(m, r_cut) -> m⁻³

Number concentration of a log-normal mode above a cut-off dry radius,

\[N(>r) = \frac{N}{2}\,\mathrm{erfc}\!\left( \frac{\ln(r/r_g)}{\sqrt2 \ln σ_g}\right)\]

exact for the log-normal, using GasOptics.erfc_series.

Aeolus.AerosolCloud.parcel_activation — Method
parcel_activation(modes, w, T_K, p; settings, q, constants) -> ParcelResult

Explicit adiabatic-parcel activation: the reference calculation.

What is integrated. Each log-normal mode is discretised into nbins dry-size bins whose number concentrations come from the exact log-normal integral (erf differences of the bin edges, so the discretisation conserves number by construction). The state is the parcel temperature, pressure, height, supersaturation, and one wet radius per bin. The equations are

\[\frac{dr_j}{dt} = \frac{G}{r_j}\bigl(s - s_\mathrm{eq}(r_j, r_{d,j}, κ_j)\bigr)\]

\[\frac{ds}{dt} = α w - γ\frac{dχ}{dt}, \qquad χ = \frac{4π ρ_w}{3}\sum_j n_j r_j^3\]

\[\frac{dT}{dt} = -\frac{g w}{c_p} + \frac{L}{c_p ρ_a}\frac{dχ}{dt}, \qquad \frac{dp}{dt} = -ρ_a g w\]

with s_eq the exact κ-Köhler supersaturation (no dilute approximation), so the haze population equilibrates correctly and the activation barrier is the real one.

Why it needs a special integrator. The growth equation is violently stiff: a 10 nm haze particle relaxes to its equilibrium radius with a time constant of ~50 μs, while the supersaturation itself evolves over seconds. Explicit stepping would need 10⁷ steps. Instead the equation is written for y = r², where it becomes dy/dt = 2G(s − s_eq(y)), s_eq is linearised about the current radius, and each bin is advanced with the exponential integrator

\[Δy_j = 2GΔt\,φ_1(-λ_jΔt)\,(\bar s - s_{eq,j}),\qquad λ_j = 2G\,\frac{∂s_\mathrm{eq}}{∂y}\]

which is exact for the linearised problem and unconditionally stable for λ > 0 — it drives strongly-relaxing bins straight onto their quasi-equilibrium instead of oscillating. Because Δy_j is affine in the step-mean supersaturation s̄, the supersaturation equation closes into a single scalar linear equation each step, solved directly (no iteration, no operator-splitting error):

\[\bar s = θ s^{n+1} + (1-θ)s^n,\quad Δχ = P\bar s - Q,\quad s^{n+1} = \frac{s^n(1-γP(1-θ)) + αwΔt + γQ}{1 + γPθ}\]

with θ = 1/2 (trapezoidal). The step is shortened whenever γP > 1/2 (the phase-relaxation limit), whenever an unstable bin would be advanced by more than 0.3/|λ|, or whenever any bin's r² would more than double.

Definition of "activated". The primary count is the dynamic one: bins whose wet radius exceeds their (numerically exact) critical radius at the moment S peaks. The cut-off dry radius is interpolated logarithmically between the two bins that straddle r = r_crit and the activated number is then evaluated from the analytic log-normal integral above that cut, so the answer is not quantised by the bin width.

What is deliberately absent. No kinetic (non-continuum) growth correction, no solute-dependent surface tension, no entrainment, no collision-coalescence, and no ice. Each of those would move S_max and none of them is part of the ARG derivation, so including them here would corrupt the comparison this model exists to make.

Aeolus.AerosolCloud.process_collisions! — Method
process_collisions!(res, f; mode = 0) -> N_merged

In-cloud aerosol processing by collision-coalescence.

When two cloud droplets collide they make one droplet carrying both solute masses. If that droplet later evaporates it leaves one dry particle of mass m₁ + m₂ — larger, by 2^{1/3} in radius, than either of its parents. The aerosol population that emerges from a cloud is therefore shifted coarser than the one that went in, even though no material was added. Since CCN activity goes as r_d^{3/2}, those processed particles activate at lower supersaturation next time: the cloud has made its own CCN more effective. This is a first-order control on the second indirect effect and is invisible to any scheme that treats aerosol as a fixed background.

f is the fraction of activated droplets removed by coalescence. Activated number falls by f; activated mass is unchanged. Pass mode = i to process a single mode, or 0 (the default) for all.

Number is intentionally not conserved here — coalescence destroys particle number, which is the whole point. Mass is conserved exactly.

Aeolus.AerosolCloud.regenerate! — Function
regenerate!(res, f = 1.0) -> N_returned

Evaporate a fraction f of the cloud droplets and return their solute to the interstitial population of the mode it came from.

This is not bookkeeping pedantry. A cloud that forms and evaporates without precipitating returns its CCN, so the aerosol is available to the next cloud; but it does not return them unchanged, because while they were droplets they coalesced and took up secondary mass. Since the interstitial population is reconstructed from its number and mass moments, the returned particles automatically widen the interstitial median radius — see process_collisions! and reservoir_dry_radius.

Number and mass are conserved exactly.

Aeolus.AerosolCloud.reservoir_dry_radius — Method
reservoir_dry_radius(res, i; phase = :interstitial) -> m

Median dry radius of one population of mode i, diagnosed from its number and mass. phase is :interstitial, :activated or :total.

This is the quantity that carries the memory of in-cloud processing: a mode that has been through a cloud cycle with coalescence comes back with a larger r_g than it went in with.

Aeolus.AerosolCloud.supersaturation_sink — Method
supersaturation_sink(T_K, p; q, constants) -> γ   [m³ kg⁻¹]

Supersaturation depletion per unit condensed water,

\[γ = \frac{R T}{e_s M_w} + \frac{M_w L^2}{c_p\,p\,M_a\,T}\]

so that dS/dt|_cond = −γ dχ/dt with χ the liquid water content in kg m⁻³. The first term is the direct removal of vapour; the second is the reduction of the supersaturation by latent heating.

Units warning. The form above is Abdul-Razzak & Ghan's, and it multiplies a mass concentration tendency. The equivalent coefficient for the liquid water mixing ratio (kg kg⁻¹) is γ ρ_a, i.e. 1/w_s + L²/(R_v c_p T²). The two differ by ρ_a ≈ 1.1, which is small enough to hide and large enough to matter.

Aeolus.AerosolCloud.supersaturation_source — Method
supersaturation_source(T_K, p; q, constants) -> α   [m⁻¹]

Adiabatic supersaturation production per unit ascent,

\[α = \frac{g M_w L}{c_p R T^2} - \frac{g M_a}{R T}\]

so that dS/dt|_dyn = α w. The first term is the cooling-driven fall in e_s; the second is the fall in the ambient vapour pressure with pressure, which opposes it. The difference is ≈ 5 × 10⁻⁴ m⁻¹ at 283 K and 900 hPa — an updraught of 1 m s⁻¹ would drive the supersaturation up at 0.05 % per second if nothing condensed.

Aeolus.AerosolCloud.thermal_conductivity_air — Method
thermal_conductivity_air(T_K) -> W m⁻¹ K⁻¹

Thermal conductivity of moist air, K_a = 10⁻³(4.39 + 0.071 T) (Beard & Pruppacher 1971). ≈ 0.0245 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹ at 283 K.

Aeolus.AerosolCloud.twomey_albedo_change — Method
twomey_albedo_change(A, ΔlnN_d) -> ΔA

First-indirect (Twomey) albedo change for a given fractional change in droplet number at fixed liquid water path, to first order: ΔA = A(1−A)/3 · Δln N_d.

Doubling N_d in a cloud of albedo 0.5 brightens it by 0.083 × ln2 ≈ 0.058 — about six albedo points, which is a very large radiative perturbation by climate standards.

Aeolus.AerosolCloud.twomey_radius_exponent — Method
twomey_radius_exponent(lwc, N1, N2; k = 0.8) -> dln r_eff / dln N_d

Measured exponent of the effective-radius response to droplet number at fixed liquid water content. Analytically exactly −1/3; this function computes it from the implementation, so it is a check rather than a restatement.

Aeolus.AerosolCloud.water_vapor_diffusivity — Method
water_vapor_diffusivity(T_K, p) -> m² s⁻¹

Diffusivity of water vapour in air (Hall & Pruppacher 1976):

\[D_v = 2.11\times10^{-5}\,(T/T_0)^{1.94}\,(p_0/p)\]

with T_0 = 273.15 K, p_0 = 101325 Pa. ≈ 2.2 × 10⁻⁵ m² s⁻¹ near cloud base. The 1/p dependence is why droplet growth is faster — and supersaturations therefore lower — at altitude for the same updraught.
