P3 sedimentation, reflectivity and precipitation fraction (Phases 40, 44)

Part of the P3 microphysics API: sedimentation with one fall speed per moment and Courant sub-stepping, the radar reflectivity diagnostics (rain Rayleigh $Z$, ice equivalent $Z_e$), and the Phase 44 sub-grid precipitation fraction carried down the column with maximum, random or maximum-random overlap (p3_precipitation_fraction, p3_fractional_process_rates, p3_step_column). The ice fall speed these moments are weighted with (p3_fall_speed, p3_bulk_fall_speed) follows from the mass–dimension relation and is documented on the P3 microphysics page. The rain sedimentation here is also what the Kessler scheme in Aeolus.Microphysics delegates its Marshall–Palmer fall speed to (see Physics modules). Theory: P3 microphysics, sections "Sedimentation, size sorting and radar reflectivity" and "Terminal-velocity spectrum and sub-grid precipitation fraction".

Aeolus.P3Microphysics.P3_K2_ICE — Constant
P3_K2_ICE = 0.176

Dielectric factor |K_i|² of solid ice (density 917 kg m⁻³) at centimetre wavelengths (Smith 1984). The ratio |K_i|²/|K_w|² = 0.189 is the factor that appears in the P3 reference code's ice reflectivity.

Aeolus.P3Microphysics.P3_K2_WATER — Constant
P3_K2_WATER = 0.93

Dielectric factor |K_w|² = |(ε-1)/(ε+2)|² of liquid water at centimetre (S/C-band) wavelengths, the value radar reflectivity is conventionally normalised by (Battan 1973; Smith 1984).

Aeolus.P3Microphysics.P3_RAIN_D_MEAN_MAX — Constant

Upper limit of the P3 rain mean diameter (μ+1)/λ, m (P3 inv_Drmax = 1/0.002).

Aeolus.P3Microphysics.P3_RAIN_D_MEAN_MIN — Constant

Lower limit of the P3 rain mean diameter (μ+1)/λ, m (P3 lammax = (μ+1)·1e5).

Aeolus.P3Microphysics.p3_bulk_fall_speed_z — Method
p3_bulk_fall_speed_z(μ, λ, md::P3MassDimension; ρ_air, η_air) -> V_Z

Reflectivity-weighted fall speed (m s⁻¹) of P3 ice, weighting each particle by m(D)² (the quantity equivalent reflectivity is made of, see p3_ice_reflectivity):

V_Z = ∫ V(D) m(D)² N(D) dD / ∫ m(D)² N(D) dD.

This is the sedimentation speed of the sixth moment in three-moment P3 (Milbrandt et al. 2021, JAS 78, 439-458, introduce it; two-moment P3 needs only V_N, V_M from p3_bulk_fall_speed). V_Z ≥ V_M ≥ V_N. Air state defaults as in p3_fall_speed.

Aeolus.P3Microphysics.p3_dbz — Method
p3_dbz(Z; Z_min = 1e-10) -> dBZ

Logarithmic reflectivity 10 log₁₀(Z / 1 mm⁶ m⁻³). Z below Z_min (including zero) is floored, giving -100 dBZ by default, so an empty cell has a finite, clearly non-meteorological value.

Aeolus.P3Microphysics.p3_ice_reflectivity — Method
p3_ice_reflectivity(N0, μ, λ, md::P3MassDimension; K2_ratio = P3_K2_ICE / P3_K2_WATER) -> Z_e in mm⁶ m⁻³

Equivalent radar reflectivity factor of P3 ice with gamma size distribution N(D) = N₀ D^μ e^{-λD} (N₀ per m³ of air). Each ice particle of mass m scatters (Rayleigh) like a sphere of solid ice of the same mass, i.e. of melted-equivalent diameter D_eq = (6m/(π ρ_ice))^{1/3}, so

Z_e = (|K_i|²/|K_w|²) ∫ D_eq⁶ N dD
    = (|K_i|²/|K_w|²) (6/(π ρ_ice))² ∫ m(D)² N(D) dD.

Equivalently a particle of bulk density ρ_p contributes (|K_i|²/|K_w|²)(ρ_p/ρ_ice)² D⁶: low-density snow is a weak target for its size. |K_i|²/|K_w|² = 0.176/0.93 = 0.189 (Smith 1984), the factor in the P3 reference code. Because m(D) is a piecewise power law, m² is too and Z_e is a sum of truncated gamma moments (gamma_psd_partial_moment) — exact, no quadrature.

References: Smith (1984, JCAM 23, 1258-1260); Morrison & Milbrandt (2015), §2; Heymsfield et al. (2005) for density scaling.

Aeolus.P3Microphysics.p3_ice_sedimentation_velocities — Method
p3_ice_sedimentation_velocities(q_i, q_rim, B_rim, N_i, ρ_air; T_air = nothing, μ = 0, p = P3_DEFAULT_ICE, q_small = 1e-14) -> (V_N, V_M)

Per-cell number- and mass-weighted P3 ice fall speeds. In each cell the mass-dimension state is diagnosed from the prognostic rime variables (p3_mass_dimension), the slope from q_i/N_i with P3's mean size limiter (μ+1)/λ ∈ [2 μm, 5 mm + 20 mm·F_rim²] (p3_size_distribution), and V_N, V_M from p3_bulk_fall_speed at the local air density and — when the temperature column T_air (K) is given — viscosity (p3_air_viscosity; otherwise at p.T_ref).

Aeolus.P3Microphysics.p3_rain_bulk_fall_speed — Method
p3_rain_bulk_fall_speed(μ, λ; ρ_air = 1.225) -> (V_N, V_M, V_Z)

Number-, mass- and reflectivity-weighted fall speeds (m s⁻¹) of a gamma rain size distribution N(D) ∝ D^μ e^{-λD}:

V_N = ∫ V N dD / ∫ N dD,
V_M = ∫ V D³ N dD / ∫ D³ N dD,
V_Z = ∫ V D⁶ N dD / ∫ D⁶ N dD,

with V(D) from p3_rain_fall_speed. V_N moves N_r, V_M moves q_r, V_Z a third (reflectivity) moment. Since V(D) increases with D, V_N ≤ V_M ≤ V_Z, equal only in the monodisperse limit. Integrated by composite Gauss-Legendre quadrature split at the kinks of V(D), as for ice (p3_bulk_fall_speed).

Reference: Morrison & Milbrandt (2015), §2d; Milbrandt & Yau (2005, JAS 62, 3051-3064) for multi-moment sedimentation.

Aeolus.P3Microphysics.p3_rain_fall_speed — Method
p3_rain_fall_speed(D; ρ_air = 1.225) -> m s⁻¹

Terminal fall speed of a raindrop of diameter D (m) in air of density ρ_air (kg m⁻³). The sea-level speed is Superdroplet.terminal_velocity (Stokes / Beard 1976 / Atlas et al. 1973 — one source of truth for drop fall speeds in the package), corrected for air density with the Foote & du Toit (1969) factor used in P3,

V(D, ρ_a) = V(D, ρ₀) (ρ₀/ρ_a)^0.54,   ρ₀ = 1.225 kg m⁻³.

A 1 mm drop falls at ≈ 4.0 m s⁻¹ and large drops level off near 9.2-9.6 m s⁻¹ at sea level.

References: Beard (1976, JAS 33, 851-864); Atlas, Srivastava & Sekhon (1973, Rev. Geophys. 11, 1-35); Foote & du Toit (1969, JAM 8, 249-253).

Aeolus.P3Microphysics.p3_rain_limit_number — Method
p3_rain_limit_number(q_r, N_r; μ_r = 0, D_mean_min = P3_RAIN_D_MEAN_MIN, D_mean_max = P3_RAIN_D_MEAN_MAX) -> N_r′

Rain number made consistent with the mean-size limits of p3_rain_size_distribution: if q_r/N_r implies a mean diameter (μ+1)/λ outside [D_mean_min, D_mean_max] (10 μm, 2 mm), return the number that puts it exactly on the limit, keeping the mass,

N_r′ = q_r Γ(μ+1) λ_lim³ / ((π/6) ρ_w Γ(μ+4));

otherwise N_r unchanged. Rain with mass but no number (N_r ≤ 0) is given the number of the largest allowed drops. Mass is never changed; number is not conserved by this step — the same trade the P3 reference code makes when it limits λ_r (get_rain_dsd2 resets nr = 6 λ³ q_r / (π ρ_w (μ+3)(μ+2)(μ+1))).

Aeolus.P3Microphysics.p3_rain_rate — Method
p3_rain_rate(N0, μ, λ; ρ_air = 1.225) -> mm h⁻¹

Surface-equivalent rain rate of a gamma drop size distribution whose intercept N₀ is per m³ of air (m⁻⁴⁻μ):

R = ∫ (π/6) D³ V(D) N(D) dD   (m³ of water m⁻² s⁻¹) × 3.6e6.

This is ρ_air q_r V_M / ρ_w converted to mm h⁻¹.

Aeolus.P3Microphysics.p3_rain_reflectivity — Method
p3_rain_reflectivity(N0, μ, λ) -> Z in mm⁶ m⁻³

Rayleigh radar reflectivity factor of rain with gamma drop size distribution N(D) = N₀ D^μ e^{-λD}, N₀ per m³ of air:

Z = ∫ D⁶ N(D) dD = N₀ Γ(μ+7) / λ^{μ+7}   (m⁶ m⁻³),

returned in the radar unit mm⁶ m⁻³ (× 10¹⁸). Valid where drops are small compared with the wavelength (Rayleigh regime; S-band and, to within ~1 dB at moderate rain rates, C-band). Convert with p3_dbz.

Reference: Battan (1973), Radar Observation of the Atmosphere; Marshall & Palmer (1948, J. Meteor. 5, 165-166).

Aeolus.P3Microphysics.p3_rain_sedimentation_velocities — Method
p3_rain_sedimentation_velocities(q_r, N_r, ρ_air; μ_r = 0, q_small = 1e-14) -> (V_N, V_M)

Per-cell number- and mass-weighted rain fall speeds (m s⁻¹) for columns of q_r (kg kg⁻¹) and N_r (kg⁻¹), from p3_rain_size_distribution and p3_rain_bulk_fall_speed with the local air-density correction. Cells with q_r < q_small or no number get zero speed.

Aeolus.P3Microphysics.p3_rain_size_distribution — Method
p3_rain_size_distribution(q_r, N_r; μ_r = 0, D_mean_min = P3_RAIN_D_MEAN_MIN, D_mean_max = P3_RAIN_D_MEAN_MAX) -> (N0, μ, λ)

Gamma size distribution N(D) = N₀ D^μ e^{-λD} of rain with mass q_r and number N_r (both per kg of air, or both per m³), drops being spheres of liquid water (ρ_w = Superdroplet.DEFAULT_MICRO.ρ_water):

λ = [ (π/6) ρ_w N_r Γ(μ+4) / (q_r Γ(μ+1)) ]^{1/3}

(gamma_psd_slope), N₀ from gamma_psd_intercept. The mean (number-weighted) diameter (μ+1)/λ is limited to [D_mean_min, D_mean_max], defaults 10 μm and 2 mm, i.e. λ ∈ (μ+1)·[500, 10⁵] m⁻¹ — the rain slope limits of the P3 reference code (get_rain_dsd2: lammax = (μ_r+1)·1e5, lammin = (μ_r+1)/0.002; earlier P3 versions used 0.8 mm). This is the single rain PSD of the package: the process rates (p3_process_rates), sedimentation and radar reflectivity all use it. The upper size limit matters in sedimentation: number falls slower than mass, so the leading edge of a rain shaft is left with mass but almost no number, and without a limit its diagnosed drops grow without bound (the "excessive size sorting" of two-moment schemes; Wacker & Seifert 2001, Milbrandt & McTaggart-Cowan 2010). When the limit is active N₀ is set from N_r, so the returned PSD carries the number but not exactly the mass; to restore consistency the caller adjusts N_r with p3_rain_limit_number, as P3 does.

Returns nothing if q_r ≤ 0 or N_r ≤ 0.

Reference: Morrison & Milbrandt (2015), §2c; Seifert & Beheng (2006); P3 microphy_p3.f90 v5.5.2, get_rain_dsd2.

Aeolus.P3Microphysics.p3_sediment! — Method
p3_sediment!(fields, velocities, ρ, dz, dt; courant_max = 1) -> (surface, nsub)

Sediment the columns in the tuple fields (vectors of specific quantities per kg of air, e.g. q_r, N_r) over time dt (s) with first-order upwind, flux-form finite volumes, sub-stepping so that the Courant number never exceeds courant_max ≤ 1.

velocities(fields) must return a tuple, one vector per field, of fall speeds (m s⁻¹, ≥ 0, positive downward) at cell centres; it is called again at every sub-step, so the speeds follow the evolving PSD (as in the P3 reference code). ρ is air density (kg m⁻³) and dz layer thickness (m, scalar or vector); cell k = 1 is at the surface.

For each field ψ the update over a sub-step h is

ρ_k ψ_k Δz_k ← ρ_k ψ_k Δz_k − h F_k + h F_{k+1},   F_k = ρ_k ψ_k V_k,

with F_{nz+1} = 0 (nothing enters through the model top) and h F_1 leaving through the surface. Properties, all exact in floating point up to round-off:

  • conservative — Σ ρ ψ Δz changes only by the surface flux;
  • positive — h is chosen so C_k = V_k h / Δz_k ≤ courant_max, whence the outflow never exceeds the cell content; the outflow is also capped at the content so rounding cannot drive ψ negative.

The sub-step is h = min(remaining, courant_max · min_k Δz_k / V_k), recomputed each sub-step. Returns the time-integrated surface flux of each field (Σ h F_1, e.g. kg m⁻² of precipitation for q) and the number of sub-steps taken.

Reference: Morrison & Milbrandt (2015), §2d; the P3 reference code sub-steps its sedimentation in the same way.

Aeolus.P3Microphysics.p3_sediment_ice! — Method
p3_sediment_ice!(q_i, q_rim, B_rim, N_i, ρ, dz, dt; T_air = nothing, μ = 0, p = P3_DEFAULT_ICE, courant_max = 1) -> (P_q, P_rim, P_B, P_N, nsub)

Sediment the single P3 ice category: q_i, q_rim and B_rim fall together at the mass-weighted speed — so the rime fraction and rime density of the falling mass are carried unchanged — and N_i at the number-weighted speed (p3_ice_sedimentation_velocities), recomputed every sub-step by p3_sediment!. Returns the surface accumulations of each field and the sub-step count.

Unlike rain, N_i is not adjusted here: the ice slope limiter bounds the diagnosed fall speeds, and the matching number adjustment belongs with the process rates.

Reference: Morrison & Milbrandt (2015), §2d.

Aeolus.P3Microphysics.p3_sediment_rain! — Method
p3_sediment_rain!(q_r, N_r, ρ, dz, dt; μ_r = 0, courant_max = 1, limit_number = true) -> (P_q, P_N, nsub)

Sediment two-moment rain in a column: q_r (kg kg⁻¹) falls at the mass-weighted and N_r (kg⁻¹) at the number-weighted fall speed (p3_rain_sedimentation_velocities), both recomputed every sub-step by p3_sediment!. Returns the accumulated surface precipitation P_q (kg m⁻², i.e. mm of water), the number of drops reaching the ground per m² P_N, and the sub-step count.

With limit_number = true (default, as in P3) N_r is passed through p3_rain_limit_number before every sub-step and at the end, so that the size-sorted leading edge of a shaft cannot hold drops with a mean size above D_mean_max; mass is conserved exactly either way, number only with limit_number = false.

Aeolus.P3Microphysics.p3_fractional_process_rates — Method
p3_fractional_process_rates(s::P3ParcelState, cloud_fraction, precip_fraction,
                            pp = P3_DEFAULT_PROCESS; f_min = 0.01, dt = nothing) -> NamedTuple

The rates of p3_process_rates for a cell only partly covered by cloud (cloud_fraction f_c) and by precipitation (precip_fraction f_p, e.g. from p3_precipitation_fraction). The cell is split into four regions, cloud and rain maximally overlapped inside it (E3SM/SCREAM P3): cloud ∩ rain (area min(f_c, f_p)), cloud only, rain only and clear. Each region gets the in-cloud q_c/f_c, N_c/f_c and/or in-precipitation q_r/f_p, N_r/f_p, vapour q_v,cld = min(q_s, q_v/f_c) in cloud and q_v,clr = (q_v - f_c q_v,cld)/(1 - f_c) in clear air (grid-mean vapour kept), and the grid-mean ice; its rates are p3_process_rates of that state, and the cell rate is the area-weighted sum. The rime density of cloud rime is combined so that the rime volume rate is the sum of the regions'.

Consequences, all asserted in the tests:

  • f_c = f_p = 1 returns p3_process_rates(s) exactly;
  • KK2000 accretion 67 (q_c q_r)^1.15 over overlap area a changes by the factor a (f_c f_p)^{-1.15} relative to the grid mean (f^{-0.15} = 1.231 for f_p = 0.25, f_c = 1); autoconversion ∝ q_c^2.47 N_c^-1.79 by f_c^{0.32};
  • rain under cloud does not evaporate (the cloudy air is saturated); rain in the clear part evaporates at the clear-sky deficit.

A species present with a fraction below f_min is given f_min, which bounds the in-region mixing ratio at q/f_min. Fractions are ignored for absent species. Water conservation is unaffected: the rates still act through p3_tendencies.

With dt given, each region's rates are limited by p3_limit_rates over dt against that region's contents and saturation state before they are weighted (with f_c, f_p ∈ {0, 1}: the grid-mean p3_limit_rates itself). The result is then a complete, limited set of rates and must not be limited again at the grid mean, which would be wrong twice over: a grid-mean limiter lets rain collect, within one step, cloud water lying outside the rain area and evaporate rain that sits in saturated cloudy air, and, where the grid mean is subsaturated with respect to ice, it switches off the deposition that the cloudy (water-saturated) region does have. p3_step uses this form. Without dt (the default) the rates are unlimited.

Aeolus.P3Microphysics.p3_precipitation_fraction — Method
p3_precipitation_fraction(cloud_fraction, q_c, q_r; overlap = :maximum_random, q_small = 1e-14) -> f_p

Precipitation fraction of every level of a column (vectors, k = 1 at the surface, k = nz at the top, as for p3_sediment!), carried downward from the top:

  • a level's cloud counts as a precipitation source where it holds cloud water (q_c ≥ q_small), with its cloud_fraction;
  • rain arriving from above is inherited only if the level above holds rain (q_r ≥ q_small): where it has all evaporated the precipitation area is reset, as in the IFS (Tiedtke 1993);
  • the two are combined by p3_precipitation_overlap;
  • rain with neither a cloud nor rain above it (e.g. advected in from a neighbouring column) is given fraction 1, i.e. grid-mean behaviour.

Levels without rain can carry a nonzero fraction (the area rain forming there would cover); it multiplies no rain.

Aeolus.P3Microphysics.p3_precipitation_overlap — Function
p3_precipitation_overlap(P_above, c_above, c_here, overlap = :maximum_random) -> P

Fraction of a level covered by precipitation, given the precipitation fraction P_above arriving from the level above, the cloud fraction c_above of that level and the cloud fraction c_here of this level (new precipitation forms under all of it). overlap:

  • :maximum — P = max(P_above, c_here);

  • :random — P = P_above + c_here - P_above c_here;

  • :maximum_random — Geleyn & Hollingsworth (1979), maximum overlap between vertically adjacent cloudy layers, random across clear gaps:

    P = 1 - (1 - P_above)(1 - max(c_here, c̃))/(1 - c̃),   c̃ = min(c_above, P_above).

    (c̃ ≤ P_above because only cloud that fed the arriving rain can overlap it; for P_above ≤ c_above this reduces to maximum overlap.)

For every input max ≤ maximum_random ≤ random, all three are ≥ max(P_above, c_here), and all are nondecreasing in P_above, so the ordering holds all the way down a column. Inputs are clamped to [0, 1].

References: Geleyn & Hollingsworth (1979, Beitr. Phys. Atmos. 52, 1-16); Jakob & Klein (2000, QJRMS 126, 2525-2544); Tiedtke (1993, MWR 121, 3040-3061).

Aeolus.P3Microphysics.p3_step_column — Function
p3_step_column(column::AbstractVector{<:P3ParcelState}, dt, pp = P3_DEFAULT_PROCESS;
               cloud_fraction, overlap = :maximum_random, adjust = true, f_min = 0.01)
    -> (new_column, precip_fraction)

p3_step for every level of a column (k = 1 at the surface) with sub-grid cloud and precipitation fractions: the precipitation fraction is first carried down the column by p3_precipitation_fraction from the given cloud_fraction vector and the start-of-step q_c, q_r, then each level is stepped with p3_fractional_process_rates. Sedimentation is separate (p3_sediment_rain!); it needs no fraction, because the fall speeds depend on q_r/N_r only, which the in-precipitation scaling leaves unchanged. Returns the new states and the fractions used.
