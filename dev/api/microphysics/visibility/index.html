Visibility diagnostic (Phase 44)

Koschmieder visibility from droplet, haze and molecular extinction at 550 nm, plus the Kunkel (1984) and Gultepe et al. (2006) fog parameterisations. Theory: Visibility.

Aeolus.Visibility — Module
Visibility

Phase 44 — visibility diagnostic for transport and air-quality use.

Meteorological visibility is the distance at which a black object against the horizon sky can just be distinguished. Koschmieder (1924) showed that, in a homogeneous atmosphere of extinction coefficient β_ext, the apparent contrast of such an object decays as C(x) = exp(−β_ext x), so the distance at which it falls to the observer's contrast threshold ε is

\[V = \frac{-\ln ε}{β_{ext}}.\]

Two thresholds are in use and they differ by 23 %:

  • ε = 0.02 — Koschmieder's original value, the one behind the 3.912/β rule and the one the Kunkel (1984) fit assumes. KOSCHMIEDER_THRESHOLD, the default here.
  • ε = 0.05 — the WMO meteorological optical range (MOR), V = 2.996/β (WMO-No. 8, 2018, Vol. I §9.1). MOR_THRESHOLD.

The extinction coefficient is built bottom-up at 550 nm (the peak of photopic sensitivity) from three contributions that simply add:

\[β_{ext} = \underbrace{\int π r^2 Q_{ext}(2πr/λ, m_w)\,n(r)\,dr}_{\text{droplets}} + \underbrace{β_{aer}(\mathrm{RH})}_{\text{haze}} + \underbrace{n_{air}\,σ_R(λ)}_{\text{Rayleigh}}\]

None of the three is re-implemented here:

  • Q_ext is the Phase 37 Mie solution AerosolOptics.mie_sphere with the Hale & Querry water index AerosolOptics.water_refractive_index. For x > 10⁴ (drops above ~0.9 mm at 550 nm) the Nussenzveig & Wiscombe (1980) large-sphere asymptote Q_ext = 2 + 1.9923861 x^{-2/3} is used instead. Single-x Mie values oscillate about it by up to 3 × 10⁻³ (interference ripple), but averaged over ±10 % in x the Mie excess Q_ext − 2 matches the asymptote to 0.03 % at x = 10³ and 0.14 % at x = 9 × 10³; the jump at the switch is 5 × 10⁻⁵.
  • The haze term is AerosolOptics.bulk_optics — κ-Köhler wet growth and Mie over the log-normal modes.
  • Rayleigh is GasOptics.rayleigh_cross_section, the same σ_R the radiation code uses for rayleigh_optical_depth.

Droplet spectra are given in SI (radius in m, concentration in m⁻³, liquid water content in kg m⁻³), as in AerosolCloud. The effective radius of a spectrum built from (LWC, N_d) comes from AerosolCloud.droplet_effective_radius, with the spectral-shape factor k = (r_v/r_eff)³ of the Hansen gamma distribution, so the droplet geometry is the same one the Phase 42 indirect-effect diagnostics use.

References

  • Koschmieder, H. (1924). Theorie der horizontalen Sichtweite. Beitr. Phys. freien Atm., 12, 33–53, 171–181.
  • Hansen, J. E. (1971). Multiple scattering of polarized light in planetary atmospheres. Part II. J. Atmos. Sci., 28, 1400–1426.
  • Nussenzveig, H. M. & Wiscombe, W. J. (1980). Efficiency factors in Mie scattering. Phys. Rev. Lett., 45, 1490–1494.
  • Kunkel, B. A. (1984). Parameterization of droplet terminal velocity and extinction coefficient in fog models. J. Climate Appl. Meteor., 23, 34–41.
  • Gultepe, I., Müller, M. D. & Boybeyi, Z. (2006). A new visibility parameterization for warm-fog applications in numerical weather prediction models. J. Appl. Meteor. Climatol., 45, 1469–1480.
  • WMO (2018). Guide to Instruments and Methods of Observation, WMO-No. 8, Vol. I, Ch. 9.
Aeolus.Visibility.KOSCHMIEDER_THRESHOLD — Constant

Koschmieder's contrast threshold, ε = 0.02 (V = 3.912/β). The default.

Aeolus.Visibility.MOR_THRESHOLD — Constant

WMO meteorological-optical-range threshold, ε = 0.05 (V = 2.996/β).

Aeolus.Visibility.VISIBILITY_WAVELENGTH — Constant

Reference wavelength for visibility, μm (peak photopic sensitivity).

Aeolus.Visibility.BinnedDroplets — Type
BinnedDroplets(r, n)

A discrete spectrum: n[i] droplets per m³ at radius r[i] (m). This is the form a bin scheme, a disdrometer or a superdroplet ensemble (see droplet_spectrum) naturally produces.

Aeolus.Visibility.DropletSpectrum — Type
DropletSpectrum

Abstract supertype of a liquid-droplet size distribution. Concrete types: MonodisperseDroplets, GammaDroplets, BinnedDroplets. All carry radius in m and number in m⁻³.

Aeolus.Visibility.GammaDroplets — Type
GammaDroplets(N, r_eff, v_eff)

Hansen (1971) gamma distribution of N droplets per m³ with effective radius r_eff (m) and effective variance v_eff (0 < v_eff < 0.5):

\[n(r) ∝ r^{(1-3b)/b}\,e^{-r/(ab)}, \qquad a = r_{eff},\; b = v_{eff}.\]

Its moments are analytic, ⟨r^k⟩ = (ab)^k Γ(α+1+k)/Γ(α+1) with α = (1−3b)/b, which gives exactly ⟨r³⟩/⟨r²⟩ = a and the shape factor k = (r_v/r_eff)³ = (1−b)(1−2b) (0.72 at the stratocumulus/fog value b = 0.1, inside the Martin et al. 1994 range 0.67–0.80).

Aeolus.Visibility.MonodisperseDroplets — Type
MonodisperseDroplets(N, r)

N droplets per m³, all of radius r (m). The analytic reference case: β_ext = N π r² Q_ext(2πr/λ).

Aeolus.Visibility.aerosol_extinction — Method
aerosol_extinction(modes; rh = 0.0, λ = 0.55, T_K = 298.15) -> m⁻¹

Extinction of an interstitial (unactivated) aerosol population, i.e. haze: AerosolOptics.bulk_optics(modes, λ; rh), with κ-Köhler wet growth of each mode at relative humidity rh (fraction, < 1; values ≥ 1 are capped at 0.9999 by AerosolOptics.wet_radius, i.e. the haze is held at its largest stable equilibrium size — above saturation the droplets belong in droplets, not here). Use AerosolOptics.aerosol_mixture(:urban) etc. for the OPAC mixtures.

At 550 nm and RH = 0.8 the library mixtures give V(ε = 0.02) of 177 km (continental clean), 26 km (continental polluted) and 12 km (urban), falling to 60, 9 and 5 km at RH = 0.95.

Aeolus.Visibility.droplet_extinction — Method
droplet_extinction(spec; λ = 0.55, method = :mie, nquad = 200) -> m⁻¹

Extinction coefficient of a droplet spectrum, β = ∫ π r² Q_ext(r) n(r) dr.

  • method = :mie — Q_ext from water_extinction_efficiency.
  • method = :geometric — the large-particle limit Q_ext = 2, i.e. β = 2πN⟨r²⟩ = 3 LWC / (2 ρ_w r_eff). This is the extinction implied by the τ = 3 LWP/(2ρ_w r_eff) cloud optical depth of AerosolCloud.cloud_optical_depth_lwp and TwoStream, and it is what the bulk parameterisations are built on. For fog spectra (r_eff 2–15 μm at 550 nm) Mie is 3–14 % larger; the excess falls with size as the edge term ≈ 2 x^{-2/3}.

For GammaDroplets the integral is composite Simpson in ln r over [θ·max(α+1−6√(α+1), 10⁻³(α+1)), θ(α+3+10√(α+3))] (θ = ab), covering the r²-weighted integrand to beyond 6 standard deviations. The quadrature computes the area-weighted mean efficiency Q̄ = ∫ r² Q_ext n dr / ∫ r² n dr and returns β = π N ⟨r²⟩ Q̄ with the analytic ⟨r²⟩, so the geometric limit is exact and number truncated at the small-r end (large for broad spectra, v_eff ≳ 0.4, where the number pdf ∝ r^{α} with α → −1) cannot bias the result. The number of intervals is at least nquad and at least 10 per unit of size parameter: water is so weakly absorbing at 550 nm that Q_ext(x) carries narrow resonance spikes on top of the ripple, and a coarser rule (5 points per π) aliased them into errors of up to 5 × 10⁻³. Measured against a 60 000-interval reference over r_eff = 2–30 μm, v_eff = 0.05–0.3: worst 1.9 × 10⁻³ (at r_eff = 2 μm), typically 3 × 10⁻⁴. Cost is ~15 ms per call at r_eff = 8 μm; use method = :geometric (analytic) where that matters.

Aeolus.Visibility.droplet_spectrum — Method
droplet_spectrum(sd::SuperdropletSet; volume, cell = nothing) -> BinnedDroplets

The Phase 41 superdroplet ensemble as a BinnedDroplets spectrum: each superdroplet contributes ξ/volume droplets per m³ at its wet radius. volume (m³) is the volume the (selected) superdroplets occupy; cell restricts to one Eulerian cell.

Aeolus.Visibility.extinction_from_visibility — Method
extinction_from_visibility(V; ε = KOSCHMIEDER_THRESHOLD) -> m⁻¹

Inverse of koschmieder_visibility: β_ext = −ln(ε)/V. Used to turn an observed visibility into an extinction, e.g. to compare a measured MOR with a model.

Aeolus.Visibility.fog_extinction — Method
fog_extinction(lwc, N_d; v_eff = 0.1, λ = 0.55, method = :mie) -> m⁻¹

Droplet extinction for a bulk liquid water content lwc (kg m⁻³) and droplet number N_d (m⁻³), through gamma_droplets and droplet_extinction. At fixed N_d and spectral shape, β ∝ N_d^{1/3} LWC^{2/3} in the geometric limit.

Aeolus.Visibility.gamma_droplets — Method
gamma_droplets(lwc, N_d; v_eff = 0.1) -> DropletSpectrum

The droplet spectrum implied by a bulk liquid water content lwc (kg m⁻³) and droplet number N_d (m⁻³): a GammaDroplets whose effective radius is AerosolCloud.droplet_effective_radius(lwc, N_d; k) with the gamma shape factor k = (1−v_eff)(1−2v_eff), so that spectrum_water_content returns lwc exactly. v_eff = 0 gives a MonodisperseDroplets at the mean-volume radius.

v_eff = 0.1 is a representative fog/stratus value; measured radiation fog spectra span roughly 0.05–0.3 and are often bimodal.

Aeolus.Visibility.gultepe_visibility — Method
gultepe_visibility(lwc, N_d) -> m

Gultepe et al. (2006) warm-fog visibility, V = 1.002 km / (LWC · N_d)^{0.6473} with LWC in g m⁻³ and Nd in cm⁻³, fitted to the FRAM-C observations (Nova Scotia). Here lwc is in kg m⁻³ and `Ndin m⁻³. Unlike [kunkelvisibility](@ref) it decreases with droplet number at fixed water content, as Koschmieder withβ ∝ Nd^{1/3}LWC^{2/3}` does, although with a different exponent.

Aeolus.Visibility.koschmieder_visibility — Method
koschmieder_visibility(β_ext; ε = KOSCHMIEDER_THRESHOLD) -> m

Koschmieder visibility V = −ln(ε)/β_ext for an extinction coefficient β_ext in m⁻¹. Returns Inf for β_ext ≤ 0 and NaN for a NaN extinction (a failed upstream calculation must not read as clear air). Pass ε = MOR_THRESHOLD for the WMO meteorological optical range, which is shorter by the factor ln 20 / ln 50 = 0.766.

Aeolus.Visibility.kunkel_visibility — Method
kunkel_visibility(lwc) -> m

Kunkel (1984) fog visibility, β = 144.7 LWC^{0.88} km⁻¹ with LWC in g m⁻³, converted through ε = 0.02: V = 27 m × LWC^{−0.88}. Here lwc is in kg m⁻³ like the rest of the module. Fitted to fog drop spectra; it carries no droplet-number information, so it cannot see the aerosol indirect effect on visibility.

Aeolus.Visibility.rayleigh_extinction — Method
rayleigh_extinction(; p = 101325, T = 288.15, λ = 0.55) -> m⁻¹

Molecular scattering of particle-free air, β_R = n σ_R(λ) with the number density n = p/(k_B T) and σ_R = GasOptics.rayleigh_cross_section. At 1013.25 hPa, 288.15 K and 550 nm this is 1.27 × 10⁻⁵ m⁻¹, i.e. a Rayleigh-limited visibility of 307 km at ε = 0.02 — the upper bound on visibility in the lower atmosphere.

Aeolus.Visibility.spectrum_moment — Method
spectrum_moment(spec, k::Integer) -> m^k

Number-weighted mean ⟨r^k⟩ of the spectrum (not multiplied by N). Analytic for the monodisperse and gamma forms.

Aeolus.Visibility.spectrum_number — Method
spectrum_number(spec) -> m⁻³

Total droplet number concentration.

Aeolus.Visibility.spectrum_water_content — Method
spectrum_water_content(spec) -> kg m⁻³

Liquid water content (4π/3) ρ_w N ⟨r³⟩, with ρ_w = AerosolCloud.RHO_WATER.

Aeolus.Visibility.total_extinction — Method
total_extinction(; droplets = nothing, aerosol = nothing, rh = 0.0,
                 p = 101325, T = 288.15, λ = 0.55,
                 rayleigh = true, method = :mie) -> m⁻¹

Sum of droplet, haze and molecular extinction. droplets is a DropletSpectrum (or nothing), aerosol a vector of AerosolOptics modes (or nothing). The one air temperature T is used both for the Rayleigh number density and for the Kelvin term of the haze wet growth. Extinction coefficients add because the three populations scatter independently.

Aeolus.Visibility.visibility — Method
visibility(; ε = KOSCHMIEDER_THRESHOLD, kwargs...) -> m

Koschmieder visibility of the atmosphere described by kwargs (see total_extinction): koschmieder_visibility(total_extinction(; kwargs...); ε).

Aeolus.Visibility.water_extinction_efficiency — Function
water_extinction_efficiency(r, λ = VISIBILITY_WAVELENGTH) -> Q_ext

Extinction efficiency of a liquid-water sphere of radius r (m) at wavelength λ (μm). Mie (AerosolOptics.mie_sphere with water_refractive_index(λ)) up to size parameter x = 10⁴; beyond, the Nussenzveig-Wiscombe asymptote Q_ext = 2 + 1.9923861 x^{-2/3}, which tends to the geometric-optics extinction-paradox limit 2.
