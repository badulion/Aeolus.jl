Turbulence-microphysics interaction (Phase 45)

Sub-grid distribution of the saturation deficit, partial cloud fraction and condensate, and the SGS variance that drives them (issue #12); that distribution fed into aerosol activation and stochastic condensational growth (issue #13); and the bulk moment closure of the broadening with its superdroplet/DNS validation case (issue #14: SpectralMoments, bulk_condensation_step!, parcel_ascent!, run_broadening_case). All of these live in one module, so the block below covers all three issues. The bulk state also extends Superdroplet.mean_radius, Superdroplet.relative_dispersion and MoistThermodynamics.effective_radius. The CCN spectrum they delegate to, ccn_fraction / ccn_spectrum, lives in AerosolCloud (Phase 42).

Theory: Sub-grid cloud and supersaturation PDF, Sub-grid supersaturation in activation and droplet growth and Spectral broadening: bulk closure and validation.

Aeolus.TurbulentMicrophysics — Module
TurbulentMicrophysics

Turbulence-microphysics interaction — Phase 45.

A grid cell of an LES is not uniformly saturated: unresolved turbulent fluctuations of total water q_t and liquid-ice potential temperature θ_li spread the cell over a distribution of saturation states. A scheme that applies the all-or-nothing saturation adjustment to the cell mean either clouds the whole cell or none of it; the real cell is partly cloudy, and the part that is cloudy contains more condensate than the mean-state adjustment would put there.

This module carries that distribution. Its pieces live in part-files under src/turbulent_microphysics/:

  • sgs_supersaturation.jl (issue #12) — the probability density of the Mellor (1977) saturation deficit s within a cell, its variance derived from the Smagorinsky-Lilly SGS closure, and the partial cloud fraction and mean condensate that follow from it.
  • sgs_activation.jl (issue #13) — that distribution fed into Phase 42 aerosol activation: the activated number is the PDF-mean of the CCN spectrum, which differs from the mean-state value by the curvature of the spectrum (Jensen).
  • sgs_growth.jl (issue #13) — the same fluctuations in Phase 41 condensational growth: superdroplets sample a stochastic (Ornstein- Uhlenbeck) supersaturation, so dr²/dt ∝ S spreads the radius distribution while the cell's total water is conserved exactly.
  • spectral_broadening.jl (issue #14) — the bulk counterpart: a moment closure (⟨r²⟩, Var r², Cov(r², S')) that carries the same broadening without droplets, and the adiabatic-parcel case that validates it against the superdroplet reference and DNS scaling.

All thermodynamics is delegated to the Phase 39 contract (MoistThermodynamics); the error function is GasOptics'; Köhler theory and the CCN spectrum are AerosolCloud's (Phase 42); droplet growth and the cell water/heat budget are Superdroplet's (Phase 41). The module holds no second copy of any of them.

Aeolus.TurbulentMicrophysics.BroadeningCase — Type
BroadeningCase(; kwargs...)

Adiabatic-parcel spectral-broadening case: a fixed-mass cloudy parcel ascending at w, its activated droplets growing in the parcel's own supersaturation plus sub-grid fluctuations (σ_S, τ). Run it with run_broadening_case as the superdroplet reference or as the bulk moment scheme; both start from the same droplets and see the same parcel dynamics, so they differ only in how the spectrum is represented.

Keyword fields (SI units):

  • T0 = 283.0, p0 = 9e4, S0 = 0.0 — initial temperature, pressure and supersaturation (the parcel starts at cloud base)
  • N = 1e8 — droplet number concentration (m⁻³; 100 cm⁻³)
  • r0 = 5e-6, ε0 = 0.1 — median radius and log-normal width of the initial activated spectrum (ε0 = 0 is monodisperse)
  • solute_mass = 1e-18 — NaCl per droplet (kg)
  • n_sd = 2000 — superdroplets (reference only)
  • w = 0.5 — updraught (m s⁻¹)
  • σ_S = 1e-3, τ = 5.0 — sub-grid supersaturation spread and its Lagrangian correlation time (s)
  • t_end = 300.0, dt = 1.0 — duration and output/coupling step (s)
  • seed = 1 — initial radii and fluctuation noise

Aeolus.TurbulentMicrophysics.DoubleGaussianSaturationPDF — Type
DoubleGaussianSaturationPDF(skewness; shape = 1.0)

Binormal (two-Gaussian mixture) distribution of s with prescribed skewness Sk, in the "analytic double Gaussian" form of Larson, Golaz & Cotton (2002). Shallow cumulus fields are strongly positively skewed in s — a few moist updrafts in a dry environment — which a single Gaussian cannot represent (Bechtold et al. 1995).

Both components share the standard deviation σ_c = σ √(1 - γ), where γ ∈ [0, 1) is the fraction of the variance carried by the separation of the component means. With S̃ = Sk / γ^{3/2},

\[w = \tfrac12\left[1 - \operatorname{sign}(\tilde S) \sqrt{\tilde S^2/(4 + \tilde S^2)}\right],\quad \mu_1 = \bar s + \sigma\sqrt{\gamma(1-w)/w},\quad \mu_2 = \bar s - \sigma\sqrt{\gamma w/(1-w)},\]

which reproduces the mean, the variance and the third moment exactly for any γ. The remaining freedom is closed here by

\[\gamma^{3/2} = \frac{|Sk|}{|Sk| + c},\qquad c = \texttt{shape} > 0,\]

a choice made in this package (not taken from Larson et al.) so that the component separation vanishes continuously as Sk → 0: the family then reduces exactly to GaussianSaturationPDF, and a very skewed field (|Sk| → ∞) approaches two delta-like components.

Aeolus.TurbulentMicrophysics.GaussianSaturationPDF — Type
GaussianSaturationPDF()

Gaussian distribution of s — the Sommeria & Deardorff (1977) / Mellor (1977) model. With Q = s̄/σ the normalised saturation excess,

\[C = \tfrac12\left[1 + \mathrm{erf}\!\left(Q/\sqrt2\right)\right],\qquad \bar q_c = \sigma\left[Q\,C + \frac{e^{-Q^2/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\right].\]

Aeolus.TurbulentMicrophysics.SpectralMoments — Type
SpectralMoments

Bulk (moment) description of a cloud-droplet population subject to sub-grid supersaturation fluctuations — the state a two-moment-plus-width bulk scheme carries in place of individual droplets.

  • N — number of real droplets in the host cell (conserved: no activation or collection here)
  • μ — mean squared radius ⟨r²⟩ (m²)
  • V — variance of the squared radius Var(r²) (m⁴)
  • C — covariance Cov(r², ξ) of the squared radius with the unit-variance sub-grid fluctuation (m²)

C is what makes the scheme non-Markovian in the right way: droplets that have spent time in moist sub-volumes are larger and still sit in moist sub-volumes for about a correlation time, so their growth advantage persists. For an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck ξ the three moment equations are closed and exact (see bulk_condensation_step!); only the Kelvin term and radius diagnostics need the shape closure (closure_radius_moment).

Aeolus.TurbulentMicrophysics.SubgridCloud — Type
SubgridCloud{T}

Partial-cloudiness diagnosis of one grid cell, returned by subgrid_cloud:

  • cloud_fraction — P(s > 0), in [0, 1]
  • condensate — cell-mean condensate q̄_c (kg kg⁻¹)
  • q — cell-mean PhasePartition (total water unchanged)
  • T — cell-mean temperature consistent with θ_li and q
  • s̄, σ_s — mean and standard deviation of the deficit
  • Q — normalised saturation excess s̄/σ_s (±Inf if σ_s = 0)
  • σ_S — standard deviation of the sub-grid relative supersaturation S = q_v/q_sat − 1 in the clear part, σ_s/(a q_sl) (dimensionless)
  • coefficients — the MellorCoefficients used
Aeolus.TurbulentMicrophysics.SupersaturationFluctuations — Type
SupersaturationFluctuations

Per-droplet sub-grid supersaturation state for stochastic condensational growth. Droplet i sees S̄ + σ_S ξ[i], where ξ[i] is a unit-variance Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process with correlation time τ (s; Inf freezes each droplet in its sub-volume).

  • ξ — unit-variance fluctuation per superdroplet (indexed like sd.radius)
  • σ_S — standard deviation of the supersaturation (fractional)
  • τ — Lagrangian correlation time of S' (s)
  • key — the 64-bit key of the per-droplet random streams
  • nadv — how many times each droplet's fluctuation has advanced

Droplet i's n-th draw comes from the counter-based stream (key, n, i) (Aeolus.Backends.counter_rng; KA-n, #305), so a droplet's noise does not depend on which other droplets advance, in what order, or on how many threads.

Build with supersaturation_fluctuations.

Aeolus.Superdroplet.relative_dispersion — Method
relative_dispersion(m::SpectralMoments) -> (σ_r, ε)

Radius standard deviation and relative dispersion ε = σ_r/r̄ of the bulk state — the same diagnostic Superdroplet.relative_dispersion measures on the ensemble. Uses ⟨r²⟩ = μ exactly, so σ_r² = μ (1 − R²) with R = Γ(k+½)/(Γ(k)√k), evaluated with expm1 to keep narrow spectra (k ≫ 1, 1 − R² ≈ 1/(4k)) accurate.

Aeolus.TurbulentMicrophysics._shape — Method

Gamma shape parameter k = μ²/V of the closure (Inf if monodisperse).

Aeolus.TurbulentMicrophysics.advance_fluctuations! — Method
advance_fluctuations!(fl, dt; indices = nothing, backend = default_backend()) -> fl

Exact Ornstein-Uhlenbeck update over dt: ξ ← ρ ξ + √(1−ρ²) N(0,1) with ρ = exp(−dt/τ). Stationary for any dt (the variance stays 1); τ = Inf leaves ξ unchanged. With indices, only those droplets advance — the ones whose time has actually moved on (e.g. the droplets of the one cell being stepped). Each droplet advances in its own work item of a launch, from its own stream.

Aeolus.TurbulentMicrophysics.broadening_initial_population — Method
broadening_initial_population(case) -> (sd, cell)

The initial superdroplets and parcel of case: n_sd equal-multiplicity superdroplets with radii r0 exp(ε0 z), z ~ N(0,1) (droplet i from the counter-based stream (seed, 0, i)), in a 1 m³ cell at (T0, p0) with vapour at 1 + S0 times saturation. The bulk run takes its initial moments from this very population.

Aeolus.TurbulentMicrophysics.bulk_condensation_step! — Method
bulk_condensation_step!(m, cell, dt; σ_S = 0, τ = Inf, nsub = 0,
                        mc, c) -> (Δm, Δq_vap, ΔT)

Bulk counterpart of sgs_condensation_step!: advance the moment state m (SpectralMoments) of the droplets in cell over dt under sub-grid supersaturation fluctuations of standard deviation σ_S and Lagrangian correlation time τ (the σ_S, τ of a SupersaturationFluctuations; σ_S is typically SubgridCloud.σ_S of issue #12), two-way coupled to the cell.

The moment equations for w = r² under the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck fluctuation (Paoli & Shariff 2009; Sardina et al. 2015),

\[\dot μ = 2G(\bar S - a⟨w^{-1/2}⟩),\quad \dot V = 4Gσ_S C - 4Ga\,\mathrm{Cov}(w, w^{-1/2}),\quad \dot C = 2Gσ_S - C/τ + Ga\,μ^{-3/2} C,\]

are integrated with C exact over each sub-step (it is linear) and S̄, T, p frozen — the same operator split as the superdroplet step. G is the Phase 41 growth factor 1/(F_k + F_d) and a the Phase 41 Kelvin coefficient, so the bulk and the superdroplet scheme differ only in representing the spectrum by moments. Without the Kelvin term the equations are exact and give supersaturation_integral_variance.

The condensed mass is (4π/3)ρ_w N Δ⟨r³⟩ with ⟨r³⟩ from the gamma closure, and goes to the cell through Phase 41's apply_condensate_tendency!, so the cell's total water is conserved to round-off. Sub-steps: at most 0.2 τ_phase (as condensation_step!) and, when σ_S > 0, τ/10.

Limits: activated droplets only (no Raoult term, no activation or complete evaporation — μ must stay positive); the fluctuation is prescribed, not depleted by the droplets it drives (see the in-cloud caveat on the theory page).

Aeolus.TurbulentMicrophysics.closure_radius_moment — Method
closure_radius_moment(m::SpectralMoments, p) -> ⟨rᵖ⟩

Radius moment implied by the bulk state under the gamma closure: w = r² is taken to be gamma-distributed with mean μ and variance V, so ⟨rᵖ⟩ = ⟨w^{p/2}⟩ = μ^{p/2} Γ(k+p/2)/(Γ(k) k^{p/2}) with k = μ²/V (gamma_moment_ratio). p = 2 returns μ exactly, whatever the shape, because growth is linear in w; every other power is an assumption. For a spectrum of relative dispersion ε the difference from a log-normal of the same μ, V is O(ε⁴) in ⟨r⟩.

Aeolus.TurbulentMicrophysics.equilibrium_variance_coefficient — Method
equilibrium_variance_coefficient(; C_k = 0.1, C_χ, Pr_t = 1/3) -> c_var

Coefficient of the local-equilibrium SGS scalar variance φ'² = c_var Δ² |∇φ̄|² implied by a Deardorff-type closure with eddy viscosity ν_t = C_k Δ √e, eddy diffusivity ν_t/Pr_t and scalar variance dissipation C_χ (√e/Δ) φ'²:

\[c_\mathrm{var} = \frac{2 C_k}{C_\chi\,\mathrm{Pr}_t}.\]

C_k = 0.1 is Deardorff's (1980) value; Pr_t = 1/3 is the default of the package's Simulations and Anelastic cores. C_χ has no default here: published values differ between closures, and the choice should be made — and stated — by the caller.

Aeolus.TurbulentMicrophysics.exceedance_fraction — Method
exceedance_fraction(pdf, x, s̄, σ) -> P(s > x)

Fraction of the cell in which the saturation deficit exceeds x. With x = 0 this is the cloud fraction; with x set to the deficit equivalent of an activation threshold it is the fraction of the cell in which that threshold is crossed. σ = 0 returns the all-or-nothing step s̄ > x.

Aeolus.TurbulentMicrophysics.extended_condensate — Function
extended_condensate(θ_li, p, q_t, c = DEFAULT_CONSTANTS) -> (s̄, T, q::PhasePartition)

Cell-mean extended condensate s̄ together with the all-or-nothing saturation_adjustment of the mean state it is anchored on. s̄ > 0 is exactly that adjustment's condensate; s̄ ≤ 0 is the Mellor-scaled deficit a (q_t − q_sat(T_l)).

Aeolus.TurbulentMicrophysics.filter_width — Method
filter_width(grid::RegularGrid) -> Δ

Smagorinsky filter width (dx dy dz)^{1/3}, the same Δ as compute_smagorinsky_νt! uses.

Aeolus.TurbulentMicrophysics.fit_power_law — Method
fit_power_law(t, y) -> (exponent, prefactor)

Least-squares fit of y = prefactor · t^exponent in log-log space (all t, y > 0). Used to measure the growth exponent of σ_{r²}(t) against the t^{1/2} law of turbulent condensational broadening (Sardina et al. 2015).

Aeolus.TurbulentMicrophysics.gamma_moment_ratio — Method
gamma_moment_ratio(k, p) -> Γ(k+p) / (Γ(k) kᵖ)

Moment factor of the gamma distribution: if w ~ Gamma(shape k, mean μ) then ⟨wᵖ⟩ = μᵖ · gamma_moment_ratio(k, p). Tends to 1 as k → ∞ (a monodisperse population) and requires k + p > 0.

Evaluated as a logarithm: the recurrence Γ(z+1) = zΓ(z) shifts k up to at least 16, where Stirling's series is summed as a difference of the two log-gammas, so neither large k (narrow spectra, k ~ 10⁶) nor small k loses precision. Relative accuracy ≈ 1e-14 (tested against Γ(3/2) = √π/2, Γ(1/2) = √π and the recurrence).

Aeolus.TurbulentMicrophysics.local_supersaturation — Method
local_supersaturation(fl, S̄) -> Vector

Supersaturation S̄ + σ_S ξ[i] seen by each droplet.

Aeolus.TurbulentMicrophysics.mellor_coefficients — Function
mellor_coefficients(θ_li, p, q_t, c = DEFAULT_CONSTANTS) -> MellorCoefficients

Coefficients of the linearised saturation deficit s' = a_q q_t' − b θ_li' at the state (θ_li, p, q_t).

∂q_s/∂T is the exact derivative of the contract's q_s = ε e_s/(p − (1−ε) e_s) with Clausius-Clapeyron de_s/dT = e_s L_v/(R_v T²), so it is consistent with saturation_specific_humidity by construction. The latent heat is the liquid/ice blend λ L_v + (1−λ) L_s with λ = liquid_fraction(T_l), matching the partition used by saturation_adjustment.

Reference: Mellor (1977); Sommeria & Deardorff (1977), eq. (7)-(10).

Aeolus.TurbulentMicrophysics.parcel_ascent! — Method
parcel_ascent!(cell, w, dt; q_liq = 0, c = DEFAULT_CONSTANTS) -> cell

Lift a fixed-mass Phase 41 EulerianCell adiabatically by w dt (m): dry-adiabatic cooling ΔT = −(g/c_pm) w dt (MoistThermodynamics.dry_adiabatic_lapse_rate), hydrostatic pressure fall Δp = −ρ g w dt, and the density that follows from the gas law at the new (T, p). The volume expands by the inverse density ratio, so the air mass ρ ΔV — the invariant denominator of the Phase 41 coupling — is unchanged, and so are the specific humidities.

Latent heating is not here: it is added by the condensation step that follows (apply_condensate_tendency!), and the pair together is a saturated adiabat. Without droplets the supersaturation rises at AerosolCloud.supersaturation_source(T, p) · w (tested).

Aeolus.TurbulentMicrophysics.pdf_cloud_fraction — Method
pdf_cloud_fraction(pdf, s̄, σ) -> C ∈ [0, 1]

Sub-grid cloud fraction P(s > 0). For the Gaussian family this is ½(1 + erf(Q/√2)) with Q = s̄/σ (Sommeria & Deardorff 1977).

Aeolus.TurbulentMicrophysics.pdf_condensate — Method
pdf_condensate(pdf, s̄, σ) -> q̄_c

Cell-mean condensate ∫₀^∞ s P(s) ds (kg kg⁻¹). For the Gaussian family this is σ [Q C + exp(-Q²/2)/√(2π)] (Mellor 1977). It is always ≥ max(s̄, 0) — by Jensen's inequality, since max(s, 0) is convex — so sub-grid variability can only add condensate relative to the mean-state saturation adjustment. σ = 0 returns max(s̄, 0).

Aeolus.TurbulentMicrophysics.pdf_expectation — Method

Aeolus.TurbulentMicrophysics.r2_standard_deviation — Method
r2_standard_deviation(m::SpectralMoments) -> σ_{r²}
r2_standard_deviation(sd::SuperdropletSet; indices = nothing) -> σ_{r²}

Standard deviation of the squared radius, σ_{r²} = √Var(r²) (m²) — the spectral-width measure DNS reports (Lanotte et al. 2009; Sardina et al. 2015), because it is the one that turbulent condensation drives directly: dr²/dt = 2G S makes it a random walk in r². Not a closure quantity: exact for both the bulk state and the ensemble.

Aeolus.TurbulentMicrophysics.saturation_deficit_variance — Function
saturation_deficit_variance(m::MellorCoefficients, var_qt, var_θ = 0, cov_qθ = 0) -> σ_s²

Variance of the saturation deficit from the sub-grid (co)variances of total water var_qt (kg² kg⁻²), liquid-ice potential temperature var_θ (K²) and their covariance cov_qθ (kg kg⁻¹ K):

\[\sigma_s^2 = a_q^2\,\overline{q_t'^2} - 2 a_q b\,\overline{q_t'\theta_{li}'} + b^2\,\overline{\theta_{li}'^2}.\]

Positively correlated q_t and θ_li fluctuations (moist and warm together, typical of surface-driven thermals) reduce σ_s, because the warm anomaly raises the saturation humidity the moist anomaly would otherwise exceed. Throws if the inputs violate Cauchy-Schwarz.

Aeolus.TurbulentMicrophysics.sgs_activation — Method
sgs_activation(modes, w, T_K, p, σ_S; pdf = GaussianSaturationPDF(), q, constants,
               kinetic_radius = Inf, method = :auto) -> SGSActivationResult
sgs_activation(modes, w, coeffs::ActivationCoefficients, σ_S; pdf, method)
sgs_activation(modes, w, T_K, p, cloud::SubgridCloud; kwargs...)

Phase 42 activation with sub-grid supersaturation fluctuations. modes are ARGModes or AerosolOptics modes, as for arg_activation. The cell-mean peak supersaturation is arg_activation's S_max for the mean updraught w; unresolved turbulence spreads it by σ_S (fractional), with the shape of pdf, and each mode's activated fraction is the PDF-mean of the CCN spectrum (sgs_ccn_fraction). Passing a SubgridCloud uses its σ_S — the clear-air relative-supersaturation spread implied by the sub-grid q_t, θ_li (co)variances of issue #12.

Physics and limits:

  • σ_S = 0 is arg_activation exactly (same fraction, N_act).
  • For small σ_S, N_act − N_act(S̄) ≈ ½ N_CCN''(S̄) σ_S²: sub-grid fluctuations increase activation where the CCN spectrum is convex in S (low activated fraction) and decrease it where it is concave.
  • The fluctuations are superimposed on S_max; they do not feed back on the competition for vapour that sets S_max (ARG's η term), and with w ≤ 0 nothing activates, as in Phase 42.

A related, widely used alternative integrates activation over a sub-grid updraught PDF instead (Ghan et al. 1997; Morrison & Gettelman 2008); this scheme fluctuates the supersaturation directly, as diagnosed from the scalar variances.

References: Abdul-Razzak & Ghan (2000); Ghan, S. J. et al. (1997), J. Geophys. Res., 102, 21777-21794; Morrison, H. & Gettelman, A. (2008), J. Climate, 21, 3642-3659.

Aeolus.TurbulentMicrophysics.sgs_ccn_fraction — Method
sgs_ccn_fraction(m::ARGMode, S̄, σ_S; pdf = GaussianSaturationPDF(),
                 T_K = 298.15, method = :auto, n = 2001) -> fraction

Activated fraction of aerosol mode m in a cell whose supersaturation is distributed as pdf with mean S̄ and standard deviation σ_S (both fractional):

\[\langle f\rangle = \int f_\mathrm{CCN}(S)\,P(S)\,dS = \int φ(u)\,P\!\left(S > s_c(u)\right)du ,\]

where f_CCN is AerosolCloud.ccn_fraction (the Phase 42 CCN spectrum), u = ln(r_d/r_g)/ln σ_g the standardised dry radius and s_c(u) = S_m e^{-3u lnσ_g/2} its critical supersaturation. The two forms are the same 2-D Gaussian probability integrated in opposite order; method = :supersaturation uses the first, :radius the second (with the closed-form exceedance_fraction of pdf), and :auto picks whichever has the smoother integrand — :radius when the width of the activation threshold in u, σ_c / (1.5 lnσ_g max(S̄, σ_c)), exceeds one, :supersaturation otherwise.

σ_S = 0 returns ccn_fraction(m, S̄; T_K) — the Phase 42 answer exactly.

Aeolus.TurbulentMicrophysics.sgs_condensation_growth! — Method
sgs_condensation_growth!(sd, fl, S̄, T, p, dt; indices = nothing,
                         substeps = 0, mc, c) -> Δm

Grow every superdroplet at the prescribed mean state (S̄, T, p) over dt, with droplet i in the local supersaturation S̄ + σ_S ξ[i] (local_supersaturation), then advance the fluctuations by dt. The growth is Phase 41's condensation_growth!; returns the real water mass condensed (kg). Does not touch a host cell — see sgs_condensation_step!. With indices, only those droplets grow and only their fluctuations advance.

Each droplet holds its S for the whole dt, so dt should be well below τ (sgs_condensation_step! sub-steps to τ/10 itself). fl.σ_S is a single value; when stepping several cells with different sub-grid spreads, set fl.σ_S to the cell's value before each call.

Aeolus.TurbulentMicrophysics.sgs_condensation_step! — Method
sgs_condensation_step!(sd, cell, dt, fl; index = nothing, nsub = 0,
                       substeps = 0, mc, c) -> (Δm, Δq_vap, ΔT)

Two-way coupled condensation with sub-grid supersaturation fluctuations: Phase 41's condensation_step! in which each superdroplet grows in S̄ + σ_S ξ[i], S̄ being the cell supersaturation. Vapour removal and latent heating go through apply_condensate_tendency! with the total condensed mass, so the cell's total water (cell_total_water) is conserved to round-off whatever σ_S; the fluctuations only redistribute the condensate among droplets, broadening the spectrum.

Sub-stepping is condensation_step!'s phase-relaxation count, raised when σ_S > 0 so that each sub-step is at most τ/10. With σ_S = 0 the result is bit for bit condensation_step!(sd, cell, dt).

Aeolus.TurbulentMicrophysics.subgrid_cloud! — Method
subgrid_cloud!(cloud_fraction, condensate, θ_li, q_t, p;
               νt = nothing, c_var = GEOMETRIC_VARIANCE_COEFF,
               pdf = GaussianSaturationPDF(), c = DEFAULT_CONSTANTS,
               T = nothing, liquid = nothing, ice = nothing)

Diagnose partial cloudiness on a grid. For every interior cell the sub-grid (co)variances of q_t and θ_li are formed from the resolved gradients by the Smagorinsky-consistent equilibrium closure of sgs_scalar_covariance!, mapped to σ_s by saturation_deficit_variance, and passed to subgrid_cloud. Halos of θ_li and q_t must be filled.

p is a number, a cell-centred Field, or a vector over the interior vertical levels (an anelastic reference profile). Optional T, liquid, ice fields receive the cell-mean temperature and phase split. θ_li and q_t are not modified: they are the conserved variables, and this is a diagnosis of how the water they describe is distributed, not a tendency.

With νt identically zero (SGS closure off) every cell has σ_s = 0 and the result is the all-or-nothing saturation_adjustment.

Aeolus.TurbulentMicrophysics.subgrid_cloud — Method
subgrid_cloud(θ_li, p, q_t; var_qt = 0, var_θ = 0, cov_qθ = 0,
              σ_s = nothing, pdf = GaussianSaturationPDF(),
              c = DEFAULT_CONSTANTS) -> SubgridCloud

Sub-grid cloud fraction and mean condensate of a cell with mean state (θ_li, p, q_t) and sub-grid (co)variances of q_t and θ_li. Pass σ_s directly to bypass the (co)variance mapping.

The deficit distribution has mean extended_condensate and standard deviation saturation_deficit_variance^{1/2}; the cloud fraction and condensate are pdf_cloud_fraction and pdf_condensate of pdf. The condensate is split between liquid and ice by liquid_fraction at the resulting mean temperature, and that temperature is recovered from θ_li by temperature_from_theta_li, so θ_li and q_t — the two conserved variables — are left exactly as they were.

With σ_s = 0 the result is the all-or-nothing saturation_adjustment (same temperature and partition, cloud fraction 0 or 1).

Mean condensate is capped at q_t: a Gaussian tail can otherwise ask for more water than the cell contains when σ_s ≳ q_t, which is outside the model's validity anyway.

References: Sommeria & Deardorff (1977); Mellor (1977); Bechtold et al. (1995); Chaboureau & Bechtold (2002).

Aeolus.TurbulentMicrophysics.supersaturation_fluctuations — Method
supersaturation_fluctuations(n; σ_S, τ = Inf, seed = nothing, rng = MersenneTwister(0))
supersaturation_fluctuations(sd::SuperdropletSet; kwargs...)
    -> SupersaturationFluctuations

Draw the initial fluctuations of n droplets (or one per superdroplet of sd) from the stationary distribution, ξ ~ N(0, 1). σ_S is typically SubgridCloud.σ_S from issue #12, and τ the sub-grid eddy turnover time (Δ^{2/3} ε^{-1/3} for filter width Δ and dissipation rate ε). Aeolus.TurbulentMicrophysics.supersaturation_integral_variance — Method
supersaturation_integral_variance(σ_S, τ, t) -> Var(∫₀ᵗ S' dt')

Variance of the time integral of an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck supersaturation with standard deviation σ_S and correlation time τ,

\[\mathrm{Var}\!\int_0^t S'\,dt' = 2σ_S^2 τ\left[t - τ\left(1 - e^{-t/τ}\right)\right],\]

which is σ_S² t² for τ = Inf. Multiplied by (2G)², with G the growth coefficient of r dr/dt = G S, it is the variance of r² acquired by an initially monodisperse population (Sardina et al. 2015, eq. 5; Paoli & Shariff 2009). For t ≫ τ it grows linearly — the spectral width σ_{r²} grows as √t, without bound, which is the mechanism by which turbulence broadens droplet spectra that pure condensation would narrow.
