Shared contract — MoistThermodynamics (Phase 39)

MoistThermodynamics is to clouds and microphysics what RadiationCore is to radiation: every microphysics scheme, activation parameterisation and cloud diagnostic in Phases 40–45 speaks it, so they agree on what temperature, saturation and buoyancy mean. It is exact in three respects that LES codes routinely approximate: the latent heats vary with temperature through Kirchhoff's relation, the saturation vapour pressure is the analytic integral of Clausius–Clapeyron with that same $L(T)$ rather than a Tetens/Magnus fit, and the moist heat capacity $c_{pm}$ carries the vapour and condensate loading. Shared quantities that several schemes compute — for instance effective_radius, which TurbulentMicrophysics extends — are declared here as generics that the implementing modules extend.

Aeolus.MoistThermodynamics — Module
MoistThermodynamics

Exact moist thermodynamics — Phase 39.

This module is the shared core for moist thermodynamics the way RadiationCore is for radiation: every microphysics scheme, activation parameterisation and cloud diagnostic in Phases 40-45 speaks it, so they agree on what temperature, saturation and buoyancy mean.

WHAT "EXACT" MEANS HERE, AND WHY IT MATTERS

Three approximations are routine in LES codes and all three are dropped:

  1. Constant latent heat. L_v is temperature dependent through Kirchhoff's relation, dL_v/dT = c_pv - c_l. Over the 220-320 K range an atmosphere actually spans, L_v varies by about 10 %. Holding it at its 273 K value biases condensational heating in deep convection by a comparable amount.

  2. Fitted saturation vapour pressure. Tetens/Bolton/Magnus fits are accurate near 273 K and degrade in the cold upper troposphere, which is exactly where ice microphysics needs them. Here e_s is the analytic integral of Clausius-Clapeyron with the Kirchhoff L(T), so it is thermodynamically consistent with the latent heat used everywhere else, by construction rather than by coincidence.

  3. Constant heat capacity. The moist heat capacity c_pm depends on the vapour and condensate loading and is carried explicitly.

CONSISTENCY IS THE POINT. A scheme that uses one L_v in its saturation adjustment and a different effective L_v implicit in its e_s fit does not conserve moist static energy, and the error accumulates over a long integration rather than cancelling.

TWO SATURATION ADJUSTMENTS (issue #82)

  • saturation_adjustment(θ_li, p, q_tot) is a diagnosis: it recovers T and the partition that a prognostic θ_li denotes, and reproduces θ_li exactly.
  • isobaric_saturation_adjustment(T, p, q_v, q_c; q_r, q_i) is a process: condensation or evaporation during an isobaric step, conserving moist_enthalpy exactly, with rain and ice in c_pm.

They differ because this module's θli is only approximately conserved through condensation: its Exner exponent Rm/cpm is taken at the current partition (Bryan & Fritsch's exact form uses total water only), so holding it fixed misstates latent heating by -0.2 % at 1000 hPa, +1.1 % at 900 hPa and +6.3 % at 600 hPa (285 K, S = 1.1). Microphysics (P3, fog) uses the process form; the sub-grid cloud scheme and parcel CAPE use the diagnosis, which must invert whatever θli the model carries.

Aeolus.MoistThermodynamics.PhasePartition — Type
PhasePartition{T}

Specific humidities of the three water phases: tot (total water), liq (cloud liquid) and ice (cloud ice), all in kg water per kg of moist air. Vapour is the remainder, tot - liq - ice.

Total water rather than vapour is the prognostic because it is conserved under phase change; vapour is not.

Aeolus.MoistThermodynamics.ThermodynamicConstants — Type
ThermodynamicConstants{T}

Thermodynamic constants, bundled so a caller can run in Float32 or substitute a different reference state without touching call sites.

Reference point: the triple point of water, T_triple = 273.16 K, p_triple = 611.657 Pa. Latent heats are given at the triple point and extrapolated by Kirchhoff's relation, which is what makes them consistent with the saturation vapour pressure below.

Aeolus.MoistThermodynamics.air_density — Function
air_density(T, p, q = PhasePartition(0.0), c = DEFAULT_CONSTANTS) -> kg m⁻³

Density of moist air (dry air + vapour + condensate) from the ideal-gas law with the moist gas constant of gas_constant_moist:

ρ = p / (R_m T)

Condensate adds mass but negligible volume, so at fixed T, p and vapour it raises ρ through the (1 - q_tot) factor in R_m. specific_volume is 1/ρ.

Convective available potential energy and convective inhibition for a parcel lifted through sounding (a vector of (p, T, q_v) from the surface up). The parcel is lifted reversibly — condensate is retained and loads the parcel — which gives a lower, more realistic CAPE than the pseudo-adiabatic assumption that precipitates everything out instantly.

density_potential_temperature(T, p, q, c) -> θ_ρ

Aeolus.MoistThermodynamics.density_temperature — Function
density_temperature(T, q, c) -> K

Density temperature — virtual temperature including condensate loading:

T_ρ = T (1 + q_vap/ε - q_tot)

Condensate adds mass without adding volume, so it is a negative buoyancy term. This is the quantity that belongs in a buoyancy calculation; using plain virtual temperature omits the weight of the cloud water and systematically over-predicts updraught strength. At 3 g/kg of liquid the loading is worth about 0.9 K of density temperature, comparable to the excess that drives shallow convection in the first place.

Aeolus.MoistThermodynamics.dewpoint_temperature — Function
dewpoint_temperature(p, q_v, c) -> K

Temperature at which the given vapour would saturate over liquid at constant pressure. Solved by bisection on e_s(T_d) = e(q_v, p), which is monotone, rather than by an inverted fit.

Aeolus.MoistThermodynamics.dry_adiabatic_lapse_rate — Function

Dry adiabatic lapse rate g/c_pm (K m⁻¹); ≈ 9.76e-3 for dry air.

Aeolus.MoistThermodynamics.effective_radius — Function
effective_radius(population, args...)

Effective radius of a size distribution: the ratio of its third to its second moment, ⟨r³⟩/⟨r²⟩.

Declared here with no method, so the modules that own a size distribution can each add one without importing each other — the same pattern RadiationCore uses for surface_albedo and material_albedo.

It is the same quantity in every case, which is why it is one name: the 3/2 moment ratio is what controls how much light a population of spheres scatters per unit mass, so it is the radius radiation actually sees.

Implemented by:

  • AerosolOptics.effective_radius for an aerosol size distribution.
  • Superdroplet.effective_radius for a superdroplet ensemble.
Aeolus.MoistThermodynamics.equivalent_potential_temperature — Function
equivalent_potential_temperature(T, p, q, c) -> θ_e

Equivalent potential temperature — the temperature a parcel would reach if all its vapour condensed and it were brought to p_ref. Conserved under both dry and moist adiabatic processes, which makes it the standard tracer for air-mass identity.

Uses the Bolton (1980) formulation, accurate to ~0.1 K, with the saturation temperature of the LCL computed from his eq. 21.

Aeolus.MoistThermodynamics.exner_function — Function
exner_function(p, q, c) -> Π

Exner function Π = (p/p_ref)^(R_m/c_pm). With a PhasePartition the moist exponent is used; the dry exponent R_d/c_pd ≈ 0.2857 is the q = 0 limit.

Aeolus.MoistThermodynamics.frost_point_temperature — Function

Frost point: as dewpoint_temperature but saturating over ice.

Aeolus.MoistThermodynamics.gas_constant_moist — Function
gas_constant_moist(q, c) -> J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹

Gas constant of moist air per unit mass of moist air:

R_m = R_d (1 - q_tot) + R_v q_vap

Note it is q_tot that is subtracted, not q_vap: condensate occupies negligible volume but carries mass, so it dilutes the gas constant.

Aeolus.MoistThermodynamics.heat_capacity_moist — Function
heat_capacity_moist(q, c) -> J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹

Isobaric heat capacity of moist air including condensate loading:

c_pm = c_pd (1 - q_tot) + c_pv q_vap + c_l q_liq + c_i q_ice

Liquid water has four times the heat capacity of dry air, so even a modest cloud loading is not negligible at the accuracy this module aims for.

Aeolus.MoistThermodynamics.isobaric_saturation_adjustment — Function
isobaric_saturation_adjustment(T, p, q_v, q_c, c = DEFAULT_CONSTANTS;
                               q_r = 0, q_i = 0, tol = 1e-14, maxiter = 100)
    -> (Δq, T)

Process saturation adjustment: condensation of vapour to cloud liquid, or evaporation of cloud liquid, at constant pressure p and constant moist enthalpy (moist_enthalpy) — the first-law invariant of an isobaric phase change. Returns the amount condensed Δq (kg kg⁻¹; negative when cloud evaporates) and the adjusted temperature T.

Only vapour q_v and cloud liquid q_c exchange mass. Rain q_r and ice q_i are non-adjusting: they do not change, but they are part of the parcel, so they enter its heat capacity c_pm (and q_i its enthalpy through L_f). Leaving them out overstates the condensational ΔT by about 1.5 % per 5 g kg⁻¹ of rain and 0.5 % per 5 g kg⁻¹ of ice.

The target is saturation over liquid (supercooled below 0 °C — cloud liquid becomes ice only by explicit microphysics); if even evaporating all of q_c leaves the air subsaturated, all of it evaporates. Subsaturated air without cloud is returned unchanged. The adjusted state satisfies, exactly up to round-off,

h(T, q_v − Δq, q_c + Δq, q_r, q_i) = h(T₀, q_v, q_c, q_r, q_i),

equivalently c_pm(q_final) ΔT = L_v(T₀) Δq, and q_v + q_c is conserved. Saturation is reached to tol in absolute specific humidity (|q_v − q_s| < 1e-14 kg kg⁻¹ by default), so relative to q_s it is ~1e-12 in warm air but up to ~2e-8 near 190 K where q_s ~ 1e-6 (measured by random sampling over 190-335 K, 200-1050 hPa).

Algorithm

h is linear in T at a fixed partition (temperature_from_moist_enthalpy), so T(Δ) is explicit and the problem is the scalar root of g(Δ) = (q_v − Δ) − q_s(T(Δ), p), which is strictly decreasing (condensing removes vapour and warms, which raises q_s) on the bracket Δ ∈ [−q_c, q_v]. A safeguarded Newton iteration (Clausius–Clapeyron slope) inside the bracket cannot fail; the convergence test is made on the current iterate before the update, so a root hit exactly is kept rather than replaced by the bracket midpoint (a bug found in review of #85, which had returned half the condensate for some cold inputs).

Compare saturation_adjustment, which diagnoses (T, q_c) from a prognostic θ_li and conserves θ_li instead (see the section comment).

References: Emanuel (1994) §4.5; issue #82.

Aeolus.MoistThermodynamics.kelvin_coefficient — Function
kelvin_coefficient(T, c = DEFAULT_CONSTANTS) -> m

The Kelvin (curvature) coefficient A = 2σ M_w / (R T ρ_w), in metres, such that the curvature term in the Köhler equation is exp(A/r).

About 1.1 nm at 293 K. Small — but divided by a 10 nm dry radius it is a supersaturation of order 10 %, which is why the smallest particles never activate.

Aeolus.MoistThermodynamics.kohler_critical_point — Function
kohler_critical_point(r_dry, κ, T, c) -> (r_crit, s_crit)

Critical radius and critical supersaturation — the maximum of the Köhler curve. A droplet held above s_crit grows without bound (it activates); below it, it sits at a stable haze equilibrium.

For κ not too small the classical closed form r_c = sqrt(3B/A), s_c = sqrt(4A³/27B) with B = κ r_d³ is exact enough to seed a refinement; the maximum is then located by golden-section search on the full κ-Köhler expression, so the returned point is the maximum of the function actually used elsewhere rather than of its approximation. For κ → 0 there is no maximum (the curve is monotone decreasing) and the particle never activates; s_crit = A/r_dry is returned as the effective barrier.

Aeolus.MoistThermodynamics.kohler_equilibrium_radius — Function
kohler_equilibrium_radius(s, r_dry, κ, T, c) -> r

Stable equilibrium wet radius of a haze particle held at subcritical supersaturation s. Bisection on the rising branch of the Köhler curve (below the critical radius), which is the branch that is stable: a perturbation there is restored, whereas beyond r_crit it runs away.

Returns NaN if s exceeds s_crit, since no equilibrium exists — the particle activates.

Aeolus.MoistThermodynamics.kohler_supersaturation — Function
kohler_supersaturation(r, r_dry, κ, T, c = DEFAULT_CONSTANTS) -> s

Equilibrium supersaturation s = S − 1 over a solution droplet of wet radius r grown on a dry particle of radius r_dry (both metres) with hygroscopicity κ.

Uses the Petters & Kreidenweis (2007) form,

\[S = \frac{r^3 - r_d^3}{r^3 - r_d^3 (1 - \kappa)} \exp\left(\frac{A}{r}\right)\]

which remains correct as r → r_d where the classical A/r − B/r³ expansion diverges from the truth.

Aeolus.MoistThermodynamics.latent_heat_fusion — Function

Latent heat of fusion, L_f = L_s - L_v. Rises with temperature.

Aeolus.MoistThermodynamics.latent_heat_sublim — Function

Latent heat of sublimation, L_s(T) = L_s0 + (c_pv - c_i)(T - T_triple).

Aeolus.MoistThermodynamics.latent_heat_vapor — Function
latent_heat_vapor(T, c = DEFAULT_CONSTANTS) -> J kg⁻¹

Latent heat of vaporisation, L_v(T) = L_v0 + (c_pv - c_l)(T - T_triple).

This is Kirchhoff's relation, and it is exact given temperature-independent heat capacities. Since c_pv - c_l ≈ -2322 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹, L_v falls with temperature: 2.50e6 at 273 K, 2.26e6 at 373 K, 2.62e6 at 223 K. Holding it constant at the triple-point value understates condensational heating in a cold cloud by ~5 % and overstates it in a warm one.

Aeolus.MoistThermodynamics.level_of_free_convection — Function
level_of_free_convection(T_p, p_p, q_p, sounding, c) -> p_lfc or NaN

Pressure of the level of free convection for a parcel lifted from (T_p, p_p, q_p) through sounding, a vector of (p, T, q_v) ordered from the surface upward. Returns NaN if the parcel never becomes positively buoyant.

Aeolus.MoistThermodynamics.lifting_condensation_level — Function
lifting_condensation_level(T, p, q_v, c) -> (T_lcl, p_lcl)

Temperature and pressure at which a parcel lifted dry-adiabatically first saturates. Solved by bisection on the residual q_v - q_sat along the dry adiabat, which is monotone in pressure, rather than by an empirical fit — the fits are good near the surface and drift aloft.

Aeolus.MoistThermodynamics.liquid_fraction — Function
liquid_fraction(T, c = DEFAULT_CONSTANTS) -> λ ∈ [0, 1]

Fraction of condensate that is liquid at temperature T.

Liquid above freezing, ice below the homogeneous-nucleation threshold (233.15 K), and a smooth ramp between — the mixed-phase regime, where supercooled liquid and ice genuinely coexist. The ramp is quadratic rather than linear so that both endpoints join with zero slope; a kink in λ(T) becomes a kink in the latent heating and shows up as grid-scale noise at the 0 °C level.

Aeolus.MoistThermodynamics.liquid_ice_potential_temperature — Function
liquid_ice_potential_temperature(T, p, q, c) -> θ_li

Liquid-ice potential temperature, in an exponential approximation of the kind reviewed by Bryan & Fritsch (2004) (cf. Tripoli & Cotton 1981):

θ_li = θ · exp( -(L_v q_liq + L_s q_ice) / (c_pm T) )

with θ = T/Π taken at the moist Exner exponent R_m/c_pm of the current partition q.

This is the quantity an LES carries as its thermodynamic prognostic, because it is approximately conserved under reversible phase change — condensation raises T and lowers q_vap in nearly the combination that leaves θ_li unchanged. Advecting θ_li and q_tot therefore means phase change never has to be advected, which is what keeps a cloud edge sharp.

The conservation is not exact, and the dominant error is the exponent. Bryan & Fritsch's exact form evaluates it with total water only, so it is fixed during phase change; here R_m/c_pm moves as vapour becomes condensate. Measured against isobaric moist enthalpy (issue #82: 285 K, 10 % supersaturation), holding this θ_li fixed gives c_pm ΔT/(L_v(T₀) Δq) = 0.998, 1.011 and 1.063 at 1000, 900 and 600 hPa. With the exponent frozen at the all-vapour partition the same measure is 0.998 at every pressure (tests), the residual being the exponential approximation, first order in the supersaturation. That is why condensation as a process uses isobaric_saturation_adjustment and not an inversion of this function.

The exponential form is used rather than the linearised θ - (L/c_p)(p₀/p)^κ q_l. The linearisation errs by ~0.3 K at 3 g/kg of liquid — small, but it is a systematic bias, so it accumulates across a deep convective column instead of averaging out.

Aeolus.MoistThermodynamics.liquid_water_static_energy — Function
liquid_water_static_energy(T, z, q, c) -> J kg⁻¹

Liquid-water static energy s_l = c_pm T + g z - L_v q_liq - L_s q_ice, the condensate-referenced counterpart of moist static energy.

Aeolus.MoistThermodynamics.moist_adiabatic_lapse_rate — Function
moist_adiabatic_lapse_rate(T, p, c) -> K m⁻¹

Saturated (pseudo)adiabatic lapse rate,

Γ_m = g (1 + L_v r_s /(R_d T)) / (c_pd + L_v² r_s ε /(R_d T²))

About 4e-3 K/m in the warm tropics (latent heat release offsets roughly half the adiabatic cooling) rising toward the dry value 9.8e-3 K/m in cold air, where there is little vapour left to condense. That variation is why the tropical troposphere is so much closer to neutral than the polar one.

Aeolus.MoistThermodynamics.moist_enthalpy — Function
moist_enthalpy(T, q, c = DEFAULT_CONSTANTS) -> J kg⁻¹

Specific enthalpy of moist air including its condensate, referenced to liquid water, and exactly consistent with the Kirchhoff latent heats of this module:

h = (c_pd q_d + c_l q_tot) T + L_v(T) q_v - L_f(T) q_ice

It is the quantity conserved by a reversible isobaric phase change, and differs from moist_static_energy (at z = 0) by (c_pv - c_l) T q_v, which is not constant under condensation — about a quarter of L_v Δq near 285 K. Use moist_static_energy as the familiar diagnostic, and this function wherever a scheme must conserve energy exactly through condensation.

A useful identity follows directly: condensing Δq of vapour to liquid at fixed h, q_tot and pressure warms the parcel by exactly

c_pm(q_final) · ΔT = L_v(T_initial) · Δq ,

with c_pm = heat_capacity_moist. It is what isobaric_saturation_adjustment conserves, and temperature_from_moist_enthalpy inverts it.

Aeolus.MoistThermodynamics.moist_static_energy — Function
moist_static_energy(T, z, q, c) -> J kg⁻¹

Moist static energy h = c_pm T + g z + L_v q_vap.

Approximately conserved under adiabatic vertical motion including condensation, which makes its drift a sharp diagnostic of whether a scheme's thermodynamics are self-consistent. A scheme using a fitted e_s with a different implicit L_v than it uses in its adjustment will show a slow systematic drift here. It is still only approximately conserved even with exact thermodynamics: it treats c_pm as fixed while vapour becomes liquid. For the exact isobaric invariant see moist_enthalpy.

Ratio of molar masses, ε ≈ 0.622.

Aeolus.MoistThermodynamics.saturated_buoyancy_frequency_squared — Function
saturated_buoyancy_frequency_squared(θ, p, q_v, q_c, dθ_dz, dq_v_dz, dq_w_dz,
                                     c = DEFAULT_CONSTANTS; θ_ref = θ, g = c.grav) -> N² in s⁻²

Squared buoyancy frequency of a saturated cell: the restoring force on a saturated parcel displaced along the moist adiabat (reversible, total water $q_w = q_v + q_c$ conserved, condensate carried along), measured against the environment's gradients. With the buoyancy variable of the dynamics, the density potential temperature $θ_ρ = θ(1 + 0.61 q_v - q_c)$, linearised about the cell,

\[N^2 = \frac{g}{θ_{\rm ref}}\Bigl[(1 + 0.61 q_v - q_c) \Bigl(\frac{∂θ}{∂z} - \frac{Γ_d - Γ_m}{Π}\Bigr) + θ\Bigl(1.61\Bigl(\frac{∂q_v}{∂z} - \frac{dq_s}{dz}\Big|_{\rm ad}\Bigr) - \frac{∂q_w}{∂z}\Bigr)\Bigr],\]

where $(Γ_d - Γ_m)/Π$ is the parcel's θ gradient on the moist adiabat ($T = θΠ$, $θ ∂Π/∂z = -Γ_d$ in a hydrostatic column) and $dq_s/dz|_{\rm ad}$ the change of its saturation humidity along it. In a saturated environment $∂q_v/∂z - dq_s/dz|_{\rm ad} = (∂q_s/∂T)(∂T/∂z + Γ_m)$, and the expression is the saturated Brunt–Väisälä frequency of Durran & Klemp (1982, eq. 36),

\[N_m^2 = \frac{g}{T}\Bigl(\frac{∂T}{∂z} + Γ_m\Bigr) \Bigl(1 + \frac{L_v r_s}{R_d T}\Bigr) - \frac{g}{1 + q_w}\frac{∂q_w}{∂z},\]

to $O(q)$ (with $g/T → g/θ_{\rm ref}$ per unit θ, the Boussinesq form): the factor $1 + L_v r_s/(R_d T)$ is the virtual effect of the vapour that the displaced parcel and its saturated environment exchange. At a cloud edge, where the environment above or below is unsaturated, Durran & Klemp's form would still assume a saturated environment and drop the virtual effect of the humidity jump; there this form keeps it ($∂q_v/∂z$ is the environment's), so a saturated parcel lifted into drier air of the same θ is buoyant, as a parcel computation says.

θ (K), p (Pa), q_v, q_c (kg kg⁻¹) are the cell's; $T = θΠ(p)$ with the dry Exner function. $Γ_m$ is moist_adiabatic_lapse_rate, $Γ_d$ dry_adiabatic_lapse_rate, $q_s$ saturation_specific_humidity ($dq_s/dz|_{\rm ad}$ from its derivatives in T and p, hydrostatic with $R_d T$): one source of truth with the rest of the contract. θ_ref is the reference temperature of a Boussinesq core ($θ_0$; default θ). The factor 1.61 is $1 + 0.61$, the 0.61 of the cores' θv. The result vanishes on a moist adiabat of constant total water, is ``(g/θ{\rm ref})∂θ/∂z$for$q → 0$, and is below the dry$N^2`` of the same θ gradient in a stable saturated layer, because condensation in a rising parcel offsets part of its adiabatic cooling.

Reference: Durran, D. R. & Klemp, J. B. (1982). On the effects of moisture on the Brunt–Väisälä frequency. J. Atmos. Sci., 39, 2152-2158.

Aeolus.MoistThermodynamics.saturation_adjustment — Function
saturation_adjustment(θ_li, p, q_tot, c; ...) -> (T, PhasePartition)

Diagnosis of the thermodynamic state from the conserved variables: given the prognostic θ_li and q_tot, find the temperature and the liquid/ice split that put the parcel exactly at saturation (or leave it subsaturated with no condensate) and reproduce θ_li exactly through liquid_ice_potential_temperature.

This is an inversion of liquid_ice_potential_temperature, so θli-exactness is the correct invariant here. It is not the right operation for condensation as a process during an isobaric step: this θli is only approximately conserved by phase change (its Exner exponent follows the partition), and holding it fixed misstates latent heating by +1.1 % at 900 hPa and +6.3 % at 600 hPa (issue #82). For that, use isobaric_saturation_adjustment, which conserves moist_enthalpy.

Why this is bisection and not a fixed-point iteration

The obvious scheme — guess T, compute q_liq = q_tot - q_sat(T), recompute T from the latent heating, repeat — does not converge. It oscillates, because condensing enough water to raise T also raises q_sat past q_tot, which un-condenses all of it, which drops T back. Measured on a marine stratocumulus sounding it cycles 281.9 → 285.2 → 281.9 → … indefinitely, and a naive implementation that caps the iteration count silently returns zero liquid water — i.e. the cloud never forms and nothing reports an error.

The residual solved here,

f(T) = θ_li(T, p, partition(T)) - θ_li_target

is continuous and monotonically increasing in T, and the bracket [T_dry_at_saturation, T_all_condensed] is guaranteed to contain the root because condensate is bounded by 0 ≤ q_cond ≤ q_tot. Bisection on it cannot fail.

Returns (T, PhasePartition).

Aeolus.MoistThermodynamics.saturation_mixing_ratio — Function

Saturation mixing ratio, r_sat = ε e_s / (p - e_s) — mass of vapour per mass of dry air, as distinct from specific humidity which is per mass of moist air. The two differ by ~2 % in the warm tropics; mixing up which one a scheme wants is a classic source of small persistent biases.

Aeolus.MoistThermodynamics.saturation_vapor_pressure — Function
saturation_vapor_pressure(T, c = DEFAULT_CONSTANTS) -> Pa

Saturation vapour pressure over a plane surface of liquid water, valid (as supercooled liquid) well below freezing.

Analytic integral of Clausius-Clapeyron with the Kirchhoff L_v(T), so it is consistent with latent_heat_vapor by construction. At 273.15 K it gives 611.2 Pa; at 373.15 K, 101.3 kPa — i.e. it reproduces the normal boiling point without being fitted to it.

Aeolus.MoistThermodynamics.saturation_vapor_pressure_ice — Function
saturation_vapor_pressure_ice(T, c = DEFAULT_CONSTANTS) -> Pa

Saturation vapour pressure over ice.

Below freezing this is lower than the value over liquid, and that gap is the engine of the Wegener-Bergeron-Findeisen process: in a mixed-phase cloud the air is supersaturated with respect to ice while subsaturated with respect to liquid, so ice grows at the expense of evaporating droplets. The gap peaks near 261 K at about 1.5 % of e_s.

Aeolus.MoistThermodynamics.temperature_from_moist_enthalpy — Function
temperature_from_moist_enthalpy(h, q, c = DEFAULT_CONSTANTS; T_ref = c.T_triple) -> T

Temperature at which the parcel with phase partition q has moist enthalpy h (moist_enthalpy), i.e. the exact inverse of moist_enthalpy at a known partition.

With Kirchhoff latent heats (constant specific heats) moist_enthalpy is exactly linear in T at fixed partition, and its slope is heat_capacity_moist(q):

∂h/∂T = c_pd q_d + c_l q_tot + (c_pv − c_l) q_v − (c_l − c_i) q_ice = c_pm(q),

so one linear step from any reference temperature is exact:

T = T_ref + (h − h(T_ref, q)) / c_pm(q).

The result does not depend on T_ref except at round-off; passing the pre-step temperature as T_ref keeps the answer closest to it in floating point.

Aeolus.MoistThermodynamics.wet_bulb_temperature — Function
wet_bulb_temperature(T, p, q_v, c = DEFAULT_CONSTANTS) -> K

Thermodynamic (adiabatic-saturation) wet-bulb temperature $T_w$ of air at T (K), pressure p (Pa) and specific humidity q_v: the temperature reached when liquid water at $T_w$ evaporates into the air at constant pressure until it saturates. With mixing ratio $r = q_v/(1-q_v)$, the isobaric enthalpy balance of this module's thermodynamics (constant $c_{pd}$, $c_{pv}$, $c_l$ and the Kirchhoff $L_v(T)$) is exactly

\[(c_{pd} + r c_{pv}) (T - T_w) = \bigl(r_s(T_w, p) - r\bigr) L_v(T_w),\]

solved by bisection on $[150 K, T]$ (the residual is monotone in $T_w$). This is the psychrometric wet bulb of ASHRAE Fundamentals (2017) ch. 1, eq. 33-35, with the same saturation curve as the rest of the model. Saturated or supersaturated air returns T.

Used to drive equipment curves rated on wet-bulb temperature (cooling coils, evaporative condensers and cooling towers).

Aeolus.MoistThermodynamics.θ_per_heat — Method
θ_per_heat(ρ, cp, Π) -> K m³ J⁻¹

The potential-temperature increment per J m⁻³ of heat added to air of density ρ, heat capacity cp and Exner function Π, $1 / (ρ\, c_p\, Π)$: heat raises the temperature by $q / (ρ c_p)$, and θ by that over $Π$, since $T = θ Π$. A flux F (W m⁻²) into a cell of thickness Δz for τ s raises θ by F τ / Δz · θ_per_heat(ρ, c_p, Π). (Moved from the urban case's reference state, ONE-e #454; there, before #253, the couplings divided by $ρ c_p$ only, and the air received $Π F$.)
