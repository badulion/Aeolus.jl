Fog and dew condensation (Phase 44)

RadiationFog couples fog (condensation in the air) and dew (deposition onto a cold surface) to the vapour field with the exact moist enthalpy of the MoistThermodynamics contract. Both steps are saturation-limited and unconditionally stable in the time step. Theory: Fog and dew.

The same module carries the radiation-fog feedback (#9): the droplet field (LWC, N_d, r_eff) is passed to the radiation stack — GasOptics, TwoStream.MieCloudOptics, RadiationCore.combine_optics! and the two-stream solver — through fog_atmospheric_column and fog_radiative_transfer!, and a 1-D column (FogColumn, parisfog_2011_column) runs fog through formation, fog-top cooling and dissipation. See Radiation fog.

Aeolus.RadiationFog — Module
RadiationFog

Fog and dew condensation coupled consistently to the atmospheric vapour field — Phase 44.

This module owns radiation fog. Its thermodynamic half (#11): how much vapour condenses in the air (fog) or onto a cold surface (dew), where the latent heat goes, and why the step cannot overshoot saturation for any time step. Its radiative half (#9, radiation_fog/coupling.jl): the droplet field handed to the radiation, so that fog cools itself by emitting longwave from its top — the feedback that thickens it. And a 1-D radiation-fog column (radiation_fog/column.jl) that couples the two with turbulence, a surface energy balance and droplet settling, validated on the ParisFog case of 14–15 Nov 2011.

WHY THIS EXISTS — THE BUG IT REPLACES

The urban example shipped apply_fog_dew! disabled because "its warming term amplifies". The diagnosis, measured in a one-cell box model with the example's own settings (rate = 1e-4, dt = 2 s, surface at 280 K, air at 288 K and qv = 0.010, i.e. RH = 0.95 and vapour resupplied by evaporation/advection as in the full run):

  1. Condensation in subsaturated air. Whenever the surface was below the dew point the old scheme removed vapour from the air and released its latent heat into the air — even though the air was subsaturated. Nothing tied the heating to air-side saturation, so there was no thermodynamic brake: the air warmed from 288 K to 363 K in 24 h while its RH fell to 0.016 and it kept "condensing". Physically, dew forms at the surface and its latent heat goes into the surface energy budget; the air loses vapour but not enthalpy per unit of dry air beyond what that vapour carried.
  2. Explicit-rate overshoot. In supersaturated air the step removed rate·dt·(q_v − q_s) without the 1 + (L/c_p) dq_s/dT latent-heat feedback, so for rate·dt ≳ 1/(1+γ) it overshot to below saturation (q_v − q_s = −6.3e-4 kg/kg at rate·dt = 1, −2.9e-3 at rate·dt = 3).

THE FIX

  • Fog (fog_condensation) conserves the isobaric moist enthalpy of the contract (moist_enthalpy) exactly, with L_v(T) and q_s(T, p) from MoistThermodynamics (one source of truth). The target is the saturation-adjusted state, found by a bracketed solve of q_v − Δ = q_s(T(Δ), p); a finite timescale τ relaxes toward it by the exact exponential factor 1 − exp(−dt/τ) ∈ [0, 1). Because the residual is monotone in the condensed amount, a partial step can never cross saturation — for any dt.
  • Dew (dew_deposition) draws vapour from the lowest air layer only while q_v > q_s(T_s), with an implicit (exponential) bulk-exchange law, so the air can at most be dried to the surface's saturation humidity. Its latent heat L_v(T_s)·m is returned for the surface energy budget and never added to the air.

References

  • Monteith, J. L. (1957). Dew. Q. J. R. Meteorol. Soc., 83, 322-341.
  • Garratt, J. R. & Segal, M. (1988). On the contribution of atmospheric moisture to dew formation. Bound.-Layer Meteorol., 45, 209-236.
  • Bott, A., Sievers, U. & Zdunkowski, W. (1990). A radiation fog model with a detailed treatment of the interaction between radiative transfer and fog microphysics. J. Atmos. Sci., 47, 2153-2166.
  • Emanuel, K. A. (1994). Atmospheric Convection, Ch. 4 (moist enthalpy).

Aeolus.RadiationFog.FogColumn — Type
FogColumn

State of the 1-D radiation-fog model. Layer arrays are bottom-up: heights z (centres) and zf (interfaces), thickness dz, fixed hydrostatic pressure p, pf and density ρ; prognostic temperature T (K), vapour qv and fog water ql (kg/kg), wind u, v (m s⁻¹); surface temperature Ts, surface water w_sfc (dew + settled droplets, kg m⁻²), elapsed time t (s). Q holds the latest radiative energy input per layer (W m⁻³), rad the FogRadiation and budget the boundary integrals. Diagnostics are appended to history every dt_out (see fog_diagnostics).

Aeolus.RadiationFog.FogColumn — Method
FogColumn(zf, T, qv; u = ug, v = vg, Ts = T[1], p_sfc = 1.0e5,
          settings = FogColumnSettings()) -> FogColumn

Column on interfaces zf (from 0, bottom-up) with initial layer temperature T, vapour qv (and optionally wind). Pressure and density are integrated hydrostatically from p_sfc with the initial virtual temperature and then held fixed (the column is shallow and quasi-Boussinesq). Any initial supersaturation is removed with fog_condensation.

Aeolus.RadiationFog.FogColumnSettings — Type
FogColumnSettings(; kwargs...)

Parameters of a FogColumn. Defaults describe the ParisFog case of 14–15 Nov 2011 at SIRTA (see parisfog_2011_column); every field can be overridden by keyword.

Switches (what the feedback tests toggle):

  • droplet_feedback = true — fog droplets are seen by the radiation. false leaves the fog in the thermodynamics but transparent to radiation.
  • shortwave = true — the sun rises. false keeps the night going.
  • settling = true — droplets sediment at terminal_velocity(r_eff).

Site and time: latitude, longitude (degrees, east positive), start = (year, month, day, hour, minute) in UTC.

Surface: z0m, z0h roughness lengths (m); albedo, emissivity; admittance (thermal admittance of the soil/vegetation top, J m⁻² K⁻¹ s⁻½) and T_deep (K) of the force-restore equation; β the soil moisture availability for bare evaporation.

Turbulence: λ asymptotic mixing length (m), K_min background diffusivity (m² s⁻¹), ug, vg geostrophic wind (m s⁻¹).

Microphysics: N_d droplet number (m⁻³) and k_shape (see fog_effective_radius).

Numerics: dt (s), radiation every dt_rad s with gas optics every dt_gas s, diagnostics every dt_out s. The eddy diffusivity is evaluated from the start-of-step state (explicit coefficients in an implicit solve), which with the 1 m lowest layer of parisfog_2011_column develops the 2Δt "fibrillation" of stability-dependent K closures (Kalnay & Kanamitsu 1988, MWR 116, 1945) for dt ≳ 10 s: at dt = 10 s the surface is 0.32 K colder after 3 h than at dt = 2.5 s and ground fog forms 45 min earlier. dt = 5 s is converged to 0.006 K over the same 3 h (tested), and the 14 h life cycle agrees with dt = 1 s to 10 min in onset and 5 min in clearance. Fog is LWC ≥ lwc_fog (kg m⁻³); "fog at the ground" is fog in the layer containing z_obs m.

Aeolus.RadiationFog.FogRadiation — Type
FogRadiation

The radiation stack configured for a 1-D fog column: longwave and shortwave RRTMG-band grids, correlated-k gas optics, the Phase 33 MieCloudOptics for the droplets and a two-stream solver for each, plus a fixed upper atmosphere above the model top so that the downwelling longwave at the fog top is that of a real sky rather than of space.

Build with fog_radiation; evaluate with fog_radiative_transfer!.

Fields of interest after a call: flux_lw, flux_sw (RadiationCore RadiativeFluxes, levels bottom-up over the full column), and nz, the number of model layers at the bottom of it.

Aeolus.RadiationFog.dew_deposition — Method
dew_deposition(qv, Ts, p, dt; dz, ρ = 1.2, C_e = 1e-3, U = 3.0,
               dew = Inf, c = DEFAULT_CONSTANTS)
    -> (; Δqv, mass, surface_heat)

Vapour exchange between the lowest air layer (thickness dz, density ρ, specific humidity qv) and a surface at temperature Ts over one step dt, by the bulk law

ρ dz dq_v/dt = −ρ g_a (q_v − q_s(T_s, p)),    g_a = C_e U.

Solved exactly over the step with T_s held fixed, so Δq_v = −(q_v − q_s(T_s))·(1 − exp(−g_a dt/dz)): the air can at most be dried to the surface saturation humidity, whatever dt is.

  • q_v > q_s(T_s) — the surface is below the dew point and dew forms: mass = −ρ dz Δq_v > 0 kg m⁻².
  • q_v < q_s(T_s) — existing dew (dew, kg m⁻², the amount available) evaporates, limited by what is there; with dew = 0 nothing happens.

Saturation is taken over liquid water. Below 273.15 K deposition is really frost (hoar), which would saturate over ice and release L_s; that regime is out of scope here, and dew on a sub-freezing surface is treated as supercooled liquid.

surface_heat = L_v(T_s)·mass (J m⁻²) is the latent heat released at the surface (negative when dew evaporates). It belongs in the surface energy budget; the air temperature is untouched, because the vapour that left carried its own sensible enthalpy with it. This is the coupling the original urban apply_fog_dew! got wrong.

Aeolus.RadiationFog.dew_exchange! — Method
dew_exchange!(qv, q_dew, Ts, p, dt; dz, ρ = 1.2, C_e = 1e-3, U = 3.0,
              c = DEFAULT_CONSTANTS, out = nothing) -> H (W m⁻²)

Apply dew_deposition column by column: qv is the 3-D vapour array (only level 1 is touched) or a 2-D array of lowest-layer values, q_dew the 2-D surface dew reservoir (kg m⁻², updated in place), Ts the 2-D surface temperature. Returns the 2-D latent heating of the surface, W m⁻², positive when dew forms — add it to the surface energy balance, not to the air. H is a new array, or out (overwritten) when given, so a time loop can avoid the allocation.

Aeolus.RadiationFog.dew_exchange_columns! — Method
dew_exchange_columns!(H, qv, q_dew, Ts, mask, k_air, p, ρ, dz, dt) -> H

dew_deposition between the air cell k_air[i, j] of each column with mask[i, j] (e.g. the first air cell above a roof, and only over surfaces without a vapour exchange of their own) and the dew reservoir q_dew (kg m⁻², updated in place) at the surface temperature Ts, at that cell's pressure p[k] and density ρ[k] (per-level vectors). qv is the 3-D vapour array; its increment is applied as qv += (q + Δqv) − q. H (W m⁻², overwritten; 0 where nothing forms or evaporates) is the latent heat released at the surface, positive when dew forms: it belongs in the surface energy budget, not the air's. The columns are independent work items, so the result is the same at any thread count. Moved from the urban case's fog_dew coupling (ONE-e, #454); dew_exchange! is the form at level 1 with one pressure.

Aeolus.RadiationFog.eddy_diffusivity — Method
eddy_diffusivity(col) -> K (m² s⁻¹) at interfaces 2..nz

First-order local closure K = l² |∂U/∂z| f(Ri) + K_min, Blackadar mixing length l = κ z / (1 + κ z / λ), gradient Richardson number from moist_stability, and f the short-tailed stability function TurbulenceStability.ShortTail (through stability_factor_m). The same K mixes heat, water and momentum.

Aeolus.RadiationFog.fog_adjust! — Method
fog_adjust!(θ, qv, ql, p, dt; τ = 0, levels = axes(θ, 3),
            c = DEFAULT_CONSTANTS, backend = backend(θ)) -> total condensed (kg/kg, summed)

Apply fog_condensation to every cell of 3-D arrays θ (potential temperature, K), qv and ql (kg/kg) in the vertical levels. p is a scalar pressure or a vector of layer pressures indexed by k. Temperature is T = θ Π(p) with the dry Exner function of the thermodynamic contract, and the enthalpy-conserving warming is written back as Δθ = ΔT / Π.

A cell without fog water (ql ≤ 0) whose vapour is below saturation, $q_v < (1 − 10^{-8})\,q_s(T, p)$, is left as it is without the adjustment (it cannot condense, and the adjustment returns Δq = 0 for it exactly, so the skip changes no bits; maintainer decision on #291).

The cells are independent: the levels run in parallel on backend (one plane per work item, KA-m #301), each plane in cell order, so the fields are identical at every thread count and to a serial sweep.

Returns the domain sum of the condensed amount (negative when fog evaporated), which callers can use as a budget check: each plane's sum in cell order, the planes added in ascending k (thread-invariant; before KA-m one running sum over all cells, so the last bits of the total moved once).

Aeolus.RadiationFog.fog_atmospheric_column — Method
fog_atmospheric_column(rad, T_lay, p_lay, p_lev, dz, ρ, qv, ql, T_sfc;
                       N_d, k = 0.8, droplet_feedback = true)
    -> AtmosphericColumn

RadiationCore column for the model layers (bottom-up) stacked under the fixed upper atmosphere of rad. Water vapour enters the gas optics through vmr[:h2o]; the fog water enters the cloud optics through ql and r_eff_liq = fog_effective_radius(ql, ρ, N_d; k).

With droplet_feedback = false the column carries ql = 0: the fog still exists thermodynamically (its water and latent heat are there) but is transparent to radiation. That is the switch the feedback tests use to isolate the droplet-radiative effect.

Aeolus.RadiationFog.fog_condensation — Method
fog_condensation(T, p, qv, ql, dt; τ = 0, c = DEFAULT_CONSTANTS)
    -> (; T, qv, ql, Δq, latent_heat)

One fog condensation/evaporation step for a single air parcel at pressure p. The parcel relaxes toward the saturation-adjusted state of fog_equilibrium by the fraction relaxation_fraction(dt, τ); τ = 0 (default) is full saturation adjustment, the standard LES choice since the phase-relaxation time of fog droplets (a few seconds at 100 cm⁻³) is shorter than any dynamical step.

Properties, all tested:

  • total water qv + ql is conserved exactly;
  • moist enthalpy (MoistThermodynamics.moist_enthalpy) is conserved to round-off, which makes the sensible warming exactly c_pm ΔT = L_v(T)·Δq (the released heat, returned as latent_heat, J/kg);
  • no condensation occurs in subsaturated air, and the step never crosses saturation for any dt and any τ ≥ 0.
Aeolus.RadiationFog.fog_diagnostics — Method
fog_diagnostics(col) -> NamedTuple

Instantaneous fog diagnostics (using the fluxes of the last radiation call):

  • t (s since start), utc_hour (hours since 00 UTC of the start day, may exceed 24), μ₀
  • fog_top (m; highest interface of a fog layer, 0 if none), fog_base, lwp (g m⁻²), fog_obs (fog at z_obs)
  • T_obs, Ts (K), lw_net_sfc, sw_abs_sfc, lw_dn_sfc (W m⁻²)
  • lw_net_top — net LW flux at the fog top (W m⁻², NaN without fog)
  • lw_fog — longwave net-flux convergence of the fog layer (W m⁻², negative = cooling), lw_top_cooling — the strongest LW cooling rate in the fog (K day⁻¹, Q/(ρ c_pd))
  • C_LW, E_SW — the Wærsted et al. (2017) radiation-driven condensation and evaporation of the fog layer, g m⁻² h⁻¹
Aeolus.RadiationFog.fog_effective_radius — Method
fog_effective_radius(ql, ρ, N_d; k = 0.8, r_min = 1.0) -> μm

Effective radius of the fog droplets, in the μm that TwoStream.MieCloudOptics consumes, for liquid specific humidity ql (kg/kg) in air of density ρ (kg m⁻³) with N_d droplets per m³.

Delegates to AerosolCloud.droplet_effective_radius (the Martin et al. 1994 relation r_eff = (3 LWC / (4π ρ_w N_d k))^{1/3}, returned there in metres); k is the spectral-shape factor. r_min only guards the ql → 0 limit, where the value is irrelevant because the optical depth τ = 1.5 LWP / r_eff ∝ ql^{2/3} vanishes anyway.

Aeolus.RadiationFog.fog_equilibrium — Function
fog_equilibrium(T, p, qv, ql, c = DEFAULT_CONSTANTS; tol = 1e-14)
    -> (Δq, T_eq)

Amount of vapour Δq (kg/kg; negative = fog evaporates) that must condense, at constant pressure p and constant moist enthalpy, to bring an air parcel with temperature T, vapour qv and fog liquid ql exactly to saturation over liquid water — or to evaporate all of ql if even that leaves the air subsaturated. T_eq is the temperature of the adjusted state.

This is the contract's process adjustment, MoistThermodynamics.isobaric_saturation_adjustment(T, p, qv, ql, c), with no rain or ice: the solver (a safeguarded Newton iteration on the monotone residual (q_v − Δ) − q_s(T(Δ), p) over Δ ∈ [−ql, qv]) lives there, one copy for every scheme (issue #82). It was written here first (#85) and moved to the contract unchanged, so fog results are bit-for-bit those of #85.

Aeolus.RadiationFog.fog_life_cycle — Method
fog_life_cycle(history; min_duration = 1800) -> NamedTuple

Onset and dissipation of fog at the observation height from a diagnostic history (see fog_diagnostics), in UTC hours: onset is the start of the first fog episode at z_obs lasting at least min_duration s, dissipation the end of the last episode (NaN if fog is still there at the end of the record), plus max_top (m), max_lwp (g m⁻²) and the time of the latter.

Liquid water path Σ ρ Δz q_l, kg m⁻².

Aeolus.RadiationFog.fog_radiation — Method
fog_radiation(nz, z_top, p_top, T_top, qv_top; z_toa = 30e3,
              n_std = 30, humid = :subarctic_winter, ngpt_per_band = 2)
    -> FogRadiation

Radiation for a fog column of nz model layers whose top interface is at height z_top (m), pressure p_top (Pa), temperature T_top (K) and specific humidity qv_top.

Above the model top the atmosphere is GasOptics.us_standard_column (n_std equal layers to z_toa), shifted so that its temperature and pressure are continuous with the model top and its water vapour scaled to the model-top value. It is held fixed: a fog night does not change the free troposphere's emission to first order, and the upper air is not the subject here. z_top must be one of the standard column's level heights (a multiple of z_toa / n_std, 1000 m by default).

Aeolus.RadiationFog.fog_radiative_heating! — Method
fog_radiative_heating!(Q, rad, col) -> Q

Radiative energy input of each model layer, W m⁻³ (LW + SW), Q_k = (F_net(k+1) − F_net(k))/Δz_k = +∂F_net/∂z with F_net positive downward (energy entering through the top minus energy leaving through the bottom), from RadiationCore.flux_divergence! on the last fluxes in rad.

Summed over the model layers, Σ Q_k Δz_k = F_net(model top) − F_net(sfc) exactly (the divergence telescopes); the column energy test relies on it.

Aeolus.RadiationFog.fog_radiative_transfer! — Method
fog_radiative_transfer!(rad, col; μ₀ = 0, solar_scale = 1,
                        albedo = 0.22, emissivity = 0.97,
                        update_gas = true) -> rad

Longwave and (if μ₀ > 0) shortwave fluxes through col, written to rad.flux_lw and rad.flux_sw.

Gas optics are the expensive part and change only with temperature and humidity, so they are recomputed only when update_gas = true (or on the first call) and reused otherwise; droplet optics, which carry the feedback, are recomputed on every call. solar_scale multiplies the shortwave fluxes (the solution is linear in the top-of-atmosphere irradiance), e.g. by the Earth–Sun distance factor.

The defaults albedo = 0.22 and emissivity = 0.97 are the SIRTA values of Wærsted et al. (2017, Sect. 2.3): median observed albedo 0.221, longwave emissivity 0.97.

Aeolus.RadiationFog.fog_top_flux_jump — Method
fog_top_flux_jump(rad, k_bot, k_top) -> (ΔF_lw, ΔF_sw) W m⁻²

Net-flux convergence of the model layers k_bot:k_top (F_net(k_top+1) − F_net(k_bot)): the energy the layer gains per unit area. Negative in the longwave for a fog layer at night — its radiative cooling. In an opaque fog it is the net LW flux at the fog top (rad.flux_lw.flux_net[k_top+1], the quantity Wærsted et al. 2017, Fig. 6b, report: −73 W m⁻² on 2 and 8 Nov 2015, −100 W m⁻² on 28 Oct 2014) minus the small net LW at the fog base (≈ −10 W m⁻² at the ground), so its magnitude is ≈ 10 W m⁻² smaller than theirs.

Aeolus.RadiationFog.moist_stability — Method
moist_stability(col, k) -> N² (s⁻²)

Squared buoyancy frequency across interface k+½, measured by displacing a parcel from each side to the other with conserved static energy and total water and the fog saturation adjustment, and comparing density potential temperatures (MoistThermodynamics. density_potential_temperature). In unsaturated air this is the usual dry N² of θ_ρ; inside fog it is the saturated stability, which is what lets fog-top cooling overturn the fog layer.

Aeolus.RadiationFog.parisfog_2011_column — Method
parisfog_2011_column(; H = 1000.0, settings = FogColumnSettings(), kwargs...)
    -> FogColumn

Semi-idealised initial state for the ParisFog radiation fog of 14–15 November 2011 at SIRTA (48.71 °N, 2.21 °E), documented by Mazoyer et al. (2017): fog appeared aloft at 02:00 UTC and at the ground at 02:30 UTC, became optically thick at ≈ 03:00 UTC when ≈ 80 m deep, peaked at LWP ≈ 70 g m⁻² around 07:30 UTC with a top of up to 300 m (sodar), and dissipated at the ground around 10:00 UTC, ≈ 3 h after the 06:59 UTC sunrise. Their LES starts at 23:20 UTC from the Trappes sounding adjusted to the SIRTA mast, with a surface temperature of 276 K and an 8 m s⁻¹ geostrophic wind; the column starts at the same time with the same surface temperature and geostrophic wind.

What is not reproduced. The sounding itself is shown only as a figure (their Fig. A.1), so the profile here is constructed, not digitised: a 60 m surface inversion of 2 K topped by a slightly stable layer to 400 m (the depth to which they adjusted the sounding), relative humidity 97 % at the ground (their observed value from 00:00 to 01:30 UTC) falling to 88 % at 400 m and 50 % above, and a 4 K km⁻¹ stable free troposphere. The comparison is therefore of fog life-cycle timing and structure, not of profiles.

Aeolus.RadiationFog.radiative_condensation_rate — Method
radiative_condensation_rate(Q, T, ρ; c = DEFAULT_CONSTANTS) -> kg m⁻³ s⁻¹

Condensation rate a radiative energy input Q (W m⁻³; negative for cooling) forces in saturated air at temperature T and density ρ, assuming the air stays saturated and nothing else acts — Wærsted et al. (2017), Eqs. (1)-(5):

\[C_\mathrm{rad} = -\frac{\frac{dρ_s}{dT}\left(\frac{dT}{dt}\right)_\mathrm{rad}} {1 + \frac{L_v}{ρ c_p}\frac{dρ_s}{dT}},\qquad \frac{dρ_s}{dT} = \frac{e_s}{R_v T^2}\left(\frac{L_v}{R_v T} - 1\right),\]

with (dT/dt)_rad = Q/(ρ c_p). e_s, L_v, R_v and c_p come from the MoistThermodynamics contract (Wærsted used Bolton's e_s; the two agree to ~0.1 % at fog temperatures). Integrated over the fog layer this is their C_LW (from the longwave Q) or −E_SW (shortwave), which they measured at 40–70 g m⁻² h⁻¹ for opaque fog (LWP ≳ 30 g m⁻²) at SIRTA.

Aeolus.RadiationFog.relaxation_fraction — Method
relaxation_fraction(dt, τ) -> f ∈ [0, 1]

Fraction of the distance to equilibrium covered in one step of length dt by linear relaxation with timescale τ: f = 1 − exp(−dt/τ). τ ≤ 0 means instantaneous adjustment (f = 1).

This is the exact solution of dx/dt = −x/τ over the step, not the explicit-Euler dt/τ, which exceeds 1 — i.e. overshoots — as soon as dt > τ.

Aeolus.RadiationFog.settling_velocity — Method
settling_velocity(ql, ρ, N_d; k = 0.8) -> m s⁻¹

Bulk sedimentation velocity of the fog water: Superdroplet. terminal_velocity (Stokes with slip correction at fog sizes) at the effective radius of fog_effective_radius. One velocity for the whole spectrum — the per-moment fall-speed spectrum is Phase 44 #8. For N_d = 50 cm⁻³ and 0.2 g m⁻³ this gives ≈ 1 cm s⁻¹.

Aeolus.RadiationFog.stretched_levels — Method
stretched_levels(H; dz0 = 1.0, r = 1.08, dzmax = 25.0) -> zf

Interface heights from 0 to H: the first layer dz0 thick, each next one r times thicker up to dzmax; the last layer absorbs the remainder to land on H, so it can be up to 1.5 times the current spacing thick. Fog forms in the lowest metres and its top is where the cooling concentrates, so both need metre-scale layers.

Aeolus.RadiationFog.surface_exchange — Method
surface_exchange(col) -> (; C_D, C_H, U, Ri_b)

Bulk transfer coefficients between the surface and the lowest layer: neutral values κ²/ln(z/z0m)² and κ²/(ln(z/z0m) ln(z/z0h)), times the same stability function as the interior (ShortTail) with the bulk Richardson number of the surface layer.

Aeolus.RadiationFog.update_radiation! — Method
update_radiation!(col; force_gas = false) -> col

Recompute the radiative energy input col.Q (W m⁻³, LW + SW), its LW part col.Q_lw, and the surface net LW and absorbed SW from the current state. Solar geometry comes from SolarGeometry.solar_position at the column's UTC time; the SW is scaled by the Earth–Sun distance factor.

Aeolus.Simulations.run! — Method
run!(col, duration) -> col

Step the column for duration seconds (rounded to whole steps) and record a final diagnostic.

Aeolus.TimeSteppers.step! — Method
step!(col) -> col

Advance the column by col.set.dt: radiation (every dt_rad), radiative heating, surface fluxes and T_s, implicit turbulent mixing of s = h + g z, q_v, q_l and momentum, Coriolis turning toward the geostrophic wind, droplet settling, and finally fog saturation adjustment with fog_condensation. Diagnostics are recorded every dt_out.
