Sub-grid surface tiles — SurfaceTiles

SurfaceTiles represents a heterogeneous grid cell as a mosaic of AbstractSurface tiles with area fractions. Each tile keeps its own state and solves its own energy balance through the SurfaceExchange contract; the cell returns the fraction-weighted fluxes (flux aggregation), not the flux of a surface with averaged parameters. Theory, the exact aggregates and the measured size of the aggregation error are in Sub-grid surface tiles.

A SurfaceMosaic is itself an AbstractSurface, so mosaics nest and a mosaic can be stepped wherever a single surface can. Any implementer of the contract can be a tile — SlabLand today, the soil, canopy, urban, snow, ice and ocean models of the surface shared core as they land.

using Aeolus
cell = SurfaceMosaic([SlabLand(T = 300.0, albedo = 0.35, z0m = 0.01),   # dry field
                      SlabLand(T = 292.0, albedo = 0.12, z0m = 1.0)],   # wood
                     [0.7, 0.3])
f  = SurfaceForcing(sw_down = 600.0, T_air = 295.0, U = 3.0)
fl = step_surface!(cell, f, 60.0)        # Σ fᵢ Fᵢ, each tile stepped
last_tile_fluxes(cell)                   # the per-tile fluxes behind it
turbulent_fluxes(parameter_aggregate(cell), f)   # the parameter-averaged alternative

Blending height

A BlendedMosaic forces the tiles with the wind, temperature and humidity at the blending height $l_b$ instead of at the forcing level, so that the tiles exchange with air already mixed over their neighbours. The same module provides the blending-height effective roughness, closures for $l_b$ and internal-boundary-layer growth laws; theory and validation are in Blending height.

bh   = BouZeidBlendingHeight(3140.0)            # patch length scale (m)
cell = BlendedMosaic([SlabLand(T = 294.0, z0m = 0.01), SlabLand(T = 294.0, z0m = 0.1)], [0.25, 0.75], bh)
blending_height(cell)                             # ≈ 289 m
roughness_lengths(cell).z0m                       # ≈ 0.065 m (LES: 0.063 m)
fl = step_surface!(cell, SurfaceForcing(T_air = 290.0, U = 8.0, z_ref = 400.0), 60.0)
last_blending(cell).U_b                           # blended wind at l_b
ibl_height(DiffusionIBL(), 300.0; z0_up = 0.008, z0_down = 0.032)   # ≈ 26 m

The energy mosaic is distinct from SurfaceOptics.TiledSurface, the optical (spectral-albedo) mosaic of the radiation stack.

Aeolus.SurfaceTiles — Module
SurfaceTiles

Sub-grid surface heterogeneity by tiling (the "mosaic" approach of Avissar & Pielke 1989): a grid cell holds $N$ surfaces — any AbstractSurface implementers — with area fractions $f_i ≥ 0$, $\sum f_i = 1$. Each tile keeps its own prognostic state and solves its own energy balance against the grid-cell forcing through the SurfaceExchange contract; the cell sees the area-weighted average of the tile fluxes

\[\overline{F} = \sum_i f_i\, F_i(\text{tile } i),\]

not the flux of one surface with averaged parameters, $F(\bar p)$. The two differ whenever the flux is a nonlinear function of the heterogeneous parameter (stability, roughness, saturation humidity), which is the whole reason to tile.

A SurfaceMosaic is itself an AbstractSurface, so it can be stepped, nested, or handed to anything that speaks the contract. Its radiative properties are the exact aggregates: albedo and emissivity are area-weighted, and effective_radiative_temperature is the temperature that reproduces the tile-summed emitted longwave, so that the one-surface formula $R_n = (1-\bar α) SW↓ + \bar ε LW↓ - \bar ε σ T_{rad}^4$ equals $\sum f_i R_{n,i}$ identically.

This is the energy-balance mosaic. Its optical sibling is SurfaceOptics.TiledSurface, which area-weights spectral albedos of AbstractSurfaceOptics components; a SurfaceMosaic instead asks each of its tiles for its own albedo (which a tile may well obtain from SurfaceOptics) and weights the result the same way.

The five-class urban surface map of examples/urban (one class per LES cell) is the special case of one tile per cell; class_fractions and tiles_from_class_map coarse-grain such a map into tiled cells.

A plain SurfaceMosaic forces every tile with the grid-cell forcing (or a forcing per tile passed to step_surface!). The blending-height formulation (Wieringa 1986; Mason 1988; Claussen 1991) is the second half of this module: effective_roughness at a blending height $l_b$, closures for $l_b$ (MasonBlendingHeight, ClaussenBlendingHeight, BouZeidBlendingHeight, IBLBlendingHeight), internal-boundary-layer growth laws (ibl_height), and BlendedMosaic, whose tiles are forced with the wind, temperature and humidity at the blending height obtained by matching the aggregate flux to the profile above it.

Aeolus.SurfaceTiles.AbstractBlendingHeight — Type
AbstractBlendingHeight

A rule for the blending height $l_b$ of a patchwork of tiles. Implementers extend blending_height(bh, z0s, fractions; c). Provided: FixedBlendingHeight, MasonBlendingHeight, ClaussenBlendingHeight, BouZeidBlendingHeight, IBLBlendingHeight.

Aeolus.SurfaceTiles.AbstractIBLModel — Type
AbstractIBLModel

A formula for the depth $δ(x)$ of the internal boundary layer at fetch $x$ downstream of a step change of roughness from z0_up to z0_down, under neutral stratification. See ibl_height and the compendium of Savelyev & Taylor (2005, Table I).

Aeolus.SurfaceTiles.BlendedMosaic — Type
BlendedMosaic(mosaic::SurfaceMosaic, height::AbstractBlendingHeight)
BlendedMosaic(tiles, fractions, height; atol = 1e-6)

A tiled grid cell whose tiles are forced at the blending height: each step_surface! or turbulent_fluxes call first finds the blended wind, temperature and humidity at $l_b$ = blending_height(height, mosaic) with blended_forcing, then lets every tile exchange with that state and returns $\sum f_i F_i$. The tiles, fractions and per-tile fluxes are those of the wrapped mosaic.

Contract methods: surface_temperature, surface_albedo, surface_emissivity, moisture_availability and net_radiation are the mosaic's; roughness_lengths returns the blending-height effective_roughness (not the log average); step_surface! and turbulent_fluxes accept one SurfaceForcing for the cell and pass kwargs to the tiles and to blended_forcing. The solution of the last call is kept in last_blending.

Compared with a plain SurfaceMosaic forced at the grid level $z$, the difference is where the tiles' surface layers end: at $z$ (each tile as if it extended to infinity upstream) or at $l_b$ (tiles exchange with air that has already been mixed over the other tiles).

Aeolus.SurfaceTiles.BlendingHeightSolution — Type
BlendingHeightSolution

Result of blended_forcing. Fields:

  • forcings — one SurfaceForcing per tile: the grid-cell forcing with U, T_air, q_air replaced by the blended values at z_ref = l_b (or the grid forcing itself when blended == false);
  • l_b — the blending height (m);
  • U_b, T_b, θ_b, q_b — wind (m s⁻¹), temperature (K), potential temperature on the surface reference (K), specific humidity at l_b;
  • u_star, θ_star, q_star, L — scales of the aggregate flux and the Obukhov length of the blended layer between l_b and the forcing level;
  • residual — the largest profile mismatch at the solution, in m s⁻¹, K and kg kg⁻¹ scaled to one number (see blended_forcing);
  • iterations, converged;
  • blended — false when the forcing level is at or below l_b, so the tiles are forced directly at the forcing level (no blending).
Aeolus.SurfaceTiles.BouZeidBlendingHeight — Type
BouZeidBlendingHeight(L; C = 1.7)

The blending height of Bou-Zeid, Meneveau & Parlange (2004), tested against LES of complex patchworks in Bou-Zeid et al. (2007): blending is complete where internal boundary layers growing by the diffusion analogy over about twice the patch length L (m) meet, $l_b[\ln(l_b/z_{0,eff}) - 1] = C κ L$. Combined with effective_roughness this is Eq. (2) of Bou-Zeid et al. (2007),

\[\left(\frac{l_b}{C κ L + l_b}\right)^2 = \sum_i \frac{f_i}{\ln^2(l_b/z_{0,i})}, \qquad z_{0,eff} = l_b \exp\!\left(-\frac{C κ L}{l_b} - 1\right),\]

with $C = 1.7$ and $κ = 0.4$. For regular stripes L is the mean patch length; for patches that are not regular stripes Bou-Zeid et al. (2007, Eq. 6) take the integral length scale of the roughness structure function.

Aeolus.SurfaceTiles.ClaussenBlendingHeight — Type
ClaussenBlendingHeight(L; C = 1.75)

Claussen's (1991) blending height for patches of horizontal scale L (m),

\[\frac{l_b}{L}\,\ln\frac{l_b}{z_{0,eff}} = C κ, \qquad C ≈ 1.75,\]

solved jointly with effective_roughness. It is BouZeidBlendingHeight without the $-1$; Bou-Zeid et al. (2007, Sect. 6) state that this is the only difference between the two $z_{0,eff}$ equations and that it matters only for short patches.

Source of the form and of the constant: the linear-in-$\ln$ form above is the one implied by that statement of Bou-Zeid et al. (2007); Claussen (1991) itself was not consulted. The constant $C ≈ 1.75$ is from Kramm & Dlugi (2009, Eq. 5.5), who however print the logarithm squared, $(l_b/L)\ln^2(l_b/z_0) ≅ C_b κ$. The two readings are not equivalent: the squared form gives a Mason-like $l_b$ of tens of metres for $L ∼ 3$ km, the linear form a few hundred metres, as does BouZeidBlendingHeight. The linear form is used here; treat the constant as uncertain.

Aeolus.SurfaceTiles.DiffusionIBL — Type
DiffusionIBL(; C = 1.25)

Miyake's diffusion analogy: the interface rises at the vertical velocity scale, $U(δ)\,dδ/dx = C u_*$ with $C = σ_w/u_*$, and a logarithmic downstream profile $U = (u_*/κ)\ln(z/z_{0D})$, integrated from $δ(0) = z_{0D}$:

\[C κ \frac{x}{z_{0D}} = \frac{δ}{z_{0D}}\left(\ln\frac{δ}{z_{0D}} - 1\right) + 1 .\]

$C = 1.25$ is Panofsky & Dutton (1984) (Savelyev & Taylor 2005, Table I); Miyake's (1965) original form has $1.73$ and starts from a finite $δ_0$. Differentiating gives $dδ/dx = Cκ/\ln(δ/z_{0D})$.

Aeolus.SurfaceTiles.ElliottIBL — Type
ElliottIBL()

Elliott (1958): $δ/z_{0D} = (0.75 - 0.03 \ln(z_{0D}/z_{0U}))\,(x/z_{0D})^{0.8}$ (Savelyev & Taylor 2005, Eq. 1).

Aeolus.SurfaceTiles.FixedBlendingHeight — Type
FixedBlendingHeight(l_b)

A prescribed blending height $l_b$ (m), e.g. taken from where the resolved flow of an LES over the patches becomes horizontally uniform (Bou-Zeid et al. 2004 determine it from where the velocity quartile profiles merge).

Aeolus.SurfaceTiles.IBLBlendingHeight — Type
IBLBlendingHeight(model::AbstractIBLModel, L; fetch_factor = 2)

Blending height from internal-boundary-layer growth: blending is complete where the IBLs that start at the patch edges have grown over fetch_factor × L (the premise of Bou-Zeid et al. 2004, who found that blending needs about twice the patch length L). $l_b$ is the deepest IBL ibl_height(model, fetch_factor * L) over every ordered pair of distinct active tiles (upstream $z_{0,j}$, downstream $z_{0,i}$); with a single active tile it is the depth over that surface. With DiffusionIBL this is, up to its constant, the IBL half of BouZeidBlendingHeight evaluated with the patch roughness instead of $z_{0,eff}$.

Aeolus.SurfaceTiles.MasonBlendingHeight — Type
MasonBlendingHeight(L; C = 2)

Mason's (1988) heuristic blending height for patches of horizontal length scale L (m): the height at which $(u_*/U(l_b))^2 ≈ l_b/(C L)$,

\[\frac{l_b}{L}\,\ln^2\!\frac{l_b}{z_{0,eff}} = C κ^2, \qquad C ≈ 2,\]

(form as quoted by Kramm & Dlugi 2009, Eq. 5.4), solved jointly with the effective_roughness $z_{0,eff}(l_b)$. For $L$ of a few kilometres and $z_0 ∼ 0.01$–$0.1$ m it gives $l_b$ of tens of metres, $O(L/100)$.

Aeolus.SurfaceTiles.ParameterAggregatedSurface — Type
ParameterAggregatedSurface(; T, albedo, emissivity, z0m, z0h, z0q, β)

A single homogeneous surface described by effective parameters — the parameter-aggregation alternative to tiling. It is a diagnostic snapshot (no prognostic state, no step_surface!): the default SurfaceExchange hooks turn it into fluxes, so turbulent_fluxes(p, forcing) is $F(\bar p)$ to set against a tiled cell's $\sum f_i F(p_i)$. Build one from a tiled cell with parameter_aggregate.

Aeolus.SurfaceTiles.PowerLawIBL — Type
PowerLawIBL(; a = 0.28, b = 0.8)

$δ/z_{0r} = a\,(x/z_{0r})^{b}$ with $z_{0r} = \max(z_{0U}, z_{0D})$. The defaults are Wood (1982), who argued that only the rougher surface matters (Savelyev & Taylor 2005, Eq. 5).

Aeolus.SurfaceTiles.SurfaceMosaic — Type
SurfaceMosaic(tiles, fractions; atol = 1e-6)

A grid cell made of the surfaces tiles (a Tuple or AbstractVector of AbstractSurfaces, which may be of different types — including other SurfaceMosaics) covering area fractions fractions.

Fractions must be non-negative and sum to one within atol; they are then renormalised by their sum, so they sum to one to rounding (a few ulp). A zero-fraction tile is allowed (it is still stepped, but carries no weight), which is how a tile that appears seasonally — snow, a flooded patch — is kept warm.

The tiles are the state: SurfaceMosaic stores no temperature of its own. After each step_surface! the per-tile fluxes that were applied are available from last_tile_fluxes.

Interface methods (all SurfaceExchange generics):

methodaggregate
step_surface!(ts, f, dt)steps every tile, returns $\sum f_i F_i$
turbulent_fluxes(ts, f)$\sum f_i F_i$ without stepping (flux aggregation)
net_radiation(ts, f)$\sum f_i R_{n,i}$
surface_albedo(ts)$\sum f_i α_i$ (exact for reflected SW)
surface_emissivity(ts)$\sum f_i ε_i$ (exact for absorbed LW)
surface_temperature(ts)effective_radiative_temperature
roughness_lengths(ts)logarithmic average — a parameter aggregate
moisture_availability(ts)$\sum f_i β_i$ — a parameter aggregate

The last two are there so a SurfaceMosaic satisfies the whole contract (and so parameter_aggregate can be built); the fluxes of a tiled cell never use them.

Aeolus.SurfaceExchange.moisture_availability — Method

Parameter aggregate $\sum f_i β_i$ of the tiles' moisture availabilities.

Aeolus.SurfaceExchange.net_radiation — Method
net_radiation(ts::SurfaceMosaic, forcing; c = DEFAULT_SURFACE_CONSTANTS)

$\sum f_i R_{n,i}$, each tile with its own albedo, emissivity and skin temperature. Identical (to rounding) to the one-surface formula with surface_albedo(ts), surface_emissivity(ts) and surface_temperature(ts).

Aeolus.SurfaceExchange.roughness_lengths — Method

Blending-height effective_roughness (z0m, z0h, z0q) of the tiles of a BlendedMosaic.

Aeolus.SurfaceExchange.roughness_lengths — Method
roughness_lengths(ts::SurfaceMosaic)

Parameter aggregate of the tile roughness lengths by logarithmic averaging, $\ln z_{0,eff} = \sum f_i \ln z_{0,i}$ (for each of z0m, z0h, z0q). Log-averaging is the natural choice because the neutral drag depends on $\ln z_0$; it is still only an approximation of the tile-aggregated stress: it is the $l_b → ∞$ limit of the blending-height effective_roughness (Mason 1988), which a BlendedMosaic reports instead. A tiled cell's own fluxes never use it.

Aeolus.SurfaceExchange.step_surface! — Method
step_surface!(b::BlendedMosaic, forcing, dt; kwargs...) -> SurfaceFluxes

Find the blended state at $l_b$ for the current tile states, then step every tile with it (step_surface! of the wrapped mosaic with one forcing per tile) and return the fraction-weighted fluxes. Conservation is that of the mosaic: the returned G is $\sum f_i G_i$.

Aeolus.SurfaceExchange.step_surface! — Method
step_surface!(ts::SurfaceMosaic, forcing, dt; kwargs...) -> SurfaceFluxes

Advance every tile by dt with its own step_surface! — each solves its own energy balance — and return the fraction-weighted mean of the fluxes the tiles actually applied. forcing is one SurfaceForcing for the whole cell or a vector with one per tile (e.g. tile-specific wind at a blending height). kwargs are passed on to every tile's step_surface!. The per-tile fluxes are kept in last_tile_fluxes.

Conservation: if each tile stores $G_i\,Δt$ per unit area, the cell stores $\sum f_i G_i Δt = \bar G Δt$ — exactly the aggregate G returned here.

Aeolus.SurfaceExchange.surface_temperature — Method

The skin temperature of a tiled cell is its effective_radiative_temperature.

Aeolus.SurfaceExchange.turbulent_fluxes — Method
turbulent_fluxes(b::BlendedMosaic, forcing; kwargs...) -> SurfaceFluxes

$\sum f_i F_i$ with every tile forced at the blending height (see blended_forcing); nothing is stepped.

Aeolus.SurfaceExchange.turbulent_fluxes — Method
turbulent_fluxes(ts::SurfaceMosaic, forcing; kwargs...) -> SurfaceFluxes

Flux aggregation: the fraction-weighted mean of the tiles' own turbulent_fluxes, $\sum f_i F_i$. Each tile uses its own skin temperature, humidity, roughness and stability; nothing is averaged before the flux is computed. Compare turbulent_fluxes(parameter_aggregate(ts), forcing).

Aeolus.SurfaceTiles.aggregate_fluxes — Method
aggregate_fluxes(fluxes, fractions) -> SurfaceFluxes

Area-weighted average $\sum_i f_i F_i$ of each component of the per-tile fluxes (Rn, H, LE, G, τ, E). Because every component is averaged with the same weights, a per-tile energy balance $R_{n,i} = H_i + LE_i + G_i$ carries over to the aggregate exactly.

The momentum flux is averaged as a magnitude: all tiles of one cell see the same wind direction, so the stress vectors are parallel.

Aeolus.SurfaceTiles.blended_forcing — Method
blended_forcing(m::SurfaceMosaic, forcing, l_b; maxiter = 50, rtol = 1e-10, kwargs...)
    -> BlendingHeightSolution

The forcing each tile of m sees under the blending-height formulation (Wieringa 1986; Mason 1988; Claussen 1991), for a grid-cell forcing at height z = forcing.z_ref and blending height l_b < z.

Between the surface and $l_b$ every tile has its own surface layer; at $l_b$ the wind $U_b$, potential temperature $θ_b$ and humidity $q_b$ are common to all tiles; between $l_b$ and $z$ a single Monin–Obukhov profile carries the aggregate flux $\sum f_i F_i$. The blended state is the one for which the two agree:

\[U(z) - U_b = \frac{u_{*}}{κ}\,Φ_m, \quad θ(z) - θ_b = \frac{θ_{*}}{κ}\,Φ_h, \quad q(z) - q_b = \frac{q_{*}}{κ}\,Φ_h,\]

with $Φ = \ln(z/l_b) - ψ(z/L) + ψ(l_b/L)$, $u_*^2 = \bar τ/ρ$, $u_* θ_* = -\bar H/(ρ c_p)$, $u_* q_* = -\bar E/ρ$ from the tiles' own turbulent_fluxes against the blended forcing, and $L$ from the contract's obukhov_length. The three equations are solved by Newton iteration with a finite-difference Jacobian (every evaluation calls each tile's turbulent_fluxes); convergence means every mismatch is below rtol times $\max(U(z), 1)$, $θ(z)$ and $\max(q(z), 10^{-3})$.

Under neutral stratification the solution is closed-form and reproduces the effective_roughness exactly: with uniform tile skin temperatures the cell stress and heat flux equal those of one surface with $z_{0m,eff}$, $z_{0h,eff}$ forced at $z$.

If $l_b ≥ z$ the forcing level lies inside the tiles' own surface layers; each tile is then forced directly with forcing and blended = false. kwargs (sf, c, …) go to every tile's turbulent_fluxes; sf and c are also used for the blended layer. Temperatures are referred to the surface by the dry adiabat of the contract, $θ = T + Γ_d z$.

Two properties of the contract's surface-layer solve limit how exactly a single tile split at $l_b$ reproduces the same tile forced at $z$. Both are exact with $ψ ≡ 0$.

  • Buoyancy is referred to $θ_v$ at the forcing level. The blended layer refers it to $θ_v$ at $l_b$, as every tile does. This is an $O(Δθ/θ)$ ambiguity in $L$, which stability amplifies: a 2.5 % stress difference in a strongly stable test case, 5e-4 in unstable.
  • The default moisture_availability hook forms $q_s$ from $q_a$ at the forcing level. A tile with $0 < β < 1$ therefore evaporates differently when it is forced at $l_b$.
Aeolus.SurfaceTiles.blending_height — Function
blending_height(bh::AbstractBlendingHeight, z0s, fractions; c = DEFAULT_SURFACE_CONSTANTS) -> l_b
blending_height(bh, m::SurfaceMosaic; c = DEFAULT_SURFACE_CONSTANTS) -> l_b

Blending height (m) of tiles with momentum roughness lengths z0s and area fractions (or of the tiles of a mosaic) under the closure bh. The von Kármán constant is c.κ.

class_fractions(classes, nclass) -> Vector{Float64}

Aeolus.SurfaceTiles.effective_radiative_temperature — Method
effective_radiative_temperature(ts) -> K

The temperature $T_{rad}$ that makes a single surface of emissivity $\bar ε = \sum f_i ε_i$ emit what the tiles emit together:

\[\bar ε\, σ T_{rad}^4 = \sum_i f_i ε_i σ T_i^4 .\]

Because $T^4$ is convex, $T_{rad} ≥ \sum f_i T_i$ (equality only for uniform temperatures). If every tile has zero emissivity the fraction-mean temperature is returned.

Aeolus.SurfaceTiles.effective_roughness — Method
effective_roughness(z0s, fractions, l_b) -> z0_eff

The effective momentum roughness length of a patchwork of tiles with roughness lengths z0s and area fractions fractions, for a blending height l_b (m):

\[\frac{1}{\ln^2(l_b/z_{0,eff})} = \sum_i \frac{f_i}{\ln^2(l_b/z_{0,i})} .\]

This is the neutral drag law applied tile by tile with the wind common to all tiles at $l_b$ (Wieringa 1986; Mason 1988): the tile stresses $ρ[κU(l_b)/\ln(l_b/z_{0,i})]^2$ average to the stress of one surface of roughness $z_{0,eff}$. Because $1/\ln^2$ is convex, $z_{0,eff}$ lies above the logarithmic mean $\exp\sum f_i\ln z_{0,i}$ and below the largest $z_{0,i}$; it tends to the logarithmic mean as $l_b → ∞$ (the log-average model of Taylor 1987) as $\ln z_{0,eff} = \overline{\ln z_0} + \tfrac32\,\mathrm{Var}(\ln z_0)/\ln(l_b/z_0) + O(\ln^{-2})$, and grows as the blending height falls, i.e. as the patches become smaller. Form drag of obstacles at patch edges is not included: this is the aggregate of the tiles' own log-law drag.

Zero-fraction tiles are ignored; $l_b$ must exceed every remaining $z_{0,i}$.

effective_roughness(m::SurfaceMosaic, l_b) -> (z0m, z0h, z0q)
effective_roughness(m::SurfaceMosaic, bh::AbstractBlendingHeight) -> (z0m, z0h, z0q)

All three effective roughness lengths of a mosaic's tiles, with the scalar ones from effective_scalar_roughness; with a blending-height model bh the height is blending_height(bh, m).

Aeolus.SurfaceTiles.effective_scalar_roughness — Method
effective_scalar_roughness(z0ms, z0hs, fractions, l_b; z0m_eff = effective_roughness(z0ms, fractions, l_b))

Effective roughness length for heat (or moisture) at blending height l_b: the neutral bulk transfer coefficient $κ^2/[\ln(l_b/z_{0m}) \ln(l_b/z_{0h})]$ is averaged over the tiles,

\[\frac{1}{\ln(l_b/z_{0m,eff})\,\ln(l_b/z_{0h,eff})} = \sum_i \frac{f_i}{\ln(l_b/z_{0m,i})\,\ln(l_b/z_{0h,i})} ,\]

which is exact for the cell heat flux when every tile has the same surface-to-$l_b$ temperature difference under neutral stratification — the same construction as effective_roughness, applied to the scalar exchange coefficient.

Aeolus.SurfaceTiles.ibl_height — Function
ibl_height(model::AbstractIBLModel, x; z0_up, z0_down, c = DEFAULT_SURFACE_CONSTANTS) -> δ

Internal-boundary-layer depth (m) at fetch x (m) past a step change of roughness z0_up → z0_down (m). The formulas are for the surface layer, roughly $δ ≲ 0.1$–$0.2 z_i$ (Wood 1982). The IBL depth at a fetch of the order of the patch length is the natural scale of the blending height (Bou-Zeid et al. 2004, 2007); see IBLBlendingHeight.

Aeolus.SurfaceTiles.macdonald_roughness — Method
macdonald_roughness(λ_p, λ_f, h; A = 4.43, β = 1.0, C_D = 1.2, κ = 0.4)
    -> (z0 = z0m, d = d)

Momentum roughness length z0 and zero-plane displacement d (both m) of an array of buildings that a grid does not resolve, from its plan area density λ_p (built area / total area), frontal area density λ_f (windward facade area / total area) and mean building height h (m), after Macdonald, Griffiths & Hall (1998):

\[\frac{d}{h} = 1 + A^{-λ_p}\,(λ_p - 1), \qquad \frac{z_0}{h} = \left(1 - \frac{d}{h}\right) \exp\!\left\{-\left[\frac{β}{2}\,\frac{C_D}{κ^2}\left(1 - \frac{d}{h}\right) λ_f\right]^{-1/2}\right\}.\]

The first is the empirical fit of the displacement to wind-tunnel arrays; the second balances the drag of the obstacles' frontal area (drag coefficient C_D, the factor β correcting for sheltering) with the log-law stress above $d$. The defaults are Macdonald et al.'s values for staggered arrays ($A = 4.43$, $β = 1.0$, $C_D = 1.2$, $κ = 0.4$, the κ their fit was made with); for square (aligned) arrays they give $A = 3.59$, $β = 0.55$. With $λ_f = λ_p$ (cubes) $z_0/h$ peaks at $λ ≈ 0.16$, the "intermediate density" maximum reviewed by Grimmond & Oke (1999). Limits: $d, z_0 → 0$ as $λ_p → 0$ (no buildings) and $d → h$, $z_0 → 0$ as $λ_p → 1$ (a closed roof at height $h$).

The wind profile above the array is $u = (u_*/κ) \ln((z - d)/z_0)$: a Monin–Obukhov reference height must be measured from $d$ (see the Berlin parent surface, src/components/urban/parent/surface.jl). This is the one implementation of the relation in Aeolus; WallModels' dynamic roughness cites it rather than carrying a copy.

Arguments are validated: $0 ≤ λ_p ≤ 1$, $λ_f ≥ 0$, $h ≥ 0$. With $h = 0$ or $λ_p = 0$ or $λ_f = 0$ the result is (z0 = 0, d = 0·h); the caller supplies the roughness of the surface itself in that case.

References

  • Macdonald, R. W., Griffiths, R. F. & Hall, D. J. (1998). An improved method for the estimation of surface roughness of obstacle arrays. Atmos. Environ., 32, 1857-1864.
  • Grimmond, C. S. B. & Oke, T. R. (1999). Aerodynamic properties of urban areas derived from analysis of surface form. J. Appl. Meteorol., 38, 1262-1292.

Aeolus.SurfaceTiles.parameter_aggregate — Method
parameter_aggregate(ts; temperature = :mean) -> ParameterAggregatedSurface

The one-surface stand-in for a tiled cell with averaged parameters: area-weighted albedo, emissivity and moisture availability, log-averaged roughness lengths, and as skin temperature either the area-weighted mean (temperature = :mean, what a single-tile scheme with averaged heat capacity would carry) or the effective radiative temperature (:radiative, which gets the emitted longwave right).

Where the fluxes are linear in the parameters that differ between tiles, turbulent_fluxes(parameter_aggregate(ts), f) equals the flux-aggregated turbulent_fluxes(ts, f); where they are not — stability, roughness, saturation humidity — the difference is the aggregation error that tiling removes (Avissar & Pielke 1989; Mahrt 1987).

Aeolus.SurfaceTiles.tiles_from_class_map — Method
tiles_from_class_map(class_map, block, make_tile; nclass = maximum(class_map))
    -> Matrix{SurfaceMosaic}

Coarse-grain a 2-D surface class map (such as the urban street/roof/park/water/wall map of examples/urban) into tiled cells. block = (bx, by) fine cells make one coarse cell; the map size must be divisible by it. In each coarse cell there is one tile per class that occurs, built by make_tile(class) (a fresh AbstractSurface each call), with the class's area fraction.

The per-cell class map of the urban example is the limit block = (1, 1), one pure tile per cell.
