Photosynthesis — Farquhar C3 / Collatz C4

Photosynthesis is the leaf biochemistry (Phase 47, #19): given the intercellular CO₂ ci, the photon flux a leaf absorbs and its temperature, how fast does it fix carbon? It implements the Farquhar, von Caemmerer & Berry (1980) model for C3 leaves and Collatz et al. (1992) for C4 leaves, in the form used by CLM4.5/CLM5, with the Bernacchi et al. (2001) Rubisco kinetics and either the non-acclimating Bonan et al. (2011) or the acclimating Kattge & Knorr (2007) temperature responses. The theory is in Leaf photosynthesis.

It is the single owner in Aeolus of:

  • the temperature responses (ArrheniusResponse, PeakedArrheniusResponse, Q10Response, temperature_factor);
  • the PAR → photon-flux conversion (PAR_PHOTONS_PER_JOULE, par_to_photon_flux);
  • the smooth co-limitation colimited_rate;
  • the CLM4.5 per-PFT photosynthetic table (CLM45_PFT_PHOTOSYNTHESIS).

Stomatal conductance (#20), the vegetated land column (#21) and the canopy leaf energy balance (#24) delegate here rather than restating any of these. Leaf-level photosynthesis is not itself a surface, so it does not implement AbstractSurface; the land column that uses it will.

Units: CO₂ in µmol mol⁻¹, O₂ in mmol mol⁻¹, rates in µmol m⁻² s⁻¹, absorbed photon flux in µmol m⁻² s⁻¹, temperature in K. Mole fractions make the CLM equations (written in Pa) pressure-independent.

using Aeolus
p  = pft_photosynthesis_parameters(:c3_grass)          # Vcmax25 = 78.2
Q  = 0.85 * par_to_photon_flux(400.0)                  # absorbed, from 400 W m⁻² PAR
r  = leaf_assimilation(p, 280.0, Q, 298.15)            # ci = 280 µmol/mol, 25 °C
r.An, r.limitation                                     # net µmol m⁻² s⁻¹, :rubisco/:light/:export
co2_compensation_point(p, Q, 298.15)                   # Γ ≈ 54 µmol/mol

Stomatal conductance and leaf gas exchange (#20)

The same module couples the biochemistry to stomata: Ball, Woodrow & Berry (1987) (BallBerryStomata, CLM4.5) and Medlyn et al. (2011) (MedlynStomata, CLM5) closures, solved exactly for the leaf-surface humidity; the leaf boundary layer (leaf_boundary_layer_conductance, CLM5 eq. 2.5.122); transpiration (leaf_transpiration); and the coupled $A_n$–$g_s$–$c_i$ solution (leaf_gas_exchange). Soil-water stress enters as a factor β through apply_water_stress; computing β from the soil column is #21. Per-PFT parameters: pft_stomatal_model, with the CLM5 Medlyn slopes in CLM5_MEDLYN_G1. Theory: Stomatal conductance.

It is the single owner of the stomatal closures, the CO₂/H₂O diffusivity ratios (STOMATAL_DIFFUSIVITY_RATIO = 1.6, BOUNDARY_LAYER_DIFFUSIVITY_RATIO = 1.4) and the leaf boundary-layer conductance; the land column (#21) and the canopy leaf energy balance (#24) delegate here. Leaf temperature is an input.

using Aeolus
using Aeolus.MoistThermodynamics: saturation_vapor_pressure
p  = pft_photosynthesis_parameters(:bdt_temperate)
m  = pft_stomatal_model(:bdt_temperate)                 # Medlyn, g1 = 4.45 kPa^½
T  = 298.15
gb = leaf_boundary_layer_conductance(1.0, T, 101325.0)  # U = 1 m/s → 2.04 mol m⁻² s⁻¹
r  = leaf_gas_exchange(p, m; ca = 400.0, Q = 0.85 * par_to_photon_flux(400.0),
                       T_leaf = T, e_air = saturation_vapor_pressure(T) - 1500.0, gb = gb)
r.An, r.ci / 400, r.gs, r.LE     # ≈ 12.7 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹, 0.78, 0.25 mol m⁻² s⁻¹, 146 W m⁻²
leaf_gas_exchange(p, m; ca = 400.0, Q = 800.0, T_leaf = T, e_air = 1500.0, β = 0.5)  # stressed
Aeolus.Photosynthesis — Module
Photosynthesis

Leaf-level biochemical photosynthesis (Phase 47): the Farquhar, von Caemmerer & Berry (1980) model for C3 leaves and the Collatz, Ribas-Carbo & Berry (1992) model for C4 leaves, in the form used by the Community Land Model (CLM4.5 / CLM5, Bonan et al. 2011).

It answers one question — given the CO₂ inside a leaf, the light it absorbs and its temperature, how fast does it fix carbon? — and is the single owner in Aeolus of that biochemistry, of its temperature responses, of the PAR → photon-flux conversion and of the per-PFT photosynthetic parameter table.

It also couples that biochemistry to the stomata (#20): the Ball-Berry (1987) and Medlyn et al. (2011) conductance closures, the leaf boundary layer, transpiration and the coupled $A_n$–$g_s$–$c_i$ solution leaf_gas_exchange, with soil-water stress as an input factor β. The vegetated land column (#21) and the leaf energy balance of the resolved canopy (#24) delegate here.

Units

QuantityUnit

CO₂ ci, Kc, Γ*µmol mol⁻¹ (mole fraction)

CLM writes the same equations in partial pressures, with every Michaelis constant and compensation point scaled by $P_{atm}$ (Kc25 = 404.9e-6 Patm, …). The Farquhar rates are homogeneous of degree zero in those quantities, so working in mole fractions is exactly equivalent and removes the pressure argument.

Aeolus.Photosynthesis.ArrheniusResponse — Type
ArrheniusResponse(ΔHa)

Plain Arrhenius scaling normalised at 25 °C (CLM4.5 eq. 8.10):

Aeolus.Photosynthesis.BallBerryStomata — Type
BallBerryStomata(; g1 = 9.0, g0 = 0.01)

Ball, Woodrow & Berry (1987) stomatal conductance to water vapour, in the form of Collatz et al. (1991) and CLM4.5 (Oleson et al. 2013, eq. 8.1):

\[g_s = g_0 + g_1 \frac{A_n\, h_s}{c_s}\qquad (A_n > 0),\qquad g_s = g_0 \quad (A_n ≤ 0),\]

with $h_s = e_s / e_{sat}(T_\ell)$ the relative humidity at the leaf surface, $c_s$ the leaf-surface CO₂ (µmol mol⁻¹), $A_n$ in µmol m⁻² s⁻¹. g1 (CLM's m) is dimensionless; g0 (CLM's b) is in mol m⁻² s⁻¹. Defaults are CLM's C3 values (m = 9, b = 10000 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹); C4 uses g1 = 4, g0 = 0.04 — see pft_stomatal_model. As in CLM the air vapour pressure entering $h_s$ is capped at saturation.

Aeolus.Photosynthesis.MedlynStomata — Type
MedlynStomata(; g1 = 4.45, g0 = 1e-4, D_min = 50.0)

Medlyn et al. (2011) optimal stomatal conductance to water vapour, as in CLM5 (Lawrence et al. 2018, eq. 2.9.1):

\[g_s = g_0 + 1.6\left(1 + \frac{g_1}{\sqrt{D_s}}\right)\frac{A_n}{c_s} \qquad (A_n > 0),\qquad g_s = g_0 \quad (A_n ≤ 0),\]

with $D_s$ the vapour-pressure deficit at the leaf surface in kPa, g1 in kPa^½ and g0 in mol m⁻² s⁻¹ (CLM5: 100 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹). The leaf-surface deficit is bounded below by D_min (Pa), so the closure stays finite in saturated air. CLM5 applies its 50 Pa bound to the air deficit $e_{sat}(T_\ell) - e_a$ instead; that leaves the leaf-surface deficit unbounded below for a weakly coupled leaf, where opening stomata humidifies the leaf surface and opens them further — at 5 °C, 98 % RH and $g_b = 0.1$ mol m⁻² s⁻¹ it gives $g_s ≈ 10^3$ mol m⁻² s⁻¹. Here the deficit entering the closure is $\max(D_s, D_{min})$, which caps $g_s$ at $g_0 + 1.6(1 + g_1/\sqrt{D_{min}})A_n/c_s$. Whenever $D_s ≥ D_{min}$ the two agree exactly. For $g_0 = 0$ and a perfectly coupled leaf it gives the optimal ratio $c_i/c_a = g_1/(g_1 + \sqrt{D})$ independent of $A_n$. The default g1 is CLM5's broadleaf-deciduous-tree value; per-PFT values are in CLM5_MEDLYN_G1.

Aeolus.Photosynthesis.PFTPhotosynthesis — Type
PFTPhotosynthesis

Photosynthetic traits of one plant functional type, transcribed from CLM4.5 Technical Note Table 8.1 (Oleson et al. 2013):

  • name — CLM's PFT label;
  • pathway — :C3 or :C4;
  • Vcmax25 — canopy-top Rubisco capacity at 25 °C, µmol m⁻² s⁻¹;
  • CN_L — leaf carbon : nitrogen ratio, g C g⁻¹ N;
  • FLNR — fraction of leaf N in Rubisco, g N g⁻¹ N;
  • SLA0 — canopy-top specific leaf area, m² g⁻¹ C;
  • α — C4 quantum efficiency, mol CO₂ mol⁻¹ photons (NaN for C3).

The tabulated Vcmax25 is derived from the other three columns by vcmax25_from_leaf_nitrogen (CLM4.5 eq. 8.17). The stomatal slope m of that table is carried by pft_stomatal_model; the soil-water (ψo, ψc) columns belong to the land column (#21).

Aeolus.Photosynthesis.PeakedArrheniusResponse — Type
PeakedArrheniusResponse(ΔHa, ΔHd, ΔS)

Arrhenius activation multiplied by high-temperature deactivation (Johnson-Eyring-Williams / Medlyn et al. 2002 eq. 18; CLM4.5 eqs. 8.10-8.11), normalised at 25 °C:

\[f(T) = \exp\left[\frac{ΔH_a}{R T_{25}}\left(1-\frac{T_{25}}{T}\right)\right] \frac{1 + \exp\left(\frac{T_{25} ΔS - ΔH_d}{R T_{25}}\right)} {1 + \exp\left(\frac{T ΔS - ΔH_d}{R T}\right)}\]

ΔHa, ΔHd in J mol⁻¹, ΔS in J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹. It has a single maximum at optimum_temperature.

Aeolus.Photosynthesis.Q10Response — Type
Q10Response(; Q10 = 2.0, s_high = 0.0, T_high = Inf, s_low = 0.0, T_low = -Inf)

The Collatz et al. (1992) form used for C4 leaves (CLM4.5 eqs. 8.12-8.14):

\[f(T) = \frac{Q_{10}^{(T - T_{25})/10}} {\left[1 + e^{s_h (T - T_h)}\right]\left[1 + e^{s_l (T_l - T)}\right]}\]

The inhibition terms are not renormalised at 25 °C, exactly as in CLM (for CLM's C4 Vcmax they reduce $f(T_{25})$ to 0.871). A term is omitted when its slope is zero.

Aeolus.Photosynthesis.apply_water_stress — Function
apply_water_stress(p::AbstractPhotosynthesisParameters, β; targets = (:vcmax, :rd, :g0))
apply_water_stress(m::AbstractStomatalModel, β; targets = (:vcmax, :rd, :g0))

The soil-water-stress hook. β ∈ [0, 1] is the plant wetness factor (CLM's btran); it is an input here — the land column (#21) computes it from soil matric potential. targets says what it multiplies:

  • :vcmax — the Rubisco capacity Vcmax25 (CLM4.5 eq. 8.10; vcmax_z = vcmax_z * btran in CLM5's non-hydraulic path);
  • :rd — day respiration Rd25 (CLM, lmr_z = lmr_z * btran);
  • :g0 — the stomatal intercept (CLM's Ball-Berry path, bbb = max(bbbopt*btran, 1 µmol));
  • :g1 — the stomatal slope (the alternative of e.g. Zhou et al. 2013 and De Kauwe et al. 2015, not CLM's default).

For BallBerryStomata the default reproduces CLM4.5 and CLM5's non-hydraulic Ball-Berry path. CLM5's Medlyn closure does not stress its intercept: ci_func_PHS in PhotosynthesisMod.F90 (release-clm5.0) uses the unstressed medlynintercept, and water stress enters through plant hydraulics instead. Scaling a MedlynStomata intercept by β is therefore an Aeolus choice, carried over from Ball-Berry. Its effect is small, because the CLM5 intercept is only 10⁻⁴ mol m⁻² s⁻¹. Pass targets = (:vcmax, :rd) to leave the Medlyn intercept unstressed. A parameter set is returned unchanged for targets that do not apply to it. The stomatal intercept is always bounded below by 1 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹ (CLM's floor), which keeps the coupled system well posed in the dark even for g0 = 0.

Aeolus.Photosynthesis.co2_compensation_point — Method

Aeolus.Photosynthesis.colimited_rate — Method
colimited_rate(a, b, Θ) -> x

Smaller root of $Θ x^2 - (a + b) x + a b = 0$ for non-negative a, b and curvature $0 ≤ Θ ≤ 1$: a smooth minimum of two limiting rates (Collatz et al. 1991). $Θ = 1$ gives $\min(a, b)$; $Θ = 0$ the rectangular hyperbola $ab/(a+b)$; for $Θ < 1$ the result is always below the minimum. Evaluated in the cancellation-free form $2ab / \left[(a+b) + \sqrt{(a+b)^2 - 4Θab}\right]$. It is homogeneous of degree one, $x(ca, cb) = c\,x(a, b)$.

Aeolus.Photosynthesis.leaf_assimilation — Method
leaf_assimilation(p, ci, Q, T; oi = 209.0, colimitation = true) -> LeafAssimilation

Leaf photosynthesis at intercellular CO₂ ci (µmol mol⁻¹), absorbed photon flux Q (µmol m⁻² s⁻¹; see par_to_photon_flux) and leaf temperature T (K).

C3 (Farquhar et al. 1980; CLM4.5 eqs. 8.3-8.8):

\[A_c = V_{cmax}\frac{c_i - Γ^*}{c_i + K_c(1 + o_i/K_o)},\quad A_j = J\frac{c_i - Γ^*}{4 c_i + 8 Γ^*},\quad A_p = 3 T_p ,\]

with J from electron_transport_rate. C4 (Collatz et al. 1992): $A_c = V_{cmax}$, $A_j = α Q$, $A_p = k_p c_i$.

With colimitation = true the gross rate is $A = \mathrm{colim}(\mathrm{colim}(A_c, A_j; Θ_{cj}), A_p; Θ_{ip})$ (colimited_rate) as in CLM; with false, the plain Farquhar minimum. Net assimilation is $A_n = A - R_d$.

Below the compensation point ($c_i < Γ^*$) the C3 gross rate is negative — photorespiration releases more CO₂ than Rubisco fixes. It is evaluated as $(c_i - Γ^*)\,\mathrm{colim}(V_{cmax}/(c_i + K_m), J/(4c_i + 8Γ^*))$, which is identical to CLM's form for $c_i ≥ Γ^*$ (the co-limitation is homogeneous of degree one) and continues it correctly below, where CLM clips; TPU cannot limit a negative flux.

Aeolus.Photosynthesis.leaf_boundary_layer_conductance — Method
leaf_boundary_layer_conductance(U, T_air, p_air; d_leaf = 0.04, Cv = 0.01,
                                c = DEFAULT_CONSTANTS) -> mol m⁻² s⁻¹

Leaf boundary-layer conductance to heat and water vapour for wind speed U (m s⁻¹) past a leaf of characteristic dimension d_leaf (m), after CLM5 eq. 2.5.122 (Oleson et al. 2013 eq. 5.122):

\[g_b = C_v \sqrt{U / d_{leaf}}\ \ [\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}], \qquad C_v = 0.01\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1/2}},\]

converted to molar units by molar_conductance at the air temperature and pressure. d_leaf = 0.04 m is CLM5 Table 2.5.1 for every PFT. The $\sqrt{U/d}$ scaling is laminar forced convection over a flat plate; $C_v$ absorbs the two leaf sides and outdoor turbulence. The CO₂ conductance is this divided by BOUNDARY_LAYER_DIFFUSIVITY_RATIO.

Aeolus.Photosynthesis.leaf_gas_exchange — Method
leaf_gas_exchange(p, model; ca, Q, T_leaf, e_air, gb = Inf, p_air = 101325.0,
                  β = 1.0, stress_targets = (:vcmax, :rd, :g0),
                  oi = nothing, colimitation = true,
                  c = DEFAULT_CONSTANTS, maxiter = 200) -> LeafGasExchange

Leaf photosynthesis, stomatal conductance and transpiration solved together. Inputs: photosynthetic parameters p (C3Parameters / C4Parameters), a stomatal model (BallBerryStomata / MedlynStomata), ambient CO₂ ca (µmol mol⁻¹), absorbed photon flux Q (µmol m⁻² s⁻¹), leaf temperature T_leaf (K), vapour pressure of the surrounding air e_air (Pa), leaf boundary-layer conductance to water vapour gb (mol m⁻² s⁻¹; see leaf_boundary_layer_conductance; Inf = perfectly coupled) and air pressure p_air (Pa, used only for transpiration). β is the soil-water-stress factor applied by apply_water_stress; oi (mmol mol⁻¹) overrides the C3 O₂ mole fraction.

Four equations close the system (CLM5 §2.9.6, eqs. 2.9.19-2.9.27): the biochemistry $A_n = f(c_i)$ (leaf_assimilation); diffusion through the boundary layer, $c_s = c_a - 1.4 A_n / g_b$; the stomatal closure $g_s(A_n, c_s, e_s)$ (stomatal_conductance, exact in the leaf-surface humidity); and diffusion through the pore, $c_i = c_s - 1.6 A_n / g_s$. They reduce to one scalar equation $R(c_i) = c_i - [c_a - A_n(c_i)(1.4/g_b + 1.6/g_s)] = 0$, solved on a bracket by the Illinois method of SurfaceExchange, like CLM5's hybrid secant/Brent solver (Sun et al. 2012), which replaced CLM4's non-convergent fixed-point iteration. $R(0) < 0$ always (no gross uptake at $c_i = 0$); the upper end is $c_a$, or, when respiration exceeds uptake there (darkness), the $c_i > c_a$ it implies. Typically 5-20 iterations; near the compensation point, where $g_s$ drops from the closure value to the intercept as $A_n$ changes sign, $R$ is steep and up to ~100 may be needed (hence maxiter = 200); converged reports the outcome.

The leaf temperature is an input: the leaf energy balance that sets it belongs to the land column (#21) and the resolved canopy (#24).

Aeolus.Photosynthesis.leaf_transpiration — Method
leaf_transpiration(gs, gb, T_leaf, e_air, p_air; c = DEFAULT_CONSTANTS) -> mol H₂O m⁻² s⁻¹

Leaf transpiration through stomata and boundary layer in series,

\[E = \frac{e_{sat}(T_\ell) - e_a}{p}\,\frac{g_s g_b}{g_s + g_b}\]

(CLM5 eq. 2.9.20; the leaf interior is saturated at leaf temperature). e_sat is MoistThermodynamics.saturation_vapor_pressure. Multiply by c.molmass_water for kg m⁻² s⁻¹. The small mass-flow ("ternary") correction of von Caemmerer & Farquhar (1981) is neglected, as in CLM. Negative E means the air is supersaturated with respect to the leaf; dew on the leaf surface is the business of the land column (#21).

Aeolus.Photosynthesis.stomatal_conductance — Function
stomatal_conductance(model, An, cs, e_air, e_sat_leaf, gb) -> gs (mol H₂O m⁻² s⁻¹)

Stomatal conductance at net assimilation An (µmol m⁻² s⁻¹) and leaf-surface CO₂ cs (µmol mol⁻¹), given the vapour pressure of the air e_air and the saturation vapour pressure at leaf temperature e_sat_leaf (Pa) and the leaf boundary-layer conductance to water vapour gb (mol m⁻² s⁻¹; Inf for a perfectly coupled leaf).

The leaf-surface humidity is not an input: with no storage at the surface, the vapour flux through the boundary layer equals the one through the stomata, $e_s = (g_b e_a + g_s e_{sat}) / (g_b + g_s)$ (CLM5 eq. 2.9.23), so $h_s$ and $D_s$ depend on $g_s$ itself. Both closures are solved exactly for that dependence:

  • Ball-Berry: a quadratic in $g_s$ — the one CLM4.5 solves (PhotosynthesisMod ci_func) — whose positive root is taken in a cancellation-free form;
  • Medlyn: since $D_s = g_b D_a / (g_b + g_s)$, the closure is a quadratic in $x = \sqrt{g_b + g_s}$ with exactly one positive root. Squaring it gives CLM5's quadratic (eqs. 2.9.24-2.9.26), whose larger root is the same $g_s$.
