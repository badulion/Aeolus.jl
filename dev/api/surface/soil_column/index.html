Soil column — SoilColumn

Phase 46: a CLM-style soil column with Richards-equation water transport (#15) and heat conduction with freeze–thaw (#16). Theory, numerics and validation are on the soil water and soil heat and freeze–thaw pages.

  • Layers: clm5_soil_grid (CLM5, 20 layers to 8.6 m), clm_exponential_soil_grid, uniform_soil_grid.

  • Hydraulics: ClappHornberger, VanGenuchtenMualem (with the Vogel/Ippisch air-entry option), GardnerExponential; texture tables CLAPP_HORNBERGER_1978, CARSEL_PARRISH_1988; cosby_clapp_hornberger.

  • Temperature and ice: water_dynamic_viscosity, viscosity_conductivity_factor, the AbstractIceImpedance hook.

  • Solver: SoilWaterColumn, step_soil_water! (modified Picard, exact water conservation), boundary conditions AtmosphericTop, HeadTop, FreeDrainage, ZeroFluxBottom, HeadBottom.

  • Heat (#16): SoilThermalProperties (clm_soil_thermal_properties, johansen_soil_thermal_properties), soil_thermal_conductivity, soil_heat_capacity; freezing curve freezing_matric_potential, supercooled_liquid_content; PowerLawIceImpedance.

  • Thermal column (#16): SoilThermalColumn wraps a SoilWaterColumn and is an AbstractSurface; step_soil_heat! (enthalpy method, exact energy conservation) under GroundHeatFluxTop, SkinTemperatureTop or LinearizedSkinBalance, with ZeroHeatFluxBottom, GeothermalHeatFlux or BottomTemperature; step_surface! couples skin energy balance, heat and water.

Boundary types and accessors are not re-exported from Aeolus; use using Aeolus.SoilColumn or qualify them.

using Aeolus, Aeolus.SoilColumn

col = SoilWaterColumn(clm5_soil_grid(), carsel_parrish_texture(:loam); ψ = -2.0)
for hour in 1:24
    st = step_soil_water!(col, 3600.0; water_input = hour < 4 ? 1e-5 : 0.0,
                          evaporation = 5e-8)
end
col.budget          # infiltration, runoff, evaporation, drainage, extraction

With heat and freeze–thaw, driven through the surface shared core (SurfaceExchange):

using Aeolus, Aeolus.SoilColumn
using Aeolus.SurfaceExchange: SurfaceForcing

water = SoilWaterColumn(clm5_soil_grid(), carsel_parrish_texture(:loam);
                        ψ = -1.0, ice_impedance = PowerLawIceImpedance())
soil = SoilThermalColumn(water, clm_soil_thermal_properties(40, 20); T_soil = 276.15,
                         bottom = GeothermalHeatFlux(0.065))
night = SurfaceForcing(sw_down = 0.0, lw_down = 220.0, T_air = 263.15, q_air = 0.001, U = 3.0)
fluxes = step_surface!(soil, night, 1800.0)   # Rn = H + LE + G, G conducted into the soil
soil.T_skin, water.T_soil, water.θ_ice, soil.budget

Extending the column

  • Freeze–thaw (#16): SoilThermalColumn fills col.θ_ice and col.T_soil of the water column it wraps; a new AbstractIceImpedance subtype implements ice_impedance_factor. A canopy (#21) drives the ground below it with step_soil_heat!(soil, dt, LinearizedSkinBalance(G0, dG_dT, T0)).
  • Water table (#17) is implemented as UnconfinedAquifer, an AbstractSoilBottomBoundary with its own state (see below). Another stateful boundary does the same: it implements bottom_flux_linearization (the five-argument form receives the sub-step dt) and finalize_bottom!, and optionally bottom_water_storage.

Water table (#17)

UnconfinedAquifer is the SIMTOP groundwater model (Niu et al. 2005, 2007; CLM4 §7.5). It provides a prognostic aquifer storage and water table, recharge as the exact exponential-K two-point flux (gardner_two_point_flux), and baseflow $q_{max} e^{-f z_∇}$ with the frozen-soil impermeable fraction (aquifer_impermeable_fraction). The diagnostics are water_table_depth, aquifer_baseflow_rate and total_water_storage, and set_water_table! starts the column in hydrostatic equilibrium. Theory and validation are on the water table page.

col = SoilWaterColumn(clm5_soil_grid(), carsel_parrish_texture(:silt_loam);
                      bottom = UnconfinedAquifer())
set_water_table!(col, 5.0)
step_soil_water!(col, 86400.0; water_input = 5e-7)
water_table_depth(col), col.bottom.baseflow, total_water_storage(col)
Aeolus.SoilColumn — Module
SoilColumn

Multi-layer soil column (Phase 46) — CLM-style vertical discretisation with Richards-equation water transport, heat conduction and freeze–thaw.

Contents:

  1. Layer structure — SoilGrid with the CLM5 layer thicknesses (clm5_soil_grid, Lawrence et al. 2019), the CLM2–4.5 exponential nodes (clm_exponential_soil_grid) and a uniform grid for benchmarks.
  2. Hydraulic functions — AbstractHydraulicModel with Clapp–Hornberger (1978), van Genuchten (1980)–Mualem (1976) and Gardner (1958) exponential soils; texture tables of Clapp & Hornberger (1978) and Carsel & Parrish (1988), and the Cosby et al. (1984) pedotransfer functions.
  3. Temperature and ice — hydraulic conductivity scales with the fluidity of water, $K ∝ 1/μ(T)$, with $μ(T)$ from Kestin et al. (1978); an AbstractIceImpedance hook multiplies $K$ by an ice-impedance factor that the freeze–thaw model (#16) supplies.
  4. Richards solver — SoilWaterColumn and step_soil_water!: the mixed form of Richards' equation, backward Euler in time, finite volumes in depth, linearised by the modified Picard iteration of Celia et al. (1990). The water content is advanced by the divergence of the converged interface fluxes, so the column conserves water to round-off. The top boundary is an atmospheric flux that switches to a head condition when the surface ponds (runoff) or dries out (soil-limited evaporation); the bottom boundary is an AbstractSoilBottomBoundary (free drainage by default; UnconfinedAquifer is the water table of #17).
  5. Heat and freeze–thaw (#16) — SoilThermalProperties with the Johansen (1975) / Farouki (1981) conductivity and a constituent heat capacity; SoilThermalColumn, which wraps a water column and solves the heat equation in enthalpy form with latent heat of fusion, a sharp or Clapeyron (supercooled-water) freezing curve and the CLM power-law ice impedance (PowerLawIceImpedance); and a skin energy balance that makes the column an AbstractSurface (step_surface!).
  6. Water table (#17) — UnconfinedAquifer, a prognostic unconfined aquifer below the column (SIMTOP; Niu et al. 2005, 2007, as in CLM4): recharge, water-table dynamics and topographic baseflow $q_{max} e^{-f z_∇}$, conserving soil + aquifer water exactly.

Depth z is positive downward from the soil surface; matric potential ψ is a pressure head in metres of water (negative when unsaturated); water fluxes are in m s⁻¹ of liquid water, positive downward inside the soil.

Aeolus.SoilColumn.SOIL_YEAR — Constant

The length of the annual cycle of annual_soil_temperature (s): 365.25 d.

Aeolus.SoilColumn.AbstractHydraulicModel — Type
AbstractHydraulicModel

Water-retention and hydraulic-conductivity functions of one soil. All potentials are pressure heads in metres of water (negative unsaturated), conductivities in m s⁻¹, water contents volumetric (m³ m⁻³). A concrete model implements

soil_water_content(h, ψ)          # θ(ψ), the retention curve
soil_matric_potential(h, θ)       # its inverse, ψ(θ)
soil_hydraulic_conductivity(h, ψ) # K(ψ) at the reference temperature
soil_water_capacity(h, ψ)         # C = dθ/dψ
soil_porosity(h), residual_water_content(h),
air_entry_potential(h), saturated_conductivity(h)

For $ψ ≥$ air_entry_potential(h) the soil is saturated: $θ = θ_s$, $K = K_s$, $C = 0$.

Aeolus.SoilColumn.AbstractIceImpedance — Type
AbstractIceImpedance

How pore ice reduces the hydraulic conductivity of a layer. A subtype implements ice_impedance_factor(imp, θ_ice, θ_s) ∈ (0, 1], the factor multiplying $K$. The default NoIceImpedance returns 1; the freeze–thaw model (issue #16) adds e.g. the CLM power-law impedance $10^{-Ω F_{ice}}$ (Swenson et al. 2012).

Aeolus.SoilColumn.AbstractSoilBottomBoundary — Type
AbstractSoilBottomBoundary

Lower boundary condition of a SoilWaterColumn. A subtype implements

bottom_flux_linearization(bc, col, ψ_N, K_N) -> (a, b)

giving the downward flux out of the column's base as $q = a + b\,ψ_N$ in terms of the bottom node potential $ψ_N$ ($b ≥ 0$; K_N is the conductivity of the bottom layer at the current Picard iterate), and may implement finalize_bottom!(bc, col, q, dt), called with the converged flux after every accepted sub-step — the hook through which a water table / aquifer (#17) updates its own storage. Provided: FreeDrainage (default), ZeroFluxBottom, HeadBottom.

Aeolus.SoilColumn.AbstractSoilHeatBottom — Type
AbstractSoilHeatBottom

Lower boundary of the heat equation: the heat flux leaving the column downward through its base is $F = α + β T_N$ ($β ≥ 0$). ZeroHeatFluxBottom (CLM's default), GeothermalHeatFlux, BottomTemperature.

Aeolus.SoilColumn.AbstractSoilHeatTop — Type
AbstractSoilHeatTop

Upper boundary of the heat equation, given to step_soil_heat!. Every subtype is reduced to a ground heat flux linear in the top-node temperature, $G = α + β T_1$ ($β ≤ 0$), and a skin temperature $T_s(T_1)$: GroundHeatFluxTop, SkinTemperatureTop, LinearizedSkinBalance.

Aeolus.SoilColumn.AbstractSoilTopBoundary — Type
AbstractSoilTopBoundary

Upper boundary condition of a SoilWaterColumn: AtmosphericTop (the land-surface condition) or HeadTop (a prescribed surface potential, for benchmarks and ponded surfaces).

Aeolus.SoilColumn.AtmosphericTop — Type
AtmosphericTop(; ψ_pond = 0, ψ_dry = -1e5)

The land-surface boundary: the net water supply $q = P - E$ (water input minus evaporation) is imposed as a flux as long as the soil can take or give it. If the surface would have to exceed ψ_pond (m) to absorb the supply, the condition switches to the head $ψ = ψ_pond$ and the excess becomes infiltration-excess (Hortonian) runoff; if it would have to fall below ψ_dry to deliver the evaporative demand, it switches to $ψ = ψ_dry$ and evaporation becomes soil-limited — the atmospheric boundary condition of e.g. HYDRUS (Šimůnek et al. 2008). The default ψ_dry is CLM's minimum soil potential (−10⁸ mm).

Aeolus.SoilColumn.BottomTemperature — Type

Prescribed temperature T (K) at the base interface of the column.

Aeolus.SoilColumn.ClappHornberger — Type
ClappHornberger(; θ_s, ψ_s, b, K_s)

Clapp & Hornberger (1978) soil, the Campbell (1974) power laws used by CLM:

ψ = ψ_s (θ/θ_s)^{-b},     K = K_s (θ/θ_s)^{2b+3}

θ_s porosity, ψ_s < 0 the saturated (air-entry) matric potential (m), b the pore-size index, K_s (m s⁻¹). No residual water content.

Aeolus.SoilColumn.FreeDrainage — Type

Unit-gradient gravity drainage, $q = K(ψ_N)$ — CLM's default lower boundary.

Aeolus.SoilColumn.GardnerExponential — Type
GardnerExponential(; θ_r, θ_s, α, K_s)

Gardner (1958) exponential soil with the retention curve of the same form (Philip 1969; Srivastava & Yeh 1991):

K = K_s e^{αψ},   θ = θ_r + (θ_s - θ_r) e^{αψ}     (ψ ≤ 0)

Because θ is linear in K, Richards' equation becomes linear in K and has closed-form solutions — which is why this soil is used for the exact benchmarks. Its diffusivity $D = K_s/(α(θ_s - θ_r))$ is constant.

Aeolus.SoilColumn.GeothermalHeatFlux — Type
GeothermalHeatFlux(q)

Heat flux q (W m⁻²) entering the column from below (upward positive; the continental mean is about 0.065 W m⁻²: Pollack, Hurter & Johnson 1993, Rev. Geophys., 31, 267-280).

Aeolus.SoilColumn.GroundHeatFluxTop — Type

Prescribed ground heat flux G (W m⁻², positive into the soil).

Aeolus.SoilColumn.HeadBottom — Type
HeadBottom(ψ)

Prescribed potential ψ (m) at the base of the column (e.g. $ψ = 0$: a fixed water table at the bottom interface).

Aeolus.SoilColumn.HeadTop — Type
HeadTop(ψ)

Prescribed matric potential ψ (m) at the soil surface (depth 0), e.g. $ψ = 0$ for a ponded surface with negligible water depth. The water input and evaporation passed to step_soil_water! are ignored; the infiltration is whatever the soil takes up.

Aeolus.SoilColumn.LinearizedSkinBalance — Type
LinearizedSkinBalance(G0, dG_dT, T0)

A massless skin whose atmospheric energy supply $R_n - H - LE$ is linearised about the skin temperature T0: $G_{atm}(T_s) = G_0 + (dG/dT_s)(T_s - T_0)$ with $dG/dT_s ≤ 0$ (radiative, sensible and latent damping). The skin temperature closes $G_{atm}(T_s) = (k_1/z_1)(T_s - T_1)$ implicitly with the soil — the coupling step_surface! uses, and the one a canopy model (#21) passes for the ground below its leaves.

Aeolus.SoilColumn.NoIceImpedance — Type

No reduction of conductivity by ice (factor 1) — the default until soil freezing is modelled.

Aeolus.SoilColumn.PowerLawIceImpedance — Type
PowerLawIceImpedance(; Ω = 6)

Ice impedance of CLM4.5/CLM5 (Swenson et al. 2012): hydraulic conductivity is multiplied by $10^{-Ω F_{ice}}$ with the ice fraction of the pore space $F_{ice} = θ_{ice}/θ_s$ (capped at 1). $Ω = 6$: a layer whose pores are half ice conducts 1000 times less.

Aeolus.SoilColumn.SkinTemperatureTop — Type

Prescribed skin (soil-surface) temperature T (K) at depth 0.

Aeolus.SoilColumn.SoilGrid — Type
SoilGrid(zh; z = midpoints)

Vertical structure of a soil column: N layers bounded by the N + 1 interface depths zh (m, positive downward, zh[1] = 0 the surface), with node depths z (default: layer midpoints) where the prognostic variables live. Fields z, zh, dz (layer thicknesses).

Aeolus.SoilColumn.SoilHeatBudget — Type
SoilHeatBudget

Cumulative energy budget of a SoilThermalColumn (J m⁻²): surface (ground heat flux into the top, ∫G dt), bottom (out of the base), water (enthalpy moved by water redistribution, infiltration and evaporation, whose water carries the enthalpy of the layer it leaves or joins at that layer's temperature), latent_mismatch (latent heat of evaporation the surface balance charged but the soil could not deliver; since #129 step_surface! returns the delivered evaporation and puts this heat into the top layer, so it is also part of surface), and time (s). $Δ(\text{enthalpy}) = surface - bottom + water$.

Aeolus.SoilColumn.SoilHeatStep — Type
SoilHeatStep

What one step_soil_heat! did: time-mean ground heat flux G and basal flux bottom_flux (W m⁻²), the final and time-mean skin temperature T_skin, T_skin_mean (K), enthalpy_change (J m⁻²), freezing (kg m⁻² of water frozen, negative = melt), substeps and Newton iterations.

Aeolus.SoilColumn.SoilThermalColumn — Type
SoilThermalColumn(water::SoilWaterColumn, thermal; T_soil = 283.15, T_skin = T_soil[1],
                  bottom = ZeroHeatFluxBottom(), freezing_point_depression = true,
                  unfrozen_fraction = 1e-3, albedo = 0.2,
                  emissivity = material_emissivity(:soil_dry),
                  z0m = 0.01, z0h = z0m / 10, z0q = z0h, hydrology = true,
                  tol_T = 1e-6, tol_H = 1.0, maxiter = 50, dt_min = 1e-3)

A multi-layer soil with heat conduction, freeze–thaw and (through the wrapped SoilWaterColumn) Richards-equation water transport — an AbstractSurface with a skin energy balance. thermal is one SoilThermalProperties or one per layer; T_soil the initial layer temperatures (scalar or vector, K); the initial water of water is partitioned into liquid and ice in equilibrium at T.

Prognostic: enthalpy

Each layer carries its volumetric enthalpy (J m⁻³), relative to liquid water at the freezing point $T_f$,

H = C(θ_liq, θ_ice) (T - T_f) - ρ_ice L_f θ_ice

with soil_heat_capacity $C$. Given H and the layer's total water $W = θ_{liq} + (ρ_{ice}/ρ_w)θ_{ice}$, temperature and phase follow from equilibrium:

  • freezing_point_depression = true (default): liquid water coexists with ice below $T_f$ up to supercooled_liquid_content (Clapeyron + retention curve), so freezing starts at the freezing_onset_temperature of the layer's matric potential and proceeds over a temperature range;
  • false: a sharp phase change at $T_f$ — the Stefan problem.

In both cases a fraction unfrozen_fraction of the pore space above the residual content stays liquid, so the water solver always has liquid to work with.

The state is shared with water: water.T_soil, water.θ_liq, water.θ_ice are this column's temperature and phases, and give the water solver its viscosity and ice-impedance factors (pass ice_impedance = PowerLawIceImpedance() to the water column).

Surface

albedo, emissivity (default the dry-soil value of SurfaceOptics) and roughness lengths z0m, z0h, z0q (m; 0.01 m is CLM4.5's bare-soil momentum roughness) make it an AbstractSurface; T_skin is the skin temperature, a massless surface at depth 0 joined to the first node through the half layer. hydrology = false freezes the water redistribution in step_surface! (heat-only runs).

Numerics: see step_soil_heat!.

Aeolus.SoilColumn.SoilThermalProperties — Type
SoilThermalProperties(; k_solids, c_solids, ρ_particle = 2700, kersten = :fine)

Thermal constants of the solid phase of one soil, from which soil_thermal_conductivity and soil_heat_capacity build the bulk values for any water and ice content:

fieldmeaning
k_solidsconductivity of the soil solids, W m⁻¹ K⁻¹
c_solidsvolumetric heat capacity of the solids, J m⁻³ K⁻¹
ρ_particleparticle density, kg m⁻³ (sets Johansen's dry conductivity)

Aeolus.SoilColumn.UnconfinedAquifer — Type
UnconfinedAquifer(; W = 4.8, S_y = 0.2, D = 25.0, q_max = 5.5e-6, f = 2.5,
                  α_ice = 3.0, frozen_restriction = true)

A prognostic water table in an unconfined aquifer below the soil column — the SIMTOP groundwater model of Niu et al. (2005, 2007) as used in CLM4.0 (Oleson et al. 2010, §7.5) — used as the column's bottom boundary:

col = SoilWaterColumn(grid, soil; bottom = UnconfinedAquifer())

State. W (m of water) is the water stored in an aquifer of thickness D (m) whose top is the base of the soil column $z_b$. While the water table $z_∇$ lies below the column the aquifer drains by gravity with specific yield $S_y$,

z_∇ = z_b + D - W / S_y                      (W ≤ W_max = S_y D)

(Oleson et al. 2010, eq. 7.165; CLM's $W_a = 5000$ mm, $z_∇ = z_b$ at $W_{max}$). When the aquifer is full the water table sits inside the soil column; the aquifer then stores water only elastically, with the column's specific_storage $S_s$, and its excess pressure at the base is $ψ_b = (W - W_{max})/(S_s D)$, so that the hydrostatic water table is $z_∇ = z_b - ψ_b$. W is thus one continuous, monotone store for both regimes (CLM switches between two separate stores instead).

Recharge — the flux across the column's base, downward positive — is the steady Darcy flux between the bottom node ($ψ_N$ at $z_N$) and the water table ($ψ = 0$ at $z_∇$), or the saturated base ($ψ_b$ at $z_b$) when the table is in the column. It is evaluated by gardner_two_point_flux, exact for a conductivity exponential in $ψ$ between the two points, which keeps capillary rise from a deep water table realistic where a mean conductivity overstates it by orders of magnitude. It vanishes exactly for the hydrostatic profile $ψ_N = -(z_∇ - z_N)$.

Baseflow (lateral, topographically controlled drainage; SIMTOP, Niu et al. 2005, CLM4 eq. 7.159):

q_drai = (1 - f_imp) q_max exp(-f z_∇)

with $q_{max} = 5.5×10^{-3}$ kg m⁻² s⁻¹ $= 5.5×10^{-6}$ m s⁻¹ and $f = 2.5$ m⁻¹ (CLM4.0 global values, Oleson et al. 2010, p. 155; CLM4.5 replaced $q_{max}$ by $10 \sin β$ kg m⁻² s⁻¹ and $f_{imp}$ by an ice impedance factor) and the frozen-soil impermeable fraction $f_{imp}$ of aquifer_impermeable_fraction (frozen_restriction = false sets it to 0).

Numerics. Within a sub-step the recharge is coupled implicitly to the aquifer (the linearisation handed to the Richards solver includes the response of the water table, which is stiff when the aquifer is full), and after convergence W is advanced by the exact solution of $dW/dt = q - q_{drai}(W)$ at the converged recharge $q$ — for a constant $q$ the substitution $u = e^{f z_∇}$ makes it linear in both regimes. Water is therefore conserved to round-off by total_water_storage, and the recession $q_{drai}(t) = q_0/(1 + t/τ)$, $τ = S_y/(f q_0)$, is reproduced for any step length.

Cumulative fields: recharge and baseflow (m of water since construction).

Aeolus.SoilColumn.VanGenuchtenMualem — Type
VanGenuchtenMualem(; θ_r, θ_s, α, n, K_s, l = 0.5, ψ_e = 0)

van Genuchten (1980) retention with the Mualem (1976) conductivity:

S_e = [1 + (α|ψ|)^n]^{-m},  m = 1 - 1/n,   θ = θ_r + (θ_s - θ_r) S_e
K   = K_s S_e^l [1 - (1 - S_e^{1/m})^m]²

α in m⁻¹, K_s in m s⁻¹, tortuosity l = 0.5 (Mualem).

With an air-entry potential ψ_e < 0 the curves are those of the modified model of Vogel et al. (2001) / Ippisch et al. (2006): the soil is saturated for $ψ ≥ ψ_e$, and with $S_c = [1 + (α|ψ_e|)^n]^{-m}$

S_e = [1 + (α|ψ|)^n]^{-m} / S_c,
K   = K_s S_e^l {[1 - (1 - (S_e S_c)^{1/m})^m] / [1 - (1 - S_c^{1/m})^m]}²

For $n < 2$ the unmodified K has an infinite slope at saturation — a non-physical cusp (Ippisch et al. 2006) that makes Richards solvers chatter near saturation; $ψ_e$ of a few centimetres removes it. The retention curve moves by at most $(θ_s - θ_r)(1 - S_c)$, reached at $ψ_e$: for $ψ_e = -2$ cm that is < 0.6 % of the pore space for loams and clays but 1.3-2.3 % for the Carsel-Parrish sands, loamy sands, sandy loams and sandy clay loams. ψ_e = 0 is the classical model.

  • Ippisch, O., Vogel, H.-J. & Bastian, P. (2006). Validity limits for the van Genuchten–Mualem model and implications for parameter estimation and numerical simulation. Adv. Water Resour., 29, 1780-1789.

Aeolus.SoilColumn.gardner_two_point_flux — Method
gardner_two_point_flux(K_1, K_2, ψ_1, ψ_2, L) -> q (m s⁻¹, downward)

Steady vertical Darcy flux from a point with potential ψ_1 and conductivity K_1 down to a point L metres below with ψ_2, K_2, assuming the conductivity varies exponentially with potential between them, $K ∝ e^{α ψ}$ with $α = \ln(K_2/K_1)/(ψ_2 - ψ_1)$. Integrating $q = K (1 - dψ/dz)$ exactly (Gardner 1958) gives

q = (K_1 - K_2 e^{-αL}) / (1 - e^{-αL}),

which is exact for a GardnerExponential soil, reduces to Darcy's law $q = K (1 - (ψ_2 - ψ_1)/L)$ for uniform $K$, and vanishes for the hydrostatic pair $ψ_1 = ψ_2 - L$ whatever the soil. Used for the recharge between the bottom soil node and the water table.

Aeolus.SoilColumn.parlange_sorptivity — Method

Sorptivity of soil h wetted from initial potential ψ_i by a boundary held at ψ_0, from Parlange's (1975) approximation

S² = ∫_{θ_i}^{θ_0} (θ_0 + θ - 2θ_i) D(θ) dθ = ∫_{ψ_i}^{ψ_0} (θ_0 + θ(ψ) - 2θ_i) K(ψ) dψ

(the second form avoids the singular D near saturation, and it keeps the saturated segment $ψ_e < ψ < ψ_0$ of a soil with an air-entry potential, where θ is constant but water still flows: a δ-function in D(θ) that the first form would miss). It lies midway between the rigorous bounds $2∫(θ - θ_i)D\,dθ ≤ S² ≤ 2(θ_0 - θ_i)∫D\,dθ$. It is built for the steeply rising diffusivities of real soils, where it is within a few per cent of the exact sorptivity; for a constant D it overestimates by 8.5 % ($\sqrt{3/2}$ against $2/\sqrt{π}$). Integrated with the trapezoid rule on a grid uniform in $\log(ψ_0 - ψ + ε)$.

Aeolus.SoilColumn.step_soil_heat! — Method
step_soil_heat!(col::SoilThermalColumn, dt, top::AbstractSoilHeatTop) -> SoilHeatStep

Advance the soil temperature and ice of col by dt seconds with the water content fixed, under the upper boundary top (GroundHeatFluxTop, SkinTemperatureTop, LinearizedSkinBalance) and the column's bottom.

The heat equation in enthalpy form,

∂H/∂t = ∂/∂z (k ∂T/∂z),   H = C (T - T_f) - ρ_ice L_f θ_ice,

is discretised by finite volumes (series interface conductivity) and backward Euler, and solved by Newton's method on the enthalpy with the conductivity lagged (the enthalpy method of e.g. Voller et al. 1990, Nedjar 2002). Because the unknown is H and not T, a sharp phase change needs no apparent-heat-capacity smoothing: layers at $T_f$ simply absorb or release latent heat. The new enthalpy is formed from the divergence of the converged fluxes, so

enthalpy_change = (G - bottom_flux) dt

to round-off. Sub-steps adapt to the Newton convergence (halved on failure). The layer temperatures, θ_liq and θ_ice of col.water are updated; its matric potential is unchanged (freezing conserves the total water, on which retention depends).

Aeolus.SoilColumn.step_soil_water! — Method
step_soil_water!(col, dt; water_input = 0, evaporation = 0, sink = nothing)
    -> SoilWaterStep

Advance the soil water of col by dt seconds. water_input (rain, throughfall, snowmelt; m s⁻¹ ≥ 0) and evaporation (m s⁻¹, upward; a negative value is dew) drive the AtmosphericTop boundary; sink is an optional per-layer extraction (m s⁻¹ of water removed from each layer, e.g. transpiration by roots).

Richards' equation in mixed form,

∂θ/∂t = ∂/∂z [K (∂ψ/∂z - 1)] - s        (z depth, positive down)

is discretised by finite volumes on the layer grid (interface conductivity set by the column's interface_mean, by default the logarithmic mean of its neighbours) and backward Euler in time, and the nonlinear system is solved by the modified Picard iteration of Celia et al. (1990) with a tridiagonal solve per iterate. The new water content is the old one plus the divergence of the converged fluxes, so

storage_change = infiltration - drainage - extraction

holds to round-off. The step is split into sub-steps that adapt to the Picard convergence (halved on failure, grown by half after fast convergence), which is what keeps the solver robust when the soil nears saturation.

Dynamic viscosity of liquid water at atmospheric pressure from the four-term correlation of Kestin, Sokolov & Wakeham (1978) relative to the 20 °C value, as tabulated with the viscosity of water in the CRC Handbook of Chemistry and Physics:

log₁₀(μ/μ₂₀) = (20 - t)/(t + 96) [1.2378 - 1.303×10⁻³ (20 - t)

with t in °C (from T K and the contract's freezing point) and $μ_{20} = 1.0016$ mPa s. It reproduces the CRC values at 0, 10, 20, 25, 40, 60 and 80 °C to better than 0.1 % (largest deviation 0.075 %, at 10 °C) and extrapolates smoothly into weakly supercooled water.

Aeolus.SurfaceExchange.step_surface! — Method
step_surface!(col::SoilThermalColumn, forcing, dt; δT = 0.01, Q_extra = 0,
              kwargs...) -> SurfaceFluxes

One coupled land-surface step: skin energy balance, soil heat and freeze–thaw, then soil water.

  1. The atmospheric fluxes of the current skin temperature $T_0$ come from SurfaceExchange.turbulent_fluxes (Monin–Obukhov, with kwargs passed on) and are linearised in the skin temperature by a finite difference of δT: $R_n$, $H$, $LE$ and $E$. Evaporation is capped by the liquid water of the top layer.
  2. step_soil_heat! with a LinearizedSkinBalance: the skin temperature and the soil temperatures are solved together, implicitly, so the ground heat flux couples the skin to the soil's heat store — a sink for a skin warmer than the soil, a source for a skin cooling below it at night.
  3. The returned SurfaceFluxes are the linearised fluxes at the time-mean skin temperature of the step, so $R_n - H - LE = G$ and $G\,dt$ is exactly the energy the soil received.
  4. With hydrology = true, step_soil_water! with the rain of forcing and the evaporation $E$ just returned. If the soil delivers less (its surface dries to the dry potential), the returned E and LE are what it delivered, and the latent heat charged but not spent goes into the top layer and into G (#129: before, the surface reported the full demand, so an atmosphere coupled to it received water the soil never lost).

Q_extra (W m⁻², positive into the surface, constant over the step) is energy the skin receives besides radiation and the turbulent fluxes — the latent heat of dew deposited on it, heat released onto it, or (negative) sunlight transmitted through glazing. It enters the skin balance with the atmospheric supply, $G_{atm} = R_n - H - LE + Q_{extra}$, so the returned $G = R_n - H - LE + Q_{extra}$ is still exactly what the soil received.
