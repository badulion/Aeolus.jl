Air–sea fluxes — AirSeaFlux

AirSeaFlux (Phase 52, issue #39) gives open water a roughness that responds to the wind and the waves, the matching heat and moisture roughness, the COARE convective stability functions and gustiness, a COARE 3.0 / 3.5 bulk algorithm, and SeaSurface, an AbstractSurface for the tile framework. Theory and validation: Air–sea fluxes and wave-dependent roughness.

Issue #40 adds the Andreas et al. (2015) sea-spray heat fluxes (AndreasSpray, sea_spray_fluxes), the rain heat flux, the full COARE algorithm with cool skin and warm layer (coare_fluxes, coare_warm_layer_series), and coare_flux_hook, which drives the prognostic OceanMixedLayer.WaterColumn with COARE fluxes. Theory and validation: Sea spray and the full COARE algorithm.

It extends the surface shared core (SurfaceExchange) rather than restating it: the Monin–Obukhov solve is SurfaceExchange.solve_surface_layer, COAREStability is a further AbstractStabilityFunctions whose Kansas and stable branches are the contract's BusingerDyerBeljaarsHoltslag, saturation and latent heat come from MoistThermodynamics, air viscosity from P3Microphysics (Sutherland), water emissivity from SurfaceOptics.

using Aeolus

# COARE 3.5, wind-speed Charnock
r = air_sea_fluxes(coare35_bulk(); U = 8.0, z_m = 10.0,
                   θ_a = 299.2, θ_s = 301.0, q_a = 0.016, q_s = 0.0235)
r.surface.u_star, r.C_D10N, r.w_g

# the same with a young sea (4 s peak period, 1 m waves)
rw = air_sea_fluxes(coare35_bulk(roughness = WaveAgeCharnock()); U = 8.0, z_m = 10.0,
                    θ_a = 299.2, θ_s = 301.0, q_a = 0.016, q_s = 0.0235,
                    sea = deep_water_sea_state(4.0, 1.0))

# as a surface
s  = SeaSurface(T_skin = 301.0)
fl = step_surface!(s, SurfaceForcing(U = 8.0, T_air = 299.0, q_air = 0.016), 600.0)
# full COARE: bulk sensor at 5 m, cool skin on, spray at 20 m/s
c = coare_fluxes(coare35_bulk(); U = 20.0, z_u = 10.0, T_air = 299.0, q_air = 0.017,
                 T_sea = 301.0, S = 35.0, lw_down = 400.0, spray = AndreasSpray())
c.H_total, c.LE_total, c.ΔT_cool, c.H_spray

# a prognostic water column driven by COARE fluxes
col = WaterColumn(dz = fill(1.0, 30), T = 301.0, S = 35.0)
step_surface!(col, SurfaceForcing(U = 20.0, T_air = 299.0, q_air = 0.017), 600.0;
              flux = coare_flux_hook(coare35_bulk(); spray = AndreasSpray()))
Aeolus.AirSeaFlux — Module
AirSeaFlux

Air–sea exchange over open water (Phase 52): the sea-surface roughness that responds to wind and waves, the scalar roughness that follows from it, the COARE convective stability functions and gustiness, and a COARE-style bulk algorithm that ties them together.

It extends the surface shared core SurfaceExchange rather than restating it: the Monin–Obukhov solve is SurfaceExchange.solve_surface_layer, the stability functions are a new AbstractStabilityFunctions subtype (COAREStability), the sea surface is an AbstractSurface (SeaSurface), saturation and latent heats come from MoistThermodynamics, the air viscosity from P3Microphysics (Sutherland's law), the water emissivity from SurfaceOptics.

Seven parts:

  1. Stability and gustiness — COAREStability (Kansas forms blended into the free-convection $(-ζ)^{-1/3}$ limit, Beljaars–Holtslag on the stable side) and ConvectiveGustiness ($w_g = β w_*$).
  2. Momentum roughness — Charnock plus the smooth-flow limit (Smith 1988), the wind-dependent Charnock of COARE 3.0 and 3.5, and the wave-dependent forms: wave age (Edson et al. 2013, Oost et al. 2002) and wave steepness / sea state (Taylor & Yelland 2001, Edson et al. 2013). See sea_roughness_length.
  3. Scalar roughness — $z_{0t}, z_{0q}$ as functions of the roughness Reynolds number (COARE 3.0, COARE 3.5, Brutsaert–Garratt). See sea_scalar_roughness.
  4. Bulk algorithm — air_sea_fluxes iterates roughness, gustiness and Monin–Obukhov stability to a joint fixed point; coare30_bulk and coare35_bulk are the published configurations.
  5. Sea spray — the fast spray heat-flux algorithm of Andreas et al. (2015) (AndreasSpray, sea_spray_fluxes), with the droplet microphysics taken from MoistThermodynamics (κ-Köhler), Superdroplet and OceanMixedLayer, and the rain heat flux (rain_heat_flux).
  6. The full COARE algorithm — coare_fluxes (bulk fluxes iterated with the OceanMixedLayer cool skin, plus rain and spray) and coare_warm_layer_series (the COARE warm-layer time loop with OceanMixedLayer.FairallWarmLayer).
  7. Coupling — coare_flux_hook drives the prognostic OceanMixedLayer.WaterColumn with COARE fluxes, spray and rain.

Sign conventions are those of SurfaceExchange: fluxes positive upward, $θ_* < 0$ for a surface warmer than the air, $ζ > 0$ stable.

Aeolus.AirSeaFlux.AbstractCharnockRoughness — Type
AbstractCharnockRoughness <: AbstractSeaRoughness

Roughness laws of Charnock form, $z_0 = α u_*²/g + β ν/u_*$, that differ only in how the Charnock parameter α is specified; a subtype implements charnock_parameter(r, u_star; g, U10N, sea) and has a field β.

AbstractScalarRoughness

sea_scalar_roughness(r, z0m, u_star; ν) -> (z0h = …, z0q = …)
Aeolus.AirSeaFlux.AbstractSeaRoughness — Type
AbstractSeaRoughness

A law for the momentum roughness length $z_0$ of the sea surface. A subtype implements

sea_roughness_length(r, u_star; ν, g, U10N, sea) -> z0 (m)

where ν is the air kinematic viscosity, U10N the 10-m neutral wind (needed by the wind-dependent Charnock laws) and sea a SeaState (needed by the wave-dependent laws). Every law here is the sum of a rough-flow part and the smooth-flow limit $z_0^{smooth} = 0.11 ν/u_*$ (Smith 1988), and charnock_parameter returns the equivalent $α = g z_0^{rough}/u_*²$ for any of them.

Aeolus.AirSeaFlux.AirSeaSolution — Type
AirSeaSolution{T}

Result of air_sea_fluxes:

  • surface — the SurfaceExchange.SurfaceLayerSolution of the final Monin–Obukhov solve ($u_*$, $θ_*$, $q_*$, $L$, ζ, and $C_D$, $C_H$, $C_E$ referred to the gust-inclusive wind surface.U);
  • z0m, z0h, z0q — roughness lengths (m); charnock — the equivalent Charnock parameter; Re_r — roughness Reynolds number;
  • w_g — gustiness velocity (m s⁻¹); B — surface buoyancy flux $(g/θ_v)\overline{w'θ_v'}$ (m² s⁻³);
  • U10N — 10-m neutral wind relative to the mean wind (COARE's $u_*/(κ G) \ln(10/z_0)$, with gustiness factor $G = S/U$);
  • C_D10N, C_H10N, C_E10N — 10-m neutral transfer coefficients;
  • ν — air kinematic viscosity used; iterations, converged.
Aeolus.AirSeaFlux.AndreasSpray — Type
AndreasSpray(; VL = (1.76e-9, 2.08e-7, 2.39, 0.1358),
               VS = (3.92e-8, 5.02e-6, 2.54, 0.1480),
               r_latent = 50e-6, r_sensible = 100e-6,
               aerosol = Superdroplet.SEASALT_KAPPA,
               eos = OceanMixedLayer.UNESCOWaterEOS())

The fast sea-spray heat-flux algorithm of Andreas et al. (2015) (Version 4.0 of the Andreas bulk algorithm; the structure is that of Andreas, Persson & Hare 2008). Spray droplets torn from wave crests cool to their equilibrium temperature within seconds (sensible heat, given up by the ocean) and evaporate much more slowly (latent heat, which the air supplies). The two fluxes are represented by bellwether droplets — the 50-µm droplets for the latent and the 100-µm droplets for the sensible flux (their eqs. 5.1–5.2):

H_{L,sp} = ρ_w L_v [1 - (r(τ_{f,50})/50 µm)³] V_L(u_*)
H_{s,sp} = ρ_w c_w (Θ_s - T_{eq,100}) V_S(u_*)

with the wind functions fitted to ~4000 eddy-covariance flux measurements (their eqs. 5.5–5.6; m s⁻¹),

V_L = 1.76e-9                  (u_* ≤ 0.1358),   2.08e-7 u_*^{2.39}  above,
V_S = 3.92e-8                  (u_* ≤ 0.1480),   5.02e-6 u_*^{2.54}  above.

The tuning constants α, β, γ of the full model ($H_{L,sp} = α Q_L$, $H_{s,sp} = β Q_S - (α - γ) Q_L$; α = 2.46, β = 15.15, γ = 1.77) are absorbed in $V_L$ and $V_S$; $H_{s,sp}$ already contains the air cooling by droplet evaporation.

VL and VS are (low value, coefficient, exponent, u_* breakpoint). The droplet microphysics — equilibrium temperature (spray_equilibrium_temperature), equilibrium radius and radius e-folding time (spray_radius_evolution) and the residence time in air (spray_residence_time) — are computed from the physics already in the package, not from Andreas's (2005) approximation formulas: κ-Köhler equilibrium from MoistThermodynamics with the sea-salt κ of Superdroplet, the diffusional growth resistances and fall speed of Superdroplet, and the seawater density of OceanMixedLayer. See sea_spray_fluxes.

Aeolus.AirSeaFlux.BrutsaertGarrattRoughness — Type
BrutsaertGarrattRoughness(; a = 2.0, b = 2.28)

Surface-renewal scalar roughness of Brutsaert (1975) in Garratt's (1992) form, as quoted by Fairall et al. (2003, eq. 29): $z_{0q} = z_0 \exp(a - b R_r^{1/4})$, applied here to heat as well ($z_{0t} = z_{0q}$). It is the form used by Zeng et al. (1998) and Beljaars (1995).

Any AbstractSeaRoughness, AbstractScalarRoughness and AbstractStabilityFunctions combine. coare30_bulk and coare35_bulk give the published configurations; pass e.g. roughness = WaveAgeCharnock() for the COARE 3.5 wave-age option.

Aeolus.AirSeaFlux.COARE30Charnock — Type
COARE30Charnock(; α_low = 0.011, α_high = 0.018, U_low = 10, U_high = 18, β = 0.11)

The COARE 3.0 Charnock parameter (Fairall et al. 2003, Fig. 1): 0.011 up to $U_{10N} = 10$ m s⁻¹, rising linearly to 0.018 at 18 m s⁻¹ and constant beyond. (The reference code evaluates it with the gust-inclusive wind at measurement height; the paper, followed here, with $U_{10N}$.)

Aeolus.AirSeaFlux.COARE30ScalarRoughness — Type
COARE30ScalarRoughness(; A = 5.5e-5, B = 0.6, z0_max = 1.1e-4)

COARE 3.0 fit to the COARE-plus and HEXOS humidity data (Fairall et al. 2003, eq. 28), a simplification of the Liu–Katsaros–Businger (1979) surface-renewal relation: $z_{0q} = \min(z_{max}, A R_r^{-B})$, and $z_{0t} = z_{0q}$. (The reference code uses $z_{max} = 1.15×10^{-4}$; the paper, followed here, $1.1×10^{-4}$.)

Aeolus.AirSeaFlux.COARE35Charnock — Type
COARE35Charnock(; m = 0.0017, b = -0.005, U_max = 19, β = 0.11)

The COARE 3.5 wind-speed-dependent Charnock parameter (Edson et al. 2013, eq. 13 with the 2014 corrigendum m = 0.0017 s m⁻¹): $α = m U_{10N} + b$ for $U_{10N} ≤ U_{max}$, constant $α = m U_{max} + b ≈ 0.028$ above. Below $U_{10N} = -b/m = 2.9$ m s⁻¹ the fit turns negative; α is floored at 0 there (pure smooth flow). The reference code does not floor it, which is harmless along its own iteration path (the smooth term dominates at such winds) but gives a negative $z_0$ for larger trial $u_*$; the difference in $C_D$ is below 1 % where it applies.

Aeolus.AirSeaFlux.COARE35ScalarRoughness — Type
COARE35ScalarRoughness(; A = 5.8e-5, B = 0.72, z0_max = 1.6e-4)

The COARE 3.5 refit (reference code coare35vn, chosen so that the Stanton and Dalton numbers stay close to COARE 3.0 with the rougher 3.5 momentum roughness): $z_{0q} = z_{0t} = \min(z_{max}, A R_r^{-B})$.

Aeolus.AirSeaFlux.COARESolution — Type
COARESolution{T}

Result of coare_fluxes (all fluxes positive upward, W m⁻², N m⁻², kg m⁻² s⁻¹):

  • bulk — the AirSeaSolution of the interfacial bulk solve;
  • T_skin (K), q_s (kg kg⁻¹) — interface temperature and humidity; ΔT_cool (K, > 0 when the skin is cooler) and δ_cool (m) — cool skin; ΔT_warm (K) — warm-layer warming between the surface and the bulk sensor that was applied;
  • H, LE, E, τ — interfacial fluxes and stress;
  • H_spray, LE_spray — spray-mediated fluxes (0 without spray);
  • H_total = H + H_spray, LE_total, E_total — what leaves the top of the droplet evaporation layer;
  • H_rain — rain heat flux (positive = ocean cooling);
  • lw_net — net longwave, positive into the ocean;
  • Q_ocean — net heat into the ocean, ``SW{net} + LW{net} - H_{total}
    • LE{total} - H{rain}``;
  • iterations — cool-skin iterations.
Aeolus.AirSeaFlux.COAREStability — Type
COAREStability(version::Symbol = :coare35)
COAREStability(; momentum, heat, γc_m = 10.15, γc_h = 34.15)

The stability functions of the COARE bulk algorithm (Fairall et al. 1996, 2003; Grachev & Fairall 1997), a further SurfaceExchange.AbstractStabilityFunctions.

Unstable ($ζ < 0$): the Kansas (Businger–Dyer) form $ψ_K$ is blended into a free-convection form $ψ_C$ that obeys the $φ ∝ (-ζ)^{-1/3}$ scaling of local free convection,

ψ = (1 - f) ψ_K + f ψ_C,        f = ζ² / (1 + ζ²),
ψ_C = (3/2) ln[(y² + y + 1)/3] - √3 atan[(2y + 1)/√3] + π/√3,
y   = (1 - γ_c ζ)^{1/3},

with $γ_c = 10.15$ for momentum and $34.15$ for scalars (Fairall et al. 2003: "gave the smoothest blend"). $ψ_C$ is the exact integral of $φ_C = (1 - γ_c ζ)^{-1/3}$.

Stable ($ζ ≥ 0$): Beljaars & Holtslag (1991).

Both the Kansas and the stable branches are the contract's BusingerDyerBeljaarsHoltslag with COARE's coefficients (the fields momentum and heat), so only the convective part is new here:

versionmomentum (Kansas γ, stable a, b)heat (γ, a, b)
:coare3015; a = 1, b = 2/3 (psiu_26, 3.0)15; a = 1, b = 2/3
:coare3515; a = 0.7, b = 3/4 (psiu_26, 3.5)15; a = 1, b = 2/3

with $c = 5$, $d = 0.35$. The reference codes write the stable heat function with the rounded constants 14.28 and 8.525, so their $ψ_h(0) = -0.0045$; the exact $c/d$ used here gives $ψ_h(0) = 0$.

Aeolus.AirSeaFlux.CharnockRoughness — Type
CharnockRoughness(; α = 0.011, β = 0.11)

Charnock (1955) with the smooth-flow term of Smith (1988): $z_0 = α u_*²/g + β ν/u_*$, the COARE 2.5 law (α = 0.011; Fairall et al. 1996). Beljaars (1995) and the ECMWF model use α = 0.018.

Aeolus.AirSeaFlux.ConvectiveGustiness — Type
ConvectiveGustiness(; β = 1.2, z_i = 600, w_min = 0.2, w_init = 0.5)

Sub-grid wind variability from boundary-layer-scale convective eddies (Godfrey & Beljaars 1991; Beljaars 1995; Fairall et al. 1996):

w_g = β w_*,   w_* = (B z_i)^{1/3}   if B > 0,
w_g = w_min                          otherwise,

with the surface buoyancy flux $B = (g/θ_v) \overline{w'θ_v'}$ (m² s⁻³) and mixed-layer depth z_i (m). The bulk solver then uses the effective wind $S = \sqrt{U² + w_g²}$, which keeps the scalar fluxes finite as $U → 0$ (free convection). β = 1.2 and $w_{min} = 0.2$ m s⁻¹ are the values of the COARE 3.0/3.5 reference codes (Fairall et al. 2003 quote β = 1.25 in the text); w_init is the first guess. As in those codes, $w_{min}$ applies only when $B ≤ 0$: a weakly unstable surface has $w_g = β w_*$ even if that is below $w_{min}$.

Aeolus.AirSeaFlux.OostRoughness — Type
OostRoughness(; A = 50, B = 4.5, β = 0.11)

Wave-age law of Oost et al. (2002), α = A (u*/cp)^{B-2}, written as a roughness length with the smooth-flow term as in COARE 3.0 (Fairall et al. 2003, eq. 25b):

z_0 = (A/2π) λ_p (u_*/c_p)^B + β ν/u_*.

Needs a SeaState (λ_p, c_p). Derived from North Sea platform data; Edson et al. (2013) find it too smooth for mature and too rough for young open-ocean seas.

Aeolus.AirSeaFlux.SeaState — Type
SeaState(; c_p, H_s, λ_p)

The dominant-wave description the wave-dependent roughness laws need: phase speed of the spectral-peak waves c_p (m s⁻¹), significant wave height H_s (m, ≈ 4 × rms elevation) and peak wavelength λ_p (m). Build it from a peak period with deep_water_sea_state or for a fully developed sea with fully_developed_sea_state.

Aeolus.AirSeaFlux.SeaStateRoughness — Type
SeaStateRoughness(; D = 0.091, B = 2, β = 0.11)

Sea-state (wave-slope and wave-age) law of COARE 3.5 (Edson et al. 2013, eqs. 18–19; Hsu 1974): the rough-flow roughness scales with the significant wave height,

z_0 = D H_s (u_*/c_p)^B + β ν/u_*,

D = 0.091, B = 2 (the fit gave 2.02). Edson et al. define the fit for the rough part and add the smooth-flow term, as here; the COARE 3.5 reference code instead subtracts the smooth term inside the fit (its total $z_0 = D H_s (u_*/c_p)^2$). Needs a SeaState.

moisture_availability(s)1
surface_specific_humidity(s,f)saturation over seawater, $e = 0.98\,e_{sat}(T_s)$ (Kraus & Businger 1994)
turbulent_fluxes(s, f)air_sea_fluxes with s.bulk and s.sea
step_surface!(s, f, dt)updates the roughness state, returns the fluxes

turbulent_fluxes specialises the contract's default: the roughness is solved with the fluxes (it depends on $u_*$) rather than read from the stored state, and gustiness is applied. The stress follows COARE, $τ = ρ u_*² U/S$: gust-driven stress has no mean direction, so only the fraction $U/S$ of $ρ u_*²$ is momentum taken from the mean wind.

T_skin is prescribed: step_surface! does not change it. The prognostic ocean temperature (mixed layer, diurnal warm layer, cool skin) belongs to OceanMixedLayer, which sets s.T_skin; the G returned is the heat flux into the ocean it must absorb. sea (a SeaState) is likewise set by whatever supplies the waves.

Aeolus.AirSeaFlux.TaylorYellandRoughness — Type
TaylorYellandRoughness(; A = 1200, B = 4.5, β = 0.11)

Wave-steepness law of Taylor & Yelland (2001), with the smooth-flow term (Fairall et al. 2003, eq. 25a):

z_0 = A H_s (H_s/λ_p)^B + β ν/u_*.

The rough part depends only on the sea state (height and steepness of the dominant waves), not on the wind. Needs a SeaState.

Aeolus.AirSeaFlux.WaveAgeCharnock — Type
WaveAgeCharnock(; A = 0.114, B = 0.622, β = 0.11)

Wave-age-dependent Charnock parameter $α = A (u_*/c_p)^B$ — younger waves (larger inverse wave age) are rougher. The default coefficients are the COARE 3.5 open-ocean fit of Edson et al. (2013, eq. 15), which gives α = 0.013 at the fully developed $c_p/u_* = 32$ and spans 0.011–0.018 for $43 > c_p/u_* > 20$. Needs a SeaState.

Aeolus.AirSeaFlux.air_kinematic_viscosity — Method
air_kinematic_viscosity(T, p; q = 0, c = DEFAULT_SURFACE_CONSTANTS) -> m² s⁻¹

Kinematic viscosity of (moist) air, $ν = μ(T)/ρ(T, p, q)$, with the dynamic viscosity from Sutherland's law (P3Microphysics.p3_air_viscosity) and the density from MoistThermodynamics.air_density — delegated, not restated. At 20 °C and 1013.25 hPa, ν = 1.506e-5 m² s⁻¹; the COARE reference polynomial gives 1.504e-5.

Aeolus.AirSeaFlux.air_sea_fluxes — Function
air_sea_fluxes(b::BulkAirSea = coare35_bulk();
               U, z_m, z_h = z_m, θ_a, θ_s, q_a, q_s,
               T_air = θ_a, p = 101325, sea = nothing, ν = nothing,
               c = DEFAULT_SURFACE_CONSTANTS, U_min = 0.1) -> AirSeaSolution

Bulk air–sea fluxes, COARE style. Inputs as for SurfaceExchange.solve_surface_layer: wind speed U relative to the surface current at height z_m, potential temperature θ_a and specific humidity q_a at z_h, and the interface values θ_s (skin temperature: a cool-skin / warm-layer correction, if any, is applied by the caller) and q_s (e.g. $0.98\,q_{sat}(T_s)$ over seawater — see SeaSurface). T_air and p set the viscosity unless ν is given; sea is the SeaState for wave laws.

Iteration: starting from COARE's first guess ($u_* = 0.035 U_{10}$, $w_g = 0.5$), each pass

  1. updates $z_0$ from $u_*$ (and $U_{10N}$ / sea) and $z_{0t}, z_{0q}$ from $R_r = z_0 u_*/ν$;
  2. solves Monin–Obukhov similarity exactly for that roughness and the effective wind $S = \sqrt{U² + w_g²}$ (SurfaceExchange.solve_surface_layer with the stability functions of b);
  3. updates the gustiness from the buoyancy flux $B = -(g/θ_{va}) u_* θ_{v*}$ and $U_{10N}$,

until $u_*$ and $w_g$ change by less than rtol (relative). Because step 2 is solved to convergence inside each pass, the outer loop only has to converge the roughness–gustiness feedback, which contracts by a factor ≈ $2/\ln(z/z_0)$ per pass for Charnock-type laws.

Aeolus.AirSeaFlux.andreas_friction_velocity — Method
andreas_friction_velocity(U10N) -> u_* (m s⁻¹)

The drag relation of Andreas, Mahrt & Vickers (2012), eq. (2.2) of Andreas et al. (2015): a hyperbola joining the smooth- and rough-flow straight lines of 5600 eddy-covariance stress measurements,

u_* = 0.239 + 0.0433 {(U - 8.271) + [0.120 (U - 8.271)² + 0.181]^{1/2}},

with U = U10N the 10-m neutral wind (m s⁻¹). For high winds it tends to $u_* = 0.0583 U - 0.243$ (their eq. 2.3), i.e. $C_{DN10}$ rolls off to $3.40×10^{-3}$ (eq. 2.4).

Andreas et al. (2015) tuned their spray wind functions (AndreasSpray) against the bulk $u_{*,B}$ of this drag relation and warn that the spray algorithm should be driven by it. It is provided so that a caller can do so; the bulk algorithm here (air_sea_fluxes) uses COARE roughness, which gives a $u_*$ within a few per cent of this relation up to ~20 m s⁻¹.

Aeolus.AirSeaFlux.charnock_parameter — Method
charnock_parameter(r, u_star; g = grav(), U10N = nothing, sea = nothing, ν = 1.5e-5) -> α

The Charnock parameter of roughness law r. For the Charnock-type laws this is the specified α; for any other law it is the equivalent $α = g (z_0 - β ν/u_*)/u_*²$ implied by its rough-flow part — the quantity Edson et al. (2013) and Fairall et al. (2003) plot.

Aeolus.AirSeaFlux.coare_flux_hook — Function
coare_flux_hook(b::BulkAirSea = coare35_bulk(); sea = nothing, spray = nothing,
                rain_heat = true) -> (col::WaterColumn, forcing) -> SurfaceFluxes

The air–sea flux function for OceanMixedLayer.step_surface!(col, forcing, dt; flux = coare_flux_hook(...)): the water column then runs with COARE fluxes and wave-dependent roughness instead of the contract's fixed-roughness default. For the column's current skin temperature (surface_temperature(col): top layer + warm layer - cool skin, which the column iterates itself) and seawater humidity it

  1. solves air_sea_fluxes with b (and sea for the wave laws) and stores the resulting roughness lengths in col, so roughness_lengths(col) reports them to a tile aggregator;
  2. adds the spray fluxes if spray is an AndreasSpray (droplets from the water just below the skin, salinity col.S, $H_s$ from sea or a fully developed sea);
  3. adds the rain heat flux of forcing.rain (if rain_heat) to the heat the column loses.

Rain and spray use the column's own specific heat col.c_p.

It returns $SurfaceFluxes(R_n, H, LE, G, τ, E)$ with total (interfacial

  • spray) H, LE, E, the COARE stress $τ = ρu_*^2 U/S$ and

$G = R_n - H - LE - H_{rain}$ — exactly what the column absorbs, so the column's heat budget stays closed.

Limitation: WaterColumn computes its cool skin from the H and LE it is handed, so with spray the skin also "sees" the spray heat, which physically leaves with the droplets. coare_fluxes keeps them apart.

Aeolus.AirSeaFlux.coare_fluxes — Function
coare_fluxes(b::BulkAirSea = coare35_bulk(); U, z_u, z_t = z_u, T_air, q_air,
             p = 101325, T_sea, S = 35, sw_net = 0, lw_down, rain = 0,
             ΔT_warm = 0, emissivity = SurfaceOptics.material_emissivity(:water),
             cool_skin = CoolSkin(), eos = UNESCOWaterEOS(), c_w = cp_water(c),
             k_w = c.k_water, spray = nothing, sea = nothing,
             maxiter = 20, tol = 1e-6, c = DEFAULT_SURFACE_CONSTANTS) -> COARESolution

The COARE bulk algorithm as the reference codes run it (Fairall et al. 1996a,b, 2003; Edson et al. 2013), assembled from the pieces that already exist rather than restated:

  • interfacial fluxes — air_sea_fluxes with the roughness, stability and gustiness of b;
  • interface temperature $T_{skin} = T_{sea} + ΔT_{warm} - ΔT_{cool}$, with $T_{sea}$ the bulk water temperature measured at some depth, ΔT_warm the warm-layer warming between that depth and the surface (see coare_warm_layer_series), and the cool skin $ΔT_{cool}$ from OceanMixedLayer.cool_skin, iterated with the fluxes to convergence (tol, K);
  • seawater humidity $q_s = (1 - 0.02 S/34) q_{sat}(T_{skin})$ (OceanMixedLayer.salinity_humidity_factor);
  • seawater density, thermal expansion and haline contraction from eos; latent heat and saturation from MoistThermodynamics;
  • net longwave $ε (LW_↓ - σ T_{skin}^4)$, the rain heat flux (rain_heat_flux, rain in kg m⁻² s⁻¹) and, if spray is an AndreasSpray, the spray fluxes (sea_spray_fluxes) for droplets torn from water at $T_{sea} + ΔT_{warm}$, with $H_s$ from sea or, without one, a fully developed sea at the computed $U_{10N}$.

Inputs: wind U (m s⁻¹) at z_u, air temperature T_air (K) and specific humidity q_air at z_t (one height for both scalars), surface pressure p (Pa), salinity S (psu), net shortwave into the sea sw_net and downwelling longwave lw_down (W m⁻²). cool_skin = nothing switches the cool skin off. The seawater specific heat c_w is used consistently by the cool skin, the rain heat flux and the spray sensible flux.

The cool skin sees only the interfacial fluxes (spray heat leaves with the droplets, not by conduction through the skin).

Aeolus.AirSeaFlux.coare_warm_layer_series — Method
coare_warm_layer_series(b::BulkAirSea, records; sensor_depth,
                        warm = FairallWarmLayer(), z_u, z_t = z_u, S = 35,
                        eos = UNESCOWaterEOS(), c_w = cp_water(c),
                        c = DEFAULT_SURFACE_CONSTANTS, kwargs...) -> Vector{COARESolution}

The COARE warm-layer time loop (Fairall et al. 1996a; the coare36vnWarm_et reference code) over a time series of bulk observations. records is a vector of named tuples with fields

jd (day of year, UTC), lon (degrees east), U, T_air, q_air, p,
T_sea (bulk sensor, K), sw_net, lw_down, rain (kg m⁻² s⁻¹)

and optionally S. For every record after the first the Fairall warm layer warm (OceanMixedLayer.FairallWarmLayer) is stepped with this record's net shortwave and the previous record's turbulent, rain and longwave heat loss and stress (the reference's order); it starts at the first local morning (≤ 06:00 local solar time, local time = UTC + (lon + 7.5)/15 h) and is reset whenever local time wraps past midnight. The warming between the surface and sensor_depth (m) is then passed to coare_fluxes as ΔT_warm; other keywords go to coare_fluxes.

Aeolus.AirSeaFlux.neutral_transfer_coefficients — Method
neutral_transfer_coefficients(roughness, scalar, U10N;
                              z_ref = 10, ν = air_kinematic_viscosity(288.15, 101325),
                              sea = nothing, c = DEFAULT_SURFACE_CONSTANTS)
    -> (C_D, C_H, C_E, u_star, z0m, z0h, z0q)

Neutral transfer coefficients at height z_ref for a neutral wind U10N at that height (the usual $C_{D10N}$, $C_{H10N}$, $C_{E10N}$ when z_ref = 10). Solves the neutral log law with a roughness that depends on its own friction velocity,

u_* = κ U / ln(z_ref / z_0(u_*)),

by a bracketed secant, then $C_D = κ²/\ln²(z/z_0)$, $C_{H,E} = κ²/[\ln(z/z_0) \ln(z/z_{0t,q})]$.

Aeolus.AirSeaFlux.rain_heat_flux — Method
rain_heat_flux(rain; T_air, q_air, T_s, q_s, p = 101325, c_w = cp_water(c),
               c = DEFAULT_SURFACE_CONSTANTS) -> W m⁻² (positive = ocean cooling)

Sensible heat carried into the sea by rain falling at the wet-bulb temperature (Gosnell, Fairall & Webster 1995; Fairall et al. 1996b, eq. 23; the form of the COARE codes):

H_rain = R c_w α [(T_s - T_a) + (q_s - q_a) L_v/c_p],
α = [1 + (L_v/c_p)(dq_sat/dT)(D_v/κ_T)]^{-1},

with rain $R$ in kg m⁻² s⁻¹ (the contract's SurfaceForcing.rain), the Clausius–Clapeyron slope $dq_{sat}/dT = L_v q_{sat}/(R_v T²)$ at the air temperature, vapour diffusivity $D_v$ and thermal diffusivity $κ_T = k_a/(ρ_a c_p)$ of air (Superdroplet). The COARE reference codes evaluate the slope with the air's actual humidity instead of $q_{sat}$; the wet-bulb derivation calls for $q_{sat}$, used here. c_w is the specific heat of the (rain) water that is warmed to the sea temperature, J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹.

Aeolus.AirSeaFlux.sea_spray_fluxes — Function
sea_spray_fluxes(sp::AndreasSpray = AndreasSpray(); u_star, T_sea, T_air, RH,
                 p = 101325, S = 34, H_s, c_w = cp_water(c),
                 c = DEFAULT_SURFACE_CONSTANTS)
    -> (H_L, H_S, E, T_eq, r_eq, τ_r, τ_f, r_f, V_L, V_S)

Spray-mediated latent and sensible heat fluxes (W m⁻², positive upward) of the Andreas et al. (2015) fast algorithm (see AndreasSpray):

  • u_star — bulk friction velocity of the interfacial algorithm (m s⁻¹);
  • T_sea — temperature of the water the droplets are torn from (K; the sub-skin / near-surface bulk temperature);
  • T_air, RH — air temperature (K) and relative humidity (fraction) at the reference height (10 m in the paper);
  • S — salinity (psu); H_s — significant wave height (m).

Returns also the spray evaporation E = H_L/L_v (kg m⁻² s⁻¹), the 100-µm equilibrium temperature T_eq, and for the 50-µm droplet the equilibrium radius r_eq, e-folding time τ_r, residence time τ_f and the radius r_f = r_eq + (r_0 - r_eq) e^{-τ_f/τ_r} it has on falling back (their eq. 2.9). The total fluxes out of the droplet evaporation layer are the interfacial ones plus these (their eq. 2.1); as in Andreas et al., the spray does not feed back on the stability.

c_w is the specific heat of the seawater the droplets carry (J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹; default the contract's fresh-water cp_water, ≈ 4.5 % above seawater's ≈ 4000).

Above $RH ≈ a_w$ (≈ 98 % at 34 psu) the droplets grow and $H_L$ is negative. Supersaturated air ($RH > 1$, which would activate the droplet and has no κ-Köhler equilibrium) is treated as $RH = 1$. Close to saturation the exponential radius law (their eq. 2.9) with the e-folding $τ_r$ underestimates growth, because $r_{eq}$ runs off to millimetres and $τ_r$ diverges: $H_L$ returns towards 0 as $RH → 1$ (Fig. 11 case: −12 W m⁻² at 99.5 %, −5 at 99.9 %, 0 at 100 %) where Andreas et al.'s curve stays near −20 W m⁻².

Aeolus.AirSeaFlux.spray_equilibrium_temperature — Method
spray_equilibrium_temperature(r0; T_air, RH, p = 101325, S = 34, T_sea = T_air,
                              eos, aerosol, c = DEFAULT_SURFACE_CONSTANTS) -> T_eq (K)

Temperature at which a saline droplet of radius r0 loses by evaporation exactly the heat it gains by conduction from the air (Andreas 1995; the "saline wet-bulb" temperature):

k_a (T_a - T) = L_v D_v [a_w(r_0, T) e_s(T)/(R_v T) - RH e_s(T_a)/(R_v T_a)],

with $a_w$ the droplet's equilibrium saturation ratio (κ-Köhler: solute and curvature, MoistThermodynamics.kohler_supersaturation), $k_a$ and $D_v$ from Superdroplet. Kinetic (gas-kinetic and ventilation) corrections affect $k_a$ and $D_v$ alike and are negligible for the 100-µm bellwether droplets; they are omitted. Below $RH = a_w$ the droplet is cooler than the air; above, warmer (Andreas et al. 2015, §6.2: above 98.1 % RH at 34 psu).

Aeolus.AirSeaFlux.spray_radius_evolution — Method
spray_radius_evolution(r0; T_air, RH, p = 101325, S = 34, T_sea = T_air,
                       eos, aerosol, c = DEFAULT_SURFACE_CONSTANTS) -> (r_eq, τ_r)

Equilibrium radius r_eq (m) of a seawater droplet of initial radius r0 at relative humidity RH (fraction) and its radius e-folding time τ_r (s), defined as in Andreas (1990): the time at which $(r - r_{eq})/(r_0 - r_{eq}) = e^{-1}$.

r_eq is the stable κ-Köhler equilibrium (MoistThermodynamics.kohler_equilibrium_radius). τ_r comes from integrating the diffusional growth equation

r dr/dt = (RH - 1 - s_eq(r)) / (F_k + F_d)

($s_eq$ the same κ-Köhler curve, $F_k$, $F_d$ from Superdroplet) with classical RK4 on steps of 1/400 of the local relaxation time. The droplet is taken at air temperature in $F_k + F_d$, which already accounts for its evaporative cooling (Mason's form). If $RH$ equals the droplet's initial saturation ratio, r_eq = r0 and τ_r = Inf.
