Urban facets — UrbanSurface

UrbanSurface (Phase 49) replaces the single heat-capacity slab of the urban example with per-facet multi-layer conduction: every roof, wall and ground (road) facet is a 1-D column through real material layers, closed on the outside by the surface energy balance of the surface shared core and on the inside by an interior boundary condition. The theory and the validation are on Urban facet conduction.

It has three layers of API:

  • Materials — ThermalMaterial, thermal_material, thermal_material_names: conductivity, density and specific heat, every row cited. Thermal properties only: an exposed material names its SurfaceOptics spectral-library entry (optical), and a facet's albedo and emissivity are derived from it. thermal_diffusivity, thermal_admittance and volumetric_heat_capacity stay qualified (Aeolus.UrbanSurface.thermal_admittance).
  • The conduction core — WallLayer, ConductionColumn, ColumnBoundary (fixed_temperature_boundary, heat_flux_boundary, convective_boundary) and step_conduction!. It knows nothing about the atmosphere; it is the code BuildingEnergy reuses for thermal mass.
  • Facets — UrbanFacet <: AbstractSurface, with step_surface!, interior_heat_flux and the interior hooks set_interior_temperature! / set_interior_boundary!.
  • Glazing (#27; theory: Glazing and window facets) — GlassPane / glass_pane_from_normal and pane_optics (Fresnel + Beer–Lambert, per polarisation), Glazing units (single_glazing, double_glazing, triple_glazing) with glazing_optics and glazing_optics_hemispherical, gas gaps (GlazingGap, FillGas, GLAZING_AIR, GLAZING_ARGON, gap_conductance), the steady glazing_u_value, inward_flowing_fractions and glazing_shgc, and WindowFacet <: AbstractSurface, whose transmitted_shortwave is the solar gain handed to a building energy model. The fill-gas property fits (gas_conductivity, gas_viscosity, gas_heat_capacity) stay qualified.
  • Radiosity coupling — UrbanFacetScene, step_urban_facets!, aggregate_facet_fluxes: one facet column per radiosity facet, with the facet-to-facet longwave exchange implicit.
  • Canyon exchange (#30; theory: BUBBLE / CAPITOUL validation) — the Town Energy Balance closures for the air next to walls and streets: canyon_wind_speed (TEB canyon wind, skimming flow) and rowley_convection_coefficient (h = 11.8 + 4.2 U, TEB's wall exchange).

Walls and street in the canyon wind

using Aeolus

H, W = 20.5, 14.0                                 # CAPITOUL, rue d'Alsace-Lorraine
U_can = canyon_wind_speed(4.0, 29.5, H, W)        # 4 m/s at 29.5 m above the roofs -> 0.74 m/s
wall = UrbanFacet(:wall, [WallLayer(:brick, 0.30)];
                  h_convective = rowley_convection_coefficient(U_can))   # 14.9 W m⁻² K⁻¹
street_air = SurfaceForcing(T_air = 290.0, U = U_can, z_ref = H / 2)    # MOST for the road

A facet in a diurnal cycle

using Aeolus

roof = UrbanFacet(:roof, example_roof_layers(); T = 293.0, T_interior = 295.0)
wall = UrbanFacet(:wall, [WallLayer(:brick, 0.24), WallLayer(:gypsum_plaster, 0.015)];
                  T_interior = 295.0, h_convective = 15.0)
thermal_transmittance(example_roof_layers(); R_si = 0.10)   # U-value, W m⁻² K⁻¹

for step in 1:1440
    t = 60.0 * step
    f = SurfaceForcing(sw_down = max(0, 800sin(2π * (t / 86400 - 0.25))),
                       lw_down = 330.0, T_air = 293.0, U = 3.0)
    fl = step_surface!(roof, f, 60.0)     # Rn, H, LE, G (G = Rn - H - LE exactly)
    interior_heat_flux(roof)              # W m⁻² into the room
end

A street canyon: every facet its own temperature

UrbanFacetScene gives each facet of a Radiosity scene its own UrbanFacet and couples their longwave exchange implicitly through Radiosity.longwave_response (theory). Albedo and emissivity come from the scene's UrbanMaterials.

hm = zeros(8, 12); hm[1:3, :] .= 20; hm[6:8, :] .= 20   # H/W = 2 street
scene = build_scene(hm, 5.0, 5.0; wall_subdivisions = 4)
vf = view_factors(scene)
u = UrbanFacetScene(scene, vf; T = 295.0,
                    options = f -> f.kind === :wall ? (h_convective = 10.0,
                                                        T_interior = 295.0) : (;))
air = SurfaceForcing(lw_down = 310.0, T_air = 291.0, U = 2.0, z_ref = 30.0)
fl = step_urban_facets!(u, air, 900.0; sun = (0.3, -0.2, 0.93), dni = 800.0, dhi = 120.0)
facet_temperatures(u)                 # one skin temperature per facet
agg = aggregate_facet_fluxes(u, fl)   # H, LE, … per unit plan area per LES column

Reusing the conduction core

col = ConductionColumn([WallLayer(:concrete, 0.20)]; T = 293.0, θ = 0.5)
F_out, F_in = step_conduction!(col, fixed_temperature_boundary(300.0),
                               convective_boundary(295.0, 7.69), 600.0)
# column_heat_content(col) changed by exactly (F_out + F_in) * 600

A window in the sun

using Aeolus

g = double_glazing()                        # 4-16-4 clear, air
glazing_optics(g, deg2rad(60))              # (R, T, A = [A_outer, A_inner])
glazing_shgc(g, 0.0), glazing_u_value(g)    # 0.733, 2.73 W m⁻² K⁻¹ (EN 410 / EN 673 coefficients)

w = WindowFacet(g; T = 293.0, T_interior = 295.0)
set_solar_incidence!(w, cosd(50); diffuse_fraction = 0.3)   # from SolarGeometry / radiosity
fl = step_surface!(w, SurfaceForcing(sw_down = 600.0, lw_down = 330.0, T_air = 293.0), 60.0)
transmitted_shortwave(w)    # W m⁻² of glazing entering the room as sunlight
interior_heat_flux(w)       # W m⁻² conducted / convected from the inner pane
Aeolus.UrbanSurface — Module
UrbanSurface

Phase 49 — the production urban surface: per-facet (roof / wall / ground) multi-layer heat conduction through real material layers, replacing the single heat-capacity slab of the urban example.

Five parts:

  1. Thermal material table — ThermalMaterial and thermal_material: conductivity, density and specific heat of the construction materials, each row cited. Only thermal properties live here. A material that can form an outer surface carries the name of its entry in the SurfaceOptics spectral library (optical), and a facet's albedo and emissivity are derived from that entry — the optical numbers are never restated here.
  2. The conduction core — ConductionColumn, ColumnBoundary and step_conduction!: a 1-D cell-centred finite-volume column through a stack of WallLayers, advanced by the θ-scheme (Crank–Nicolson at θ = ½, backward Euler at θ = 1) with Robin boundary conditions at both faces. It knows nothing about the atmosphere: the interior face hook (indoor air temperature and surface coefficient, or a deep-soil temperature) is an ordinary ColumnBoundary, which is what lets Phase 50's BuildingEnergy reuse the same code for building thermal mass.
  3. Facets — UrbanFacet, an implementer of the SurfaceExchange.AbstractSurface interface: a conduction column whose outer face is closed by the surface energy balance $R_n = H + LE + G$, linearised about the current skin temperature so the step stays implicit and conserves energy exactly.
  4. Glazing (src/urban_surface/glazing.jl) — angle-dependent shortwave optics of uncoated panes (GlassPane, pane_optics) and multi-pane units (Glazing, glazing_optics), ISO 15099 gap heat transfer, the steady glazing_u_value and glazing_shgc, and WindowFacet, a glazed AbstractSurface whose transmitted shortwave (transmitted_shortwave) is the hook for the building energy model.
  5. Canyon exchange (src/urban_surface/canyon_exchange.jl) — the Town Energy Balance closures for the air next to walls and streets: the canyon wind canyon_wind_speed and the wall convective coefficient rowley_convection_coefficient.

Sign convention

As in SurfaceExchange: G is positive into the substrate (from the outer face into the column). Column boundary fluxes returned by step_conduction! are positive into the column at both faces; interior_heat_flux is positive from the facet into the interior (the room, or the deep ground).

Aeolus.UrbanSurface.CLEAR_FLOAT_EXTINCTION — Constant
CLEAR_FLOAT_EXTINCTION = 32.0  # m⁻¹

Broadband solar extinction coefficient $K$ of ordinary ("greenish edge") float glass. Duffie & Beckman (2013, §5.2) give $K$ from about 4 m⁻¹ for water-white (low-iron) glass to about 32 m⁻¹ for glass with a greenish cast of edge — the ordinary clear float of building glazing. It is taken from the book, not fitted: with it a 3 mm pane has $T_{sol} = 0.833$, $R_{sol} = 0.076$ against the 0.83 / 0.08 of the Vitro Clear data sheet (ISO 9050), and inverting that data sheet row with glass_pane_from_normal returns $K = 31.6$ m⁻¹.

Aeolus.UrbanSurface.CLEAR_FLOAT_REFRACTIVE_INDEX — Constant
CLEAR_FLOAT_REFRACTIVE_INDEX = 1.526

Broadband solar refractive index of soda-lime float glass (Duffie & Beckman 2013, §5.1 and Example 5.1.1). The normal-incidence reflectance of one air–glass interface is $((n-1)/(n+1))^2 = 0.04336$.

Air, ISO 15099:2003 Table B.1. The conductivity fit gives 0.02406 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹ at 273.15 K, the still-air value SurfaceConstants.k_air (0.024) of the surface shared core.

Aeolus.UrbanSurface.GLAZING_ARGON — Constant

Argon, ISO 15099:2003 Table B.1.

Aeolus.UrbanSurface.ISO6946_SURFACE_RESISTANCE — Constant
ISO6946_SURFACE_RESISTANCE

Conventional surface resistances of ISO 6946:2017, Table 7 (m² K W⁻¹), combining convection and long-wave radiation at a plane surface:

Aeolus.UrbanSurface.UNCOATED_GLASS_EMISSIVITY — Constant
UNCOATED_GLASS_EMISSIVITY = 0.837

Corrected (hemispherical) thermal emissivity of uncoated soda-lime glass, the standard value of EN 673 / EN 12898 and ISO 15099 (Vitro's data sheet quotes 0.84). It governs the pane–pane radiative exchange in a gap and the long-wave balance of the outer face.

Note: the broadband emissivity of the SurfaceOptics :glass entry is 0.94, a value for a generic glass-clad surface in the urban radiation library. A pane's emissivity is a property of its surface finish (a low-E coating has 0.02–0.2), so it is a GlassPane field.

Aeolus.UrbanSurface.ColumnBoundary — Type
ColumnBoundary(T_env, h, q)

A Robin boundary condition at one face of a ConductionColumn. The heat flux crossing the face into the column is

F = h (T_env - T_face) + q

with h (W m⁻² K⁻¹) a surface coefficient to an environment at T_env (K) and q (W m⁻²) an imposed flux. Special cases, with their constructors:

  • fixed_temperature_boundary(T) — h = Inf: Dirichlet, T_face = T (deep soil, a prescribed surface temperature);
  • heat_flux_boundary(q) — h = 0: Neumann, F = q (q = 0 is adiabatic);
  • convective_boundary(T_env, h) — q = 0: an interior face exchanging with indoor air at T_env through $h = 1/R_{si}$.

A facet's outer face is also a ColumnBoundary: the surface energy balance, linearised about the skin temperature $T_s^n$, $R_n - H - LE ≈ F_0 + F'(T - T_s^n)$, is exactly ColumnBoundary(T_s^n, -F', F_0).

Aeolus.UrbanSurface.ConductionColumn — Type
ConductionColumn(layers; T = 293.15, θ = 0.5)

A 1-D heat-conduction column through layers (outside first), each WallLayer split into equal finite-volume cells. Solves

\[ρ c\,\frac{∂T}{∂t} = \frac{∂}{∂z}\Bigl(k \frac{∂T}{∂z}\Bigr)\]

with cell-centred temperatures and face conductances $K_{i+½} = 1/(Δz_i/2k_i + Δz_{i+1}/2k_{i+1})$ — the series resistance of the two half cells, so a material interface lying on a cell face is represented exactly and the steady composite-wall flux is exact for any resolution.

Time stepping is the θ-scheme (step_conduction!): θ = 0.5 is Crank–Nicolson (second order, A-stable), θ = 1 backward Euler (first order, L-stable — damps the stiffest modes, preferable for time steps much longer than $Δz²/κ$ of the thinnest cell). θ must lie in [0.5, 1]: below ½ the scheme is only conditionally stable, so it is rejected.

T is the initial temperature: a scalar, or a vector of cell values. Fields: T (cell temperatures, K), dz, z (cell centres measured from the outer face, m), k, C ($ρc$ per cell), layer (layer index of each cell), K (the n+1 face conductances, including the half-cell conductances $2k/Δz$ of the two boundary cells).

This is the reusable core: it knows nothing about the atmosphere. A facet (UrbanFacet) and the building energy model close it with ColumnBoundarys.

Aeolus.UrbanSurface.FillGas — Type
FillGas(name, k, μ, cp, M)

A glazing fill gas with ISO 15099 (Annex B, Table B.1) linear fits in the absolute temperature T (K): conductivity $k = k_a + k_b T$ (W m⁻¹ K⁻¹), dynamic viscosity $μ = μ_a + μ_b T$ (Pa s), specific heat $c_p = c_a + c_b T$ (J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹), each a 2-tuple, and molar mass M (kg kmol⁻¹). The density is the ideal gas at the gap pressure.

Two are provided: GLAZING_AIR and GLAZING_ARGON.

Aeolus.UrbanSurface.GlassPane — Type
GlassPane(thickness; n = 1.526, K = 32.0, emissivity = 0.837,
          emissivity_back = emissivity, material = :glass)

One uncoated, homogeneous, specular glass pane of thickness metres: real refractive index n, broadband solar extinction coefficient K (m⁻¹), thermal emissivities of the outer (emissivity) and inner face, and the ThermalMaterial (default the :glass row of the table) whose $ρ c d$ is the pane's heat capacity and whose $k$ its conduction resistance.

Optics are those of pane_optics. To build a pane from a data sheet's normal-incidence solar transmittance and reflectance use glass_pane_from_normal.

Aeolus.UrbanSurface.Glazing — Type
Glazing(panes, gaps)

A glazing unit: panes (outside first) separated by gaps (length(gaps) == length(panes) - 1). Convenience constructors: single_glazing, double_glazing, triple_glazing.

Aeolus.UrbanSurface.GlazingGap — Type
GlazingGap(width; gas = GLAZING_AIR, height = 1.0, pressure = 101325.0)

A sealed gas gap of width metres between two panes, filled with gas (FillGas) at pressure (Pa); height (m) is the gap's vertical extent, which enters the convection correlation through the aspect ratio.

Aeolus.UrbanSurface.ThermalMaterial — Type
ThermalMaterial(name, k, ρ, c; optical = nothing, source = "")

Thermal properties of a homogeneous construction material:

  • conductivity k — W m⁻¹ K⁻¹
  • density ρ — kg m⁻³
  • specific_heat c — J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹
  • optical — the name of the matching entry in the SurfaceOptics spectral library (SurfaceOptics.material_names()), or nothing for a material that is never an exposed surface (insulation, plaster, soil). This is the link to the optical properties; the albedo and emissivity themselves are not stored here.
  • source — the citation of the row.

Derived quantities: volumetric_heat_capacity $C = ρc$, thermal_diffusivity $κ = k/(ρc)$ and thermal_admittance (thermal effusivity) $μ = \sqrt{kρc}$, which sets how strongly the surface temperature of a thick layer responds to a periodic heat flux.

Aeolus.UrbanSurface.UrbanFacet — Type
UrbanFacet(kind, layers; T = 293.15, T_interior = T, h_interior = default,
           interior = default, albedo = derived, emissivity = derived,
           z0m = 0.01, z0h = z0m / 10, h_convective = nothing, θ = 0.5)

A roof, wall or ground (road) facet: a ConductionColumn through layers (outside first) whose outer face is closed by the surface energy balance and whose inner face is the interior hook.

kind ∈ (:roof, :wall, :ground) sets the default interior condition:

kinddefault interior
:roofconvective_boundary(T_interior, 1/0.10) — ISO 6946 R_si, heat flow up
:wallconvective_boundary(T_interior, 1/0.13) — ISO 6946 R_si, horizontal
:groundfixed_temperature_boundary(T_interior) — deep-ground temperature at the base of the column

Pass h_interior to change only the coefficient, or interior (any ColumnBoundary) to replace it; set_interior_temperature! and set_interior_boundary! change it between steps — this is the hook through which a building energy model imposes its indoor air temperature.

Optics are linked, not stored. Unless albedo / emissivity are given, they are derived from the SurfaceOptics spectral-library entry named by the outer layer's optical field: the white-sky broadband albedo broadband_albedo(material_albedo(name)) and the broadband material_emissivity(name). To use a Radiosity urban class instead, pass albedo = material_albedo(mats, URBAN_WALL) etc. An outer material without an optical entry requires both keywords.

Turbulent exchange. With h_convective = nothing (default) H and LE come from SurfaceExchange.turbulent_fluxes — Monin–Obukhov similarity with roughness lengths z0m, z0h (defaults are generic placeholders, not published values; set them per site). A number h_convective (W m⁻² K⁻¹) instead gives $H = h (T_s - T_{air})$, $LE = 0$, $τ = 0$ — the usual closure for a vertical wall, for which MOST has no formal basis. Facets are dry (moisture_availability = 0).

The skin is the outer face, not an extra prognostic layer: its temperature is whatever closes the energy balance against the half-cell conductance $2k_1/Δz_1$ of the first cell. The constructor starts it at the first cell's temperature, so the first step moves it onto the balance (≈ 1 K for a sunlit roof); each step is one Newton iteration on that balance and the residual falls quadratically.

For a wall, SurfaceForcing.sw_down / lw_down are the irradiances on the facet plane. UrbanFacetScene supplies them from the radiosity solves and treats the facet-to-facet longwave implicitly.

Aeolus.UrbanSurface.UrbanFacetScene — Type
UrbanFacetScene(scene, vf; layers = example_facet_layers,
                options = f -> (;), T = 293.15, θ = 0.5)
UrbanFacetScene(scene, vf, facets::Vector{<:UrbanFacet})

Every facet of a Radiosity.RadiosityScene given its own UrbanFacet — its own conduction column and its own skin temperature — and coupled to all the others through the longwave radiosity system on the view factors vf.

This removes the one-temperature-per-column approximation of the urban example, where a whole column's roof, walls and street shared a single temperature and the radiosity solution was averaged back onto it. Here the radiosity scene is the surface: facet i of scene is facet i of the energy balance.

  • layers(f) — the construction of radiosity facet f (outside first); the default picks the illustrative roof / wall / road by f.kind.
  • options(f) — extra UrbanFacet keywords per facet, e.g. f -> f.kind === :wall ? (h_convective = 10.0, T_interior = 295.0) : (;).
  • T — initial temperature, a scalar or one value per facet.

Optics have one source: the scene. Each facet's albedo and emissivity are taken from scene.materials for its material class at construction and re-synchronised at every step_urban_facets!, so the radiosity solve and the facet energy balances can never use different optical properties (an albedo/emissivity passed through options is overwritten).

Fields of interest after a step (per facet, W m⁻² of facet area): sw_absorbed, lw_net (net longwave gain actually applied), lw_emission (the emission εσT⁴ it corresponds to) and skin_increment (the θ-weighted skin-temperature change the linearised fluxes were evaluated at). facet_temperatures(u) returns the skin temperatures. solve_iterations and solve_residual report the last coupled solve (see step_urban_facets!).

Aeolus.UrbanSurface.WallLayer — Type
WallLayer(material, thickness; ncells = nothing, max_cell = 0.02)

One homogeneous layer of a construction: a ThermalMaterial (or the Symbol of a table entry) of thickness metres, resolved by ncells equal finite-volume cells — by default max(1, ceil(thickness / max_cell)), i.e. cells of at most 2 cm.

Layers are listed outside first: the first layer of a wall is the facade, of a roof the waterproofing, of a road the wearing course.

Aeolus.UrbanSurface.WindowFacet — Type
WindowFacet(glazing; T = 293.15, T_interior = T, h_interior = 1/0.13,
            interior = convective_boundary(T_interior, h_interior),
            cos_incidence = 1.0, diffuse_fraction = 1.0,
            z0m = 0.01, z0h = z0m / 10, h_convective = nothing, θ = 0.5,
            c = DEFAULT_SURFACE_CONSTANTS, nquad = 48)

A glazed facet: the pane network of a Glazing whose outer face is closed by the surface energy balance of the surface shared core (SurfaceExchange) and whose inner face exchanges with the room through interior (a ColumnBoundary; by default ISO 6946 $R_{si} = 0.13$, the same hook as UrbanFacet).

Shortwave. Incident shortwave sw_down (on the facet plane) is split into a direct beam at the incidence angle acos(cos_incidence) and a diffuse part diffuse_fraction; each is reflected, absorbed per pane and transmitted by glazing_optics / glazing_optics_hemispherical. Set the geometry each step with set_solar_incidence! (the radiosity / solar-geometry coupling supplies it). With the default diffuse_fraction = 1 the facet uses its hemispherical optics.

  • surface_albedo(w) is the effective solar reflectance of the glazing at the current geometry, so net_radiation and the atmosphere see the correct reflected shortwave;
  • the absorbed shortwave heats the panes;
  • the transmitted shortwave enters the room: transmitted_shortwave (W m⁻² of glazing) is the solar gain a building energy model receives, in addition to the conductive / convective interior_heat_flux.

Energy bookkeeping. SurfaceFluxes.G = R_n - H - LE is everything the facet takes from the outside — storage in the panes, the heat conducted to the room, and the transmitted shortwave: $G\,Δt = ΔE_{panes} + (Q_{interior} + SW_{trans})\,Δt$ to round-off.

The panes are lumped nodes stepped by the θ-scheme; the outer face is linearised about the current skin temperature (one Newton step per time step, as in UrbanFacet), and the gap coefficients are evaluated at the start of the step. Turbulent exchange: Monin–Obukhov through SurfaceExchange.turbulent_fluxes, or $H = h (T_s - T_{air})$ with a given h_convective. Facets are dry.

Aeolus.SurfaceExchange.step_surface! — Method
step_surface!(f::UrbanFacet, forcing, dt; δT = 0.01, kwargs...) -> SurfaceFluxes

Advance a facet by dt seconds. The surface energy balance $F(T_s) = R_n - H - LE$ is evaluated at the current skin temperature $T_s^n$ and its derivative $F'$ by a one-sided difference of width δT (so the Monin–Obukhov stability dependence of H is included), and the linearisation $F_0 + F'(T - T_s^n)$ becomes the outer ColumnBoundary — $h = -F'$, the combined radiative and turbulent surface coefficient. The column step is then fully implicit in the skin temperature, and the new skin temperature is the outer face temperature.

The returned SurfaceFluxes are the θ-weighted fluxes actually applied: $R_n - H - LE = G$ holds exactly, and G·dt plus the interior flux equals the change in column heat content to round-off. kwargs go to SurfaceExchange.turbulent_fluxes (sf, c, U_min, gustiness).

Aeolus.SurfaceExchange.step_surface! — Method
step_surface!(w::WindowFacet, forcing, dt; δT = 0.01, kwargs...) -> SurfaceFluxes

Advance a window facet by dt seconds. Aeolus.UrbanSurface.canyon_wind_speed — Method
canyon_wind_speed(U, z_ref, H, W; z0_town = H / 10) -> m s⁻¹

Mean wind speed inside a street canyon of height H and width W (m), given the wind speed U measured z_ref metres above roof level — the Town Energy Balance closure (Masson 2000, eq. 13; Lemonsu et al. 2004, appendix B, eq. B1):

\[U_{can} = \frac{2}{\pi}\,\exp\!\left(-\frac{H}{4W}\right) \frac{\ln\big((H/3)/z_{0,town}\big)} {\ln\big((z_{ref} + H/3)/z_{0,town}\big)}\;U .\]

The logarithmic ratio carries the wind down from the forcing level to the top of the canyon (a log profile with displacement $2H/3$ and roughness $z_{0,town}$); the exponential is the decay into the canyon (Rotach 1995); and $2/π$ is the average over all canyon orientations of the along-canyon component $|\cos(\text{wind} - \text{street})|$ — TEB takes the flow across the canyon to be a recirculating skimming flow that contributes nothing to the mean along-canyon speed.

The default $z_{0,town} = H/10$ is the rule of thumb for the roughness of a built-up area (Grimmond & Oke 1999, z0 ≈ 0.1 z_H); set it per site.

Domain. Lemonsu et al. (2004) keep this expression for skimming flow, $H/W ≥ 1$, and replace it by wake-interference and isolated-roughness forms for shallower canyons. Those extensions are not implemented, so H / W < 1 throws.

Aeolus.UrbanSurface.step_conduction! — Method
step_conduction!(col, outer, inner, dt; θ = col.θ) -> (F_outer, F_inner)

Advance col by dt seconds with ColumnBoundary outer at the first layer's face and inner at the last. The θ-scheme

\[ρ_i c_i Δz_i \frac{T_i^{n+1} - T_i^n}{Δt} = θ R_i(T^{n+1}) + (1-θ) R_i(T^n)\]

($R_i$ the net conductive and boundary flux into cell $i$) is a tridiagonal system solved by the Thomas algorithm, O(n).

Returns the θ-weighted boundary fluxes into the column (W m⁻²) that were applied over the step, so that

column_heat_content(after) - column_heat_content(before) = (F_outer + F_inner) dt

holds to round-off: the scheme is conservative by construction.

Aeolus.UrbanSurface.step_urban_facets! — Method
step_urban_facets!(u::UrbanFacetScene, forcing, dt; L_sky, sw = nothing,
                   sun = nothing, dni = 0, dhi = 0, samples = 2,
                   coupling = :implicit, solver = :iterative, tol = 1e-12,
                   maxiter = 200, δT = 0.01, kwargs...) -> Vector{SurfaceFluxes}

Advance every facet of u by dt seconds with the longwave exchange between facets treated implicitly, and return each facet's SurfaceFluxes (per unit facet area, SurfaceExchange sign convention).

forcing is the air state — one SurfaceForcing for all facets, or one per facet (canyon air for the street and walls, above-roof air for the roofs). Its sw_down and lw_down are not used as facet irradiances: the facets see what the radiosity solves deliver. L_sky is the sky's downwelling longwave on a horizontal surface and defaults to forcing.lw_down. Shortwave is a precomputed sw::ShortwaveSolution, or is solved here from sun (anything Radiosity.solar_unit_vector accepts), dni and dhi; with sun = nothing only the diffuse sky dhi is applied. It does not depend on the facet temperatures.

The implicit longwave exchange

The net longwave a facet absorbs is linear in the emission of every facet, $N_i = \sum_j J_{ij} E_j + j^{sky}_i L_{sky}$ (Radiosity.longwave_response). Linearising $E_j = ε_jσT_j^4$ about the current skin temperatures gives the coupled balance of facet i,

\[R_{n,i} - H_i - LE_i \approx F_{0,i} + F'_{ii}\,x_i + \sum_{j\ne i} G_{ij}\,x_j, \qquad G_{ij} = J_{ij}\,4ε_jσT_j^3,\]

where x are the θ-weighted skin increments of the step and $F'_{ii}$ combines the radiative $G_{ii}$ with the turbulent derivatives. The diagonal term closes each facet column as in step_surface!(::UrbanFacet, …). For the off-diagonal term, each column step is affine in an extra imposed surface flux, $x_i = a_i + b_i c_i$ (two trial steps give a and b); the dense system $(I - \mathrm{diag}(b)\,G_{off})\,x = a$ then gives every facet's increment at once, and the columns are advanced with $c_i = \sum_{j≠i} G_{ij} x_j$.

Solving the coupled system

solver = :iterative (the default) solves it matrix-free with restarted GMRES: each iteration is one product with the precomputed radiosity response J ($G\,v = J\,(∂E/∂T ∘ v)$), O(n²) work and O(n) extra memory, and no n × n matrix is formed or factorised per step. The system is strongly diagonally dominant — $b_i$ is the skin warming per W m⁻² of extra flux (≲ 1/(4εσT³ + h + conduction)) and $\sum_j |G_{ij}|$ is at most the longwave conductance towards the other facets, so $b_i\sum_{j≠i}|G_{ij}| < 1$ — and GMRES converges in a few iterations; it stops when the residual 2-norm is below tol × max(‖a‖₂, 1 K) and throws if maxiter products do not get there. The longwave at the start of the step is also evaluated from J (Radiosity.solve_longwave(scene, vf, response)), so a step costs O(n²); only a change of emissivities refactorises (O(n³), once). solver = :direct assembles the dense $(n+m)^2$ matrix and LU-factorises it (O(n³) per step): the reference the test suite compares against, to 1e-10 K. u.solve_iterations and u.solve_residual report the last solve.

What this buys, and why a lagged coupling is not enough: the longwave that facet j emits extra during the step, $G_{\cdot j}x_j$, is the longwave the other facets absorb. So the exchange conserves energy exactly: in an enclosure with no sky, $\sum_i A_i N_i = 0$ for the applied fluxes to round-off, and with a sky, lw_net equals the radiosity solution for the applied emission lw_emission. With coupling = :lagged each facet instead steps alone against the incident longwave frozen at the start of the step (step_surface! with lw_down = Q_i) — its own extra emission leaves it but reaches no one — which is provided only as the reference the test suite measures the conservation defect of.

kwargs go to SurfaceExchange.turbulent_fluxes.

Steady thermal transmittance (U-value, W m⁻² K⁻¹) of a plane construction between inside and outside air, $U = 1 / (R_{si} + Σ d_i/k_i + R_{se})$ (ISO 6946). Defaults are the ISO 6946 surface resistances for a wall.
