Building interior — BuildingEnergy

BuildingEnergy (Phase 50) puts a room behind the urban facets: a prognostic indoor air temperature coupled to the heat stored in the envelope and in the building's internal structure, with glazing, infiltration and ventilation. Theory and validation are on Building energy: indoor air and thermal mass.

Nothing about conduction is re-implemented here. Every wall, roof, floor and partition is an UrbanSurface.ConductionColumn, stepped by UrbanSurface.step_conduction!, or a whole UrbanFacet, stepped by its own step_surface!. The zone only supplies their interior boundary condition (the air temperature and the radiative gains) and closes the air-node balance.

The API has three parts:

  • Elements: EnvelopeElement (the exterior is outdoor air with a sol-air source, the ground, or adiabatic), FacetElement (an UrbanFacet with the full surface energy balance outside) and InternalMass (one or two faces exposed to the room).

  • The zone: BuildingZone, ZoneForcing and step_zone!, which returns a ZoneBudget. Diagnostics: zone_air_temperature, zone_operative_temperature, zone_mean_radiant_temperature, zone_heat_content, zone_heat_loss_coefficient, ventilation_conductance, air_heat_capacity and element_transmittance.

  • The ISO 13790 reference: ISO13790Zone, step_iso13790!, iso13790_zone (the 5R1C parameters derived from a BuildingZone), iso13790_ideal_load! (the §C.4.2 heating/cooling need) and iso13786_areal_heat_capacity.

  • HVAC and exhaust heat (#32; theory: HVAC demand and exhaust heat): IdealLoadsHVAC, passed as step_zone!(zone, forcing, dt, hvac), which also returns an HVACBudget. Equipment curves: BiquadraticCurve, DXCoolingCurves, DX_CURVES_WINDOW_AC, DX_CURVES_PACKAGED_AC, DX_RATING_POINT, cooling_cop, available_cooling_capacity and condenser_inlet_temperature. Humidity: ZoneForcing(q_out, Q_latent), zone_specific_humidity, zone_relative_humidity.

  • Anthropogenic heat (#29; theory: Anthropogenic heat from the building energy model): BuildingHeatRelease, building_heat_release, energy_consumption, total_heat_release, anthropogenic_sensible_heat, anthropogenic_latent_heat; zones behind the facets of an UrbanFacetScene: building_facets, scene_building_zone, step_buildings!, anthropogenic_heat_maps.

  • Occupancy and internal gains (#33; theory and ASHRAE 140 validation: Occupancy schedules and BESTEST validation): DayProfile, WeeklySchedule, schedule_value, schedule_mean, full_load_hours; InternalGains → internal_gains → InternalGainRates, passed as ZoneForcing(rates; T_out, …); occupant_sensible_heat; ThermostatSchedule, scheduled_hvac; the DOE medium-office reference: DOE_MEDIUM_OFFICE_SCHEDULES, office_internal_gains, office_thermostat.

A zone behind two facets

using Aeolus

roof  = FacetElement(UrbanFacet(:roof, example_roof_layers(); T_interior = 294.0), 48.0)
walls = FacetElement(UrbanFacet(:wall, example_wall_layers(); T_interior = 294.0,
                                h_convective = 15.0), 63.6)
floor = EnvelopeElement([WallLayer(:xps_insulation, 0.1), WallLayer(:concrete, 0.15)], 48.0;
                        exterior = :adiabatic, h_inside = 1 / 0.17)
part  = InternalMass([WallLayer(:brick, 0.12)], 20.0; sides = 2)

zone = BuildingZone(; volume = 129.6, floor_area = 48.0, envelope = [roof, walls, floor],
                    internal_mass = [part], window_area = 12.0, window_U = 2.8,
                    infiltration_ach = 0.5, T_air = 294.0)

for step in 1:144
    t = 600.0 * step
    day = sin(2π * (t / 86400 - 0.25))
    sf = SurfaceForcing(sw_down = max(0, 800day), lw_down = 330.0, T_air = 290 + 5day, U = 3.0)
    b = step_zone!(zone, ZoneForcing(T_out = 290 + 5day, Q_internal = 500.0,
                                     Q_solar = max(0, 600day), surface = sf), 600.0)
    # b.T_air, b.Q_ventilation, … ; facet_fluxes(roof) is what the canopy air receives
end

    # r.lighting + r.equipment is the electric demand; h.Q_heating the heating load

Anthropogenic heat: buildings behind the facets of a street

Issue #29 (theory: Anthropogenic heat from the building energy model). scene_building_zone makes one zone per building whose envelope is the scene's roof and wall facets; step_buildings! solves the facets and the zones in one implicit system. building_heat_release splits what a zone and its plant give to the outdoors (BuildingHeatRelease: total_heat_release, energy_consumption, anthropogenic_sensible_heat, anthropogenic_latent_heat), and anthropogenic_heat_maps puts the direct part on the plan-area grid next to aggregate_facet_fluxes.

hm = zeros(8, 12); hm[1:3, :] .= 20; hm[6:8, :] .= 20      # two blocks, H/W = 2
scene = build_scene(hm, 5.0, 5.0; wall_subdivisions = 2)
u = UrbanFacetScene(scene, view_factors(scene); T = 285.0,
                    options = f -> f.kind === :wall ? (h_convective = 10.0, T_interior = 293.15) :
                                   f.kind === :roof ? (T_interior = 293.15,) : (T_interior = 285.15,))
west = falses(8, 12); west[1:3, :] .= true
east = falses(8, 12); east[6:8, :] .= true
zones = [scene_building_zone(u, fp; window_area = 120.0, window_U = 2.8) for fp in (west, east)]
hvac  = [IdealLoadsHVAC(T_heat = 293.15, T_cool = 297.15, heating = :boiler) for _ in zones]
air = SurfaceForcing(lw_down = 280.0, T_air = 278.15, U = 2.0, z_ref = 30.0)
f = [ZoneForcing(T_out = 278.15, Q_internal = 5.0 * z.floor_area) for z in zones]
fl, budgets, hb = step_buildings!(u, zones, f, air, 900.0; hvac)
rel = [building_heat_release(z, fk, b; dt = 900.0, hvac = h, hvac_budget = hbk)
       for (z, fk, b, h, hbk) in zip(zones, f, budgets, hvac, hb)]
QF = anthropogenic_heat_maps(u, zones, rel)       # W m⁻² of plan area, (H, LE)
agg = aggregate_facet_fluxes(u, fl)                # the facets' own H, LE, …
H_total = agg.H .+ QF.H                            # what the air above each column receives

Aeolus.BuildingEnergy — Module
BuildingEnergy

Phase 50 — the building interior behind the urban facets: a prognostic indoor air temperature coupled to the thermal mass of the envelope and of the building's internal structure, with ventilation and infiltration.

The zone is an RC network whose capacitances are resolved:

  • Air node — the zone air, heat capacity $C_a = ρ c_p V$ (ρ and $c_p$ from MoistThermodynamics), advanced implicitly.
  • Envelope — walls, roofs and floors are UrbanSurface conduction columns, reused as they are (no copy of the solver): either a bare EnvelopeElement (outside air with a sol-air source, the ground, or an adiabatic party wall) or a FacetElement wrapping a full UrbanFacet whose outer face is closed by the surface energy balance of SurfaceExchange.
  • Internal mass — floors, partitions and furniture as InternalMass columns exposed to the room on one or both faces.
  • Massless conductances — glazing (a U-value times area; glazing physics is issue #27), infiltration and mechanical ventilation with optional heat recovery.

Interior surfaces exchange with the air node through the combined (convective + radiative) ISO 6946 surface coefficient $h = 1/R_{si}$, and receive the radiative part of internal gains and the transmitted solar gain as an imposed flux. step_zone! is implicit in the air temperature and conserves the zone's heat content exactly.

Occupancy (issue #33): WeeklySchedules of piecewise-constant DayProfiles drive InternalGains (people, lighting, equipment, after the EnergyPlus objects) and a ThermostatSchedule; the DOE medium-office reference building's schedules are DOE_MEDIUM_OFFICE_SCHEDULES.

For reference the module also carries the ISO 13790 simple hourly 5R1C model (ISO13790Zone), with a mapping (iso13790_zone) that derives its parameters from the same construction.

Sign conventions

All zone budget terms (ZoneBudget) are in W and positive when they add heat to the zone. Column fluxes follow UrbanSurface: positive into the column.

  • ISO 13786:2007. Thermal performance of building components — Dynamic thermal characteristics — Calculation methods. Annex A (simplified estimate of the internal areal heat capacity).
  • ISO 6946:2017, Table 7 (interior surface resistances).
  • Bueno, B., Norford, L., Pigeon, G. & Britter, R. (2011). Combining a detailed building energy model with a physically-based urban canopy model. Boundary-Layer Meteorol., 140, 471-489 (TEB-BEM: the zone air node coupled to TEB wall and roof columns, with internal mass).
  • Incropera / Bergman et al. (2011), Fundamentals of Heat and Mass Transfer, 7th ed., §5.2 (lumped capacitance) and §5.5 (plane wall with convection, $β \tan β = Bi$), used in the tests.

Aeolus.BuildingEnergy.BuildingHeatRelease — Type
BuildingHeatRelease

Where the energy a building uses goes, over one zone step (issue #29). All terms are in W. The released terms are positive when heat goes from the building to the outdoor environment.

Energy consumed

  • P_electric — lighting and plug loads, cooling equipment, and electric heating (resistance or heat pump);
  • P_fuel — boiler fuel input;
  • Q_metabolic — occupants' metabolic heat (sensible + latent).

Released to the outdoor air directly — what an urban canopy model receives as the building part of the anthropogenic heat flux $Q_F$:

  • Q_exhaust_sensible, Q_exhaust_latent — HVAC plant heat rejection (condenser, cooling tower, boiler flue; see HVACBudget);
  • Q_ventilation_sensible — $H_{ve}(T_{air} - T_{out})$, carried out by infiltration and ventilation air;
  • Q_ventilation_latent — $L_v$ times the net vapour carried out by the same air ($-L_v W_{ventilation}$);
  • Q_windows — conduction through glazing, $U_w A_w (T_{air} - T_{out})$;
  • Q_lost — internal gains removed by return or exhaust air without entering the zone (the lost part of InternalGainRates);
  • Q_latent_vented — latent internal gains that did not enter the zone's vapour balance (all of them when the zone humidity is not advanced), assumed vented;
  • Q_envelope_air — net heat leaving the outer faces of :air EnvelopeElements. Their outer face has no surface energy balance of its own, so what they give up is a direct source for the air (negative in sunshine, when they absorb more than they lose).

Released through the urban surfaces

  • Q_envelope_facets — heat conducted out through the outer faces of FacetElements, $-Σ A G$. It is not a direct source: it is already in the facet's surface energy balance, where it raises the skin temperature and leaves as the facet's $H$, $LE$ and emitted long-wave. Adding it to the air as well would count it twice.
  • Q_envelope_ground — heat conducted into the ground.

Other inputs and storage

  • Q_solar — solar gain transmitted through the glazing (not anthropogenic);
  • Q_prescribed — a prescribed ZoneForcing.Q_hvac, an energy input from a system the model does not represent;
  • storage_sensible — rate of change of the zone's sensible heat content, $ΔE/Δt$ (zone_heat_content);
  • storage_latent — rate of change of the latent heat held by the zone air's vapour, $L_v ρV Δq/Δt$.

The first law

Summing the zone balance (ZoneBudget), the vapour balance, the plant (HVACBudget) and the split of the internal gains gives, exactly for every step,

\[Q_{released} + \frac{ΔE}{Δt} + L_v ρV\frac{Δq}{Δt} = P_{electric} + P_{fuel} + Q_{metabolic} + Q_{solar} + Q_{prescribed},\]

with $Q_{released}$ the sum of all released terms, total_heat_release. Over a period at the end of which the building is back in its initial state, the heat released equals the energy consumed plus the transmitted sunshine: that is the premise of the inventory method of estimating $Q_F$ (Sailor 2011), which a building energy model resolves in time, because storage delays the release (a building heated at night gives the heat back through the next day).

References

  • Sailor, D. J. (2011). A review of methods for estimating anthropogenic heat and moisture emissions in the urban environment. Int. J. Climatol., 31, 189-199.
  • Bueno, B., Pigeon, G., Norford, L. K., Zibouche, K. & Marchadier, C. (2012). Development and evaluation of a building energy model integrated in the TEB scheme. Geosci. Model Dev., 5, 433-448 (TEB-BEM: HVAC waste heat and the air exchanged by the building are released to the canyon; the envelope exchanges through the wall and roof energy balances).
Aeolus.BuildingEnergy.BuildingZone — Type
BuildingZone(; volume, floor_area, envelope = [], internal_mass = [],
             window_area = 0, window_U = 0, infiltration_ach = 0.5,
             ventilation_ach = 0, heat_recovery = 0,
             radiative_fraction = 0.5, T_air = 293.15,
             q_air = nothing, p_ref = 101325,
             thermo = DEFAULT_CONSTANTS, air_capacity = nothing)

A single thermal zone of volume m³ and floor_area m².

  • envelope — EnvelopeElements and FacetElements; internal_mass — InternalMasses.
  • Glazing: a massless conductance $H_w = U_w A_w$ (W K⁻¹). Solar transmitted through it enters through ZoneForcing.Q_solar.
  • Air exchange (ISO 13790 §9.3): infiltration and mechanical ventilation air-change rates (h⁻¹), both supplied from outdoors; a heat-recovery efficiency $η$ preheats the ventilation supply to $T_{out} + η (T_{air} - T_{out})$. The ventilation conductance is $H_{ve} = ρ c_p V (n_{inf} + (1 - η) n_{vent}) / 3600$.
  • radiative_fraction of internal gains, and all transmitted solar gain, is spread over the interior surfaces in proportion to area; the rest of the internal gains is convective (to the air node).
  • air_capacity (J K⁻¹) overrides $C_a = ρ c_p V$, where $ρ = p_{ref}/(R_d T_{air})$ and $c_p$ are the dry-air values of thermo (MoistThermodynamics) at construction. Many BEMs multiply $C_a$ by a furniture factor; here furniture is internal mass.
  • q_air — indoor specific humidity (kg kg⁻¹); by default 50 % relative humidity at the initial T_air and p_ref. It is advanced only when the forcing supplies an outdoor humidity (ZoneForcing.q_out), by an implicit moisture balance of the air mass $ρV$ with the same air-change rates (heat recovery is sensible only), latent internal gains, and dehumidification by an IdealLoadsHVAC. Hygroscopic buffering by the walls and furniture is not modelled.

The interior long-wave exchange between surfaces is lumped into the surface coefficients to the air node (the ISO 6946 combined $h$), so the node is strictly a mix of air and radiant temperature; see zone_operative_temperature.

Aeolus.BuildingEnergy.DXCoolingCurves — Type
DXCoolingCurves(capacity, eir; name = :custom)

The temperature modifiers of a direct-expansion (DX) cooling unit in the DOE-2 / EnergyPlus Coil:Cooling:DX:SingleSpeed form: two BiquadraticCurves of the coil entering wet-bulb temperature $x$ (°C, the indoor air) and the condenser entering dry-bulb temperature $y$ (°C, see condenser_inlet_temperature), normalised to 1 at the rating point (ANSI/AHRI 210/240 "A": 26.7 °C dry bulb / 19.4 °C wet bulb indoors, 35 °C outdoors):

  • capacity — $CAP_{FT}$: available total cooling capacity over its rated value;
  • eir — $EIR_{FT}$: energy input ratio (electric power per unit cooling, $1/COP$) over its rated value, so $COP = COP_{rated} / EIR_{FT}$.

Two published curve sets from the EnergyPlus distribution are provided: DX_CURVES_WINDOW_AC and DX_CURVES_PACKAGED_AC.

Aeolus.BuildingEnergy.DayProfile — Type

DayProfile(value)

A piecewise-constant daily profile in the form of an EnergyPlus Schedule:Compact day: values[i] holds from until[i-1] (0 for the first entry) up to until[i] hours of local clock time. The breakpoints must increase strictly and the last must be 24. DayProfile(value) is constant all day.

Aeolus.BuildingEnergy.HVACBudget — Type
HVACBudget

What an IdealLoadsHVAC did over the last step_zone!(zone, forcing, dt, hvac), in W (kg s⁻¹ for water) and non-negative unless stated:

  • Q_heating — sensible heat delivered to the zone air;
  • Q_cooling_sensible, Q_cooling_latent — sensible heat and latent heat ($L_v$ times the condensed vapour W_condensed) removed from the zone; Q_cooling is their sum, the coil load;
  • COP — operating cooling COP; T_condenser — condenser inlet temperature (K); capacity — available total cooling capacity;
  • P_cooling — electric power of the cooling equipment; P_heating — energy input of the heating (fuel for a boiler);
  • Q_exhaust_sensible (may be negative: heat-pump heating), Q_exhaust_latent — heat released to the outdoor air; W_evaporated — water evaporated by an evaporative condenser (kg s⁻¹);

Aeolus.BuildingEnergy.ISO13790Zone — Type

             h_is = 3.45, h_ms = 9.1, θ_m = 293.15)

The ISO 13790:2008 simple hourly model (§7.2.2, Annex C): five conductances and one capacitance between the outdoor, supply-air, air ($θ_{air}$), central surface ($θ_s$) and mass ($θ_m$) nodes.

  • $H_{tr,is} = h_{is} A_t$, $A_t = Λ_{at} A_f$;
  • $H_{tr,ms} = h_{ms} A_m$;
  • $H_{tr,em} = 1/(1/H_{tr,op} - 1/H_{tr,ms})$ — the opaque envelope conductance $H_{tr,op}$ minus the mass-to-surface part;

Aeolus.BuildingEnergy.IdealLoadsHVAC — Type
IdealLoadsHVAC(; T_heat = 293.15, T_cool = 299.15,
               heating_capacity = Inf, cooling_capacity = Inf,
               heating = :boiler, heating_efficiency = <by kind>,
               cop_rated = 3.0, curves = DX_CURVES_WINDOW_AC,
               condenser = :air_cooled, evaporative_effectiveness = 0.9,
               latent_fraction = 0.85, dehumidify = false, RH_max = 0.6)

An ideal-loads HVAC system (the EnergyPlus ZoneHVAC:IdealLoadsAirSystem idea, as in the TEB-BEM of Bueno et al. 2012): each step it supplies to the air node exactly the sensible heat that puts the end-of-step air temperature on the violated setpoint — T_heat below, T_cool above; nothing in the dead band — limited by the available capacity. Because the zone step is implicit and affine in the air temperature, the load is obtained in closed form from the zone's two-solve assembly and the setpoint is held exactly when the capacity suffices.

Cooling equipment. A DX unit with rated cop_rated and rated total (sensible + latent) cooling_capacity, modified by the DXCoolingCurves curves (nothing: constant COP and capacity) evaluated at the zone air wet bulb and the condenser inlet temperature at the start of the step. Its electric power is $P = Q_{cool}/COP$. With dehumidify = true the coil also removes the vapour that would push the indoor relative humidity above RH_max; that latent load shares the capacity (sensible first) and the COP. Re-heat, part-load degradation and fan power are not modelled.

Heat rejection — the exhaust heat. The condenser rejects $Q_{cool} + P = Q_{cool}(1 + 1/COP)$ to the outdoor air:

  • condenser = :air_cooled: all of it as sensible heat;

  • condenser = :evaporative (evaporatively cooled condenser or a cooling tower): a fraction latent_fraction as latent heat, i.e. as evaporated water, the rest sensible. The default 0.85 is the engineering rule of thumb for wet cooling towers — evaporation of 0.00085 of the circulating flow per °F of range, against 0.001 if all the heat went into evaporation; published values span 60-95 % (Perry's Chemical Engineers' Handbook, as summarised in Primo, Cooling Towers — Basic Calculations, PDHonline M374). The curves then see the evaporatively cooled condenser inlet temperature.

    The two halves come from different models. The COP uses the EnergyPlus pre-cooling-pad inlet temperature. The sensible/latent split uses the wet-tower rule. In the EnergyPlus pad model the evaporated water is $\dot m_{cond}\,Δw$ across the pad. It depends on the condenser air flow, not on the heat rejected, and the coil itself rejects sensible heat. W_evaporated is therefore an estimate within the 60-95 % band, not the EnergyPlus water use. The sum $Q_{exh,s} + Q_{exh,l}$ is exact either way.

Heating equipment (heating, with heating_efficiency):

  • :boiler — fuel input $Q/η$ (default $η = 0.9$); the flue loss $Q(1/η - 1)$ is sensible exhaust;
  • :electric — resistance heating, $η = 1$, no exhaust;
  • :heat_pump — electric input $Q/COP_h$ (default $COP_h = 3$); the $Q(1 - 1/COP_h)$ taken from the outdoor air is a negative sensible exhaust.

heating_capacity = 0 or cooling_capacity = 0 removes the heating or cooling system.

The plant obeys the first law exactly: exhaust (sensible + latent) = energy input + heat extracted from the zone − heat delivered to it (see HVACBudget).

Aeolus.BuildingEnergy.step_buildings! — Method
step_buildings!(u::UrbanFacetScene, zones, forcings, air, dt;
                hvac = nothing, L_sky, sw = nothing, sun = nothing,
                dni = 0, dhi = 0, samples = 2, δT = 0.01, kwargs...)
    -> (fluxes, budgets, hvac_budgets)

Advance the facets of an urban scene and the building zones behind them together, as one implicit system (issue #29).

A zone is behind scene facets when its envelope holds FacetElements wrapping the scene's own UrbanFacets — the same objects, FacetElement(u.facets[i], area) with area the facet's area (see scene_building_zone). Each such facet then has the zone air as its interior environment and the rest of the canyon outside, and the zone sees the facet as part of its envelope. Other envelope elements and internal mass are stepped by the zone as in step_zone!.

zones and forcings are vectors of BuildingZone and ZoneForcing (the surface of a ZoneForcing only matters for facet elements that are not in the scene). air, L_sky and the shortwave keywords are those of UrbanSurface.step_urban_facets!. hvac is nothing or a vector with an IdealLoadsHVAC (or nothing) per zone.

The coupled step

step_urban_facets! treats the longwave exchange between facets implicitly by measuring each facet column's affine response to an extra imposed surface flux. The same column is also affine in the temperature of its interior environment, and the zone air balance is affine in the air temperature, so the facets' skin increments and the zones' air temperature increments are solved together in one dense linear system. The exchange across the envelope is as implicit as the exchange between facets and between a zone and its internal mass: a lagged coupling of the air node (air time constant: minutes) would be unstable at canopy time steps.

An ideal-loads HVAC is solved by active set: a zone whose free-floating temperature leaves its dead band is pinned on the violated setpoint and the load that holds it becomes the unknown; a load beyond capacity is clamped and the zone freed again. For one zone this is exactly the logic of step_zone!(zone, forcing, dt, hvac), and the two agree.

Returns the per-facet SurfaceFluxes (as step_urban_facets!), a ZoneBudget per zone, and an HVACBudget (or nothing) per zone. Energy is conserved exactly: every zone satisfies its budget identity with its facet columns included, and heat crosses the envelope without loss.

Aeolus.BuildingEnergy.step_iso13790! — Function
step_iso13790!(z::ISO13790Zone, θ_e, dt = 3600; Φ_int = 0, Φ_sol = 0,
               Φ_HC = 0, θ_sup = θ_e) -> (θ_air, θ_s, θ_m)

One step of the ISO 13790 Annex C scheme (eqs. C.1-C.11), with the standard gain split

  • $Φ_{ia} = 0.5 Φ_{int}$ to the air node,
  • $Φ_m = (A_m/A_t)(0.5 Φ_{int} + Φ_{sol})$ to the mass node,
  • $Φ_{st} = (1 - A_m/A_t - H_{tr,w}/(9.1 A_t))(0.5 Φ_{int} + Φ_{sol})$ to the surface node,

and the Crank–Nicolson mass update

\[θ_{m,t} = \frac{θ_{m,t-1} (C_m/Δt - (H_{tr,3} + H_{tr,em})/2) + Φ_{m,tot}} {C_m/Δt + (H_{tr,3} + H_{tr,em})/2}\]

(the standard fixes $Δt$ = 3600 s). Φ_HC is a heating/cooling input to the air node. Returns the step's air and surface temperatures and the step-mean mass temperature.

Aeolus.BuildingEnergy.step_zone! — Method
step_zone!(zone, forcing::ZoneForcing, dt; kwargs...) -> ZoneBudget

Advance the zone by dt seconds. The air node balance

\[C_a \frac{T^{n+1} - T^n}{Δt} = Σ_j A_j h_j (T_{s,j} - T^{n+1}) + (U_w A_w + H_{ve})(T_{out} - T^{n+1}) + (1 - f_r) Φ_{int} + Φ_{hvac}\]

is solved implicitly in the air temperature, simultaneously with every element column (θ-scheme, UrbanSurface.step_conduction! / step_surface!) whose interior environment is $T^{n+1}$. The zone air time constant (minutes) is far shorter than any sensible step, so an explicit air update would be unstable; this one is unconditionally stable. Radiative gains $f_r Φ_{int} + Φ_{sol}$ are imposed on the interior faces, spread by exposed area; with no interior surface they go to the air.

When the forcing carries an outdoor humidity q_out, the indoor specific humidity is advanced after the temperature by the implicit vapour balance of the air mass $M = ρV$,

\[M \frac{q^{n+1} - q^n}{Δt} = M \frac{n_{inf} + n_{vent}}{3600} (q_{out} - q^{n+1}) + \frac{Φ_{lat}}{L_v(T^{n+1})} + W_{hvac}.\]

kwargs are passed to the step_surface! of facet elements (sf, c, δT, …). The step conserves zone_heat_content exactly.

step_zone!(zone, forcing, dt, hvac::IdealLoadsHVAC; kwargs...) -> (ZoneBudget, HVACBudget)

The same step with an ideal-loads HVAC system that holds the air temperature between its setpoints; see IdealLoadsHVAC. The ideal load is added to forcing.Q_hvac.

Aeolus.BuildingEnergy.traffic_heat_flux — Method
traffic_heat_flux(Q_urban, n_cells, n_road) -> W m⁻²

The heat flux released over each of n_road road cells when the vehicle part of the anthropogenic heat flux, $Q_V =$ Q_urban W m⁻² of urban area (n_cells columns), is concentrated on the roads: $Q_V n_\mathrm{cells} / n_\mathrm{road}$, so the domain mean is Q_urban.
