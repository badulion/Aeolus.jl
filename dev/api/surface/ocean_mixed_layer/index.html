Lake and sea surface — OceanMixedLayer

OceanMixedLayer (Phase 52, #38) makes the temperature of a water surface prognostic: a layered column with a Kraus–Turner mixed layer, shortwave penetration (Jerlov / Soloviev), a Fairall cool skin and a diurnal warm layer (Fairall 1996b / COARE, or Zeng & Beljaars 2005). The physics and the measured validation are in Water surfaces: mixed layer, cool skin and warm layer.

WaterColumn is an AbstractSurface of the surface shared core (SurfaceExchange): its fluxes come from SurfaceExchange.turbulent_fluxes, or from any flux(col, forcing) -> SurfaceFluxes passed to step_surface! — the hook for air–sea algorithms (AirSeaFlux, #39). Its emissivity default is SurfaceOptics.material_emissivity(:water); gravity, the von Kármán constant, the heat capacity and density of water, and the water conductivity used by the cool skin come from SurfaceConstants.

using Aeolus

# A 20 m lake of 1 m layers (resolves its own warm layer)…
lake = WaterColumn(dz = fill(1.0, 20), T = 290.0, S = 0.0,
                   solar = jerlov_water(:III))
# …or a 10 m coastal slab with a sub-grid warm layer.
sea = WaterColumn(dz = [10.0], T = 298.0, S = 35.0,
                  warm_layer = ZengBeljaarsWarmLayer())

f = SurfaceForcing(sw_down = 800.0, lw_down = 400.0, T_air = 297.0,
                   q_air = 0.016, U = 4.0)
fl = step_surface!(sea, f, 300.0)      # SurfaceFluxes; the column absorbs fl.G
surface_temperature(sea)               # top layer + warm layer - cool skin
Aeolus.OceanMixedLayer — Module
OceanMixedLayer

Lake and coastal-sea surface (Phase 52, issue #38): a layered water column with a Kraus–Turner / Niiler–Kraus bulk mixed layer, shortwave penetration, a Fairall et al. (1996a) cool skin and a diurnal warm layer (Fairall et al. 1996b, the COARE form, or the prognostic Zeng & Beljaars 2005 scheme), so the water surface temperature is prognostic.

The pieces, bottom-up:

  1. Equation of state — AbstractWaterEOS: the UNESCO (EOS-80) one-atmosphere seawater density, valid for fresh water (with its 4 °C density maximum) and seawater alike, and a linear EOS for analytic tests. Thermal expansion and haline contraction are its analytic derivatives.
  2. Shortwave penetration — SolarBands: sums of exponentials, with the Paulson & Simpson (1977) fits to the Jerlov water types (jerlov_water) and the Soloviev three-band profile that COARE and ECMWF use for the warm layer (soloviev_solar).
  3. Cool skin — cool_skin: the Saunders/Fairall conductive sublayer.
  4. Warm layer — FairallWarmLayer and ZengBeljaarsWarmLayer, both behind step_warm_layer!.
  5. The column — WaterColumn, an SurfaceExchange.AbstractSurface: heating by the surface fluxes and the penetrating shortwave, convective adjustment, and wind-driven entrainment by a mixed-layer energy budget.

Air–sea fluxes are not computed here: step_surface! takes them from SurfaceExchange.turbulent_fluxes or from any flux function passed in (the hook that AirSeaFlux, issue #39, will use for COARE and wave-dependent roughness). Energy bookkeeping: every joule the surface flux delivers ends up in the column or leaves through its bottom, to round-off; the cool skin and warm layer are diagnostic skin corrections that hold no heat of their own.

Aeolus.OceanMixedLayer.AbstractWarmLayer — Type
AbstractWarmLayer

A diurnal warm-layer scheme: the stably stratified, solar-heated layer a few metres deep that forms under weak wind. Implementations carry their own state and provide

  • step_warm_layer!(wl, sw_net, q_out, τ, dt; α, ρ_w, c_w, g, κ) — advance by dt (s) under net shortwave sw_net into the water, non-solar heat loss q_out ($LW_{up,net} + H + LE$, W m⁻², positive cooling the water) and wind stress τ (N m⁻²); α is the thermal expansion of the water;
  • warm_layer_warming(wl) — warming across the whole layer, $T(0^-) - T(-D)$ (K), and warm_layer_warming(wl, z) the part between the surface and depth z (m);
  • warm_layer_depth(wl) (m) and reset_warm_layer!(wl).
Aeolus.OceanMixedLayer.AbstractWaterEOS — Type
AbstractWaterEOS

An equation of state for lake or sea water at the surface pressure. A subtype implements water_density(eos, T, S) (kg m⁻³) with T in kelvin and salinity S in psu, and the derivatives water_thermal_expansion $α = -ρ^{-1} ∂ρ/∂T$ and water_haline_contraction $β = ρ^{-1} ∂ρ/∂S$.

Pressure (thermobaric) effects are ignored: the column compares densities at one atmosphere, which for lakes shallower than ~100 m shifts the 4 °C density maximum by less than 0.2 K.

Aeolus.OceanMixedLayer.CoolSkin — Type
CoolSkin(; ν_w = 1e-6, λ0 = 6.0, δ_max = 0.01,
           f_s = (0.065, 11.0, 6.6e-5, 8e-4), maxiter = 50)

Parameters of the Fairall et al. (1996a) cool skin: kinematic viscosity of water ν_w (m² s⁻¹), Saunders' constant λ0, the cap δ_max (m) on the sublayer thickness when the skin is not convectively unstable, and the coefficients of the fraction of net shortwave absorbed in the skin,

f_s(δ) = a + b δ - (c/δ) (1 - e^{-δ/d})

(Fairall et al. 1996a eq. 16 with the COARE 3.0+ value a = 0.065 of Fairall et al. 2003, replacing the original 0.137).

Aeolus.OceanMixedLayer.FairallWarmLayer — Type
FairallWarmLayer(; Ri_c = 0.65, D_max = 19, q_onset = 50, τ_min = 0.002,
                   solar = soloviev_solar(:coare))

The diurnal warm layer of Fairall et al. (1996b), exactly as coded in COARE 3.x (coare36vnWarm_et). From the moment the absorbed heat $q = f_x SW_{net} - q_{out}$ first exceeds q_onset (W m⁻²) in a day, the heat and momentum put into the layer are accumulated,

Q_ac = ∫ q dt,    I_ac = ∫ max(τ, τ_min) dt,

and a layer with a linear temperature and velocity profile, marginally stable at critical Richardson number Ri_c, has depth and warming

D  = I_ac [2 Ri_c c_w / (α g ρ_w Q_ac)]^{1/2}  (≤ D_max),
ΔT = 2 Q_ac / (ρ_w c_w D).

The absorbed solar fraction $f_x = 1 - (1/D)∫_0^D I(z)/I_0 dz$ is iterated five times with D, as in the reference code. If the accumulated heat turns negative the layer is wiped out ($f_x = 0.75$, $D = D_{max}$). Where the water's thermal expansion α ≤ 0 (fresh water below 3.98 °C) heating does not stabilise and the layer is reset; the same holds for ZengBeljaarsWarmLayer. The scheme is diagnostic in the accumulated integrals and assumes a daily reset: call reset_warm_layer! at local midnight (the WaterColumn does this from its local_time).

Aeolus.OceanMixedLayer.KrausTurnerMixing — Type
KrausTurnerMixing(; m = 0.5, n = 0.2, decay_depth = Inf)

Energetics of the bulk mixed layer (Kraus & Turner 1967; Niiler & Kraus 1977). Over a step dt the turbulence available to entrain water from below is

E = ρ₀ m u_{*w}³ dt e^{-h/h_d} + n ΔPE_conv          (J m⁻²)

— the wind-stirring work, m times the rate $ρ_0 u_{*w}^3$ at which the water-side friction velocity supplies turbulent energy (optionally dissipated over decay_depth $h_d$), plus the fraction n of the potential energy released by convective overturning. The mixed layer is then deepened (see wind_mixing!) until the potential-energy increase of mixing equals E. With n = 0 convection is non-penetrative (encroachment).

The coefficients are tunable; published bulk-model values span m ≈ 0.4–1.25 and n ≈ 0.15–0.2 (Niiler & Kraus 1977 and the models it reviews). The defaults sit inside that range and are not tuned against the tests here.

Aeolus.OceanMixedLayer.LinearWaterEOS — Type
LinearWaterEOS(; ρ0 = 1000, T0 = 283.15, S0 = 0, α = 2e-4, β = 7.6e-4)

$ρ = ρ_0 [1 - α (T - T_0) + β (S - S_0)]$ — constant expansion coefficients, for analytic tests (a linear temperature profile is then a constant buoyancy frequency).

Aeolus.OceanMixedLayer.NoWarmLayer — Type
NoWarmLayer()

No sub-grid warm layer: the top layer of the column is the bulk temperature right up to the skin. The right choice when the column's top layer is thin enough (≲ 0.5 m) to resolve the warm layer itself.

Aeolus.OceanMixedLayer.SolarBands — Type
SolarBands(fractions, scales)

Net shortwave transmitted to depth z (m, positive down) as a sum of exponentials,

I(z)/I(0) = Σᵢ aᵢ exp(-z/ζᵢ),    Σᵢ aᵢ = 1,

with fractions aᵢ and e-folding depths ζᵢ (m). Construct the standard profiles with jerlov_water or soloviev_solar.

Aeolus.OceanMixedLayer.UNESCOWaterEOS — Type
UNESCOWaterEOS()

The one-atmosphere International Equation of State of Seawater 1980 (EOS-80; Millero & Poisson 1981, UNESCO 1981):

ρ(t, S) = ρ_w(t) + A(t) S + B(t) S^{3/2} + C S²

with $ρ_w$ the SMOW density polynomial. Valid 0–40 °C, 0–42 psu; at S = 0 it is pure water, with the density maximum at 3.98 °C that drives lake overturning. t is converted from kelvin as $T - 273.15$ (the IPTS-68 / ITS-90 difference, < 0.01 K here, is ignored). Check values (Fofonoff & Millard 1983): ρ(5 °C, 0) = 999.96675, ρ(5 °C, 35) = 1027.67547, ρ(25 °C, 35) = 1023.34306 kg m⁻³.

Aeolus.OceanMixedLayer.WaterColumn — Type
WaterColumn(; dz = fill(1.0, 20), T = 288.15, S = 0.0,
              eos = UNESCOWaterEOS(), solar = jerlov_water(:IB),
              mixing = KrausTurnerMixing(), warm_layer = NoWarmLayer(),
              cool_skin = CoolSkin(), albedo = 0.06,
              emissivity = SurfaceOptics.material_emissivity(:water),
              z0m = 1e-4, z0h = 1e-5, z0q = z0h,
              bottom = :absorbing, bottom_flux = 0.0, local_time = 0.0,
              c = DEFAULT_SURFACE_CONSTANTS,
              ρ_ref = c.ρ_water, c_p = cp_water(c))

A lake or coastal-sea water column and an AbstractSurface. Layers of thickness dz (m, top first; the top one can be a thick slab) carry temperatures T (K; a scalar, a vector, or a function of centre depth) at a constant salinity S (psu).

Skin temperature (surface_temperature):

T_s = T₁ + ΔT_warm(dz₁/2) - ΔT_cool

— the top layer, plus the sub-grid warm layer's warming between the surface and the top layer's centre (warm_layer_warming), minus the cool skin. Use a warm-layer scheme when the top layer is thicker than the warm layer (a slab of a few metres or more); with thin top layers (≲ 0.5 m) keep NoWarmLayer(), since the column then resolves the warm layer itself. cool_skin = nothing switches the cool skin off.

One step_surface!:

  1. fluxes from SurfaceExchange.turbulent_fluxes (or the flux keyword), iterated with the cool skin;
  2. absorb_heat!: the non-solar flux $G - SW_{net}$ into the top layer, the net shortwave distributed by solar, the shortwave left at the bottom absorbed by the bottom layer (bottom = :absorbing, a lake bed) or lost (:transmitting, deep water), and bottom_flux (W m⁻², positive downward) removed from the bottom layer;
  3. mix_column!: convective adjustment, then wind and convective entrainment with the KrausTurnerMixing budget;
  4. the warm layer and the local clock (a FairallWarmLayer is reset at local midnight; set local_time, seconds after local midnight, to the start time).

Heat is conserved exactly: the column's heat content changes by $(G - SW_{bottom} - F_{bottom}) dt$ per step, to round-off.

Not included (left to other modules or later work): ice formation below the freezing point (IceThermodynamics, #37), wave-dependent roughness and COARE fluxes (AirSeaFlux, #39; pass them through flux), the rain heat flux, currents, shear instability at the mixed-layer base and background diffusion below it.

Aeolus.OceanMixedLayer.ZengBeljaarsWarmLayer — Type
ZengBeljaarsWarmLayer(; d = 3, ν = 0.3, solar = soloviev_solar(:zeng_beljaars),
                        stability = BusingerDyerLogLinear(), ΔT = 0)

The prognostic warm layer of Zeng & Beljaars (2005), as in the IFS (Cy43r1 Part IV §8.10.2). With the similarity profile $T(z) - T(-d) = ΔT [1 - (-z/d)^ν]$, the warming $ΔT = T_{-δ} - T_{-d}$ obeys

∂ΔT/∂t = (Q + R_s - R(-d)) / (d ρ_w c_w ν/(ν+1)) - (ν+1) κ u_{*w} ΔT / (d φ_t(d/L))

with $Q = -q_{out}$ the net non-solar flux into the water, $R_s - R(-d)$ the shortwave absorbed above depth d, water-side friction velocity $u_{*w} = \sqrt{τ/ρ_w}$, $L = ρ_w c_w u_{*w}^3/(κ F_d)$ and buoyancy flux

F_d = g α (Q + R_s - R(-d))                            if ΔT ≤ 0
F_d = ρ_w c_w u_{*w}² (ν g α ΔT / (5 d))^{1/2}          if ΔT > 0.

Integrated with the damping term implicit (unconditionally stable). Being prognostic, it needs no daily reset and a residual layer can survive a calm night. ΔT is kept ≥ 0: a layer colder than its base is convectively unstable and is mixed away.

At steady state under constant forcing the neutral (φt = 1) limit is ``ΔT = (Q + Rs - R(-d)) / (ρw cw ν κ u_{*w})``.

Aeolus.OceanMixedLayer.absorb_heat! — Method
absorb_heat!(col, Q_ns, sw_net, dt) -> SW leaving through the bottom (W m⁻²)

Heat the column for dt seconds: the non-solar flux Q_ns (W m⁻², positive into the water) into the top layer, the net shortwave sw_net layer by layer from col.solar, the shortwave reaching the bottom absorbed by the bottom layer (:absorbing) or returned (:transmitting), and col.bottom_flux removed from the bottom layer.

Aeolus.OceanMixedLayer.column_potential_energy — Method
column_potential_energy(col) -> J m⁻²

$Σ_k ρ(T_k, S) g z_k Δz_k$ with z the (negative) height of the layer centre: mixing a stable column raises it.

Aeolus.OceanMixedLayer.convective_adjustment! — Method
convective_adjustment!(col) -> released potential energy (J m⁻², ≥ 0)

Remove static instability by mixing (heat-conserving, thickness-weighted) every group of layers in which denser water lies above lighter water, using the column's equation of state — so fresh water cooled below 3.98 °C stays at the surface. Returns the potential energy the overturn released, the convective energy source of KrausTurnerMixing.

Aeolus.OceanMixedLayer.cool_skin — Method
cool_skin(cs::CoolSkin, Q_out, sw_net, LE, u_star_w;
          α, βS = 0, ρ_w, c_w, k_w, g, L_v, δ0 = 1e-3)
    -> (ΔT, δ, f_s)

Cool-skin temperature depression $ΔT = T_{-δ} - T_{skin}$ (K, positive when the skin is cooler than the water just below it) and the conductive sublayer thickness δ (m) (Saunders 1967; Fairall et al. 1996a):

q_c = Q_out - f_s(δ) SW_net,          ΔT = q_c δ / k_w
δ   = λ ν_w / u_{*w},  λ = λ0 [1 + (16 g c_w ρ_w ν_w³ B / (k_w² u_{*w}⁴))^{3/4}]^{-1/3}

with the water-side buoyancy flux (in heat-flux units) $B = α q_c + βS c_w LE / L_v$ — the second term is the salt left behind by evaporation, with βS = haline contraction × salinity (0 for a lake). If $B ≤ 0$ the skin is not convective and $δ = \min(δ_{max}, λ_0 ν_w / u_{*w})$. The fixed point in δ (f_s depends on δ) is iterated to machine precision.

Inputs (W m⁻², positive as named): Q_out the non-solar heat loss $LW_{up,net} + H + LE$, sw_net the net shortwave into the water, LE the latent heat flux, u_star_w the water-side friction velocity $u_* \sqrt{ρ_a/ρ_w}$.

jerlov_water(type = :IB) -> SolarBands

Paulson & Simpson (1977) two-band fit $I/I_0 = R e^{-z/ζ_1} + (1-R) e^{-z/ζ_2}$ for Jerlov's oceanic water types :I (clearest), :IA, :IB, :II, :III (most turbid; a fair choice for productive lakes and coastal water):

typeRζ₁ (m)ζ₂ (m)
I0.580.3523
IA0.620.6020
IB0.671.017
II0.771.514
III0.781.47.9

The first band is the red/near-infrared absorbed within a metre or so, the second the blue-green that penetrates.

Aeolus.OceanMixedLayer.salinity_humidity_factor — Method

Aeolus.OceanMixedLayer.solar_absorbed_between — Method

Aeolus.OceanMixedLayer.solar_mean_transmission — Method
solar_mean_transmission(p::SolarBands, D) -> (1/D) ∫₀ᴰ I(z)/I(0) dz

Depth-averaged transmission over the top D metres, $Σ a_i ζ_i (1 - e^{-D/ζ_i}) / D$. One minus it is the solar fraction that heats a layer with a linear warming profile — the fxp of the COARE warm-layer code.

Aeolus.OceanMixedLayer.solar_transmission — Method
solar_transmission(p::SolarBands, z) -> I(z)/I(0)

Fraction of the net surface shortwave reaching depth z ≥ 0 (m).

Aeolus.OceanMixedLayer.soloviev_solar — Function
soloviev_solar(variant = :coare) -> SolarBands

The three-band Soloviev profile used by the diurnal warm-layer schemes, fractions (0.28, 0.27, 0.45):

  • :coare — e-folding depths (0.014, 0.357, 12.82) m, as in the COARE 3.x warm-layer code (Fairall et al. 1996b);
  • :zeng_beljaars — attenuation (71.5, 2.8, 0.06) m⁻¹, i.e. depths (0.0140, 0.357, 16.7) m, as in Zeng & Beljaars (2005) and the IFS (Cy43r1 Part IV eq. 8.157).
Aeolus.OceanMixedLayer.step_warm_layer! — Function
step_warm_layer!(wl, sw_net, q_out, τ, dt; α, ρ_w, c_w, g, κ = 0.4) -> ΔT

Advance warm layer wl by dt seconds under net shortwave sw_net into the water, non-solar heat loss q_out (W m⁻², positive cooling) and wind stress τ (N m⁻²), with water thermal expansion α, density ρ_w, heat capacity c_w, gravity g and von Kármán κ. Returns the warming across the layer. See AbstractWarmLayer.

Aeolus.OceanMixedLayer.warm_layer_depth — Function

Depth of the warm layer (m): D for the Fairall scheme, the fixed d for Zeng–Beljaars.

Aeolus.OceanMixedLayer.warm_layer_warming — Function

Aeolus.OceanMixedLayer.wind_mixing! — Method
wind_mixing!(col, E) -> energy used (J m⁻²)

Deepen the mixed layer from the surface with turbulent energy E (J m⁻²): layers are absorbed one by one while the potential-energy cost of mixing them in is affordable; the first unaffordable layer is entrained partially — a fraction f of it joins the mixed layer, the rest keeps its temperature, and f is found by bisection so the cost equals the energy left. Heat is conserved exactly and the column's potential energy rises by the energy used. Sets col.h_mix.

Aeolus.SurfaceExchange.step_surface! — Method
step_surface!(col::WaterColumn, forcing, dt; flux = nothing,
              skin_iterations = 2, kwargs...) -> SurfaceFluxes

Advance the water column by dt seconds (see WaterColumn for the sequence). flux(col, forcing) -> SurfaceFluxes replaces the default turbulent_fluxes(col, forcing; c = col.c, kwargs...) — the hook for COARE-type air–sea fluxes; whatever it returns is exactly what the column absorbs ($G = R_n - H - LE$). The cool skin and the fluxes are iterated skin_iterations times so the returned fluxes are those of the final skin temperature.
