Snowpack

Snowpack (Phase 51, #34) is a multi-layer seasonal snowpack in the CLM / Crocus tradition: settling under overburden and destructive metamorphism, heat conduction with a linearised surface energy balance, melt, sublimation and deposition, fresh-snow accumulation, and layer splitting and merging that conserve mass and enthalpy exactly. LayeredSnowpack is an AbstractSurface: step_surface!(pack, forcing, dt) returns the SurfaceFluxes it applied, computed through SurfaceExchange.turbulent_fluxes. The physics is developed in Layered snowpack.

Material laws are chosen by dispatch (Anderson1976Compaction, Vionnet2012Compaction; Yen1981Conductivity, Sturm1997Conductivity, Jordan1991Conductivity, Calonne2011Conductivity; Anderson1976FreshSnow, Vionnet2012FreshSnow). Grain and albedo ageing (#35) are Flanner2006Metamorphism — the CLM/SNICAR dry-ageing table plus Brun (1989) wet growth — and SnowOpticsAlbedo, which hands the prognostic grain radius to SurfaceOptics.SnowSurface (Snow metamorphism and albedo). Liquid water (#36) plugs in through AbstractSnowLiquidWater.

Liquid water (#36): SnowBucketPercolation (Crocus / CLM tipping bucket) and SnowGravityDrainage (Colbeck 1972 kinematic wave, i.e. Richards' equation without capillarity, with the Calonne et al. 2012 permeability) retain water up to the irreducible content of ColeouLesaffre1998Retention or PoreFractionRetention, refreeze it in cold layers (snow_refreeze!) and return the rest as runoff. See Layered snowpack, Sect. 8.

pack = LayeredSnowpack(depth = 0.6, density = 300.0, T = 268.0,
                       liquid = SnowBucketPercolation())
add_rainfall!(pack, 10.0, 274.0)
runoff = snow_liquid_water!(pack.liquid, pack, 1800.0)
pack.budget.refreeze, snow_liquid_water_content(pack)
using Aeolus
pack = LayeredSnowpack(depth = 0.8, density = 200.0, T = 265.0,
                       compaction = Vionnet2012Compaction(),
                       conductivity = Sturm1997Conductivity(),
                       bottom = SnowGroundTemperature(273.15),
                       metamorphism = Flanner2006Metamorphism(),
                       albedo = SnowOpticsAlbedo(soot_ppmw = 0.02))
f = SurfaceForcing(sw_down = 200.0, lw_down = 250.0, T_air = 266.0,
                   q_air = 0.002, U = 4.0, p = 90000.0, z_ref = 10.0,
                   rain = 0.0, snow = 1e-4)
fl = step_surface!(pack, f, 3600.0)      # fl.Rn == fl.H + fl.LE + fl.G
snow_depth(pack), snow_bulk_density(pack), pack.grain[1], surface_albedo(pack)
Aeolus.Snowpack — Module
Snowpack

A layered seasonal snowpack in the tradition of CLM, SNOWPACK and Crocus: a column of snow layers, each carrying its ice and liquid-water mass, thickness and temperature, that settles under its own weight, conducts heat, melts, sublimates and accumulates fresh snow. The pack is a surface model: LayeredSnowpack implements SurfaceExchange.AbstractSurface, so its turbulent fluxes, net radiation and coupling to the atmosphere come from the shared core.

Material laws are dispatch hierarchies so a user (or a later phase) chooses among published parameterisations without touching the solver:

lawabstract typeimplementations
effective thermal conductivityAbstractSnowConductivityYen1981Conductivity, Sturm1997Conductivity, Jordan1991Conductivity, Calonne2011Conductivity
fresh-snow densityAbstractFreshSnowDensityAnderson1976FreshSnow, Vionnet2012FreshSnow
settling / densificationAbstractSnowCompactionAnderson1976Compaction, Vionnet2012Compaction

Grain and albedo ageing (#35) plug in through two hooks:

  • AbstractSnowMetamorphism (snow_metamorphism!, viscosity_grain_factor, fresh_grain_radius, refrozen_grain_radius) — Flanner2006Metamorphism evolves the optical grain radius with the CLM/SNICAR dry-ageing fits and Brun (1989) wet growth; ParabolicGrainGrowth is the SurfaceOptics ageing law; the default NoSnowMetamorphism keeps grains fixed.
  • AbstractSnowAlbedo (snowpack_albedo) — SnowOpticsAlbedo delegates to the Wiscombe-Warren SurfaceOptics.SnowSurface at the prognostic grain radius; the default is a constant.

Liquid water (#36) plugs in through one more:

  • liquid water (#36) — AbstractSnowLiquidWater (snow_liquid_water!). The default, InstantSnowRunoff, drains all liquid water — melt and rain — as runoff within the step. SnowBucketPercolation (Crocus / CLM) and SnowGravityDrainage (Colbeck 1972 kinematic wave) retain water up to an irreducible content (AbstractSnowWaterRetention, Coléou & Lesaffre 1998), refreeze it in cold layers (snow_refreeze!) and release the rest at the base.

Constants come from the contracts: ice and water density, $k_{ice}$, $k_{air}$ from SurfaceExchange.SurfaceConstants; $c_{ice}$, $c_w$, $L_f$ and g through its accessors (i.e. from MoistThermodynamics); the snow emissivity from SurfaceOptics.material_emissivity(:snow_fresh).

Thermal condition at the base of the snowpack.

Aeolus.Snowpack.AbstractSnowCompaction — Type
AbstractSnowCompaction

A densification law. Implementers extend snow_compaction_rate(law, layer; c), returning the fractional rate $C_R = (1/Δz) ∂Δz/∂t$ (s⁻¹, ≤ 0) of one layer, where layer is a named tuple with fields

fieldmeaning
ρbulk density, ice + liquid, kg m⁻³
ρ_icebulk density of the ice alone, $w_{ice}/Δz$, kg m⁻³
ρ_liqbulk density of liquid water, $w_{liq}/Δz$, kg m⁻³
Tlayer temperature, K
loadoverburden, kg m⁻² (mass above + half the layer's own)
f2grain-shape viscosity factor from the metamorphism hook

Layer mass is conserved by compaction, so $∂ \ln ρ/∂t = -C_R$.

Aeolus.Snowpack.AbstractSnowConductivity — Type
AbstractSnowConductivity

Effective thermal conductivity of snow as a function of bulk density. Implementers extend snow_thermal_conductivity(law, ρ; c) (W m⁻¹ K⁻¹, ρ in kg m⁻³). The spread between the published laws is real — at 300 kg m⁻³ they disagree by nearly a factor two — and reflects the dependence of heat transport on the ice microstructure, not just on ρ (Sturm et al. 1997; Calonne et al. 2011).

Aeolus.Snowpack.AbstractSnowLiquidWater — Type
AbstractSnowLiquidWater

Liquid water in the pack — the hook for percolation and refreezing (#36). Implementers extend snow_liquid_water!(scheme, pack, dt), which moves, retains or refreezes the per-layer m_liq (kg m⁻²) and returns the runoff leaving the base of the pack in the step (kg m⁻²). The pack records the enthalpy the runoff carries away, so a scheme only has to keep its own internal processes enthalpy-conserving.

Aeolus.Snowpack.AbstractSnowMetamorphism — Type
AbstractSnowMetamorphism

Grain metamorphism — the hook for snow ageing (#35). Implementers extend

  • snow_metamorphism!(scheme, pack, dt) — evolve the per-layer grain (optical radius, μm) and age (s) of pack;
  • viscosity_grain_factor(scheme, pack, i) — the Crocus $f_2$ factor by which angular grains (depth hoar) stiffen layer i against settling (Vionnet et al. 2012, Eq. 9). Default 1;
  • optionally fresh_grain_radius(scheme, pack, T_air) and refrozen_grain_radius(scheme).

Implementations: NoSnowMetamorphism, Flanner2006Metamorphism, ParabolicGrainGrowth. Neither #35 scheme tracks grain shape, so both keep $f_2 = 1$.

Aeolus.Snowpack.AbstractSnowWaterRetention — Type
AbstractSnowWaterRetention

Irreducible (residual) liquid water content of snow: the water a layer holds against gravity by capillarity. Implementers extend snow_irreducible_water(law, θ_i), returning the volumetric content $θ_r$ (m³ of water per m³ of snow) of snow whose volumetric ice content is $θ_i = w_{ice}/(ρ_{ice} Δz)$.

Aeolus.Snowpack.Anderson1976Compaction — Type
Anderson1976Compaction(; destructive = DestructiveMetamorphism(),
                         η0 = 9e5, c5 = 0.08, c6 = 0.023)

CLM4 compaction (Anderson 1976; Oleson et al. 2010, Eqs. 7.40-7.44): destructive metamorphism plus overburden,

C_R2 = -P_s / η,     η = η0 exp[c5 (T_f - T) + c6 ρ_ice]

with the load $P_s$ in kg m⁻² and η in kg s m⁻² ($η_0 = 9·10^5$). Pass destructive = nothing for overburden settling alone.

Aeolus.Snowpack.Anderson1976FreshSnow — Type
Anderson1976FreshSnow()

The temperature-only law of Anderson (1976) as used in CLM4 (Oleson et al. 2010):

ρ = 50                                  T ≤ T_f - 15
ρ = 50 + 1.7 (T - T_f + 15)^{3/2}       T_f - 15 < T ≤ T_f + 2
ρ = 50 + 1.7 · 17^{3/2} ≈ 169           T > T_f + 2
Aeolus.Snowpack.Calonne2011Conductivity — Type
Calonne2011Conductivity()

$k = 2.5·10^{-6} ρ² - 1.23·10^{-4} ρ + 0.024$ (Calonne et al. 2011), fitted to conductivities computed on 3-D tomography images of snow; it tends to still air (0.024) as ρ → 0.

Aeolus.Snowpack.ColeouLesaffre1998Retention — Type
ColeouLesaffre1998Retention()

The irreducible water content Coléou & Lesaffre (1998) measured by gravity drainage of wet-snow samples in a cold laboratory, in the volumetric form used by SNOWPACK (Wever et al. 2014, Eq. 1):

θ_r = 0.0264 + 0.0099 (1 - θ_i)/θ_i        θ_i ≤ 0.23
θ_r = 0.08 - 0.1023 (θ_i - 0.03)           0.23 < θ_i ≤ 0.812
θ_r = 0                                    θ_i > 0.812

The branches meet at $θ_i = 0.23$ (θr = 0.0595, to 4·10⁻⁶); at ``θi = 0.812$(745 kg m⁻³) the pores are too few and too closed to hold water. The content is capped at the pore volume$1 - θi``. Light snow holds more water per unit volume than dense snow: 0.062 at 200 kg m⁻³, 0.038 at 400 kg m⁻³ (ρice = 917).

Aeolus.Snowpack.ConstantSnowAlbedo — Type
ConstantSnowAlbedo(; α = 0.8)

A fixed broadband albedo (0.8: aged dry snow; fresh snow is ≈ 0.85-0.9).

Aeolus.Snowpack.DestructiveMetamorphism — Type
DestructiveMetamorphism(; c3 = 2.777e-6, c4 = 0.04, ρ_threshold = 100,
                          decay = 0.046, wet_factor = 2, wet_threshold = 0.01)

Settling by destructive metamorphism of new snow — the breakdown of dendritic crystals — after Anderson (1976), in the form of CLM4 (Oleson et al. 2010, Eqs. 7.40-7.41):

C_R1 = -c3 c1 c2 exp[-c4 (T_f - T)]

with $c_1 = 1$ for $ρ_{ice} ≤ ρ_{thr}$ and $\exp[-0.046 (ρ_{ice} - ρ_{thr})]$ above, and $c_2 = 2$ if the liquid bulk density exceeds 0.01 kg m⁻³. At the melting point new snow halves its thickness in ≈ 2.9 days from this term alone. CLM5 raised ρ_threshold to 175 kg m⁻³ (van Kampenhout et al. 2017).

Aeolus.Snowpack.Flanner2006Metamorphism — Type
Flanner2006Metamorphism(; table = snicar_aging_table(), interpolation = :linear,
                          C_wet = 4.22e-13, f_liq_max = 0.1,
                          r_min = 54.5, r_max = 1500, r_refrozen = 1000,
                          fresh_radius_max = r_min, scale = 1)

The CLM / SNICAR grain-ageing scheme (Flanner & Zender 2006; CLM5 Technical Note, Sect. 2.3.2.3, Eqs. 2.3.72-2.3.75; CTSM SnowAge_grain). Each step, in each layer:

  1. dry ageing — flanner_grain_radius with parameters from the SnicarAgingTable at the layer's temperature, temperature gradient (snow_temperature_gradient) and density, integrated exactly over the step at fixed parameters;
  2. wet ageing — Brun (1989), as in CLM: $dr/dt = 10^{18} C_1 f_{liq}^3 / (4π r^2)$ μm s⁻¹ with $C_1 = 4.22·10^{-13}$ m³ s⁻¹ and the liquid mass fraction $f_{liq}$ capped at 0.1, integrated exactly ($r^3$ grows linearly in time);
  3. bounded to [r_min, r_max] (the range of the SNICAR Mie tables).

scale multiplies both rates (CLM's xdrdt). Fresh snow falls with r_min (SSA = 60 m² kg⁻¹, the state the table's fits start from); fresh_radius_max > r_min enables CLM5's linear ramp from r_min at −30 °C to fresh_radius_max at 0 °C (the CLM5 tech note gives 204.5 μm). Refrozen water has r_refrozen (see mix_refrozen_grain!).

interpolation = :nearest reproduces CLM's table look-up; the default :linear blends the exact node solutions trilinearly (snicar_grain_radius), which removes the jumps in growth rate as a layer crosses a 5 K, 10 K m⁻¹ or 50 kg m⁻³ cell boundary.

Deviation from CLM: CLM integrates both rates with one forward-Euler step; here each is integrated exactly over the step at the layer's current temperature, gradient, density and liquid fraction.

Aeolus.Snowpack.InstantSnowRunoff — Type
InstantSnowRunoff()

Default: all liquid water (melt and rain) leaves the pack as runoff in the step it appears. There is no retention and no refreezing.

Aeolus.Snowpack.Jordan1991Conductivity — Type
Jordan1991Conductivity()

$k = k_{air} + (7.75·10^{-5} ρ + 1.105·10^{-6} ρ²)(k_{ice} - k_{air})$ (Jordan 1991), the CLM law (Oleson et al. 2010, Eq. 6.71). It reaches $k_{ice}$ at the density of ice.

Aeolus.Snowpack.LayeredSnowpack — Type
LayeredSnowpack <: AbstractSurface

A multi-layer snowpack. Per-layer state, ordered top (1) to bottom: dz (m), m_ice, m_liq (kg m⁻²), T (K), grain (optical grain radius, μm; evolved by #35) and age (s). Laws: compaction, conductivity, fresh_density; hooks: metamorphism, albedo, liquid; lower boundary bottom; layering; surface properties emissivity, z0m, z0h; heat-equation time weight θ (1 = backward Euler, ½ = Crank-Nicolson); constants consts; cumulative budget.

The skin temperature is the temperature of the top layer (as in CLM), which is why the default top layer is only 2 cm thick.

Aeolus.Snowpack.LayeredSnowpack — Method
LayeredSnowpack(; depth = 0, density = 250, T = 268.15,
                  compaction = Anderson1976Compaction(),
                  conductivity = Jordan1991Conductivity(),

A pack of uniform density (kg m⁻³) and temperature T (K), depth metres deep, divided into layers by the layering rules. The defaults are the CLM4 set of laws (Oleson et al. 2010). depth = 0 is bare ground waiting for snow.

Aeolus.Snowpack.NoSnowMetamorphism — Type
NoSnowMetamorphism()

Default metamorphism: layers age (age += dt), grains keep their size.

Aeolus.Snowpack.ParabolicGrainGrowth — Type
ParabolicGrainGrowth(; growth_rate = SurfaceOptics.SNOW_GROWTH_RATE)

The grain growth that SurfaceOptics.SnowSurface applies for its age_days argument, $r^2 = r_0^2 + G t$ (G in μm² day⁻¹), made prognostic: each step delegates to SurfaceOptics.snow_grain_radius with the layer's current radius as $r_0$. Parabolic growth composes exactly, so a pack aged with this scheme and a SnowSurface of the same age agree to round-off. It has no temperature, gradient or liquid-water dependence — a reference, not a substitute for Flanner2006Metamorphism.

Aeolus.Snowpack.PoreFractionRetention — Type
PoreFractionRetention(; fraction = 0.05)

A holding capacity that is a fixed fraction of the pore volume, $θ_r = f (1 - θ_i)$. Crocus uses $f = 0.05$ (Vionnet et al. 2012, Eq. 19, after Pahaut 1976); CLM uses the irreducible saturation $S_r = 0.033$ (Oleson et al. 2010, Sect. 7.2.2, Eq. 7.26).

Aeolus.Snowpack.SnicarAgingTable — Type
SnicarAgingTable

Best-fit parameters of the Flanner & Zender (2006) dry-snow ageing model, on a grid of temperature T (K, 223.15-273.15, 11 points), temperature gradient dTdz (K m⁻¹, 0-300, 31 points) and density ρ (kg m⁻³, 50-400, 8 points). Arrays are indexed [ρ, dTdz, T]:

fieldmeaningunit
drdt0initial growth rate $(dr/dt)_0$μm h⁻¹
τgrowth-decay length scale $η$μm
κgrowth-decay exponent–
r0fresh radius the fits start from (54.5)μm

The data are the file snicar_drdt_bst_fit_60_c070416.nc (M. Flanner, 16 Apr 2007; "r_e0 = 54.5 um") distributed with CESM/CLM as lnd/clm2/snicardata/ input data, shipped unchanged in src/data/ and read with NCDatasets (the file is NetCDF-3 classic).

Aeolus.Snowpack.SnowBottomFlux — Type
SnowBottomFlux(F = 0)

Prescribed heat flux F (W m⁻²) leaving the base of the snowpack into the substrate (positive downward, the contract's sign for G); F = 0 is an insulated base, F < 0 a geothermal / soil heat supply.

Aeolus.Snowpack.SnowBucketPercolation — Type
SnowBucketPercolation(; retention = ColeouLesaffre1998Retention())

The "tipping bucket" liquid-water scheme of Crocus (Vionnet et al. 2012, Sect. 3.10) and CLM (Oleson et al. 2010, Sect. 7.2.2), with the holding capacity from retention. Proceeding downward from the top layer, water entering a layer first refreezes as far as the layer's cold content allows (snow_refreeze!); the layer then retains liquid up to its holding capacity $ρ_w Δz θ_r$; any excess passes to the layer below within the step, and what leaves the bottom layer is runoff. Capillary barriers are not represented. If refreezing would fill a layer's pores with ice (an impermeable ice layer), the water that does not fit passes on. Mass and enthalpy are conserved exactly.

Aeolus.Snowpack.SnowBudget — Type
SnowBudget

Cumulative mass (kg m⁻²) and energy (J m⁻²) exchanged by a pack since it was built or its budget reset — the bookkeeping behind the conservation tests:

fieldmeaning
snowfall, rainfallprecipitation received
sublimationnet vapour loss (negative: net deposition)
runoffliquid water leaving the base
meltice melted
refreezeliquid water refrozen (#36)
surface_heat$∫ G\,dt$ conducted in at the surface
bottom_heatheat leaving through the base (incl. melt surplus of a vanished bottom layer)
advected_enthalpyenthalpy brought in by precipitation and deposition, minus that carried off by sublimation
runoff_enthalpyenthalpy carried off by runoff

Enthalpy is referred to liquid water at $T_f$: $h = w_{ice}[c_{ice}(T - T_f) - L_f] + w_{liq} c_w (T - T_f)$, so

ΔM = snowfall + rainfall - sublimation - runoff
ΔH = surface_heat - bottom_heat + advected_enthalpy - runoff_enthalpy
Aeolus.Snowpack.SnowGravityDrainage — Type
SnowGravityDrainage(; retention = ColeouLesaffre1998Retention(),
                      exponent = 3, μ = 1.792e-3, cfl = 0.5)

Richards-type percolation in the gravity-flow limit: the kinematic-wave model of Colbeck (1972), as in SNTHERM (Jordan 1991) and early Crocus (Brun et al. 1989). The capillary-pressure gradient of Richards' equation is neglected, so the liquid flux is the unsaturated hydraulic conductivity,

q = ρ_w K_s S_e^n,     S_e = (θ_w - θ_r)/(θ_s - θ_r) ∈ [0, 1]

with $n = 3$ (Colbeck 1972), $θ_r$ from retention, $θ_s = 1 - θ_i$ the porosity and $K_s$ from snow_saturated_hydraulic_conductivity with each layer's dry density and grain radius (p.grain, μm, the optical radius evolved by #35). The layer fluxes are integrated with an explicit upwind scheme in sub-steps limited by the Courant number cfl of the kinematic wave (speed $dq/dθ = n ρ_w K_s S_e^{n-1}/(θ_s - θ_r)/ρ_w$), no layer drains below its irreducible content and none receives more than its pores hold. Water arriving in a cold layer refreezes (snow_refreeze!). Unlike SnowBucketPercolation the water takes time to cross the pack: a constant input $q_0$ on dry snow advances as a shock at $v = q_0 / (ρ_w θ_1)$, where $ρ_w K_s S_e(θ_1)^n = q_0$.

Aeolus.Snowpack.SnowGroundTemperature — Type
SnowGroundTemperature(T)

The snow/ground interface is held at temperature T (K); the flux into the ground is conducted across the lower half of the bottom layer. Mutable, so a soil model can update T every step.

Aeolus.Snowpack.SnowLayering — Type
SnowLayering(; max_layers = 10, dz_top = 0.02, growth = 1.6,
               min_mass = 0.01, min_total_mass = 1e-6)

Rules for the numerical layers. Layer i (1 = top) has a target thickness $Δz_i = Δz_{top}\, r^{i-1}$ (r = growth), so the grid is fine at the surface, where the temperature gradients are, and coarse below — the Crocus / CLM philosophy. After every step relayer_snowpack!

  1. merges any layer thinner than $Δz_i/2$ or lighter than min_mass (kg m⁻²) with its thinner neighbour;
  2. splits any layer thicker than $2Δz_i$ in two while fewer than max_layers exist;
  3. merges the thinnest adjacent pair while more than max_layers exist.

Merging conserves ice, liquid, thickness and enthalpy exactly; splitting copies the intensive state. A pack lighter than min_total_mass vanishes (its mass is booked as runoff). growth must lie in (1, 2] so a split layer's halves are never immediately re-merged.

Aeolus.Snowpack.SnowOpticsAlbedo — Type
SnowOpticsAlbedo(; spec = shortwave_bands(), soot_ppmw = 0, μ₀ = nothing,
                   sensing_depth = 0.02)

Broadband snowpack albedo from SurfaceOptics.SnowSurface — the Wiscombe & Warren (1980) grain-size and soot dependence already in the code base — evaluated at the pack's prognostic optical grain radius (snowpack_optical_radius), solar-weighted over the shortwave bands of spec by SurfaceOptics.broadband_albedo. μ₀ = nothing gives the diffuse (white-sky) albedo, a number the direct-beam albedo at that cosine solar zenith angle. soot_ppmw is the black-carbon mixing ratio (ppm by mass) of the surface snow. The albedo ages exactly as fast as the grains grow: this is a delegating scheme, not a second snow albedo.

Not represented: the underlying ground showing through thin snow (patchy cover belongs to the tile framework, #41), impurity deposition and enrichment by melt, and ponded water.

Aeolus.Snowpack.Sturm1997Conductivity — Type

k = 0.023 + 0.234 ρ               (ρ < 0.156)

Aeolus.Snowpack.Vionnet2012Compaction — Type
Vionnet2012Compaction(; destructive = nothing, η0 = 7.62237e6,
                        a_η = 0.1, b_η = 0.023, c_η = 250, liquid_factor = 60)

Crocus viscous settling (Vionnet et al. 2012, Eqs. 5-8):

(1/D) dD/dt = -σ/η,     σ = g P_s,
η = f1 f2 η0 (ρ/c_η) exp[a_η (T_f - T) + b_η ρ]

with η0 in Pa s, $f_1 = 1/(1 + 60 w_{liq}/(ρ_w D))$ for liquid water, and $f_2$ the grain-shape factor supplied by the metamorphism hook (viscosity_grain_factor; Crocus's $\min(4, \exp(\min(0.4, g_s - 0.2)/0.1))$ needs the grain size of #35, so the default is 1). Crocus has no separate destructive term; one may be added with destructive = DestructiveMetamorphism().

Under a constant load at constant temperature this law integrates in closed form: $e^{b_η ρ(t)} = e^{b_η ρ_0} + b_η K t$ with $K = σ c_η / (f_1 f_2 η_0 e^{a_η (T_f - T)})$.

Aeolus.Snowpack.Vionnet2012FreshSnow — Type
Vionnet2012FreshSnow(; a = 109, b = 6, c = 26, ρ_min = 50)

Crocus fresh snow (Vionnet et al. 2012, Eq. 1, after Pahaut 1976 at Col de Porte): $ρ = a + b (T - T_f) + c U^{1/2}$, bounded below by ρ_min. Wind packs falling snow: at −5 °C, 0 m s⁻¹ gives 79 kg m⁻³ and 10 m s⁻¹ gives 161 kg m⁻³.

Aeolus.Snowpack.flanner_grain_radius — Method
flanner_grain_radius(r, t_hours, drdt0, τ, κ; r0 = 54.5) -> μm

Grain radius after t_hours of dry ageing at fixed parameters, starting from r — the exact solution of the Flanner & Zender (2006) growth law

\[\frac{dr}{dt} = \left(\frac{dr}{dt}\right)_0 \left(\frac{τ}{(r - r_0) + τ}\right)^{1/κ},\]

which with $x = r - r_0 + τ$ integrates to $x^{1+1/κ} = x_0^{1+1/κ} + (1 + 1/κ)(dr/dt)_0\,τ^{1/κ}\,t$. Composes exactly: ageing for $t_1$ then $t_2$ equals ageing for $t_1 + t_2$, so the result does not depend on the model time step.

Aeolus.Snowpack.snicar_aging_parameters — Method
snicar_aging_parameters(tab, T, dTdz, ρ) -> (drdt0 [μm h⁻¹], τ [μm], κ)

Aeolus.Snowpack.snow_heat_conduction! — Method
snow_heat_conduction!(p, dt; top_flux = 0, dtop_flux_dT = 0, T_top = nothing)
    -> (F_top, F_bottom)

One θ-weighted step of heat conduction through the layers,

C_i dT_i/dt = F_{i-1/2} - F_{i+1/2},   F_{i+1/2} = (T_i - T_{i+1}) / (Δz_i/2k_i + Δz_{i+1}/2k_{i+1})

with $C_i = w_{ice} c_{ice} + w_{liq} c_w$ and $k_i$ from the pack's conductivity law. Upper boundary: either a flux into the top layer linearised about the current top temperature, $G = G_0 + (∂G/∂T)(T_1^* - T_1^n)$ (top_flux, dtop_flux_dT), or, with T_top, a fixed surface temperature half a layer above the top node. Lower boundary: the pack's bottom. $T^* = θT^{n+1} + (1-θ)T^n$, so θ = 1 is backward Euler and θ = ½ Crank-Nicolson.

Returns the applied flux into the top of the pack and out of its base (W m⁻²); $Σ C_i ΔT_i = (F_{top} - F_{bottom})\,dt$ holds to round-off. Temperatures may leave the pack above the melting point: snow_phase_change! turns the excess into melt.

Aeolus.Snowpack.snow_phase_change! — Method
snow_phase_change!(p) -> melted mass (kg m⁻²)

Melt ice in every layer warmer than $T_f$: the sensible excess $C_i (T_i - T_f)$ melts $Δw = \min(C_i(T_i - T_f)/L_f, w_{ice})$ and the layer is returned to $T_f$. The layer thins in proportion to the ice lost — its density is unchanged, which is CLM's compaction-by-melt term $C_{R3}$ applied exactly. Energy left over when a layer melts out passes to the layer below, and from the bottom layer into the substrate. Refreezing of retained water is the liquid-water scheme's (snow_refreeze!).

Aeolus.Snowpack.snow_refreeze! — Method
snow_refreeze!(p) -> refrozen mass (kg m⁻²)

Refreeze the liquid water of every layer colder than the melting point as far as its cold content allows, at constant enthalpy: the latent heat released warms the layer, to $T_f$ if liquid remains. Layer thickness is unchanged (the ice fills pores). Adds the refrozen mass to p.budget.refreeze.

Aeolus.Snowpack.snow_saturated_hydraulic_conductivity — Method
snow_saturated_hydraulic_conductivity(ρ_dry, r_es; μ = 1.792e-3,
                                      c = DEFAULT_SURFACE_CONSTANTS) -> m s⁻¹

Saturated hydraulic conductivity of snow of dry density ρ_dry (kg m⁻³) and equivalent-sphere (optical) grain radius r_es (m), from the permeability Calonne et al. (2012) computed on 3-D tomography images, $k = 3.0\, r_{es}^2 \exp(-0.013 ρ_{dry})$ (m²; their abstract, with $r_{es} = 3/(SSA\,ρ_{ice})$ the equivalent-sphere radius), converted with $K_s = k ρ_w g / μ$ (μ: viscosity of water at 0 °C, Pa s; Wever et al. 2014, Eq. 14). Aeolus.SurfaceExchange.step_surface! — Method
step_surface!(p::LayeredSnowpack, forcing, dt; δT = 0.01, kwargs...) -> SurfaceFluxes

Advance the snowpack by dt seconds:

  1. surface fluxes from SurfaceExchange.turbulent_fluxes at the current skin temperature and at $T_1 + δT$, giving $R_n, H, LE, E, τ$ and their derivatives with respect to the skin temperature;
  2. heat conduction with the surface energy balance $G = R_n - H - LE$ linearised in the top-layer temperature (implicit, so the stiff radiative/turbulent coupling of a 2 cm top layer is stable at any step);
  3. the returned fluxes are those actually applied: each is updated linearly to the time-weighted skin temperature, so $R_n = H + LE + G$ holds exactly and G is what entered the pack;
  4. sublimation/deposition of $E\,dt$ (if the pack sublimates away within the step, E and LE are reduced to the mass actually removed and the unspent latent heat is added to G and passed to the substrate, so $R_n = H + LE + G$ still holds); rain and snowfall (density from fresh_density, at forcing.T_air and forcing.U);
  5. melt, the liquid-water hook (percolation, refreezing and runoff of retained water under SnowBucketPercolation / SnowGravityDrainage), compaction, the metamorphism hook, and relayering.

Without snow, the fluxes of a surface at the bare-ground temperature are returned (not applied) and precipitation is processed. kwargs go to turbulent_fluxes.
