P3 process rates and secondary ice (Phase 40)

Part of the P3 microphysics API: the warm-rain, ice and mixed-phase process rates, the parcel tendencies and limited time step built from them (p3_tendencies, p3_step), and secondary ice production by Hallett–Mossop rime splintering, which is driven by the riming rates. Theory: P3 microphysics, sections "Process rates" and "Secondary ice".

Aeolus.P3Microphysics.P3_DEFAULT_PROCESS — Constant

Default P3 process parameters (Float64), see P3ProcessParameters.

Aeolus.P3Microphysics.P3ParcelState — Type
P3ParcelState(; T, p, q_v = 0, q_c = 0, N_c = 0, q_r = 0, N_r = 0,
                q_i = 0, N_i = 0, q_rim = 0, B_rim = 0)

Thermodynamic and microphysical state of one grid cell for the P3 scheme: temperature T (K), pressure p (Pa), and the prognostic variables of MM15 — mixing ratios q_* (kg kg⁻¹), number mixing ratios N_* (kg⁻¹), rime mass q_rim (kg kg⁻¹, part of q_i) and rime volume B_rim (m³ kg⁻¹). All per kg of moist air. Cloud liquid, rain and ice are two-moment; the rime pair gives the predicted rime density q_rim/B_rim (p3_rime_state).

p3_tendencies returns its time derivatives in the same type.

Aeolus.P3Microphysics.p3_accretion — Function
p3_accretion(q_c, N_c, q_r, pp = P3_DEFAULT_PROCESS) -> (dq_r, dN_c)

Accretion of cloud water by rain, Khairoutdinov & Kogan (2000):

∂q_r/∂t = 67 (q_c q_r)^1.15,       ∂N_c/∂t = -(∂q_r/∂t) N_c / q_c,

mixing ratios in kg kg⁻¹. Rain number is unchanged (a drop that collects droplets stays one drop). Zero unless both q_c and q_r exceed q_small. Returns the non-negative rain source and cloud-number sink.

Aeolus.P3Microphysics.p3_autoconversion — Function
p3_autoconversion(q_c, N_c, ρ_air, pp = P3_DEFAULT_PROCESS) -> (dq_r, dN_r, dN_c)

Cloud-to-rain autoconversion of Khairoutdinov & Kogan (2000, KK2000), the formulation used by P3 (MM15 §2c):

∂q_r/∂t = 1350 q_c^2.47 (N_c ρ_air · 10⁻⁶)^{-1.79}       kg kg⁻¹ s⁻¹

with q_c in kg kg⁻¹ and the droplet number converted from kg⁻¹ to cm⁻³. The rate itself is delegated to AerosolCloud.autoconversion_rate (one source of truth for KK2000). New drops are drizzle drops of radius r_auto = 25 μm (KK2000), so

∂N_r/∂t = (∂q_r/∂t) / ((4/3) π ρ_w r_auto³),
∂N_c/∂t = -(∂q_r/∂t) N_c / q_c

(droplets are removed at the mean droplet mass). Returns the three tendencies as non-negative magnitudes of the rain source, rain number source and cloud number sink.

The KK2000 regression was fitted to LES of stratocumulus with q_c ≲ 1 g kg⁻¹; it has no hard threshold of its own, so, as in the P3 code, it is switched off below q_c_auto_min = 10⁻⁸ kg kg⁻¹ (and for N_c ≤ 0). The P3 code draws the new drops with diameter 25 μm; this implementation follows the KK2000 paper (radius 25 μm), so it creates 8× fewer rain drops per unit mass than the P3 code.

Aeolus.P3Microphysics.p3_cober_list_density — Method
p3_cober_list_density(R_i) -> kg m⁻³

Density of rime accreted from cloud droplets as a function of the Macklin impact parameter R_i, after Cober & List (1993, JAS 50, 1591-1609) in the form used by the P3 code (MM15 §2c):

ρ_rim = (0.051 + 0.114 R_i - 0.0055 R_i²) · 10³      1 ≤ R_i ≤ 8
ρ_rim = 611 + 72.25 (R_i - 8)                        8 < R_i ≤ 12

with R_i clamped to [1, 12]. Continuous at R_i = 8 (611 kg m⁻³), increasing, and bounded by 159.5 kg m⁻³ (R_i = 1) and 900 kg m⁻³ (R_i = 12), inside the P3 limits [ρ_rim_min, ρ_rim_max] = [50, 900].

Aeolus.P3Microphysics.p3_condensation — Function
p3_condensation(s::P3ParcelState, pp = P3_DEFAULT_PROCESS) -> P3ParcelState

Condensation of vapour to cloud water, or evaporation of cloud water, by saturation adjustment with respect to liquid: MoistThermodynamics.isobaric_saturation_adjustment — the contract's process adjustment — is applied to the vapour + cloud subsystem at constant pressure and constant moist enthalpy (MoistThermodynamics.moist_enthalpy), and returns the temperature and cloud water that leave the parcel exactly saturated over liquid (or subsaturated with no cloud). Nothing thermodynamic is re-derived here.

Rain and ice are passed as non-adjusting condensate (q_r, q_i): they do not change, but they are part of the parcel's heat capacity, so the heating is exactly c_pm ΔT = L_v(T₀) Δq_c with the full-state c_pm (issue #82). The contract saturates cloud water over liquid at every temperature, which is P3's convention: cloud water stays supercooled below 0 °C and becomes ice only by riming or freezing.

q_v + q_c is conserved exactly, and moist enthalpy to round-off (|Δh|/h ≈ 1e-16). Measured c_pm ΔT / (L_v(T₀) Δq_c) is 1 to 1e-12 at 1000, 900 and 600 hPa (tests). Before #82 this step used the θli diagnosis `MoistThermodynamics.saturationadjustment, which gave 0.998, 1.012 and 1.064 there and ignored rain and ice inc_pm`.

Cloud number: newly formed cloud (N_c = 0) receives the prescribed N_c_prescribed / ρ_air; complete evaporation removes all droplets, partial evaporation keeps N_c (the P3 convention — droplets shrink before they disappear). A cloud remainder below q_small is evaporated too, at constant moist enthalpy.

The explicit supersaturation-relaxation condensation of the P3 code (Morrison & Grabowski 2008) is replaced by this adjustment; the two agree whenever the phase relaxation time (~1-10 s) is short compared with the time step.

Aeolus.P3Microphysics.p3_limit_rates — Method
p3_limit_rates(s::P3ParcelState, r, dt, pp = P3_DEFAULT_PROCESS) -> NamedTuple

Scale the process rates r so that one forward-Euler step of length dt can neither drive a species negative nor overshoot saturation, in the manner of the P3 code's conservation checks:

  • the sinks of each of q_c, q_r, q_i are scaled by a common factor so their total over dt does not exceed what is there;
  • deposition may not remove more vapour than brings the parcel to ice saturation, and sublimation / rain evaporation no more than brings it back to ice / liquid saturation, each reduced by the psychrometric factor Γ = 1 + (L/c_pm) dq_s/dT that accounts for its own latent heating shifting q_s during the step; the saturation values are taken at the temperature after the step's riming and melting; sublimation may fill only the part of the ice-saturation deficit that the (limited) rain evaporation leaves, so the two vapour sources together cannot push the parcel past ice saturation;
  • number rates are scaled with the mass rate they belong to, and the rain self-collection and ice-collection number sinks so N_r ≥ 0.

Each process drains exactly one species, so scaling it by that species' factor keeps every species non-negative while the linear tendency map keeps water conserved exactly.

Aeolus.P3Microphysics.p3_process_rates — Method
p3_process_rates(s::P3ParcelState, pp = P3_DEFAULT_PROCESS) -> NamedTuple

Instantaneous rates of every P3 process for the state s, each a non-negative magnitude of the transfer it names (kg kg⁻¹ s⁻¹ for mass, kg⁻¹ s⁻¹ for number), except n_self_r, a net number sink that is negative when breakup dominates:

fieldtransferformulation
evap_r, n_evap_rrain → vapourventilated diffusion, S_w < 0 only
depvapour → iceventilated diffusion, S_i > 0, T < T₀
sub, n_subice → vapourventilated diffusion, S_i < 0, T < T₀
auto, n_auto_r, n_auto_ccloud → rainKK2000 (p3_autoconversion)
accr, n_accr_ccloud → rainKK2000 (p3_accretion)
n_self_rrain numberSB2001 + breakup (p3_rain_self_collection)
rime_c, n_rime_c, ρ_rime_ccloud → rimecontinuous collection, T < T₀, Cober & List density
rime_r, n_rime_rrain → rimecontinuous collection, T < T₀, density ρ_rim_rain
shed, n_shed_c, n_shed_rcloud → raincloud collected by ice at T ≥ T₀, shed as 1 mm drops
melt, n_meltice → rainheat balance, T > T₀

Diffusional growth. One particle of capacitance C and ventilation coefficient f_v (p3_ventilation) grows as dm/dt = 4π C f_v S / (A + B) with S the super- or subsaturation over water (rain) or ice, A the heat-conduction and B the vapour-diffusion resistance (Pruppacher & Klett 1997, §13.2). C = D/2 for spheres and graupel, 0.48 D/2 for unrimed and partially rimed nonspherical ice. The rate of a population is N · 4π E_N[C f_v] S/(A+B), the expectation over the PSD taken by quadrature; Re uses p3_fall_speed for ice and the Superdroplet drop terminal velocity for rain. Sublimation and evaporation remove number in proportion to mass.

Riming. Continuous collection with the geometric kernel of the ice particle, ∂q_c/∂t = -E_ci ρ_a q_c N_i E_N[A V] (cloud droplets' own fall speed neglected), and for rain the kernel A |V_i - V_r| with the rain mass- (number-) weighted fall speed for mass (number). The rime density of cloud rime is p3_rime_density at the droplet mean-volume diameter and the impact speed |V_M,i - V_c|. Below 0 °C collected water becomes rime (q_rim, B_rim += dq/ρ_rime); at or above 0 °C collected cloud water is shed as rain (MM15 §2c) and collected rain stays rain but carries its sensible heat into melting.

Melting. For T > T₀ (Mason 1971; Rutledge & Hobbs 1983; MM15),

melt = [N_i 4π E_N[C f_v] (K_a (T-T₀) + L_v D_v (ρ_v - ρ_vs(T₀)))
        + c_w (T-T₀) (coll_c + coll_r)] / L_f,

floored at zero (dry air can hold a particle below 0 °C at its surface by evaporative cooling). Each melted particle becomes one drop, and the rime melts in proportion.

Ice PSD: gamma with shape μ_i and λ from p3_slope with the P3 mean-size limiter (2 μm to 5 mm + 20 mm Frim²). Rain PSD: gamma with shape `μrfrom [p3rainsizedistribution](@ref) after [p3rainlimitnumber](@ref) — the same PSD sedimentation and radar use, mean diameter(μr+1)/λrin[10 μm, 2 mm](P3getraindsd2`).

Aeolus.P3Microphysics.p3_rain_self_collection — Function
p3_rain_self_collection(q_r, N_r, λ_r, ρ_air, pp = P3_DEFAULT_PROCESS) -> dN_r

Net rate at which rain drop number is lost (kg⁻¹ s⁻¹) to self-collection, reduced by collisional breakup, following Seifert & Beheng (2001) with the breakup efficiency of Seifert (2008) in the form used by the P3 code:

∂N_r/∂t = -φ(D_m) k_rr ρ_air q_r N_r,          k_rr = 5.78 m³ kg⁻¹ s⁻¹
φ = 1                                   for D_m < D_break = 280 μm
φ = 2 - exp(k_break (D_m - D_break))    otherwise,  k_break = 2300 m⁻¹

with D_m = 1/λ_r. Mass is unchanged. Breakup balances self-collection (φ = 0) at D_m = D_break + ln 2 / k_break = 581.4 μm; for larger drops the result is negative, i.e. breakup is a net number source, which is what holds rain at an equilibrium mean size.

Aeolus.P3Microphysics.p3_rime_density — Function
p3_rime_density(D_c, V_impact, T, pp = P3_DEFAULT_PROCESS) -> kg m⁻³

Density of rime formed by ice collecting cloud droplets of diameter D_c (m) at impact speed V_impact (m s⁻¹) and air temperature T (K). The Macklin (1962) parameter is

R_i = -(r_c [μm]) V_impact / T_s[°C],

(droplet radius in μm, surface temperature taken as the air temperature, bounded away from 0 °C at -0.001 °C as in the P3 code), and the density is p3_cober_list_density(R_i). Faster impacts, larger drops and warmer surfaces give denser rime: the droplets spread before they freeze.

Aeolus.P3Microphysics.p3_step — Method
p3_step(s::P3ParcelState, dt, pp = P3_DEFAULT_PROCESS; adjust = true,
        cloud_fraction = nothing, precip_fraction = nothing, f_min = 0.01,
        hallett_mossop = nothing) -> P3ParcelState

Advance the P3 microphysical source terms (no transport, no sedimentation) of one cell by dt seconds:

  1. p3_process_rates at the start-of-step state;
  2. p3_limit_rates for positivity and no saturation overshoot;
  3. a forward-Euler update with p3_tendencies;
  4. clean-up: species below q_small are evaporated/sublimated (with their latent heat), rime is clamped to 0 ≤ q_rim ≤ q_i and ρ_rim ∈ [50, 900] via p3_rime_state, N_i is adjusted when the ice mean size leaves P3's limiter and N_r when the rain mean diameter leaves [10 μm, 2 mm] (p3_rain_limit_number);
  5. if adjust, cloud condensation/evaporation by p3_condensation (MoistThermodynamics.isobaric_saturation_adjustment).

hallett_mossop = P3_DEFAULT_HM (a P3HallettMossop) adds rime splintering to step 3; it is off by default. See p3_step_with_rates for the variant that also returns the rates.

Total water q_v + q_c + q_r + q_i is conserved to rounding in every stage. Steps 1-4 conserve the enthalpy form c_pm ΔT = -L_v Δq_v + L_f Δq_i exactly for a step without clean-up; step 5 conserves the contract's moist enthalpy exactly, rain and ice included (see p3_condensation).

Sub-grid fractions (Phase 44, off by default): passing cloud_fraction and/or precip_fraction (a missing one is taken as 1) evaluates step 1 with p3_fractional_process_rates — accretion, riming, autoconversion with in-cloud / in-precipitation mixing ratios weighted by area, rain evaporation only in the clear part; the rates are limited per region, so no region loses more than it holds. With both nothing (the default) the step is the grid-mean one, bit for bit. Step 5 is still the grid-mean all-or-nothing adjustment.

Aeolus.P3Microphysics.p3_step_with_rates — Method
p3_step_with_rates(s, dt, pp = P3_DEFAULT_PROCESS; adjust = true,
                   cloud_fraction = nothing, precip_fraction = nothing,
                   f_min = 0.01, hallett_mossop = nothing)
    -> (P3ParcelState, rates)

p3_step, also returning the limited process rates that were applied (the NamedTuple of p3_limit_rates, or the area-weighted, region-by-region limited rates of p3_fractional_process_rates when a cloud or precipitation fraction is given), so a host can book side effects of individual processes — e.g. release the ice-nucleating particles of sublimated ice (n_sub). With hallett_mossop a P3HallettMossop, rime splintering (p3_hm_tendencies) is added to the tendencies, driven by the same limited riming rates; the default nothing leaves it off.

Aeolus.P3Microphysics.p3_tendencies — Method
p3_tendencies(s::P3ParcelState, r = p3_process_rates(s, pp), pp = P3_DEFAULT_PROCESS)
    -> P3ParcelState

Time derivatives of every prognostic variable implied by the process rates r (p3_process_rates), returned as a P3ParcelState whose fields are d/dt of the corresponding fields (p is held fixed, dp/dt = 0). The mapping is linear in the rates:

dq_v  =  evap_r - dep + sub
dq_c  = -auto - accr - rime_c - shed
dq_r  = -evap_r + auto + accr - rime_r + shed + melt
dq_i  =  dep - sub + rime_c + rime_r - melt
dq_rim = rime_c + rime_r - (sub + melt) q_rim/q_i
dB_rim = rime_c/ρ_rime_c + rime_r/ρ_rim_rain - (sub + melt) B_rim/q_i
c_pm dT = -L_v(T) dq_v + L_f(T) dq_i

so dq_v + dq_c + dq_r + dq_i = 0 identically, sublimation and melting leave the rime density q_rim/B_rim unchanged, and the heating is exactly the latent heat of the net phase change (L_s = L_v + L_f for deposition, L_f for riming, -L_f for melting). Latent heats and c_pm are those of MoistThermodynamics at the current state.

Aeolus.P3Microphysics.p3_total_water — Method

Total water q_v + q_c + q_r + q_i of a P3ParcelState, kg kg⁻¹.

Aeolus.P3Microphysics.p3_ventilation — Method
p3_ventilation(X, kind::Symbol) -> f_v

Ventilation coefficient f_v of diffusional growth as a function of X = Sc^{1/3} Re^{1/2}. For ice (kind = :ice), Hall & Pruppacher (1976):

f_v = 1 + 0.14 X²        X < 1,       f_v = 0.86 + 0.28 X        X ≥ 1;

for drops (kind = :rain), Beard & Pruppacher (1971):

f_v = 1 + 0.108 X²       X < 1.4,     f_v = 0.78 + 0.308 X       X ≥ 1.4.

Both are ≥ 1 and continuous (to 5e-4 at the rain junction). The P3 reference code applies only the linear large-X branches (0.86, 0.28 for ice and 0.78, 0.32 for rain), which give f_v < 1 for the smallest particles; the piecewise forms keep the stagnant-air limit f_v → 1.

Aeolus.P3Microphysics.P3_DEFAULT_HM — Constant

Default Hallett–Mossop parameters (Float64), see P3HallettMossop.

Aeolus.P3Microphysics.P3_HM_SPLINTER_MASS — Constant
P3_HM_SPLINTER_MASS = (π/6) × 900 kg m⁻³ × (10 μm)³ ≈ 4.71e-13 kg

Mass of one Hallett–Mossop splinter: an ice sphere of 10 μm diameter at 900 kg m⁻³, piov6*900*Dinit_HM**3 with Dinit_HM = 10e-6 in the rime-splintering block of the P3 reference code (microphy_p3.f90). It is not P3_NUCLEATED_ICE_MASS (mi0, a 2 μm-diameter sphere): the P3 code gives splinters and nucleated crystals different sizes.

Aeolus.P3Microphysics.P3_NUCLEATED_ICE_MASS — Constant
P3_NUCLEATED_ICE_MASS = 4π/3 × 900 kg m⁻³ × (1 μm)³ ≈ 3.77e-15 kg

Mass of a newly formed ice crystal in P3 — a sphere of 1 μm radius at 900 kg m⁻³ (mi0 of the P3 reference code, Morrison & Milbrandt 2015). It is the mass of a crystal nucleated by deposition in IceNucleation (NUCLEATED_ICE_MASS is defined as this constant, so the two schemes cannot drift apart). Hallett–Mossop splinters are larger, see P3_HM_SPLINTER_MASS.

Aeolus.P3Microphysics.P3HallettMossop — Type
P3HallettMossop{T}(; kwargs...)

Parameters of Hallett–Mossop rime splintering in P3.

fielddefaultmeaning
splinters_per_kg3.5e8splinters per kg of rime at the optimum temperature (350 per mg)
T_lo265.15cold edge of the window, K (-8 °C)
T_peak268.15optimum, K (-5 °C)
T_hi270.15warm edge of the window, K (-3 °C)
m_splinterP3_HM_SPLINTER_MASSmass of one splinter, kg (10 μm sphere, P3 code)
D_ice_min250e-6rimer size threshold: no splinters unless the ice mean diameter is at least this, m (P3 code, one ice category)
D_drop_min0only cloud droplets larger than this diameter (m) produce splinters; 0 = all

The window, the 350 mg⁻¹ yield, the splinter size and the rimer-size threshold are those of the P3 reference code (microphy_p3.f90, rime-splintering block: Dinit_HM = 10e-6, Dmin_HM = 250e-6 for one ice category), which follows Hallett & Mossop (1974) and Mossop (1976); the P3 code also requires the ice to be rimed already (q_rim ≥ q_small), which is applied here too. Splinters come from large rimers collecting large drops: D_ice_min switches the process off for populations of small crystals, whose rime is mostly frozen small droplets. Mossop (1976) and Choularton et al. (1980) found splintering needs drops larger than about 24 μm diameter; the P3 code does not apply that restriction to cloud rime (D_drop_min = 0), setting D_drop_min = 25e-6 applies it through the droplet gamma size distribution (see p3_hm_large_drop_fraction). Rime collected from rain is counted in full: rain drops are all larger than 25 μm.

Aeolus.P3Microphysics.p3_hallett_mossop — Method
p3_hallett_mossop(s::P3ParcelState, r, hm = P3_DEFAULT_HM, pp = P3_DEFAULT_PROCESS)
    -> (n_hm, q_hm)

Rime-splintering source of ice number n_hm (kg⁻¹ s⁻¹) and the mass q_hm = n_hm m_splinter (kg kg⁻¹ s⁻¹) it carries, from the P3 riming rates in r (the NamedTuple of p3_process_rates, or its limited form from p3_limit_rates):

n_hm = splinters_per_kg · f(T) · (φ_c rime_c + rime_r),

f = p3_hm_temperature_factor, φ_c = p3_hm_large_drop_fraction (1 unless hm.D_drop_min > 0). At -5 °C this is exactly 350 splinters per mg of rime. Zero outside -8…-3 °C, whenever there is no riming, when the ice carries no rime yet (q_rim < q_small), and when the ice mean diameter (p3_hm_rimer_diameter) is below hm.D_ice_min, as in the P3 code. The riming rates are used as given — they are not recomputed here.

How the splinters are booked is p3_hm_tendencies.

References: Hallett & Mossop (1974, Nature 249, 26); Mossop (1976, QJRMS 102, 45); Morrison & Milbrandt (2015, JAS 72, 287), §2c.

Aeolus.P3Microphysics.p3_hm_large_drop_fraction — Function
p3_hm_large_drop_fraction(q_c, N_c, ρ_air, D_min, pp = P3_DEFAULT_PROCESS) -> f ∈ [0, 1]

Fraction of the cloud-water mass carried by droplets larger than D_min (m), for droplets distributed as a gamma PSD whose shape μ_c follows Martin et al. (1994) as in the P3 code and whose slope matches q_c and N_c (per kg) as spheres of liquid water:

f = Q(μ_c + 4, λ_c D_min),      λ_c from gamma_psd_slope(N_c, 0, q_c/(π/6 ρ_w), 3, μ_c),

Q the regularised upper incomplete gamma function (gamma_inc_upper). With the geometric sweep-out kernel of P3 riming (droplet fall speed neglected) each droplet is collected in proportion to the volume it occupies, so f is also the fraction of cloud rime that comes from droplets larger than D_min. Returns 1 for D_min ≤ 0 and 0 without cloud.

Aeolus.P3Microphysics.p3_hm_rimer_diameter — Method
p3_hm_rimer_diameter(s::P3ParcelState, pp = P3_DEFAULT_PROCESS) -> D (m)

Mean diameter of the P3 ice population used by the Hallett–Mossop rimer-size threshold, as diam_ice of the P3 code:

D = (6 q_i / (π N_i ρ_bulk))^{1/3},

with ρ_bulk = p3_bulk_density of the gamma PSD (shape pp.μ_i, slope from p3_slope within the P3 mean-size limits of _p3_ice_λ_limits). The P3 code uses the mass-weighted mean density from its lookup table in place of ρ_bulk. Returns 0 without ice.

Aeolus.P3Microphysics.p3_hm_temperature_factor — Function
p3_hm_temperature_factor(T, hm = P3_DEFAULT_HM) -> f ∈ [0, 1]

Temperature dependence of rime splintering: a triangle that is zero at or outside [T_lo, T_hi] = [-8, -3] °C and rises linearly to 1 at T_peak = -5 °C from either side,

f = (T - T_lo)/(T_peak - T_lo)     T_lo < T ≤ T_peak
f = (T_hi - T)/(T_hi - T_peak)     T_peak < T < T_hi.

This is the shape used by the P3 reference code (dum in the nimul block of microphy_p3.f90), a piecewise-linear fit to the laboratory yield curve of Hallett & Mossop (1974, Nature 249, 26), their Fig. 2.

Aeolus.P3Microphysics.p3_hm_tendencies — Method
p3_hm_tendencies(s::P3ParcelState, r, hm = P3_DEFAULT_HM, pp = P3_DEFAULT_PROCESS)
    -> P3ParcelState

Tendencies (a P3ParcelState of d/dt, like p3_tendencies) of rime splintering, to be added to those of the other processes:

dN_i   = +n_hm
dq_rim = -q_hm
dB_rim = -q_hm / ρ_new,     1/ρ_new = (rime_c/ρ_rime_c + rime_r/ρ_rim_rain)/(rime_c + rime_r)
dq_i = dq_v = dq_c = dq_r = dT = 0.

A splinter is a fragment of the freezing rime, so its mass is ice that already exists: total ice mass and every other species are unchanged, and so is the temperature (no phase change). What changes is the split of that ice: the splinters are small unrimed particles in P3's single ice category, so their mass leaves the rime mass, and rime volume leaves with it at the density of the rime being formed this step, which leaves the density of the new rime — and hence the rime-volume bookkeeping — unchanged. This is the P3 code's treatment (qcmul, qrmul go to q_i but not to q_rim). The mass is small (350 mg⁻¹ × 4.7e-13 kg = 1.65×10⁻⁴ of the rime), so the rime cannot be driven negative by it.
