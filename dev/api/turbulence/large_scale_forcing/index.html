Large-scale forcing — LargeScaleForcing

LargeScaleForcing holds the prescribed forcing of a case whose larger scales are not resolved: the geostrophic pressure gradient, nudging of the plane mean of u, v, θ, qv (ProfileNudging; GeostrophicNudging is its constructor), large-scale subsidence (Subsidence), prescribed large-scale advective tendencies and a prescribed surface-temperature series. Every quantity is a number, a height profile or a height-time ForcingTable, and every forcing is applied through one entry point, apply_forcing!(sim, forcing, t, Δt), from the body_force! hook of step!. Theory and the measured validation are in Large-scale forcing.

using Aeolus
mesh = RegularGrid(; size = (32, 32, 64), extent = (1000.0, 1000.0, 400.0))
sim  = BoussinesqSimulation(mesh; f_coriolis = 1.39e-4, C_s = 0.17, θ₀ = 263.5)

# GABLS1: U_g = 8 m/s, cooling surface (the T_s series feeds a wall law)
pg  = GeostrophicPressureGradient(8.0, 0.0)
Ts  = SurfaceTemperatureSeries([0.0, 9 * 3600.0], [265.0, 262.75])

# a height-time table: U_g backing with height and time
Ug  = ForcingTable([0.0, 1000.0], [0.0, 86400.0], [5.0 3.0; 8.0 6.0])
adv = AdvectiveTendencies(; θ = ForcingTable([0.0, 400.0], [-1e-5, -2e-5]))  # K/s

step!(sim, 1.0; body_force! = (s, Δt) -> apply_forcing!(s, (GeostrophicPressureGradient(Ug), adv), s.t, Δt))
surface_temperature(Ts, 3600.0)     # 264.75 K

# free-troposphere θ nudging above 1.2 z_i (τ = 6 h) and subsidence w_s = -D z
zi(s) = 800.0                                        # any boundary-layer-height diagnostic
nud = ProfileNudging((θ = ForcingTable([0.0, 3000.0], [290.0, 302.0]),), 6 * 3600.0;
                     weight = NudgingWeight(zi; factor = 1.2, depth = 200.0))
sub = Subsidence((z, t) -> -3e-6 * z; fields = (:θ,))
I = apply_forcing!(sim, sub, sim.t, 1.0)             # (θ = ∫ (∂θ/∂t)_s dV,) in K m³ s⁻¹

ReferenceDamping(field, profile, target) (N-parent-b, #254) is the Rayleigh damping of θ, qv or a velocity component toward a number, a level vector, a Field, a ForcingTable or the slab mean (:mean), with the height-dependent rate of a DampingProfile; it runs through the same apply_forcing! and the one damping kernel of add_sponge!, and damping_tendency(F) returns the integral it added (theory):

using Aeolus.Operators: sponge_profile
zc = collect(Aeolus.Grids.znodes(mesh, CellCenter()))
θdamp = ReferenceDamping(:θ, sponge_profile(1e-3, 300.0, 400.0), 263.5 .+ 0.01 .* zc)
step!(sim, 1.0; body_force! = (s, Δt) -> apply_forcing!(s, θdamp, s.t, Δt))
damping_tendency(θdamp).integral     # ∫ ∂θ/∂t|_d dV, K m³ s⁻¹

The Berlin case runs GeostrophicPressureGradient by default (:geostrophic coupling, mode = :pressure_gradient) and GeostrophicNudging as the option mode = :nudging (The Berlin case).

Radiosonde soundings (#247): read_sounding reads a sounding CSV (e.g. examples/urban/data/lindenberg_10393_20190629.csv), and sounding_profiles turns it into θ, $q_v$, u, v and p ForcingTables on heights above the station. The Berlin case takes its initial state from them. See Radiosonde soundings.

Aeolus.LargeScaleForcing.AbstractLargeScaleForcing — Type
AbstractLargeScaleForcing

Supertype of the prescribed large-scale forcings. Each one implements apply_forcing!(sim, forcing, t, Δt), which adds its tendency to the simulation's tendency fields (sim.tu, sim.tv, sim.tθ, …) and is meant to be called from the body_force! hook of step!. A Tuple of forcings is applied in order.

Aeolus.LargeScaleForcing.AdvectiveTendencies — Type
AdvectiveTendencies(z, t, dθdt, dqdt, dudt, dvdt)
AdvectiveTendencies(; θ = nothing, q = nothing, u = nothing, v = nothing)

Prescribed large-scale advective tendencies of potential temperature (K/s), specific humidity (kg kg⁻¹ s⁻¹) and the horizontal wind (m s⁻²). In the first form each of dθdt, dqdt, dudt, dvdt is a length(z) × length(t) matrix (or nothing for none) on the common height-time grid; in the second each is any forcing quantity (number, ForcingTable, function of (z, t)).

The tendency is added uniformly over each model level. With no surface flux it changes $\int θ\,dV$ by exactly $\iint (∂θ/∂t)_{adv}\,dV\,dt$ (the step mean is used, see forcing_mean). Large-scale subsidence, which depends on the model state, is not part of it: it is Subsidence.

Reference: the GABLS2 and DICE intercomparison forcing (Svensson et al. 2011, Bound.-Layer Meteor. 140, 177–206; Best et al. 2013, DICE protocol).

Aeolus.LargeScaleForcing.ForcingTable — Type
ForcingTable(z, t, values)
ForcingTable(z, values::AbstractVector)

A forcing quantity tabulated at heights z (m, strictly increasing) and times t (s, strictly increasing): values[k, n] is the value at (z[k], t[n]). The second form is a time-independent profile.

The table is interpolated linearly in z and in t and held at its end values outside the tabulated range (no extrapolation), the convention of the GABLS and DICE forcing files. See forcing_value and forcing_mean.

Aeolus.LargeScaleForcing.GeostrophicPressureGradient — Type
GeostrophicPressureGradient(U_g, V_g = 0)

The large-scale pressure gradient in geostrophic balance with the wind (U_g, V_g) (m/s):

\[\left.\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}\right|_{pg} = -f V_g, \qquad \left.\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}\right|_{pg} = +f U_g,\]

with f = sim.f_coriolis, so that together with the Coriolis term (+f v, -f u) the forced, frictionless flow has the steady state (u, v) = (U_g, V_g) about which ageostrophic departures perform inertial oscillations of period $2π/f$ (Blackadar 1957), and a viscous flow over a no-slip wall forms the Ekman spiral (Ekman 1905).

U_g and V_g are each a number, a height profile or a height-time ForcingTable (or a function (z, t) -> value).

References: Holton & Hakim (2013), An Introduction to Dynamic Meteorology, 5th ed., §3.2 and §8.4; Blackadar (1957), Bull. Amer. Meteor. Soc. 38, 283–290; Ekman (1905), Ark. Mat. Astron. Fys. 2(11).

Aeolus.LargeScaleForcing.NudgingWeight — Type
NudgingWeight(z_n; factor = 1, depth = 0)

The height weight $w(z) \in [0, 1]$ of a ProfileNudging: zero up to $z_0 = \mathrm{factor}\cdot z_n$, one above $z_0 + \mathrm{depth}$, and the smooth ramp $\sin^2\!\big(\tfrac{π}{2}(z - z_0)/\mathrm{depth}\big)$ in between (the profile of the core's Rayleigh sponge; depth = 0 is a step).

z_n is a height (m) — "nudge above $z_n$" — or any callable sim -> height, evaluated once per call of apply_forcing! on the current state. The second form is the "multiple of a diagnosed boundary-layer depth" of LES case set-ups that nudge only the free troposphere (Heinze et al. 2017, ACP 17, 7083, §2.3), e.g. NudgingWeight(sim -> zi(sim); factor = 1.5, depth = 200) with zi a boundary-layer-height diagnostic.

Any other callable z -> w can be passed as the weight of a ProfileNudging directly.

Aeolus.LargeScaleForcing.ProfileNudging — Type
ProfileNudging(targets::NamedTuple, τ; weight = nothing)

Relaxation of the plane (horizontal) mean of any of u, v, θ, qv toward a reference on the time scale τ (s):

\[\left.\frac{\partial φ}{\partial t}\right|_{n} = -\,w(z)\,\frac{\langle φ\rangle_{xy}(z) - φ_{ref}(z, t)}{τ},\]

added uniformly over each model level, so the fluctuations $φ - \langle φ\rangle$ are not touched. targets names the fields and their references, e.g. (θ = θ_profile, qv = q_profile); each reference is any forcing quantity (a number, a profile ForcingTable(z, values), a height-time ForcingTable or a function (z, t) -> value) and enters as its exact step mean (forcing_mean) over [t, t + Δt]. weight is nothing ($w ≡ 1$), a NudgingWeight ("above $z_n$" or above a multiple of a diagnosed boundary-layer depth, with a smooth ramp) or any callable z -> w.

This is the nudging of the LES large-scale forcing frameworks: DALES (Heus et al. 2010, GMD 3, 415, Eq. 92), the HOPE PALM runs of Heinze et al. (2017, ACP 17, 7083, Eq. 4; τ = 6 h for θ and q) and MicroHH (van Heerwaarden et al. 2017, GMD 10, 3145). With a forward-Euler step the departure of a level mean from a constant reference decays by exactly $1 - wΔt/τ$ per step.

apply_forcing! returns the volume integrals $\int (\partial φ/\partial t)_n\,dV$ it added, a NamedTuple keyed like targets (units of φ times m³ s⁻¹); they are also kept for tendency_integrals. GeostrophicNudging is ProfileNudging((u = U_g, v = V_g), τ).

Aeolus.LargeScaleForcing.ReferenceDamping — Type
ReferenceDamping(field::Symbol, profile::DampingProfile, target)

Rayleigh damping of the prognostic field field of a simulation (:θ, :qv, or a velocity component :u, :v, :w) toward target, with the height-dependent rate λ(z) of profile (a DampingProfile, usually sponge_profile(α_max, z_start, z_top)):

\[\left.\frac{\partial φ}{\partial t}\right|_{d} = -λ(z)\,\bigl(φ - φ_{ref}\bigr).\]

It is applied with apply_forcing! from the body_force! hook of a core's step!, so the core's time integrator integrates it like every other tendency (an RK3 stage sees it at the stage value). It is the one place θ or qv is Rayleigh-damped: the cores' own sponge damps the velocity only.

target is

  • a number, a level vector (one value per interior level, length Nz), a Field or a halo-padded 3-D array — passed to the kernel as is (see add_sponge!);
  • a ForcingTable or a function (z, t) -> value — evaluated at the level heights at the time t of the call (the stage time);
  • :mean — the horizontal (slab) mean of the field at each level, at the time of the call: only the deviations from the slab mean are damped, the mean profile is left free (DALES, Heus et al. 2010, Eqs. 40-41). Every level's integral is then zero to round-off.

After each application, damping_tendency(F) returns the tendency it added: levels[k], the sum over the columns of level k (field units s⁻¹), and integral, its volume integral $∫ ∂_tφ|_d\,dV$ (field units m³ s⁻¹) — the term a budget (the air-heat ledger) books for it.

Works on a RegularGrid and a StretchedGrid (the profile is sampled at the actual level heights).

References: Klemp, J. B. & Lilly, D. K. (1978), J. Atmos. Sci., 35, 78-107 (the damping layer); Durran, D. R. (2010), Numerical Methods for Fluid Dynamics, 2nd ed., §8.4; Skamarock, W. C. et al. (2019), A Description of the Advanced Research WRF Model Version 4, NCAR/TN-556, §4.4.3 (damping θ as well as the velocity); Heus, T. et al. (2010), Geosci. Model Dev., 3, 415-444 (DALES; damping toward the slab mean); Maronga, B. et al. (2015), Geosci. Model Dev., 8, 2515-2551 (PALM).

Aeolus.LargeScaleForcing.Sounding — Type
Sounding(launches; station = "", elevation = NaN, epoch = (1970, 1, 1))

A set of SoundingLaunches from one station (station name, launch-site elevation in m above sea level), ordered by time. Times are seconds from 00 UTC of the date epoch = (y, m, d) (sounding_time converts a calendar time). Read one from a CSV with read_sounding; turn it into profiles with sounding_profiles.

Aeolus.LargeScaleForcing.SoundingLaunch — Type
SoundingLaunch(t, p, T, Td; pw = p, u = nothing, v = nothing, gph = nothing,
               tag = "", c = MoistThermodynamics.DEFAULT_CONSTANTS)

One radiosonde ascent. Thermodynamic levels: pressure p (Pa, strictly decreasing, the surface first), temperature T and dew point Td (K; NaN where the dew point is missing). Wind levels: pressures pw with components u, v (m/s; omit for none). gph is the reported geopotential height above sea level (m, NaN where not reported), kept only as a check. t is the time stamp of the profile (s from the sounding's epoch; the release time).

The heights above the surface level are computed, not read: hypsometric integration of the virtual temperature,

\[z_{k+1} = z_k + \frac{R_d}{g_0}\,\frac{T_{v,k} + T_{v,k+1}}{2}\, \ln\frac{p_k}{p_{k+1}},\]

exact for $T_v$ linear in $\ln p$ between levels, with $T_v =$ virtual_temperature(T, PhasePartition(q_v)) and $q_v =$ specific_humidity_from_relative_humidity(Td, p, 1) (the specific humidity whose vapour pressure is $e_s(T_d)$; $q_v = 0$ where Td is missing). $g_0 = 9.80665$ m s⁻² is the standard gravity that defines the geopotential metre, so $z$ + station elevation is directly comparable with gph. Wind-level heights are interpolated linearly in $\ln p$ between the thermodynamic levels (levels outside their pressure range are dropped).

Reference: Wallace & Hobbs (2006), Atmospheric Science, 2nd ed., §3.2.2 (the hypsometric equation, eq. 3.29).

Aeolus.LargeScaleForcing.Subsidence — Type
Subsidence(w_s; fields = (:θ, :qv), form = :mean_gradient)

Large-scale subsidence (vertical advection by the prescribed large-scale vertical velocity $w_s(z, t)$, m s⁻¹, negative downward) of the fields fields (any of :u, :v, :θ, :qv):

\[\left.\frac{\partial φ}{\partial t}\right|_{s} = -\,w_s(z, t)\,\frac{\partial \langle φ\rangle_{xy}}{\partial z} \quad (\texttt{form = :mean\_gradient}), \qquad -\,w_s(z, t)\,\frac{\partial φ}{\partial z} \quad (\texttt{form = :local}).\]

:mean_gradient (the default; DALES, Heus et al. 2010, GMD 3, 415, Eq. 91; MicroHH, van Heerwaarden et al. 2017, GMD 10, 3145) uses the centred gradient of the plane mean, $(⟨φ⟩_{k+1} - ⟨φ⟩_{k-1})/(z_{k+1} - z_{k-1})$, and adds the tendency uniformly per level, so it moves the mean profile and leaves the fluctuations alone; it is exact for a linear profile on any grid. :local (Heinze et al. 2017, ACP 17, 7083, Eq. 3) uses each column's own gradient, first-order upwind with respect to $w_s$. On the bottom and top levels the gradient is the one-sided difference toward the interior. A centred :mean_gradient needs a time scheme stable for centred advection (SSP-RK3); with forward Euler use :local or a small $|w_s|Δt/Δz$.

w_s is any forcing quantity (number, profile, height-time ForcingTable, function (z, t) -> value), applied as its step mean. The default fields = (:θ, :qv) subsides qv only when the simulation carries moisture; a field named explicitly that the simulation does not carry is an error.

Unlike the prescribed AdvectiveTendencies, the tendency depends on the state and does not conserve $\int φ\,dV$: apply_forcing! returns the volume integrals $\int (\partial φ/\partial t)_s\,dV$ it added, a NamedTuple keyed by field (also kept for tendency_integrals), for the ledgers.

Aeolus.LargeScaleForcing.SurfaceTemperatureSeries — Type
SurfaceTemperatureSeries(t, T_s)

A prescribed surface (skin) temperature T_s (K) at times t (s), linearly interpolated and held at the end values (GABLS1: 265 K cooling at 0.25 K/h; GABLS2 / CASES-99: the observed series). Read with surface_temperature(series, t), a method of the SurfaceExchange.surface_temperature generic. This is not a surface model: it is the boundary value a wall law (P57-a, #169) or a surface step is given.

Aeolus.LargeScaleForcing.GeostrophicNudging — Method
GeostrophicNudging(U_g, V_g, τ) -> ProfileNudging

Rayleigh relaxation of the horizontal mean wind toward (U_g, V_g) on the time scale τ (s):

\[\left.\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}\right|_{n} = -\frac{\langle u\rangle_{xy} - U_g}{τ}, \qquad \left.\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}\right|_{n} = -\frac{\langle v\rangle_{xy} - V_g}{τ},\]

applied uniformly over each level, so turbulent fluctuations are not damped. This is a nudging, not a pressure gradient: it has no Coriolis counterpart, suppresses inertial oscillations and the nocturnal low-level jet, and is the behaviour of the Berlin case before #171. Since #248 it is a constructor of ProfileNudging, ProfileNudging((u = U_g, v = V_g), τ), not a second implementation (bit for bit the former type). U_g, V_g take the same forms as in GeostrophicPressureGradient.

Aeolus.LargeScaleForcing.apply_forcing! — Function
apply_forcing!(sim, forcing, t, Δt) -> sim

Add the tendency of forcing (an AbstractLargeScaleForcing or a Tuple of them) over the step [t, t + Δt] to the tendency fields of sim. Call it from the body_force! hook of step!, e.g. step!(sim, dt; body_force! = (s, Δt) -> apply_forcing!(s, F, s.t, Δt)). sim is duck-typed on mesh, u, v, tu, tv, tθ and f_coriolis, so any dynamical core of the package works. The GeostrophicPressureGradient takes the keyword f (default sim.f_coriolis): the Coriolis parameter of a model whose Coriolis term is a component of the stage pipeline rather than the core's.

For AdvectiveTendencies the humidity tendency goes to the keyword tq (a cell-centred field; default sim.tqv when the simulation carries moisture). A humidity tendency with no field to receive it is an error.

Aeolus.LargeScaleForcing.damping_tendency — Method
damping_tendency(F::ReferenceDamping) -> (; levels, integral)

The tendency the last apply_forcing! of F added: per level summed over the columns (levels, field units s⁻¹) and its volume integral (integral, field units m³ s⁻¹).

Aeolus.LargeScaleForcing.forcing_mean — Method
forcing_mean(x, z, t0, t1)

The time mean of the forcing quantity x at height z over the step [t0, t1]. For a ForcingTable it is exact (the table is piecewise linear in t), so that summing Δt · forcing_mean over steps reproduces $\int F\,dt$ to round-off whatever the step size; for a function it is Simpson's rule; a number is its own mean. With t1 == t0 it is forcing_value.

Aeolus.LargeScaleForcing.forcing_value — Method
forcing_value(x, z, t)

The forcing quantity x at height z (m) and time t (s): x itself for a number, the bilinear interpolant for a ForcingTable (held at the table ends), x(z, t) for a function.

Aeolus.LargeScaleForcing.nudging_weight — Method
nudging_weight(W::NudgingWeight, z, sim) -> w ∈ [0, 1]

The weight of W at height z (m) for the state sim (used when W.z_n is a callable).

Aeolus.LargeScaleForcing.read_sounding — Method
read_sounding(path; c = MoistThermodynamics.DEFAULT_CONSTANTS) -> Sounding

Read a sounding CSV in the format written by AeolusPrep.jl's aeolusprep fetch-sounding: comment lines start with #; lines #! key = value carry epoch (yyyy-mm-ddT00:00Z) and station_elevation_m; then a header and one row per level with (at least) the columns launch, t_s, p_Pa, gph_m, T_K, Td_K, wdir_deg, wspd_ms (NaN = missing). Rows with a temperature are thermodynamic levels, rows with a wind are wind levels (a row can be both). Wind direction is meteorological (the direction the wind blows from): $u = -U\sin φ$, $v = -U\cos φ$.

Aeolus.LargeScaleForcing.sounding_profiles — Method
sounding_profiles(s::Sounding; z_top = 3000.0, z = nothing,
                  c = MoistThermodynamics.DEFAULT_CONSTANTS)
    -> (; θ, qv, u, v, p, z)

The launches of s as height-time ForcingTables: potential temperature θ (K), specific humidity qv (kg kg⁻¹), wind components u, v (m/s) and pressure p (Pa), each at heights above the launch site (m) and the launch times (s from the epoch, sounding_time); z is the common height grid.

  • $θ = T/Π(p)$ with the dry Exner function $Π = (p/p_{ref})^{R_d/c_{pd}}$ of MoistThermodynamics (exner_function(p)), $q_v$ from the dew point (see SoundingLaunch), heights hypsometric.
  • Between the levels of one launch every quantity is linear in $z$; the table holds that interpolant exactly: the default grid z is the union of all launches' level heights in $[0, z_{top}]$ (plus 0 and z_top), so each launch's piecewise-linear profile is reproduced without loss at every height. Pass z for another grid.
  • Between launches forcing_value interpolates linearly in time; before the first and after the last launch, and above a launch's highest level, the table holds its end values (the ForcingTable convention).
Aeolus.LargeScaleForcing.sounding_time — Function
sounding_time(s::Sounding, y, m, d, hour_utc = 0) -> t (s)

Seconds from the epoch of s (00 UTC of s.epoch) to the UTC time hour_utc (fractional hours) of the date (y, m, d): the t at which to read the tables of sounding_profiles.

Aeolus.LargeScaleForcing.tendency_integrals — Method
tendency_integrals(F) -> NamedTuple

The volume integrals $\int (\partial φ/\partial t)\,dV$ (units of φ times m³ s⁻¹) that the last apply_forcing! of the state-dependent forcing F (ProfileNudging, Subsidence) added, keyed by field; an empty NamedTuple for forcings that keep no record and before the first call. tendency_integrals(F::Tuple, name) sums the entry name over a tuple of forcings (0 when none has it). Multiplied by the stage's weight times Δt they are what a ledger books.

Aeolus.SurfaceExchange.surface_temperature — Method
surface_temperature(s::SurfaceTemperatureSeries, t) -> T_s (K)

The prescribed surface temperature at time t (s), linear between the tabulated times and held at the end values.
