Shared contract — SurfaceExchange

SurfaceExchange is to Era V what RadiationCore is to Era III and MoistThermodynamics to Era IV: every surface model of the era — soil column, canopy, urban facets, building energy, snowpack, sea and lake ice, ocean mixed layer, sub-grid tiles — speaks it, so they agree on what a roughness length, a stability correction and a turbulent flux mean. It holds three things:

  • Material constants (SurfaceConstants). Latent heats, the heat capacities of water and ice, and g are delegated to MoistThermodynamics.ThermodynamicConstants through accessors (LH_v0, LH_s0, LH_f0, grav, cp_water, cp_ice, qualified as Aeolus.SurfaceExchange.grav etc.); the Stefan–Boltzmann constant is RadiationCore.STEFAN_BOLTZMANN.
  • Monin–Obukhov similarity: stability functions as a dispatch hierarchy (AbstractStabilityFunctions, default BusingerDyerBeljaarsHoltslag) and the surface-layer solver solve_surface_layer. A new family only needs psi_m/psi_h (and phi_m/phi_h for diagnostics) to plug into the solver. The maximum sustainable heat flux (Era VI, P58-e, #193: max_sustainable_heat_flux, mshf_solution, flux_friction_velocity, surface_mshf_ratio; closed form where log_linear_slope is defined) is the largest downward buoyancy flux MOST can carry at a given wind (Van de Wiel et al. 2012). See the derivation.
  • The surface interface: AbstractSurface and the generics an implementer extends. surface_albedo and surface_emissivity are the RadiationCore generics; the one-argument methods are broadband. Radiation.SlabLand is the first implementer.

Fluxes are positive upward (H, LE, E, τ); Rn is positive into the surface and G into the substrate, so $R_n = H + LE + G$.

Implementing a surface

using Aeolus
import Aeolus.SurfaceExchange: surface_temperature, roughness_lengths,
    surface_albedo, surface_emissivity, moisture_availability, step_surface!

mutable struct Pond <: AbstractSurface
    T::Float64
end
surface_temperature(p::Pond) = p.T
roughness_lengths(::Pond)   = (z0m = 1e-4, z0h = 1e-5, z0q = 1e-5)
surface_albedo(::Pond)      = 0.06
surface_emissivity(::Pond)  = 0.97
moisture_availability(::Pond) = 1.0          # open water: saturated skin

function step_surface!(p::Pond, f::SurfaceForcing, dt)
    fl = turbulent_fluxes(p, f)              # H, LE, τ, Rn and G = Rn - H - LE
    p.T += fl.G * dt / (1000 * 4181 * 1.0)   # 1 m of water
    return fl
end
Aeolus.SurfaceExchange — Module
SurfaceExchange

The Era V shared contract: the physics every surface model needs, in one place, so the soil, canopy, urban-facet, snow, sea-ice, ocean and tile models of Era V delegate rather than re-derive. It is to Era V what RadiationCore is to Era III and MoistThermodynamics to Era IV.

Three parts:

  1. Material constants — SurfaceConstants: densities and conductivities of water, ice and air, the Stefan–Boltzmann and von Kármán constants. Latent heats, heat capacities of water and ice and g are delegated to MoistThermodynamics.ThermodynamicConstants through accessors; they are never restated here.
  2. Monin–Obukhov similarity — the stability functions $φ_m, φ_h, ψ_m, ψ_h$ as a dispatch hierarchy (AbstractStabilityFunctions) and a surface-layer solver (solve_surface_layer) for $u_*$, $θ_*$, $q_*$, the Obukhov length and the bulk transfer coefficients.
  3. The surface interface — AbstractSurface and the generics a surface model implements (surface_temperature, roughness_lengths, surface_albedo, surface_emissivity, step_surface!), plus a default turbulent_fluxes that turns any implementer into sensible, latent and momentum fluxes.

Sign convention

All turbulent and radiative fluxes are positive upward (away from the surface) except net radiation Rn, which is positive into the surface, and G, positive into the substrate — so the surface energy balance reads $R_n = H + LE + G$. Scales follow $\overline{w'θ'} = -u_* θ_*$ and $\overline{w'q'} = -u_* q_*$: a surface warmer than the air gives $θ_* < 0$, $H > 0$ and $L < 0$ (unstable). $ζ = z/L$ is positive for stable stratification.

Aeolus.SurfaceExchange.AbstractStabilityFunctions — Type
AbstractStabilityFunctions

A family of Monin–Obukhov similarity functions. A concrete subtype implements the four generics

phi_m(sf, ζ), phi_h(sf, ζ)   # dimensionless gradients
psi_m(sf, ζ), psi_h(sf, ζ)   # integrated forms

with $ζ = z/L$ (positive stable), normalised so $φ(0) = 1$ and $ψ(0) = 0$, and related by $ψ(ζ) = ∫₀^ζ [1 - φ(x)]/x dx$, i.e. $dψ/dζ = (1 - φ)/ζ$. Profiles then read

u(z) = (u_*/κ) [ln(z/z₀ₘ) - ψ_m(z/L) + ψ_m(z₀ₘ/L)]

and likewise for θ and q with $ψ_h$. The surface-layer solver only calls psi_m and psi_h, so a new family (e.g. the COARE convective form of Grachev & Fairall) needs nothing else to be usable.

Aeolus.SurfaceExchange.AbstractSurface — Type
AbstractSurface

Supertype of every Era V surface model (slab, soil column, canopy, urban facet, snowpack, sea ice, ocean mixed layer, tile mosaic). An implementer S <: AbstractSurface provides

methodreturns
surface_temperature(s)skin temperature, K
roughness_lengths(s)(z0m = …, z0h = …, z0q = …), m
surface_albedo(s)broadband shortwave albedo
surface_emissivity(s)broadband longwave emissivity
step_surface!(s, forcing, dt)advances s, returns the SurfaceFluxes used

and may override the optional hooks, whose defaults describe a dry, liquid-water surface:

hookdefault
moisture_availability(s)0 (no evaporation)
surface_specific_humidity(s, forcing)$q_a + β (q_{sat}(T_s, p) - q_a)$
surface_latent_heat(s)$L_v(T_s)$ (snow/ice: override with $L_s$)

turbulent_fluxes then works for any implementer. The spectral surface_albedo(s, band, μ₀) / surface_emissivity(s, band) of RadiationCore are the same functions; the one-argument broadband methods are what surface energy balances use.

Aeolus.SurfaceExchange.BusingerDyerBeljaarsHoltslag — Type
BusingerDyerBeljaarsHoltslag(; γ_m = 16, γ_h = 16, a = 1, b = 2/3, c = 5, d = 0.35)

The default stability functions.

Unstable ($ζ < 0$): the Businger–Dyer gradients in Dyer's (1974) form with κ = 0.40, $φ_m = (1 - γ_m ζ)^{-1/4}$, $φ_h = (1 - γ_h ζ)^{-1/2}$, and their exact integrals (Paulson 1970), with $x = (1 - γ_m ζ)^{1/4}$, $y = (1 - γ_h ζ)^{1/2}$:

ψ_m = 2 ln[(1+x)/2] + ln[(1+x²)/2] - 2 atan(x) + π/2
ψ_h = 2 ln[(1+y)/2]

Stable ($ζ ≥ 0$): Beljaars & Holtslag (1991), which stay finite and well-behaved in very stable conditions where the log-linear $φ = 1 + 5ζ$ shuts turbulence off:

ψ_m = -[a ζ + b (ζ - c/d) e^{-dζ} + b c/d]
ψ_h = -[(1 + 2aζ/3)^{3/2} + b (ζ - c/d) e^{-dζ} + b c/d - 1]

with φ obtained analytically as $φ = 1 - ζ dψ/dζ$.

Aeolus.SurfaceExchange.BusingerDyerLogLinear — Type
BusingerDyerLogLinear(; γ_m = 16, γ_h = 16, β = 5, β_m = β, β_h = β)

Monin–Obukhov functions with the classical log-linear stable branch, $φ_m = 1 + β_m ζ$, $φ_h = 1 + β_h ζ$ and $ψ_m = -β_m ζ$, $ψ_h = -β_h ζ$ for $ζ ≥ 0$ (Businger et al. 1971; Dyer 1974). The unstable branch is delegated to BusingerDyerBeljaarsHoltslag with the same γ, so the two families agree exactly for $ζ < 0$.

β sets both coefficients; β_m, β_h set them separately. Uses:

  • $β_m = β_h = 5$ — the water-side $φ_t$ of Zeng & Beljaars (2005) (IFS Cy43r1 eq. 8.158), used by OceanMixedLayer.ZengBeljaarsWarmLayer;
  • $β_m = 4.8$, $β_h = 7.8$ — the surface-layer functions prescribed by the GABLS1 LES intercomparison (Beare et al. 2006, BLM 118, 247; quoted by Basu & Porté-Agel 2006, JAS 63, 2074, §4a), used by the stable-boundary-layer driver examples/sbl/driver.jl.
Aeolus.SurfaceExchange.MOSTConsistent — Type
MOSTConsistent(sf = BusingerDyerBeljaarsHoltslag(); ζ_min = -1e3, ζ_max = 1e4)

The gradient-Richardson stability factors that make a local closure $K = (κz)^2 |∂u/∂z| f(Ri)$ reproduce Monin–Obukhov similarity with the functions sf exactly:

\[f_m = φ_m(ζ)^{-2}, \qquad f_h = [φ_m(ζ)\, φ_h(ζ)]^{-1}, \qquad Ri = ζ\,φ_h(ζ)/φ_m(ζ)^2 .\]

With $∂u/∂z = u_*φ_m/(κz)$ this gives $K_m ∂u/∂z = u_*^2$ and $K_h ∂θ/∂z = u_*θ_*$ at every height (the derivation is on the theory page theory/turbulence_stability.md). The factor needs $ζ(Ri)$, the inverse of most_gradient_richardson, found by the same Illinois solver as solve_surface_layer on $[ζ_{\min}, 0]$ or $[0, ζ_{\max}]$ ($Ri(ζ)$ is monotone for BDBH and the log-linear form). A stable $Ri$ at or above $Ri(ζ_{\max})$ — beyond the critical Richardson number of a log-linear family — gives $f = 0$; an unstable $Ri$ below $Ri(ζ_{\min})$ is evaluated at $ζ_{\min}$. $Pr_n = φ_h(0)/φ_m(0) = 1$.

Aeolus.SurfaceExchange.MSHFSolution — Type
MSHFSolution{T}

The maximum of the Monin–Obukhov flux curve, from mshf_solution:

  • Q_max — the maximum sustainable kinematic downward buoyancy flux $-\overline{w'θ_v'}$ (K m s⁻¹, positive); $ρc_p Q_{max}$ is $H_{max}$ in W m⁻²;
  • u_star — the friction velocity at which it is attained (m s⁻¹);
  • ζ, L — the stability $ζ = z/L$ and the Obukhov length there;
  • method — :closed_form (log-linear) or :numerical;
  • interior — false when the maximum lay at the search bound ζ_max (a family whose flux curve does not turn over there: no finite MSHF up to that stability).

Aeolus.SurfaceExchange.SurfaceFluxes — Type
SurfaceFluxes{T}

Surface energy and momentum fluxes (W m⁻², N m⁻², kg m⁻² s⁻¹):

  • Rn — net radiation, positive into the surface;
  • H, LE — sensible and latent heat, positive upward;
  • G — heat into the substrate, positive downward; the default turbulent_fluxes sets it to the residual $R_n - H - LE$;
  • τ — momentum flux magnitude $ρ u_*²$;
  • E — evaporation mass flux $-ρ u_* q_*$, positive upward (negative = dew / deposition).
Aeolus.SurfaceExchange.SurfaceForcing — Type
SurfaceForcing(; sw_down = 0, lw_down = 350, T_air = 288.15, q_air = 0.008,
                 U = 5, p = 101325, z_ref = 10, rain = 0, snow = 0)

Atmospheric forcing seen by a surface over one step: downwelling shortwave and longwave irradiance (W m⁻²), air temperature (K) and specific humidity (kg kg⁻¹) at the reference height z_ref (m), wind speed U (m s⁻¹) at z_ref, surface pressure p (Pa), and rain and snow mass fluxes (kg m⁻² s⁻¹, positive downward).

Aeolus.SurfaceExchange.SurfaceLayerSolution — Type
SurfaceLayerSolution{T}

Result of solve_surface_layer. Fields:

  • u_star, θ_star, q_star — friction velocity (m s⁻¹) and the temperature (K) and humidity (kg kg⁻¹) scales, with $\overline{w'θ'} = -u_* θ_*$, $\overline{w'q'} = -u_* q_*$;
  • θv_star — the virtual-temperature scale that sets the buoyancy flux;
  • L, ζ — Obukhov length (m) and $ζ = z_m/L$;
  • C_D, C_H, C_E — bulk transfer coefficients referred to the effective wind U: $τ = ρ C_D U²$, $H = ρ c_p C_H U (θ_s - θ_a)$, $E = ρ C_E U (q_s - q_a)$;
  • U — the effective wind speed actually used (after the gustiness / minimum-wind safeguard);
  • iterations, converged — converged = false means the root lay beyond [ζ_min, ζ_max] and ζ was clamped there.

Aeolus.SurfaceExchange.bulk_richardson — Method
bulk_richardson(z, U, θv_a, θv_s; c = DEFAULT_SURFACE_CONSTANTS) -> Ri_B

Bulk Richardson number between the surface and height z,

Ri_B = g z (θv_a - θv_s) / (θv_a U²)

from virtual potential temperatures θv_a (at z) and θv_s (surface). Positive is stable. With $z_m = z_h$ and no moisture it is related to the solver's ζ by $Ri_B = ζ Φ_h/Φ_m²$, with Φ the stability-corrected log terms.

Aeolus.SurfaceExchange.flux_friction_velocity — Method
flux_friction_velocity(sf, U, z, z0m, Q, θ_ref; c = DEFAULT_SURFACE_CONSTANTS,
                       ζ_min = -1000, ζ_max = 1000) -> u_*

The friction velocity of Monin–Obukhov similarity for a prescribed upward kinematic buoyancy flux Q (K m s⁻¹; negative = surface cooling) at wind U and height z over roughness z0m: the root of $u_* = κU/Φ_m(ζ)$ with $ζ = -zκgQ/(u_*^3θ_{ref})$.

  • $Q ≥ 0$ (neutral or unstable): the unique root, $u_* ≥ κU/Λ$ (ζ clamped at ζ_min).
  • $0 < -Q ≤ Q_{max}$ (mshf_solution): two roots; the weakly stable one, on the rising branch of the flux curve between neutral and the MSHF point, which is the one continuously connected to the neutral state.
  • $-Q > Q_{max}$: no turbulent solution — the surface layer cannot carry the flux; returns 0 (collapse; Van de Wiel et al. 2012).

This is the flux-prescribed wall law of a stably stratified LES (WallModels.MOSTFluxWallLaw) and the Berlin MSHF diagnostic's definition of collapse.

Aeolus.SurfaceExchange.grav — Function

Gravitational acceleration (delegated to MoistThermodynamics).

Aeolus.SurfaceExchange.log_linear_slope — Method
log_linear_slope(sf::AbstractStabilityFunctions) -> α or nothing

The slope α of a log-linear stable branch, $φ_m = 1 + αζ$ and $ψ_m = -αζ$ for $ζ ≥ 0$, or nothing if sf is not log-linear there. max_sustainable_heat_flux uses the closed form when it is a number. The default is nothing; a family with a log-linear stable branch (e.g. BusingerDyerLogLinear, with $α = β_m$) extends it.

Aeolus.SurfaceExchange.max_sustainable_heat_flux — Method
max_sustainable_heat_flux(sf, U, z, z0m, z0h, θ_ref;
                          c = DEFAULT_SURFACE_CONSTANTS, method = :auto,
                          ζ_max = 1000) -> Q_max

The maximum sustainable heat flux (MSHF) of Van de Wiel et al. (2012): the largest downward kinematic buoyancy flux $Q = -\overline{w'θ_v'}$ (K m s⁻¹, positive; multiply by $ρc_p$ for W m⁻²) that Monin–Obukhov similarity with the stability functions sf can carry when the wind speed at height z (m) over roughness z0m (m) is U (m s⁻¹); θ_ref (K) is the reference (virtual) potential temperature of the buoyancy parameter $g/θ_{ref}$.

With U fixed, $u_* = κU/Φ_m(ζ)$ and $ζ = zκgQ/(u_*^3θ_{ref})$ give the flux as a function of the stability alone,

\[Q(ζ) = \frac{κ^2 U^3 θ_{ref}}{g z}\,\frac{ζ}{Φ_m(ζ)^3}, \qquad Φ_m = \ln\frac{z}{z_{0m}} - ψ_m(ζ) + ψ_m\!\left(ζ\frac{z_{0m}}{z}\right),\]

which is zero at neutral, rises to a maximum and falls as the very stable regime is approached. A surface that has to give away more than $Q_{max}$ (radiative cooling of a skin with little heat supply from below) cannot be in a turbulent equilibrium at that wind: the turbulence collapses (Van de Wiel et al. 2012; Donda et al. 2015).

Log-linear $φ_m = 1 + αζ$ (log_linear_slope returns α): $Φ_m = Λ + α(z - z_{0m})ζ/z$ with $Λ = \ln(z/z_{0m})$, and in terms of $u_*$, $Q = (κU u_*^2 - Λu_*^3)θ_{ref}/(α g (z - z_{0m}) κ)$, a cubic whose maximum is

\[Q_{max} = \frac{4}{27}\,\frac{κ^2 U^3 θ_{ref}}{α\, g\,(z - z_{0m})\,Λ^2} \quad\text{at}\quad u_* = \frac{2κU}{3Λ},\; ζ = \frac{Λ\,z}{2α(z - z_{0m})}\]

(for $z_{0m} ≪ z$ the form of Van de Wiel et al. 2012 with $z$ in place of $z - z_{0m}$). Other families are maximised numerically (mshf_solution).

z0h does not enter: with the wind (not the temperature difference) held, the flux–stability relation is the momentum half of Monin–Obukhov similarity, so $Q_{max}$ is independent of the heat roughness. It is accepted so that the call has the same surface parameters as solve_surface_layer. Every solution of that solver with wind U (its effective wind, sol.U), z = z_m and $θ_{ref} = θ_{va}$ lies on the curve, so its downward buoyancy flux $u_*θ_{v*}$ is at most Q_max.

  • Donda, J. M. M., van Hooijdonk, I. G. S., Moene, A. F., Jonker, H. J. J., van Heijst, G. J. F., Clercx, H. J. H. & van de Wiel, B. J. H. (2015). Collapse of turbulence in stably stratified channel flow: a transient phenomenon. Q. J. R. Meteorol. Soc., 141, 2137-2147.

Aeolus.SurfaceExchange.most_gradient_richardson — Method
most_gradient_richardson(sf::AbstractStabilityFunctions, ζ) -> Ri

The gradient Richardson number of the Monin–Obukhov profiles at $ζ = z/L$:

\[Ri = \frac{(g/θ_0)\,∂θ/∂z}{(∂u/∂z)^2} = ζ\,\frac{φ_h(ζ)}{φ_m(ζ)^2},\]

from $∂u/∂z = u_* φ_m/(κz)$, $∂θ/∂z = θ_* φ_h/(κz)$ and $L = u_*^2 θ_0/(κ g θ_*)$. For the log-linear $φ = 1 + αζ$ it tends to the critical value $1/α$ as $ζ → ∞$; for Beljaars–Holtslag it grows without bound (no critical Richardson number).

Aeolus.SurfaceExchange.most_stability_parameter — Method
most_stability_parameter(m::MOSTConsistent, Ri) -> ζ

$ζ = z/L$ with $Ri = ζφ_h/φ_m^2$ (see MOSTConsistent); Inf for a stable Ri at or beyond $Ri(ζ_{\max})$.

Aeolus.SurfaceExchange.mshf_solution — Method
mshf_solution(sf, U, z, z0m, θ_ref; c = DEFAULT_SURFACE_CONSTANTS,
              method = :auto, ζ_max = 1000) -> MSHFSolution

The maximum sustainable heat flux and where it is attained; see max_sustainable_heat_flux for the physics and the arguments. method = :auto uses the closed form when log_linear_slope returns a number and the numerical maximisation otherwise; :numerical forces the maximisation (used to validate it against the closed form); :closed_form requires a log-linear sf.

The numerical maximum of $f(ζ) = ζ/Φ_m(ζ)^3$ is found in $s = \ln ζ$: a 48-point scan of $ζ ∈ [10^{-6}, ζ_{max}]$ brackets the largest sample, then golden-section search narrows the bracket to $|Δs| ≤ 10^{-12}$. One maximisation costs about 120 evaluations of $ψ_m$.

Aeolus.SurfaceExchange.solve_surface_layer — Function
solve_surface_layer(sf = BusingerDyerBeljaarsHoltslag();
                    U, z_m, z_h = z_m, θ_a, θ_s, q_a = 0, q_s = 0,
                    z0m, z0h, z0q = z0h,
                    c = DEFAULT_SURFACE_CONSTANTS,
                    U_min = 0.1, gustiness = 0,
                    ζ_min = -1000, ζ_max = 1000, maxiter = 100)
    -> SurfaceLayerSolution

Monin–Obukhov similarity between the surface and the lowest model level: wind U at height z_m, potential temperature θ_a and specific humidity q_a at z_h; surface (skin) values θ_s, q_s; roughness lengths z0m, z0h, z0q. Solves

u_* = κ U / Φ_m(ζ),  θ_* = κ (θ_a - θ_s) / Φ_h(ζ),  q_* = κ (q_a - q_s) / Φ_q(ζ)
ζ   = z_m κ g θ_{v*} / (u_*² θ_{va})

with $Φ_m = \ln(z_m/z_{0m}) - ψ_m(z_m/L) + ψ_m(z_{0m}/L)$ (and Φh, Φq likewise with ψh and z0h, z0q), buoyancy from the virtual potential temperature ``θv = θ(1 + ε' q)$,$θ{v*} = θ(1 + ε' qa) + ε' θa q_``.

The single equation for ζ is solved by a bracketed secant (Illinois) iteration, which cannot diverge the way the textbook fixed-point on $L$ does in strongly stable conditions. The root lies in $[ζ_min, 0]$ for unstable and $[0, ζ_max]$ for stable stratification; if it lies beyond, ζ is clamped and converged = false.

Safeguards: the effective wind is $U_e = \max(\sqrt{U² + w_g²}, U_{min})$ with gustiness w_g (e.g. $1.2 w_*$, Beljaars 1995), so free convection at $U → 0$ keeps a finite $u_*$ instead of $L → 0$.

θ_a and θ_s must be potential temperatures on a common reference (e.g. $θ_a = T_a + g z/c_p$, $θ_s = T_s$).

Aeolus.SurfaceExchange.surface_mshf_ratio — Method
surface_mshf_ratio(sf, fl::SurfaceFluxes, forcing::SurfaceForcing, z0m;
                   U_min = 0.1, gustiness = 0, Q_max = nothing,
                   c = DEFAULT_SURFACE_CONSTANTS)
    -> (ratio, Q_down, Q_max, H_max, ζ_mshf)

How close the surface fluxes fl of a column are to its maximum sustainable heat flux (P58-e, #193). With the air state of forcing converted exactly as in turbulent_fluxes — $ρ$, $c_{pm}$, $θ_a = T_a + Γ_d z_{ref}$, $θ_{va} = θ_a(1 + ε' q_a)$ — the downward kinematic buoyancy flux is

\[Q_{down} = -\overline{w'θ_v'} = -\frac{H}{ρ c_{pm}}(1 + ε' q_a) - ε' θ_a \frac{E}{ρ} \;(= u_* θ_{v*}),\]

and $Q_{max}$ = max_sustainable_heat_flux(sf, U_e, z_ref, z0m, z0m, θ_va) at the effective wind $U_e = \max(\sqrt{U^2 + w_g^2}, U_{min})$ of solve_surface_layer. Returns ratio = Q_down/Q_max (negative for an upward, unstable flux), both fluxes, and $H_{max} = ρ c_{pm} Q_{max}$ (W m⁻²). For fluxes that turbulent_fluxes computed with the same sf, U_min and gustiness the ratio is at most 1 (the solver's solutions lie on the MSHF curve); ζ_mshf is the stability $z_{ref}/L$ at which the curve peaks (NaN when Q_max is passed in): a column whose $z_{ref}/L$ exceeds it is on the very stable branch, beyond the MSHF point, where a stronger temperature contrast carries less heat — the collapsed regime; the moisture term makes it the buoyancy flux, which is what MOST constrains, rather than the sensible heat flux alone. Q_max (K m s⁻¹) skips the maximisation and uses a value already computed for the same column and forcing (the Berlin coupling compares two sets of fluxes of one column against one MSHF).

Aeolus.SurfaceExchange.surface_specific_humidity — Method
surface_specific_humidity(s, forcing; c = DEFAULT_SURFACE_CONSTANTS) -> q_s

Effective specific humidity at the surface. The default is the β form $q_s = q_a + β\,(q_{sat}(T_s, p) - q_a)$ with β = moisture_availability, which gives exactly $E = β ρ C_E U (q_{sat} - q_a)$. A dry surface (β = 0) therefore has $q_s = q_a$ and zero moisture flux — note that $q_s = 0$ would instead be a strong vapour sink. Saturation is from MoistThermodynamics.saturation_specific_humidity (over liquid). Implementers with a resistance formulation, dew, or an ice surface override this.

Aeolus.SurfaceExchange.turbulent_fluxes — Method
turbulent_fluxes(s, forcing; sf = BusingerDyerBeljaarsHoltslag(),
                 c = DEFAULT_SURFACE_CONSTANTS, kwargs...) -> SurfaceFluxes

Default flux computation for any AbstractSurface: solves the surface layer between the skin (surface_temperature, surface_specific_humidity, roughness_lengths) and the forcing level, then

τ = ρ u_*²,   H = -ρ c_pm u_* θ_*,   E = -ρ u_* q_*,   LE = L E

with ρ and $c_{pm}$ of the moist air from MoistThermodynamics, and the air temperature referred to the surface by the dry adiabat, $θ_a = T_a + Γ_d z_{ref}$, $θ_s = T_s$. Rn is net_radiation and G the residual $R_n - H - LE$. kwargs (U_min, gustiness, …) are passed to solve_surface_layer.
