Lake and sea ice — IceThermodynamics

IceThermodynamics is the Era V model of floating ice (Phase 51, issue #37): the energy-conserving multi-layer thermodynamics of Bitz & Lipscomb (1999) with brine pockets, a snow layer, surface and basal melt, basal accretion, sublimation, snow-ice formation, and an ocean or lake heat flux. The same code does sea ice (a salinity profile, the water at its liquidus $T_0 - μS$) and lake ice (salinity zero, water at 0 °C). Theory, validation and the one discretisation subtlety are on the theory page.

IceColumn <: AbstractSurface, so step_surface!(col, forcing, dt) couples it to the atmosphere through the SurfaceExchange contract (Monin–Obukhov fluxes, saturation over ice, latent heat of sublimation). For benchmarks and offline use, step_ice! also takes a prescribed surface temperature or prescribed fluxes.

using Aeolus

# Lake ice under a held -10 °C surface: Stefan / Neumann growth.
lake = lake_ice_column(h_ice = 0.1, T_surface = 263.15)
for _ in 1:30*24
    step_ice!(lake, PrescribedSurfaceTemperature(263.15), 3600.0)
end
ice_thickness(lake)   # 0.616 m; Stefan gives 0.621 m, the ice's heat capacity slows it 1 %

# Multiyear sea ice with snow, coupled to the atmosphere.
sea = sea_ice_column(h_ice = 2.5, h_snow = 0.3)
f = SurfaceForcing(sw_down = 0.0, lw_down = 170.0, T_air = 243.15,
                   q_air = 3e-4, U = 5.0)
fl = step_surface!(sea, f, 1800.0)           # SurfaceFluxes; sea.budget has the details

Every step reports an IceStepBudget; its energy_residual (W m⁻²) is the change of ice_column_enthalpy minus every flux applied, and is zero to round-off.

Aeolus.IceThermodynamics — Module
IceThermodynamics

Lake- and sea-ice thermodynamics for Era V (Phase 51, issue #37): the energy-conserving multi-layer model of Bitz & Lipscomb (1999, "BL99") with brine pockets, an optional snow layer, surface and basal melting, basal accretion, sublimation, snow-ice formation and an ocean (or lake) heat flux, exposed as an AbstractSurface so the tile framework can aggregate it.

The physics in one paragraph

Sea ice is a mushy mixture of fresh ice and brine pockets held at the liquidus, $T_m = -μS$ (Assur 1958). Warming it melts the pocket walls, so its heat capacity $c(T,S) = c_0 + L μ S / T^2$ (Untersteiner 1961, Ono 1967) diverges near 0 °C, and the energy needed to finish melting a unit volume — the energy of melting (BL99 eq. 5)

q(S,T) = ρ_i [ c_0 (T_m - T) + L (1 - T_m/T) ]

— goes to zero at $T = T_m$. BL99's point is that a model which melts the surface with $q = ρ_i L$ while also letting brine pockets melt internally counts the same energy twice. Here the prognostic variable of every layer is its enthalpy $E = -q$ (J m⁻³, zero for meltwater at $T_m$), the heat equation is solved implicitly for temperature and the enthalpies are then updated from the interface fluxes, so the column conserves energy to round-off by construction; growth and melt consume exactly $-E$ per unit volume.

Ownership (Era V "one source of truth")

  • $ρ_i$, $k_i$ (fresh-ice conductivity $k_0$), $c_0 = c_{p,ice}$ and $L = L_f$ come from SurfaceExchange.SurfaceConstants — never restated here. The BL99 benchmark overrides $k_i$ through a SurfaceConstants(k_ice = 2.034) instance, not a local constant.
  • Saturation humidity over ice and the latent heat of sublimation come from MoistThermodynamics; the turbulent fluxes from SurfaceExchange.turbulent_fluxes (Monin–Obukhov).
  • The error function used by the Neumann solution is GasOptics.erf_series.
  • This module owns the sea-water freezing point seawater_freezing_point and the sea-ice material functions; other Era V modules (e.g. the ocean mixed layer) should delegate.

Aeolus.IceThermodynamics.IceColumn — Type
IceColumn

Thermodynamic ice column (BL99): N equal ice layers of thickness h_ice/N with prognostic enthalpy E (J m⁻³) and a fixed salinity profile S (ppt, attached to relative depth), one snow layer of depth h_snow with enthalpy E_snow, a skin temperature T_sfc (K), and the water below at its freezing point T_bottom (K) delivering the heat flux F_ocean (W m⁻², into the ice). S_new is the salinity of ice accreted at the base; ρ_water the density of the water (for the freeboard and snow-ice formation). budget holds the last IceStepBudget.

Build one with sea_ice_column or lake_ice_column. IceColumn <: AbstractSurface: step_surface!(col, forcing, dt) couples it to the atmosphere through SurfaceExchange (Monin–Obukhov fluxes, sublimation); step_ice! drives it with a prescribed surface temperature or prescribed fluxes.

Aeolus.IceThermodynamics.IceStepBudget — Type
IceStepBudget{T}

What one step_ice! did, as fluxes (W m⁻², positive into the ice/snow column unless stated) and thickness changes (m):

  • F_surface — non-penetrating energy entering at the top surface (conduction into the column plus the energy spent on surface melt);
  • F_penetrating — shortwave that entered below the surface ($i_0$ fraction), F_transmitted — the part of it that left through the base into the water;
  • F_conductive_top — conductive flux at the top, positive downward; F_conductive_bottom — conductive flux at the base, positive upward (heat drawn from the freezing interface);
  • F_ocean — ocean/lake heat flux applied at the base;
  • F_to_water — energy handed to the water because the ice melted through or the surface was open water;
  • enthalpy_in, enthalpy_out — enthalpy (J m⁻²) carried in by snowfall/deposition and out by sublimation or by ice/snow dumped into the water;
  • dh_top, dh_bottom, dh_snow — net changes of ice (top, base) and snow thickness; dh_snow_ice — ice formed from flooded snow;
  • T_surface (K), melting — whether the surface was at melting point;
  • energy_residual — $ΔU/Δt$ minus all the sources above (W m⁻²); zero to round-off in an energy-conserving model;
  • iterations — Newton iterations of the heat equation.
Aeolus.IceThermodynamics.PrescribedSurfaceFluxes — Type
PrescribedSurfaceFluxes(; sw_down, lw_down, H, LE, snowfall = 0,
                          T_snowfall = 273.15)

Atmospheric forcing as fluxes, the MU71/Semtner/BL99 protocol: downwelling short- and longwave (W m⁻²), and turbulent sensible and latent heat fluxes H, LE (W m⁻², positive upward, the contract sign) that do not depend on the surface temperature. The surface balance is

F(T_s) = (1-α)(1-i_0) SW↓ + ε LW↓ - ε σ T_s⁴ - H - LE.

As in MU71 and BL99, LE is an energy flux only (no sublimation mass loss). snowfall in kg m⁻² s⁻¹, added at T_snowfall (K).

Aeolus.IceThermodynamics.PrescribedSurfaceTemperature — Type
PrescribedSurfaceTemperature(T; snowfall = 0, T_snowfall = 273.15)

Hold the top surface at T (K) — the Stefan-problem boundary condition. If T is at or above the melting point the surface melts with whatever energy conduction cannot remove. snowfall in kg m⁻² s⁻¹.

Aeolus.IceThermodynamics.bl99_salinity_profile — Method
bl99_salinity_profile(N; S_max = 3.2) -> Vector

Time-independent multiyear salinity profile of MU71/BL99 (eq. 12, a fit to Schwarzacher 1959) at the midpoints of N equal layers,

S(z) = (S_max/2) [1 - cos(π z^{a/(z+b)})],  a = 0.407, b = 0.573,

with $z$ the relative depth (0 at the top, 1 at the base): fresh at the surface, S_max at the base.

Aeolus.IceThermodynamics.ice_column_enthalpy — Method
ice_column_enthalpy(col) -> J m⁻²

Total enthalpy of the ice and snow relative to meltwater at the melting point, $U = Σ_j E_j h/N + E_s h_s$ (negative: the energy the column would need to melt completely). Its change over a step is what the energy budget closes against.

Aeolus.IceThermodynamics.ice_conductivity — Method
ice_conductivity(T, S; p = IceParameters()) -> W m⁻¹ K⁻¹

Thermal conductivity of sea ice, $k = k_0 + β S / T$ (Untersteiner 1964; BL99 eq. 9), T in °C capped at p.T_k_cap and the result floored at p.k_min as in the BL99 code. $k_0$ is the contract's k_ice.

Aeolus.IceThermodynamics.ice_energy_of_melting — Method
ice_energy_of_melting(T, S; p = IceParameters()) -> J m⁻³

Energy needed to melt a unit volume of sea ice of salinity S (ppt) at temperature T (K) into brine at its melting temperature, BL99 eq. (5),

q(S,T) = ρ_i [ c_0 (T_m - T) + L (1 - T_m/T) ]     (T, T_m in °C).

For fresh ice it is $ρ_i [c_0 (0 - T) + L]$; for saline ice it falls to zero at $T = T_m$ (the ice is then all brine). At -2 °C and S = 3.2 ppt it is 0.92 $ρ_i L$ (BL99 §2).

Aeolus.IceThermodynamics.ice_enthalpy — Method
ice_enthalpy(T, S; p = IceParameters()) -> J m⁻³

Enthalpy of sea ice per unit volume relative to meltwater at its melting point, $E = -q(S,T)$ — the prognostic variable of every ice layer.

Aeolus.IceThermodynamics.ice_freeboard — Method
ice_freeboard(col) -> m

Height of the ice surface above the waterline by Archimedes, $h - (ρ_i h + ρ_s h_s)/ρ_w$. Negative freeboard floods the snow, which step_ice! then converts to snow ice (when params.snow_ice).

Aeolus.IceThermodynamics.ice_heat_capacity — Method
ice_heat_capacity(T, S; p = IceParameters()) -> J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹

Effective specific heat of sea ice including brine-pocket melting, $c(T,S) = c_0 + L μ S / T^2$ (Untersteiner 1961; Ono 1967; BL99 eq. 1, with γ = Lμ ≈ 18 kJ K kg⁻¹). Equals $(1/ρ_i) ∂E/∂T$.

Aeolus.IceThermodynamics.ice_layer_temperatures — Method

Layer temperatures (K), top to bottom, diagnosed from the enthalpies.

Aeolus.IceThermodynamics.ice_melting_temperature — Method
ice_melting_temperature(S; p = IceParameters()) -> K

Melting (liquidus) temperature of sea ice of bulk salinity S (ppt), $T_m = T_0 - μS$: the temperature at which the brine pockets have swallowed the whole volume.

Aeolus.IceThermodynamics.ice_snow_depth — Method

Snow depth on the ice (m).

Aeolus.IceThermodynamics.ice_snow_enthalpy — Method
ice_snow_enthalpy(T; p = IceParameters()) -> J m⁻³

Enthalpy of (fresh) snow per unit volume relative to meltwater at 0 °C, $E_s = ρ_s (c_0 T - L)$ (T in °C, ≤ 0).

Aeolus.IceThermodynamics.ice_temperature_from_enthalpy — Method
ice_temperature_from_enthalpy(E, S; p = IceParameters()) -> K

Inverse of ice_enthalpy: the temperature of ice of salinity S with enthalpy E (J m⁻³), from the quadratic $c_0 T^2 - (c_0 T_m + L + E/ρ_i) T + L T_m = 0$ (T in °C), capped at $T_m$. Fresh ice with $-ρ_i L < E ≤ 0$ is partly melted and sits at 0 °C.

Aeolus.IceThermodynamics.ice_thickness — Method

Ice thickness (m).

Aeolus.IceThermodynamics.lake_ice_column — Method
lake_ice_column(; h_ice = 0.1, h_snow = 0.0, N = 10, T_surface = 263.15,
                  F_ocean = 0.0, params = IceParameters(),
                  T_layers = nothing, T_snow = nothing) -> IceColumn

Fresh-water (lake) ice: salinity zero everywhere, the water below at 0 °C (seawater_freezing_point(0)), fresh-water density from the contract. With zero salinity the BL99 formulas reduce exactly to pure ice ($c = c_0$, $k = k_0$, $q = ρ_i [c_0(0 - T) + L]$). h_ice = 0 starts from open water, which freezes over once it loses heat.

Aeolus.IceThermodynamics.neumann_ice_thickness — Method
neumann_ice_thickness(t; ΔT, c = DEFAULT_SURFACE_CONSTANTS, k = c.k_ice,
                      ρ = c.ρ_ice, cp = cp_ice(c), L = LH_f0(c)) -> m

Exact thickness of fresh ice grown from water at its freezing point with the surface held ΔT below it, including the sensible heat of the ice (Neumann solution; Carslaw & Jaeger 1959 §11.2): $h = 2λ\sqrt{α t}$, $α = k/(ρ c_p)$, λ from neumann_lambda. The temperature profile is $T(z) = T_s + ΔT\,\mathrm{erf}(z/2\sqrt{αt})/\mathrm{erf}(λ)$.

Aeolus.IceThermodynamics.neumann_lambda — Method
neumann_lambda(St) -> λ

Root of $λ e^{λ²} \mathrm{erf}(λ) = St/\sqrt{π}$, the similarity constant of the one-phase Stefan (Neumann) problem with Stefan number $St = c ΔT / L$. For small St, $λ ≈ \sqrt{St/2}\,(1 - St/6)$ (the Stefan limit plus the first heat-capacity correction). Solved by Newton's method; the error function is GasOptics.erf_series.

Aeolus.IceThermodynamics.sea_ice_column — Method
sea_ice_column(; h_ice = 2.5, h_snow = 0.0, N = 10, T_surface = 253.15,
                 S_water = 34, T_bottom = seawater_freezing_point(S_water),
                 salinity = :bl99, S_max = 3.2, F_ocean = 2.0,
                 ρ_water = 1026, params = IceParameters(),
                 T_layers = nothing, T_snow = nothing) -> IceColumn

Sea-ice column. salinity is :bl99 (the MU71/BL99 multiyear profile bl99_salinity_profile with base value S_max), a number (isosaline ice) or a vector of N layer salinities; new basal ice gets S_max (BL99). The initial temperature is linear from T_surface to T_bottom unless T_layers (K, top to bottom) is given. F_ocean defaults to the 2 W m⁻² of MU71. ρ_water = 1026 kg m⁻³ is a representative sea-water density, used only for the freeboard.

Aeolus.IceThermodynamics.seawater_freezing_point — Method
seawater_freezing_point(S; μ = 0.054, c = DEFAULT_SURFACE_CONSTANTS) -> K

Freezing temperature of water of salinity S (ppt), the linear liquidus $T_f = T_0 - μS$ (Assur 1958) used by BL99 and CICE. S = 0 gives the fresh-water (lake) freezing point $T_0$ = 273.15 K; $S = 34$ gives -1.84 °C.

Aeolus.IceThermodynamics.stefan_ice_thickness — Method
stefan_ice_thickness(t; ΔT, h0 = 0, c = DEFAULT_SURFACE_CONSTANTS,
                     k = c.k_ice, ρ = c.ρ_ice, L = LH_f0(c)) -> m

Stefan's (1891) law for ice growing on water at its freezing point under a surface held ΔT kelvin below it, neglecting the heat capacity of the ice (quasi-steady linear profile) and any ocean heat flux:

h(t)² = h0² + 2 k ΔT t / (ρ L).

Hence $h ∝ \sqrt{t}$ from $h_0 = 0$; the basis of the degree-day ice-growth rules used on lakes.

Aeolus.IceThermodynamics.step_ice! — Method
step_ice!(col, bc, dt; kwargs...) -> IceStepBudget

Advance the ice column col by dt seconds under the top boundary condition bc — a PrescribedSurfaceTemperature, a PrescribedSurfaceFluxes or a SurfaceExchange.SurfaceForcing (Monin–Obukhov fluxes; kwargs go to turbulent_fluxes). The order of operations follows BL99:

  1. shortwave: $I_0 = i_0(1-α)SW↓$ penetrates snow-free ice and is absorbed with Beer's law ($κ_i$); what reaches the base goes to the water;
  2. heat equation, backward Euler in enthalpy form: Newton on the tridiagonal system for the layer temperatures (and the skin temperature from the surface balance $F(T_s) =$ conduction), brine-pocket heat capacity $ρ c(T,S)$ in the Jacobian; layers driven past their melting point are pinned there; the enthalpies are then updated from the interface fluxes, so energy is conserved;
  3. sublimation / deposition (only with a SurfaceForcing);
  4. surface melt with the surplus $F(T_{melt}) - F_c$ — snow first, then ice, each consuming $-E$ per unit volume; plus any layer whose enthalpy was pushed above zero (fully melted brine);
  5. basal accretion $Δh = Δt(F_w - F_{cb})/E(T_f, S_{new})$ or basal melt layer by layer from below;
  6. snowfall, snow-ice formation below the waterline, and a conservative remap to N equal layers.

Open water ($h = 0$) freezes when the net surface flux at $T_f$ plus F_ocean is negative (the new ice has $E(T_f, S_{new})$); otherwise the energy goes to the water. Returns the step's IceStepBudget, also stored in col.budget.

Aeolus.SurfaceExchange.moisture_availability — Method

Ice, snow and open water are saturated at the skin: β = 1.

Aeolus.SurfaceExchange.step_surface! — Method
step_surface!(col::IceColumn, forcing::SurfaceForcing, dt; kwargs...) -> SurfaceFluxes

AbstractSurface entry point: one step_ice! under Monin–Obukhov coupling, returning the fluxes at the new skin temperature, with G the energy that actually entered the ice/snow column (surface conduction + surface melt + penetrating shortwave), so $R_n - H - LE - G$ is the (Newton-converged, ~1e-9 W m⁻²) residual of the surface balance. Over open water G is what the water received.

Aeolus.SurfaceExchange.surface_latent_heat — Method

Latent heat of sublimation over ice/snow, of vaporisation over open water.

Aeolus.SurfaceExchange.surface_specific_humidity — Method

Saturation humidity over ice (over water when the ice has melted away).
