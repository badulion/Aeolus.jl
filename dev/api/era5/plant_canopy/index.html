Plant canopy — PlantCanopy

PlantCanopy resolves vegetation that spans several model layers (Phase 48). It owns the leaf-area-density profiles (UniformLAD, LalicMihailovicLAD, BetaLAD, TabulatedLAD), the shortwave and longwave radiation through them, the leaf energy balance and leaf temperature, and the canopy drag $-C_d a |U| U$, applied implicitly as $U/(1 + Δt C_d a |U|)$ — the exact solution of the drag equation over the step, stable at any dt. The theory, including the comparison of the 1-D column with the Inoue / Harman–Finnigan exponential wind profile, is in Resolved plant canopy.

Adding a canopy to an LES

using Aeolus
mesh = RegularGrid(; size = (64, 64, 32), extent = (1000.0, 1000.0, 200.0))
sim  = BoussinesqSimulation(mesh; bcs = FieldBoundaryConditions(:periodic, :periodic, :wall))

forest = LalicMihailovicLAD(4.0, 20.0; z_m = 12.0)       # LAI 4, 20 m tall
mask   = [i <= 32 for i in 1:64, j in 1:64]               # western half
drag   = ResolvedCanopyDrag(mesh, canopy_lad_field(mesh, forest, mask); C_d = 0.2)

step!(sim, 2.0; post_predictor! = (s, Δt) -> apply_canopy_drag!(s, drag, Δt))

Radiation through the canopy

The shortwave is solved per band with the canopy two-stream of Sellers (1985), using the Era III TwoStream layer formulae and adding solver; the sun angle can come straight from SolarGeometry.

sp  = solar_position(solar_time(2024, 6, 21, 11, 0, 0; latitude = 52.52, longitude = 13.40))
irr = clear_sky_irradiance(sp, BirdClearSky())
zf  = collect(0.0:1.0:30.0)
par = canopy_shortwave(forest, zf; sun = sp, sw_direct = 0.5irr.dni * sp.μ₀,
                       sw_diffuse = 0.5irr.dhi, leaves = RossGoudriaanLeaves(0.25),
                       optics = LeafOptics(0.10, 0.05), albedo_direct = 0.1)
par.absorbed_sunlit ./ par.lai_sunlit      # W m⁻² of sunlit leaf, per layer
lw  = canopy_longwave(forest, zf; lw_down = 330.0, T_leaf = 298.0, T_ground = 293.0)

The 50 % PAR share of the broadband flux above is only for illustration.

Leaf temperature

The leaf energy balance turns the absorbed radiation into leaf temperatures, sensible heat and transpiration. A single leaf:

p  = pft_photosynthesis_parameters(:bdt_temperate)
st = CoupledLeafPhysiology(p, pft_stomatal_model(:bdt_temperate); ca = 420.0)
leaf = leaf_energy_balance(st, CampbellNormanBoundaryLayer(leaf_width = 0.05);
                           R_abs = 1300.0, T_air = 298.15, e_air = 1200.0, U = 1.0,
                           Q = 1100.0)       # R_abs: SW + LW, both sides; Q: PAR photons
leaf.T_leaf - 298.15, leaf.H, leaf.LE

A resolved canopy, with the sunlit and shaded leaves of every layer and their longwave exchange solved together:

nir = canopy_shortwave(forest, zf; sun = sp, sw_direct = 0.5irr.dni * sp.μ₀,
                       sw_diffuse = 0.5irr.dhi, leaves = RossGoudriaanLeaves(0.25),
                       optics = LeafOptics(0.45, 0.25), albedo_direct = 0.2)
sol = canopy_leaf_energy_balance(forest, zf; shortwave = (par, nir), par = 1,
                                 T_air = 298.15, e_air = 1500.0, U = 1.0,
                                 lw_down = 330.0, T_ground = 296.0,
                                 leaves = RossGoudriaanLeaves(0.25), stomata = st)
sol.T_sunlit, sol.T_shaded        # K, per layer
sol.heat_source, sol.vapour_source  # W m⁻³ and kg m⁻³ s⁻¹ for the air

Pass T_prev = sol, heat_capacity (J m⁻² K⁻¹) and dt to make the leaf temperature prognostic (backward Euler). leaf_penman_monteith gives the linearised solution. The theory and the comparison with measured leaf temperatures are in Resolved plant canopy §5.

Within-canopy profiles

A 1-D column gives the mean wind and, coupled to the leaf energy balance, the air temperature inside the canopy. With a measured leaf-area profile:

h  = 15.0
p  = TabulatedLAD([0.0, 6.0, 8.3, 10.5, 12.8, 15.0], [0.0, 0.0, 0.70, 0.75, 0.20, 0.0]; LAI = 3.5)
zf = collect(range(0, 1.5h; length = 301))
lad = layer_leaf_area_density(p, zf)
l_c = harman_finnigan_mixing_length(0.3, 0.15, leaf_area_index(p) / h)
w  = solve_canopy_wind_column(zf, lad; C_d = 0.15, u_top = 5.0, bottom = :no_slip,
                              l_mix = z -> canopy_mixing_length(z; h = h, l_c = l_c))
canopy_displacement_height(w, lad; C_d = 0.15)       # m

# air temperature from leaf heat sources S [K s⁻¹] per layer
K  = [harman_finnigan_scalar_diffusivity(z; h = h, u_star = w.u_star, l_mix = l_c) for z in zf]
S  = zeros(length(lad)); S[lad .> 0] .= 2e-3
T  = solve_canopy_scalar_column(zf, K, S; top_value = 293.15, bottom_flux = 0.0)
T.c, T.flux                                          # K; K m s⁻¹ at the faces

In a coupled run S is canopy_leaf_energy_balance(...).heat_source divided by $ρc_p$, iterated with T.c as the leaves' air temperature. The comparison with a wind tunnel, the CHATS orchard and the Hyytiälä pine forest is in Resolved plant canopy §6 and examples/validation/canopy_profiles.jl.

Aeolus.PlantCanopy — Module
PlantCanopy

Resolved plant canopies for the LES (Era V, Phase 48).

This module owns the vertical structure of vegetation and the momentum sink it exerts on the resolved flow:

  1. Leaf-area density profiles — AbstractLeafAreaDensity with a uniform crown (UniformLAD), the empirical forest profile of Lalic & Mihailovic (2004) (LalicMihailovicLAD) and the beta-distribution profile of Markkanen et al. (2003) (BetaLAD). Every profile integrates to its leaf-area index by construction; layer_leaf_area_density maps a profile onto model layers so that the discrete column still sums to the LAI.

  2. Implicit canopy drag — the form drag $F_i = -C_d\,a(z)\,|U|\,U_i$ applied as the pointwise factor $U^{n+1} = U^* / (1 + Δt\,C_d\,a\,|U^*|)$ (canopy_drag_factor, apply_canopy_drag!). For the pure drag equation $dU/dt = -C_d a |U| U$ this factor is the exact solution over the step, so it is unconditionally stable and never reverses the flow; explicit Euler of the same term is unstable once $Δt\,C_d\,a\,|U| > 2$.

  3. In-canopy mean wind — a 1-D column solver (solve_canopy_wind_column) with a mixing-length closure and the same implicit drag, and the analytic exponential profile of Inoue (1963) in the form of Harman & Finnigan (2007) (canopy_exponential_wind) that it reproduces.

  4. Radiation in the canopy — leaf-angle distributions with Ross's $G$-function in the Goudriaan (1977) fit (RossGoudriaanLeaves), Beer–Lambert beam and diffuse transmittance, and the canopy two-stream model of Sellers (1985) for the shortwave (canopy_shortwave) with the sunlit/shaded split of de Pury & Farquhar (1997), plus the longwave exchange between sky, leaf layers and ground (canopy_longwave). The optical-depth coordinate is the cumulative leaf area cumulative_leaf_area, and the two-stream layer algebra and adding solver are those of the Era III TwoStream module, supplied with the canopy γ coefficients.

  5. Leaf energy balance — leaf temperature from the balance of absorbed radiation, thermal emission, sensible heat and transpiration (leaf_energy_balance), with the leaf boundary layer of Campbell & Norman (1998) (CampbellNormanBoundaryLayer) or CLM5 (CLMLeafBoundaryLayer) and transpiration from a fixed stomatal conductance or the coupled photosynthesis–stomata solution of Photosynthesis (CoupledLeafPhysiology). Diagnostic (steady) or prognostic (backward Euler with a leaf heat capacity). Over a resolved canopy canopy_leaf_energy_balance solves the sunlit and shaded leaves of every layer together with the longwave exchange of item 4, and returns the heat and vapour sources for the air. leaf_penman_monteith is the linearised (Penman–Monteith) limit.

  6. Within-canopy profiles — measured leaf-area profiles (TabulatedLAD), the wall-limited canopy mixing length (canopy_mixing_length), the displacement height as the centroid of momentum absorption (canopy_displacement_height), and the steady K-theory scalar column (solve_canopy_scalar_column) with the Harman & Finnigan (2008) diffusivity (harman_finnigan_scalar_diffusivity), which turns the leaf heat sources of item 5 into an air-temperature profile. These are what the #25 validation against wind-tunnel, orchard and forest profiles uses (test/era5_canopy_profiles.jl).

Aeolus.PlantCanopy.AbstractLeafAngleDistribution — Type
AbstractLeafAngleDistribution

Leaf orientation of a canopy. An implementer provides

  • leaf_projection_function(d, μ) — Ross's $G(μ)$: mean projection of unit leaf area onto the plane normal to a direction with cosine of zenith angle $μ$;
  • average_inverse_optical_depth(d) — Sellers' $\bar μ = \int_0^1 μ'/G(μ')\,dμ'$, the diffuse-stream cosine;
  • canopy_scattering_parameters(d, optics, μ) — $(ω, β, β_0)$, the leaf single-scattering albedo and the up-scatter fractions of diffuse and of direct-beam radiation.
Aeolus.PlantCanopy.AbstractLeafAreaDensity — Type
AbstractLeafAreaDensity

Supertype of vertical leaf-area-density (LAD) profiles $a(z)$ [m² m⁻³], one-sided leaf area per unit volume, with $z$ the height above ground. An implementer provides

  • leaf_area_density(p, z) — $a(z)$, zero outside the canopy;
  • canopy_height(p) — the canopy top $h$ [m];
  • leaf_area_index(p) — $\int_0^h a\,dz$ [m² m⁻²];

and may add height breakpoints (kinks of $a$) through _lad_breakpoints(p) so that layer integrals stay at round-off accuracy.

Aeolus.PlantCanopy.AbstractLeafBoundaryLayer — Type
AbstractLeafBoundaryLayer

Leaf boundary-layer model. Implementers extend leaf_boundary_layer_conductances, which returns the conductance to heat (both leaf sides) and to water vapour (the stomata-bearing sides), in mol m⁻² s⁻¹ per unit one-sided leaf area. Concrete models: CampbellNormanBoundaryLayer, CLMLeafBoundaryLayer.

Aeolus.PlantCanopy.AbstractLeafStomata — Type
AbstractLeafStomata

What controls transpiration in the leaf energy balance: FixedStomatalConductance or CoupledLeafPhysiology. A plain number passed where a stomata model is expected is a fixed conductance.

Aeolus.PlantCanopy.BetaLAD — Type
BetaLAD(LAI, h; p = 3, q = 2)

Beta-distribution LAD profile (Markkanen et al. 2003):

\[a(z) = \frac{LAI}{h}\, \frac{(z/h)^{p-1}\,(1 - z/h)^{q-1}}{B(p, q)},\qquad 0 ≤ z ≤ h,\]

which integrates to LAI exactly. The mean leaf height is $h\,p/(p+q)$ and the maximum is at $h\,(p-1)/(p+q-2)$. p, q ≥ 1 keeps the profile finite. $B(p,q)$ is evaluated from P3Microphysics.lgamma_lanczos.

Aeolus.PlantCanopy.CLMLeafBoundaryLayer — Type
CLMLeafBoundaryLayer(; d_leaf = 0.04, Cv = 0.01)

The leaf boundary layer of CLM5 (eq. 2.5.122), evaluated by Photosynthesis.leaf_boundary_layer_conductance: one conductance $C_v\sqrt{U/d_{leaf}}$ (m s⁻¹, converted to molar units) used for both heat and water vapour per unit one-sided leaf area, as in CLM's canopy fluxes. No free convection.

Aeolus.PlantCanopy.CampbellNormanBoundaryLayer — Type
CampbellNormanBoundaryLayer(; leaf_width = 0.05, turbulence_factor = 1.4,
                              free_convection = true, stomatal_sides = 1)

Leaf boundary layer of Campbell & Norman (1998, ch. 7) for a flat leaf of width leaf_width [m], characteristic dimension $d = 0.72 w$. Per leaf side, in mol m⁻² s⁻¹:

  • forced convection (laminar flat plate), multiplied by turbulence_factor for outdoor turbulence (the factor 1.4 they recommend): $g_{Ha} = 0.135\sqrt{u/d}$, $g_{va} = 0.147\sqrt{u/d}$;
  • free convection (laminar, heated plate): $g_{Ha} = 0.05\,(|T_\ell - T_a|/d)^{1/4}$, $g_{va} = 0.055\,(|T_\ell - T_a|/d)^{1/4}$;

the larger of the two is used (free_convection = false keeps only the forced value). These are the textbook flat-plate correlations $\overline{Nu} = 0.664\,Re^{1/2}Pr^{1/3}$ and $\overline{Nu} = 0.54\,Ra^{1/4}$ (Incropera et al. 2007, chs. 7 and 9) evaluated for air at 20 °C; test/era5_leaf_energy.jl re-derives them to 1–3 %. The heat conductance of the leaf is that of both sides, $2g_{Ha}$; the vapour conductance is $n\,g_{va}$ with n = stomatal_sides = 1 for hypostomatous leaves (most broadleaf trees) and 2 for amphistomatous ones. The coefficients are C&N's values at 20 °C and 101.3 kPa; their weak temperature and pressure dependence is neglected.

Aeolus.PlantCanopy.CanopyLeafEnergySolution — Type
CanopyLeafEnergySolution

Result of canopy_leaf_energy_balance. Layer arrays are ordered bottom-up like z_faces. Per-class quantities are per unit leaf area of that class; layer quantities are per unit ground area of the layer.

  • T_sunlit, T_shaded [K], gs_sunlit, gs_shaded [mol m⁻² s⁻¹] — for a layer without sunlit (or any) leaves the sunlit value repeats the shaded one (and a layer without leaves reports the air temperature), so the fields can seed the next prognostic step
  • lai_sunlit, lai_shaded — leaf area of each class in each layer
  • sw_absorbed, lw_net — absorbed shortwave (all bands) and net longwave of the leaves [W m⁻²]
  • H, LE, storage [W m⁻²], E [mol H₂O m⁻² s⁻¹], An [µmol CO₂ m⁻² s⁻¹] — per layer; sw_absorbed + lw_net = H + LE + storage
  • heat_source [W m⁻³] and vapour_source [kg m⁻³ s⁻¹] — H and the transpired mass divided by the layer depth: the sources of the air's heat and moisture equations
  • H_total, LE_total, E_total — column sums
  • longwave — the CanopyLongwaveSolution at the final leaf temperatures
  • residual — max over layers of $|sw\_absorbed + lw\_net - H - LE - storage|$ evaluated with that final longwave [W m⁻²]; iterations, converged
Aeolus.PlantCanopy.CanopyLongwaveSolution — Type
CanopyLongwaveSolution

Result of canopy_longwave; arrays ordered bottom-up, fluxes [W m⁻²] per unit ground area.

  • flux_up, flux_dn — at the faces
  • net_absorbed — net longwave gain (absorbed minus emitted) of the leaves of each layer
  • ground_net — net longwave into the ground
  • outgoing — upward longwave leaving the canopy top
  • emissivity_layer — emissivity $1 - R - T$ of each layer
Aeolus.PlantCanopy.CanopyScalarSolution — Type
CanopyScalarSolution

Result of solve_canopy_scalar_column: layer-centre heights z, the scalar c there, the faces z_faces and the upward turbulent flux flux at every face (flux[1] into the air from the ground, flux[end] leaving through the top), in the units of K·c/z.

Aeolus.PlantCanopy.CanopyShortwaveSolution — Type
CanopyShortwaveSolution

Result of canopy_shortwave for one spectral band. Face arrays (length N+1) and layer arrays (length N) are ordered bottom-up like z_faces; fluxes are per unit ground area [W m⁻²].

  • z_faces, Λ_faces — heights and cumulative leaf area above each face
  • flux_dn_dir, flux_dn_dif, flux_up — direct and diffuse downward, and upward, fluxes at the faces
  • absorbed — absorbed by the leaves of each layer
  • absorbed_sunlit, absorbed_shaded — the same split between sunlit and shaded leaves (they sum to absorbed)
  • lai_sunlit, lai_shaded — sunlit and shaded leaf area of each layer (sum to the layer LAI)
  • incident, reflected, canopy_absorbed, ground_absorbed — column totals; incident = reflected + canopy_absorbed + ground_absorbed
  • K, μ̄, ω, β, β0 — the optical parameters used
Aeolus.PlantCanopy.CanopyWindSolution — Type
CanopyWindSolution

Result of solve_canopy_wind_column: layer-centre heights z and wind u [m s⁻¹], the friction velocity at the column top u_star (from the resolved stress $K\,∂U/∂z$ there), the canopy drag integrated over the column drag_integral = $\sum C_d a_k |U_k| U_k Δz_k$ [m² s⁻²], the kinematic stress on the ground tau_ground (zero for a free-slip bottom), the number of iterations and the final update norm residual (max $|ΔU|/U_{top}$). z_faces are the layer faces and K the eddy viscosity $l^2|∂U/∂z| + ν$ [m² s⁻¹] at those faces at convergence (K[1] is the ground exchange coefficient, zero for a free-slip bottom): the diffusivity that solve_canopy_scalar_column needs, divided by a turbulent Schmidt number.

Aeolus.PlantCanopy.CoupledLeafPhysiology — Type
CoupledLeafPhysiology(photosynthesis, stomata; ca = 400, β = 1,
                      stress_targets = (:vcmax, :rd, :g0))

Stomatal conductance and transpiration from the coupled photosynthesis–stomata solution Photosynthesis.leaf_gas_exchange, with photosynthetic parameters photosynthesis (C3/C4), a stomatal closure stomata (Ball-Berry / Medlyn), ambient CO₂ ca [µmol mol⁻¹] and the soil-water-stress factor β. The leaf boundary-layer vapour conductance of the energy balance is passed to it as gb, so the CO₂, water and heat exchange of the leaf see the same boundary layer, and the leaf temperature of the energy balance is the one the biochemistry runs at.

Aeolus.PlantCanopy.FixedStomatalConductance — Type
FixedStomatalConductance(gs)

A prescribed stomatal conductance to water vapour gs [mol m⁻² s⁻¹]; 0 is a leaf with closed stomata (no transpiration). Transpiration is Photosynthesis.leaf_transpiration.

Aeolus.PlantCanopy.HorizontalLeaves — Type
HorizontalLeaves()

All leaves horizontal: $G(μ) = μ$, so every direction is extinguished at exactly one unit per unit leaf area ($K = 1$, $\bar μ = 1$) and a scattering event sends the leaf reflectance up and the leaf transmittance down whatever the incidence angle ($β = β_0 = α/ω$). For this canopy the two-stream equations are exact rather than an approximation, which makes it a reference case (Goudriaan 1977).

Aeolus.PlantCanopy.LalicMihailovicLAD — Type
LalicMihailovicLAD(LAI, h; z_m, n_below = 6, n_above = 1/2)

Empirical forest LAD profile of Lalic & Mihailovic (2004, eq. 1):

\[a(z) = L_m \left(\frac{h - z_m}{h - z}\right)^n \exp\!\left[n\left(1 - \frac{h - z_m}{h - z}\right)\right], \qquad n = \begin{cases} 6 & 0 ≤ z < z_m \\ 1/2 & z_m ≤ z ≤ h \end{cases}\]

$z_m$ is the height of maximum LAD and $L_m = a(z_m)$. Here $L_m$ is not a free parameter: it is fixed at construction so that the profile integrates to LAI (the paper's own empirical fit for $L_m$ is not used). The profile vanishes smoothly at the canopy top.

Aeolus.PlantCanopy.apply_canopy_drag! — Method
apply_canopy_drag!(sim, drag::ResolvedCanopyDrag, dt) -> (Fx, Fy, Fz)

Apply the canopy drag implicitly to the velocity of sim: every face velocity is multiplied by $1/(1 + Δt\,C_d\,a\,|U|)$ with $a$ the LAD at that face and $|U|$ the full speed there (the other two components averaged from their four neighbouring faces), all factors computed from the velocity before any is applied.

Call it from the post_predictor! hook of step!, i.e. on the predicted velocity $u^*$ and before the pressure projection:

drag = ResolvedCanopyDrag(mesh, canopy_lad_field(mesh, LalicMihailovicLAD(4.0, 20.0; z_m = 12.0), mask))
step!(sim, dt; post_predictor! = (s, Δt) -> apply_canopy_drag!(s, drag, Δt))

This replaces the explicit tendency $-C_d a |U| U$: that one is unstable once $Δt\,C_d\,a\,|U| > 2$ (for $C_d = 0.2$, $a = 1\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$ and $|U| = 5\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ that is any $Δt > 2\,\mathrm{s}$); this one is stable for every $Δt$.

Returns the volume-integrated kinematic force the step exerted, $\sum (U' - U)/Δt\,ΔV$ [m⁴ s⁻²] per component (negative for a flow in the positive direction); divide by the domain area for the canopy momentum sink per unit ground area (a stress, m² s⁻²).

Aeolus.PlantCanopy.canopy_attenuation_coefficient — Method
canopy_attenuation_coefficient(C_d, a, l) -> γ [m⁻¹]

Exponential decay rate of the mean wind into a uniform canopy for a constant mixing length l (Inoue 1963):

\[γ = \left(\frac{C_d\,a}{2\,l^2}\right)^{1/3},\]

from the steady balance $\frac{d}{dz}\left(l^2 \left|\frac{dU}{dz}\right|\frac{dU}{dz}\right) = C_d\,a\,U^2$ with $U = U_h e^{γ(z-h)}$. Inoue's dimensionless attenuation coefficient is $γh$. With the Harman–Finnigan mixing length this is $γ = β/l$.

Aeolus.PlantCanopy.canopy_diffuse_transmittance — Method
canopy_diffuse_transmittance(d, Λ; n = 64) -> T_d

which for spherical leaves is $2E_3(Λ/2)$. The two-stream model replaces this by $e^{-Λ/\bar μ}$; the difference is the angular-closure error of the two-stream approximation (see the theory page).

Aeolus.PlantCanopy.canopy_displacement_height — Method
canopy_displacement_height(sol::CanopyWindSolution, lad; C_d) -> d [m]

Zero-plane displacement of a canopy as the mean height of momentum absorption (Thom 1971; Jackson 1981):

\[d = \frac{\sum_k z_k\,C_d a_k |U_k| U_k\,Δz_k + 0\cdot τ_g} {\sum_k C_d a_k |U_k| U_k\,Δz_k + τ_g},\]

with the drag and ground stress $τ_g$ of a converged solve_canopy_wind_column solution on the same layers (lad the layer LAD it was solved with). Heights are measured from z_faces[1]. This is what tower studies report as $d$ when they infer it from the within-canopy momentum-flux profile.

  • Thom, A. S. (1971). Momentum absorption by vegetation. Q. J. R. Meteorol. Soc., 97, 414-428.
  • Jackson, P. S. (1981). On the displacement height in the logarithmic velocity profile. J. Fluid Mech., 111, 15-25.
Aeolus.PlantCanopy.canopy_drag_factor — Method
canopy_drag_factor(C_d, a, speed, dt) -> f ∈ (0, 1]

Factor by which the canopy drag $dU_i/dt = -C_d\,a\,|U|\,U_i$ (Shaw & Schumann 1992) multiplies the velocity over a step dt:

\[f = \frac{1}{1 + Δt\,C_d\,a\,|U|}.\]

With the speed taken at the start of the step, $U\,f$ is the exact solution of the pure drag equation — the speed obeys $d|U|/dt = -C_d a |U|^2$ with solution $|U|_0/(1 + C_d a |U|_0 t)$ and the direction does not change — and equivalently a backward-Euler step with the drag coefficient lagged. Because $0 < f ≤ 1$ for every dt, the update is unconditionally stable and can never reverse the flow, which explicit Euler ($1 - Δt C_d a |U|$) does once $Δt\,C_d\,a\,|U| > 1$ and amplifies once it exceeds 2.

canopy_drag_force(C_d, a, u, v, w) -> (Fu, Fv, Fw)

Instantaneous kinematic canopy drag force per unit mass [m s⁻²], $F_i = -C_d\,a\,|U|\,U_i$. A diagnostic; the model applies the drag through implicit_canopy_drag.

Aeolus.PlantCanopy.canopy_leaf_energy_balance — Method
canopy_leaf_energy_balance(z_faces, lad; shortwave, par = 1, T_air, e_air, U,
                           p_air = 101325, lw_down, T_ground, ε_leaf = 0.98,
                           ε_ground = 0.95, leaves = SphericalLeaves(), stomata,
                           boundary_layer = CampbellNormanBoundaryLayer(),
                           T_prev = nothing, heat_capacity = 0, dt = Inf,
                           c = DEFAULT_CONSTANTS, maxiter = 100, tol = 1e-9)
    -> CanopyLeafEnergySolution
canopy_leaf_energy_balance(profile::AbstractLeafAreaDensity, z_faces; kwargs...)

Temperatures of the sunlit and shaded leaves of every layer of a resolved canopy, and the heat and water vapour they give to the air.

  • shortwave — a CanopyShortwaveSolution from canopy_shortwave on the same layers, or a collection of them (one per band, e.g. (par, nir)), or nothing at night. The absorbed shortwave of each class is the sum over bands of absorbed_sunlit / absorbed_shaded divided by the class leaf area, and the sunlit and shaded leaf areas are those of the first band.
  • par — index into shortwave of the PAR band, whose absorbed flux per leaf area is converted to photons by Photosynthesis.par_to_photon_flux for a CoupledLeafPhysiology; nothing for no PAR
  • T_air [K], e_air [Pa], U [m s⁻¹] — scalars or one value per layer
  • lw_down, T_ground, ε_leaf, ε_ground, leaves — as in canopy_longwave
  • stomata, boundary_layer — as in leaf_energy_balance
  • T_prev — a previous CanopyLeafEnergySolution (or a named tuple (sunlit = …, shaded = …) of per-layer temperatures) with heat_capacity $C$ [J m⁻² K⁻¹] and dt for a prognostic step

The leaves exchange longwave radiation with each other, the sky and the ground, so the layers are coupled. They are solved by an outer iteration: the longwave field of canopy_longwave is computed with each layer at the leaf-area-weighted $T^4$ of its two classes; the longwave absorbed per unit leaf area of a layer is then held fixed while every class solves its own leaf_energy_balance, with its emission $ε_e σT^4$, $ε_e = 2(1 - R_k - T_k)/ΔΛ_k$ the layer's two-stream emissivity per unit leaf area ($2ε_{leaf}$ for spherical leaves in the thin-layer limit). That is repeated until no leaf temperature changes by more than tol [K]. The longwave is recomputed at the final temperatures for the reported fluxes, and residual measures how well each layer's energy balance closes with it.

For a prognostic step each class uses its own previous temperature. That conserves energy exactly while the class leaf areas are unchanged; when the sun moves, leaves that change class carry their old class's temperature, which is not tracked.

Aeolus.PlantCanopy.canopy_longwave — Method
canopy_longwave(z_faces, lad; lw_down, T_leaf, T_ground, ε_leaf = 0.98,
                ε_ground = 0.95, leaves = SphericalLeaves()) -> CanopyLongwaveSolution
canopy_longwave(profile::AbstractLeafAreaDensity, z_faces; kwargs...)

Thermal-infrared exchange between sky, leaves and ground. T_leaf [K] is a scalar or one value per layer; lw_down is the sky longwave at the top face [W m⁻²].

Each layer is a two-stream slab with leaf reflectance $1 - ε_{leaf}$ and zero leaf transmittance, whose diffuse reflectance $R$ and transmittance $T$ come from the same TwoStream layer formulae as the shortwave (diffuse extinction $1/\bar μ$ per unit leaf area). By Kirchhoff's law the layer emits $(1 - R - T)\,σT_{leaf}^4$ both up and down. The coupled layer–ground system, including every multiple reflection, is then solved exactly as a linear system in the face fluxes, so the result conserves energy to round-off and gives zero net exchange when sky, leaves and ground share one temperature and the sky is black.

Aeolus.PlantCanopy.canopy_mixing_length — Method
canopy_mixing_length(z; h, l_c, κ = SurfaceExchange.von_karman()) -> l [m]

Mixing-length profile for solve_canopy_wind_column over a canopy of height h with canopy mixing length l_c (e.g. harman_finnigan_mixing_length):

\[l(z) = \min(l_c,\ κ z)\quad (z ≤ h),\qquad l(z) = l_c + κ (z - h)\quad (z > h).\]

Inside the canopy the length is the constant $l_c$ of the Inoue / Harman–Finnigan theory, limited by the wall value $κz$ near the ground so that eddies cannot be larger than their distance from it (with a constant $l_c$ down to the ground a no-slip floor takes a spurious share of the stress). Above the canopy it grows at the surface-layer rate $κ$ from $l_c$, continuous at $h$; the roughness-sublayer enhancement of Harman & Finnigan (2007) is not included.

Aeolus.PlantCanopy.canopy_scattering_parameters — Method
canopy_scattering_parameters(d, optics::LeafOptics, μ) -> (ω, β, β₀)

Leaf single-scattering albedo $ω = α + τ_l$ and the fractions of scattered radiation sent upward, $β$ for diffuse and $β_0$ for direct radiation from a sun at zenith cosine μ, as in Sellers (1985) and the CLM5.0 Technical Note (§2.3.1, eqs. 2.3.13–2.3.16):

\[ωβ = \tfrac12\left[α + τ_l + (α - τ_l)\cos^2\bar θ\right], \qquad \cos\bar θ = \frac{1 + χ_L}{2}, \qquad ωβ_0 = \frac{1 + \bar μ K}{\bar μ K}\,a_s(μ),\]

with the single-scattering albedo of the canopy for isotropically scattering leaves

\[a_s(μ) = \frac{ω}{2}\int_0^1 \frac{μ' G(μ)}{μ G(μ') + μ' G(μ)}\,dμ' = \frac{ω}{2}\frac{G}{μφ_2 + G}\left[1 - \frac{μφ_1}{μφ_2 + G} \ln\frac{μφ_1 + μφ_2 + G}{μφ_1}\right].\]

Aeolus.PlantCanopy.canopy_shortwave — Method
canopy_shortwave(z_faces, lad; sun, sw_direct, sw_diffuse, leaves = SphericalLeaves(),
                 optics::LeafOptics, albedo_direct, albedo_diffuse = albedo_direct)
    -> CanopyShortwaveSolution
canopy_shortwave(profile::AbstractLeafAreaDensity, z_faces; kwargs...)

Shortwave radiation in one spectral band through a canopy of layer-mean leaf-area densities lad [m² m⁻³] on the layers bounded by z_faces (bottom-up), by the canopy two-stream model of Sellers (1985).

  • sun — cosine of the solar zenith angle, or a SolarGeometry.SolarPosition (its apparent $μ_0$ is used); a sun at or below the horizon has no direct beam and no sunlit leaves
  • sw_direct — direct-beam irradiance on a horizontal plane at the top face [W m⁻²] (i.e. DNI·μ, or the flux_dn_dir of an Era III atmospheric solve at the surface level)
  • sw_diffuse — diffuse downward irradiance at the top face [W m⁻²]
  • albedo_direct, albedo_diffuse — ground (soil) albedo for the two

The layer reflectances and transmittances and the adding solution are those of TwoStream (layer_reflectance_transmittance_sw, adding_solver!) with the canopy γ coefficients ($τ = Λ$, $μ_0 → 1/K$, see canopy_scattering_parameters). Because the equations depend on height only through $Λ$, the solution is independent of how the leaf area is layered.

Absorption is split between sunlit and shaded leaves as in de Pury & Farquhar (1997) and CLM: sunlit leaves absorb the fraction $1-ω$ of the unscattered beam they intercept, and the absorbed diffuse (sky plus scattered) radiation of a layer is shared in proportion to leaf area. The sunlit leaf area of a layer between cumulative leaf areas $Λ_t < Λ_b$ is $(e^{-KΛ_t} - e^{-KΛ_b})/K$.

Shortwave absorbed by the leaves of every cell of a resolved canopy, written to q (Nx, Ny, Nz) as a volumetric source [W m⁻³]; returns the (Nx, Ny) shortwave absorbed by the ground under each column [W m⁻²]. lad is the cell-centred leaf-area density of canopy_lad_field. sw_direct, sw_diffuse and the albedos are scalars or (Nx, Ny) matrices (e.g. the cloud-shadowed direct beam of SolarGeometry.cloud_shadow_map times the clear-sky flux).

Aeolus.PlantCanopy.harman_finnigan_scalar_diffusivity — Method
harman_finnigan_scalar_diffusivity(z; h, u_star, l_mix, β = 0.3, Sc = 0.5,
                                   κ = SurfaceExchange.von_karman()) -> K_c [m² s⁻¹]

Turbulent diffusivity for scalars in and above a canopy in the form of Harman & Finnigan (2008), as used by the multilayer canopy of Bonan et al. (2018, eqs. 22-23):

\[K_c(z) = \frac{l\,u_*}{Sc}\,\exp\!\left[\frac{(z-h)\,β}{l}\right]\quad (z ≤ h), \qquad K_c(z) = \frac{(l + κ(z-h))\,u_*}{Sc}\quad (z > h),\]

with the canopy mixing length l_mix (constant in the canopy, e.g. harman_finnigan_mixing_length), $β = u_*/U_h$ and the canopy-top turbulent Schmidt number Sc (0.5 in neutral flow, Harman & Finnigan 2008). Inside the canopy the diffusivity decays with the wind, $e^{(z-h)β/l}$ being the exponential wind profile of canopy_exponential_wind. Above the canopy the constant-stress eddy viscosity $l\,u_*$ of the mixing length $l + κ(z-h)$ is used, which is continuous at $z = h$; the roughness-sublayer correction of Harman & Finnigan is not included.

Unlike the local $l^2|∂U/∂z|$ of solve_canopy_wind_column, this form does not collapse in a sheltered trunk space, where turbulence is imported from the crown rather than made by local shear.

Aeolus.PlantCanopy.leaf_energy_balance — Function
leaf_energy_balance(stomata, boundary_layer = CampbellNormanBoundaryLayer();
                    R_abs, T_air, e_air, U, p_air = 101325, Q = 0,
                    emissivity = 0.98, emission = 2emissivity,
                    T_prev = nothing, heat_capacity = 0, dt = Inf,
                    c = DEFAULT_CONSTANTS, maxiter = 200) -> LeafEnergyBalance

Temperature of a leaf from its energy balance, per unit one-sided leaf area (Campbell & Norman 1998, ch. 14; Monteith & Unsworth 2013):

\[C\,\frac{T_\ell - T_{prev}}{Δt} = R_{abs} - ε_e σ T_\ell^4 - c_p g_H (T_\ell - T_a) - λ(T_\ell)\,E(T_\ell).\]

  • R_abs — absorbed short- and longwave radiation [W m⁻²]
  • T_air [K], e_air [Pa], U [m s⁻¹], p_air [Pa] — the air around the leaf
  • Q — absorbed PAR photon flux [µmol m⁻² s⁻¹], used by a CoupledLeafPhysiology
  • stomata — a FixedStomatalConductance, a number (the same), or a CoupledLeafPhysiology
  • boundary_layer — an AbstractLeafBoundaryLayer giving $g_H$ and the vapour conductance $g_v$
  • emission — $ε_e$, thermal emission per unit leaf area in units of $σT^4$: $2ε$ for an isolated flat leaf (both sides); inside a canopy canopy_leaf_energy_balance supplies the two-stream value
  • heat_capacity $C$ [J m⁻² K⁻¹], dt [s], T_prev [K] — with $C > 0$ and finite $Δt$ this is one backward-Euler step of the prognostic leaf temperature (unconditionally stable); the default is the diagnostic (steady) balance, which is also the $Δt → ∞$ limit. A leaf holding ≈ 0.3 mm of water has $C ≈ 1.3\,\mathrm{kJ\,m^{-2}\,K^{-1}}$.

$c_p$ is the molar heat capacity of dry air, $λ$ the latent heat of vaporisation at leaf temperature (MoistThermodynamics), $E$ the transpiration of the stomata model with $g_v$ in series. The scalar equation is solved on a bracket by the Illinois method of SurfaceExchange (the residual is monotone in $T_\ell$ except through the photosynthetic response of $g_s$, which a bracketing method tolerates). Negative $E$ (dew onto a leaf colder than the dew point) is returned as computed, through the stomatal path only; wet-leaf interception and dew are the land column's (#21).

Aeolus.PlantCanopy.leaf_penman_monteith — Method
leaf_penman_monteith(; R_abs, T_air, e_air, g_H, g_s, g_v, p_air = 101325,
                       emissivity = 0.98, emission = 2emissivity,
                       c = DEFAULT_CONSTANTS)
    -> (; ΔT, T_leaf, H, LE, E, R_ni, g_r, s, g_w)

The linearised (Penman–Monteith) solution of the diagnostic leaf energy balance for a fixed stomatal conductance (Campbell & Norman 1998, §14.3; Monteith & Unsworth 2013, ch. 13). Linearising the emission, $ε_eσT_\ell^4 ≈ ε_eσT_a^4 + c_p g_r ΔT$ with the radiative conductance $g_r = 4ε_eσT_a^3/c_p$, and the saturation vapour pressure, $e_s(T_\ell) ≈ e_s(T_a) + sΔT$, gives

\[ΔT = T_\ell - T_a = \frac{R_{ni} - λ g_w D/p}{c_p (g_H + g_r) + λ g_w s/p}, \qquad R_{ni} = R_{abs} - ε_eσT_a^4,\]

with $D = e_s(T_a) - e_a$, $g_w = g_s g_v/(g_s + g_v)$ the total leaf conductance to vapour, $λ$ in J mol⁻¹, all evaluated at air temperature. Then $H = c_p g_H ΔT$ and $λE = λ g_w (D + sΔT)/p$. $s = de_s/dT = L_v e_s/(R_v T^2)$ is the exact derivative of the MoistThermodynamics saturation curve. The nonlinear leaf_energy_balance tends to this as the forcing ($R_{ni}$, $D$) goes to zero, with an error quadratic in $ΔT$.

Aeolus.PlantCanopy.solve_canopy_scalar_column — Method
solve_canopy_scalar_column(z_faces, K, source; top_value,
                           bottom_flux = nothing, bottom_value = nothing)
    -> CanopyScalarSolution

Steady profile of a scalar $c$ (potential temperature, humidity, a tracer) in a canopy column with first-order (K-theory) turbulent transport:

\[\frac{d}{dz}\left(K \frac{dc}{dz}\right) + S(z) = 0,\qquad F = -K\frac{dc}{dz} \ \text{(upward flux)},\]

with the per-layer source source [c-units s⁻¹] (e.g. the leaf heat_source of canopy_leaf_energy_balance divided by $ρc_p$), the diffusivity K [m² s⁻¹] at the N+1 faces (e.g. the K of a CanopyWindSolution divided by a turbulent Schmidt number), c = top_value at the top face and at the bottom either an upward flux bottom_flux (face 1) or a surface value bottom_value exchanged through K[1] over the half layer. Exactly one bottom condition must be given.

In one dimension the steady flux is fixed by the sources alone, $F_{f+1} = F_f + S_k Δz_k$, so the discrete budget holds exactly: the flux out of the top is the bottom flux plus the column-integrated source. The scalar then follows by integrating $dc/dz = -F/K$ down from the top.

This is the closure of Harman & Finnigan (2008) and of multilayer land models (e.g. Bonan et al. 2018). K-theory cannot represent counter-gradient transport by canopy-scale eddies (Denmead & Bradley 1985), so in a canopy with a sparse trunk space it describes the crown better than the air beneath it.

  • Harman, I. N. & Finnigan, J. J. (2008). Scalar concentration profiles in the canopy and roughness sublayer. Boundary-Layer Meteorol., 129, 323-351.
  • Bonan, G. B. et al. (2018). Modeling canopy-induced turbulence in the Earth system. Geosci. Model Dev., 11, 1467-1496.
  • Denmead, O. T. & Bradley, E. F. (1985). Flux-gradient relationships in a forest canopy. In The Forest-Atmosphere Interaction, Reidel, 421-442.
Aeolus.PlantCanopy.solve_canopy_wind_column — Method
solve_canopy_wind_column(z_faces, lad; C_d, l_mix, u_top, ν = 1.5e-5,
                         bottom = :free_slip, dt = 1e4, maxiter = 5_000,
                         tol = 1e-12) -> CanopyWindSolution

Steady mean wind in a 1-D canopy column driven by a prescribed wind u_top at the top face z_faces[end]:

\[0 = \frac{∂}{∂z}\left(K \frac{∂U}{∂z}\right) - C_d\,a(z)\,|U|\,U,\qquad K = l^2 \left|\frac{∂U}{∂z}\right| + ν .\]

lad holds layer-mean LAD values (e.g. from layer_leaf_area_density); l_mix is a number or a function of height evaluated at layer faces. bottom = :free_slip (zero stress) or :no_slip ($U = 0$ at z_faces[1]).

The steady state is reached by pseudo-time marching in which the diffusion is backward Euler with $K$ lagged one iteration and the drag is the implicit factor of canopy_drag_factor folded into the same tridiagonal solve — so every iteration is unconditionally stable and, at convergence, the discrete balance holds exactly: the stress at the top equals drag_integral + tau_ground.

Keep ν small against $l^2|∂U/∂z|$ where the comparison with the inviscid exponential profile matters: deep in a dense canopy the mixing-length viscosity decays like $U$ and a molecular floor becomes comparable.

Aeolus.PlantCanopy.sunlit_leaf_fraction — Method
sunlit_leaf_fraction(d, μ, Λ) -> f_sun

Fraction of the leaves at cumulative leaf area Λ that see the sun, which for randomly placed leaves equals the beam transmittance $e^{-KΛ}$ (Monsi & Saeki 1953; de Pury & Farquhar 1997).
