Stretched and terrain-following grids (Phase 24)

Aeolus.StretchedGrids.StretchedGrid — Type
StretchedGrid{T} <: AbstractMesh{T}

Finite-volume mesh that is uniform Cartesian in x and y and carries an arbitrary non-uniform vertical coordinate. Storage layout, halo convention and interior index ranges are byte-compatible with RegularGrid — only the vertical metrics differ.

Vertical metric arrays (all include halo levels)

fieldlengthlives atmeaning
zfNz+2Hz+1facesbottom face of cell k
zcNz+2Hzcentres½(zf[k]+zf[k+1])
dzcNz+2Hzcentreszf[k+1]-zf[k] — cell thickness
dzfNz+2Hz+1faceszc[k]-zc[k-1] — centre spacing

dzc is the control-volume height (use it for face→centre differences and for the divergence); dzf is the distance between the two centres a face separates (use it for centre→face gradients). See grid_stretching_ratio for the quality metric.

Construction

StretchedGrid(T=Float64; size, extent, halo=(2,2,2), origin=(0,0,0),
              stretching=:uniform)

stretching accepts

  • :uniform — reproduces RegularGrid bit-for-bit.
  • (:geometric, r) — dz[k+1] = r·dz[k], rescaled to fill Lz.
  • (:tanh, β) — tanh clustering at the bottom wall.
  • (:tanh_both, β) — tanh clustering at both walls (channel LES).
  • (:custom, zf_vector) — user-supplied interior faces, length Nz+1.

See also TerrainFollowingGrid, hydrostatic_balance_error.

Aeolus.StretchedGrids.StretchedGrid — Method
StretchedGrid(g::RegularGrid)

Lift a uniform RegularGrid into the stretched representation. The resulting mesh is numerically identical (stretching = :uniform); this exists so that terrain-following grids and other stretched-grid machinery can accept either mesh type.

Aeolus.StretchedGrids.StretchedPoissonSolver — Type
StretchedPoissonSolver(g::StretchedGrid; bc_x = :periodic, bc_y = :periodic)

Direct solver of the conservative stretched-z Poisson equation (the divergence of the gradient of the grid's own ∂x, ∂y, ∂z stencils), cell-centred, with homogeneous-Neumann conditions at the bottom and top (walls) and bc_x, bc_y ∈ (:periodic, :neumann) on the sides.

Horizontally the operator is diagonalised by the real-to-real FFT (:periodic, half-complex DFT) or the DCT-II (:neumann), with the exact 3-point eigenvalues -4/Δx² sin²(πm/Nx) and -4/Δx² sin²(πm/2Nx); in z it leaves one tridiagonal system per horizontal wavenumber,

\[\frac{1}{\Delta z_c[k]}\left[\frac{\hat φ_{k+1}-\hat φ_k}{\Delta z_f[k+1]} - \frac{\hat φ_k-\hat φ_{k-1}}{\Delta z_f[k]}\right] - λ_h \hat φ_k = \hat b_k ,\]

solved by the Thomas algorithm, whose factorisation is computed once at construction (Schumann & Sweet 1988, J. Comput. Phys. 75, 123). The mean mode (λ_h = 0) is singular with Neumann ends: the volume-weighted mean of the source is removed (the compatibility condition of a closed domain) and the column is integrated directly. The returned φ has zero volume-weighted mean. Metrics come from cell_thickness and center_spacing; exact to round-off on any face distribution.

The transform, the Thomas sweep and the mean-mode integration are those of ColumnTridiagonalSolver (field core), which also solves the density-weighted projection of the sound-proof cores (#309): h = 1, a_k = 1/(Δz_c[k] Δz_f[k]), c_k = 1/(Δz_c[k] Δz_f[k+1]), measure Δz_c, gauge Δz_c.

The default solver of BoussinesqSimulation(g::StretchedGrid) for a non-uniform grid; periodic-in-z is not supported (a stretched vertical coordinate has no periodic meaning).

Aeolus.StretchedGrids.TerrainFollowingGrid — Type
TerrainFollowingGrid{T} <: AbstractMesh{T}

Gal-Chen/Somerville terrain-following (σ-) mesh built on top of a StretchedGrid that supplies the computational vertical coordinate. See terrain_following_grid to construct one.

z(x, y, σ) = h(x,y) + σ (H - h(x,y)) / H,   σ ∈ [0, H], H = Lz

Stored metrics (all on the full storage array, halos included):

  • h, hx, hy — terrain height and its horizontal slopes.
  • J — vertical Jacobian ∂z/∂σ = (H-h)/H, at cell centres; Jx, Jy, Jzf are the same quantity at x-, y- and σ-faces.
  • dσdx, dσdy — ∂σ/∂x|_z, ∂σ/∂y|_z at cell centres, plus the _xf / _yf / _zf variants co-located with the faces where the cross-derivative terms of each stencil are needed.
  • zc3, zf3 — physical heights of cell centres and σ-faces.

znodes returns the computational σ levels (it must return a vector); physical heights are three-dimensional and live in physical_heights. Initialise fields with set_physical!, not Fields.set!, so that samples land at their true height.

Accessors: metric_jacobian, terrain_height, physical_heights.

Aeolus.Grids.znodes — Method
znodes(g::TerrainFollowingGrid, loc)

Computational σ levels, not physical heights — the AbstractMesh interface requires a vector and physical height varies with (x,y). Use physical_heights for the real geometry.

Aeolus.Operators.FiniteVolume._add_buoyancy! — Method
_add_buoyancy!(tw, θ, θ₀, g_accel, g::TerrainFollowingGrid)

Boussinesq buoyancy at σ-faces with volume-weighted σ-interpolation of θ. Buoyancy acts along true vertical; in a σ-coordinate the w-equation is the one for the physical vertical velocity, so no metric factor enters here — the metric appears in the pressure gradient instead.

Aeolus.Operators.FiniteVolume._add_sponge! — Method
_add_sponge!(tf, f, α_max, z_start, z_top, f_ref, g::TerrainFollowingGrid)

Rayleigh damping keyed on the physical height of each sample, so the sponge is a horizontal slab in real space even though the coordinate surfaces below it are tilted by the orography. The one damping kernel _add_damping!, sampled at the 3-D physical heights; f_ref is a number, a Field or a halo-padded array (a level vector is a target per σ-level).

Aeolus.Operators.FiniteVolume._interp_to_face! — Method
_interp_to_face!(out::Field{FaceZ}, f, g::TerrainFollowingGrid)

Volume-weighted σ-interpolation, delegated to the underlying StretchedGrid. The Jacobian cancels: physical thicknesses along a column are J·Δσ with the same J, so the weights are the σ-space ones.

Aeolus.Operators._buoyancy_frequency_squared! — Method
_buoyancy_frequency_squared!(N2, θ, qv, θ₀, g, m::StretchedGrid)

$N^2_k = (g/θ_0)(θ_{v,k+1/2} - θ_{v,k-1/2})/Δz_c[k]$: the face values of the uniform form (the mean of the two adjacent centres) differenced over the cell thickness. Then $N^2_k = (g/θ_0)(θ_{k+1} - θ_{k-1})/(2Δz_c[k])$ is exactly the per-cell share of the stretched κt operator's potential-energy change (`FiniteVolume.addeddydiffusionscalar!with the stretched metrics: face gradients overdzf, divergence overdzc), so`Σ(−κt N^2)V$is the buoyancy flux of the SGS heat flux, as on the uniform grid. For a linear θ it is$Γ(Δzc[k-1]/2 + Δzc[k] + Δzc[k+1]/2)/(2Δzc[k])$: Γ on the uniform levels, Γ(1 + 1.5e-3) on a geometric part of ratio 1.08, and first order where the ratio jumps (+1.7 % in the last 25 m cell under the first stretched one of the nesting parent, #259). With condensate (`Operators._Cloudy`, #239) the per-cell stencil is the uniform one's (condensate loading, the saturated N² in cells with$qc > 0$), over the same$Δzc[k]``.

Aeolus.Simulations._default_solver — Method
_default_solver(g::StretchedGrid, bcs) -> AbstractPressureSolver

The pressure solver BoussinesqSimulation(g::StretchedGrid) uses when none is passed. A :uniform grid takes the RegularGrid default of the same grid (so it reproduces the RegularGrid run); a stretched grid takes the direct StretchedPoissonSolver, periodic on each side with Periodic velocity BCs and Neumann otherwise. The vertical BCs must not be periodic.

Aeolus.StretchedGrids._b_tf_lap_fx! — Method
_laplacian!(out::Field{L}, f::Field{L}, g::TerrainFollowingGrid) where {L}

Laplace–Beltrami operator of the Gal-Chen transformation, assembled in conservative flux form so that it is self-adjoint (needed by the CG pressure solve):

\[\nabla^2 f = \frac{1}{J}\Big[ \partial_x\!\big(J F^x\big) + \partial_y\!\big(J F^y\big) + \partial_\sigma\!\big(J F^\sigma\big)\Big],\]

\[F^x = \partial_x f + \sigma_x \partial_\sigma f, \quad F^y = \partial_y f + \sigma_y \partial_\sigma f, \quad F^\sigma = \sigma_x \partial_x f + \sigma_y \partial_y f + \big(\sigma_x^2+\sigma_y^2+J^{-2}\big)\partial_\sigma f ,\]

i.e. the contravariant metric (GC6). Fluxes are built on the faces they cross (F^x on x-faces with σ_x co-located there, F^σ on σ-faces), then differenced back to the centre — the same discrete div(grad) pairing of dzf/dzc as on a StretchedGrid. For h ≡ 0 the cross terms vanish, J = 1, G^{σσ} = 1, and the result is identical to the stretched-grid Laplacian.

Face-located inputs

For L ∈ {FaceX, FaceY, FaceZ} the cell-centre terrain metrics (J, σ_x, σ_y) are reused rather than re-interpolated to the staggered position; that is an O(Δ·∂²h) approximation of the metric (exact for flat or constant-slope terrain) and is intended for diffusion-type terms, not for the pressure Poisson operator, which acts on cell-centred fields. The vertical σ-metrics do follow the location: a z-face field has control volumes of height Δσ_f[k] and neighbours Δσ_c apart (before #151 the centre metrics were used for it too, a first-order error on a stretched base grid even over flat terrain).

Allocation

Three flux scratch arrays of totalsize(g) are allocated per call. Acceptable for diagnostics and once-per-substep diffusion; a scratch-carrying in-place variant is deferred.

Aeolus.StretchedGrids._b_tf_surface_flux! — Method
_add_surface_flux!(tϕ, F_bot, F_top, g::TerrainFollowingGrid)

Wall fluxes divided by the physical thickness J·Δσ_c of the first and last interior cell, so a cell sitting on high ground (small J, thin cell) receives the correspondingly larger tendency.

Aeolus.StretchedGrids._b_tf_∂x_cc! — Method
_∂x!(out::Field{CellCenter}, f::Field{CellCenter}, g::TerrainFollowingGrid)

Horizontal derivative at constant physical height:

\[\left.\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\right|_z = \left.\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\right|_\sigma + \frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial x}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\sigma}, \qquad \frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial x} = -\frac{H-\sigma}{H-h}\frac{\partial h}{\partial x} .\]

The first term is the ordinary centred difference along the coordinate surface; the second is the terrain correction. Omitting it is the standard terrain-following error and shows up immediately as a spurious pressure-gradient force over sloping ground.

Aeolus.StretchedGrids._b_tf_∂x_cf! — Method
_∂x!(out::Field{FaceX}, f::Field{CellCenter}, g::TerrainFollowingGrid)

Centre → x-face gradient with the terrain cross-term, using ∂σ/∂x evaluated at the x-face and ∂f/∂σ averaged from the two straddling columns. This is the kernel the pressure-gradient force goes through, so the metric must be co-located with the face or the hydrostatic cancellation over a slope is lost.

Aeolus.StretchedGrids._b_tf_∂x_fc! — Method
_∂x!(out::Field{CellCenter}, u::Field{FaceX}, g::TerrainFollowingGrid)

Face → centre horizontal derivative of u at constant height. The σ-derivative of u is taken after averaging u to the cell centre in x, keeping the stencil symmetric about the cell.

Aeolus.StretchedGrids._b_tf_∂z_cc! — Method
_∂z!(out::Field{CellCenter}, f::Field{CellCenter}, g::TerrainFollowingGrid)

Collocated ∂f/∂z = (1/J) ∂f/∂σ with the unequal-spacing three-point σ-derivative.

Aeolus.StretchedGrids._b_tf_∂z_cf! — Method
_∂z!(out::Field{FaceZ}, f::Field{CellCenter}, g::TerrainFollowingGrid)

∂f/∂z = (1/J) ∂f/∂σ at the σ-face. No cross terms appear: the two cell centres straddling a σ-face share the same (x,y), so their difference is a genuine vertical difference, and J·Δσ_f is the physical distance between them.

Aeolus.StretchedGrids._b_tf_∂z_fc! — Method
_∂z!(out::Field{CellCenter}, w::Field{FaceZ}, g::TerrainFollowingGrid)

∂w/∂z = (1/J)(w_{k+1}-w_k)/Δσ_c[k]. This is the plain vertical derivative of w; the divergence additionally needs the contravariant flux J w̃ (see _divergence!).

Aeolus.StretchedGrids._extend_faces — Method
_extend_faces(zf_interior, Nz, Hz)

Pad the interior face vector with Hz ghost levels on each side by linear extrapolation of the face sequence (zf[k] = 2zf[k+1] - zf[k+2] below, mirror image above). Ghost cells therefore inherit the thickness of the adjacent interior cell, which keeps every dzc/dzf strictly positive and makes the near-boundary stencils well defined.

Aeolus.StretchedGrids._faces_geometric — Method
_faces_geometric(T, Nz, z0, Lz, r)

Geometric (constant-ratio) stretching: cell k+1 is r times thicker than cell k. The first thickness follows from Σ dz = Lz:

\[dz_1 = L_z \frac{r - 1}{r^{N_z} - 1}, \qquad dz_k = dz_1 r^{k-1}.\]

r = 1 degenerates to the uniform distribution. r < 1 coarsens upward.

Aeolus.StretchedGrids._faces_tanh — Method
_faces_tanh(T, Nz, z0, Lz, β)

Hyperbolic-tangent clustering at the bottom wall — the standard channel-LES stretching with one resolved wall:

\[\xi_k = \frac{k-1}{N_z}, \qquad z_k = z_0 + L_z\left(1 - \frac{\tanh\!\big(\beta(1-\xi_k)\big)}{\tanh\beta}\right).\]

Note the reflection ξ → 1-ξ. The unreflected form L·tanh(βξ)/tanh(β) is the same one-parameter family but clusters at the top of the domain (its Jacobian dz/dξ ∝ sech²(βξ) is largest at ξ=0), which is the opposite of what a surface-layer LES wants. β → 0 recovers the uniform grid; β ≈ 1.5–3 is the useful range for LES (larger β pushes grid_stretching_ratio past the accuracy limit).

Aeolus.StretchedGrids._faces_tanh_both — Method
_faces_tanh_both(T, Nz, z0, Lz, β)

Symmetric tanh clustering at both walls (closed channel):

\[z_k = z_0 + \frac{L_z}{2}\left(1 + \frac{\tanh\!\big(\beta(2\xi_k-1)\big)}{\tanh\beta}\right).\]

Aeolus.StretchedGrids._faces_uniform — Method
_faces_uniform(T, Nz, z0, Lz)

Uniform face distribution. Computed from the exact affine formula z0 + (k-1)·dz rather than by accumulation so that a StretchedGrid with stretching=:uniform reproduces RegularGrid to the last bit.

Aeolus.StretchedGrids.grid_stretching_ratio — Method
grid_stretching_ratio(g) -> Real

Largest ratio between the thicknesses of two adjacent interior cells,

\[r_{\max} = \max_k \max\!\left(\frac{\Delta z_{k+1}}{\Delta z_k}, \frac{\Delta z_k}{\Delta z_{k+1}}\right) \ge 1 .\]

A grid-quality metric, not a diagnostic of the solver. The truncation error of the non-uniform second-order stencils contains a term proportional to (r-1), so accuracy degrades gracefully but noticeably once r ≳ 1.2; most operational LES setups keep r < 1.1. Returns exactly 1 for a uniform grid.

Aeolus.StretchedGrids.hydrostatic_balance_error — Function
hydrostatic_balance_error(θ, p, grid = mesh(p);
                          g_accel = 9.80665, R_d = 287.05, c_p = 1004.6,
                          p_ref = 100000.0, relative = false)

Maximum residual of the hydrostatic relation

\[\mathcal{R} = \frac{\partial p}{\partial z} + \rho g , \qquad \rho = \frac{p}{R_d T}, \quad T = \theta\,\Pi, \quad \Pi = \left(\frac{p}{p_{\text{ref}}}\right)^{R_d/c_p} ,\]

evaluated at every interior z-face of a StretchedGrid, TerrainFollowingGrid or RegularGrid, from cell-centred potential temperature θ and pressure p.

This is the validation quantity for a new vertical coordinate. An atmosphere at rest satisfies R = 0 exactly in the continuum, so whatever the discretisation leaves behind is pure numerical error — and on a stretched or terrain-following grid it is precisely the error produced by using the wrong metric (dzc instead of dzf), by naive ½ averaging of density onto faces, or by dropping a terrain cross-term. A first-order metric bug shows up here as a residual thousands of times larger than the second-order one.

Discretisation, chosen to mirror the model's own pressure-gradient term:

  • ∂p/∂z at face k is (p_k - p_{k-1})/Δz, with Δz the physical centre spacing (dzf[k], or J·Δσ_f[k] over terrain — the two centres share a column, so no cross-term arises);
  • ρ is interpolated to the face with the volume weights of _interp_to_face!.

Keyword relative = true divides each residual by the local |ρ g|, giving a dimensionless number that is directly comparable across resolutions and domains. Returns a scalar.

Aeolus.StretchedGrids.level_metrics — Method
level_metrics(dx, dy, zf, zc, dzc, dzf) -> FiniteVolume.LevelZ

The per-level vertical metrics of a stretched column (storage indices, halos included) in the form the finite-volume kernels read them: the reciprocal thicknesses, the interpolation weights, the three-point derivative weights and the Smagorinsky filter widths. Each entry is the expression the pre-port StretchedGrid kernels evaluated per level, so the kernels give the same bits; an entry whose stencil leaves the storage (the outermost halo levels) is zero.

Aeolus.StretchedGrids.metric_jacobian — Method
metric_jacobian(g::TerrainFollowingGrid) -> Array{T,3}

The vertical Jacobian J = ∂z/∂σ = (H - h)/H at cell centres, over the full storage array. Physical cell thickness at (i,j,k) is J[i,j,k] * cell_thickness(g)[k]; J ≡ 1 for flat terrain. J is the volume element of the transformation, so the conservative kernels weight every flux by it.

Aeolus.StretchedGrids.physical_heights — Function
physical_heights(g, loc = CellCenter())

True geometric height of each sample.

  • StretchedGrid: a vector over the full vertical storage (zc for centre-like locations, zf for FaceZ).
  • TerrainFollowingGrid: an Array{T,3} — height depends on (x, y) through the terrain.
Aeolus.StretchedGrids.set_physical! — Method
set_physical!(f::Field, fun) -> f

Fill every entry of f — interior and halos — with fun(x, y, z) evaluated at the true physical position of each sample.

Differs from Fields.set! in two ways that matter for stretched and terrain-following meshes:

  • halos are filled analytically instead of being left to a fill_halos! extrapolation, which is what makes exactness tests on polynomial profiles meaningful right up to the boundary;
  • on a TerrainFollowingGrid the vertical coordinate is genuinely three-dimensional (z = z(x,y,σ)), so a separable set!(f, (x,y,z) -> …) over znodes would sample the wrong heights.
Aeolus.StretchedGrids.surface_refined_faces — Method
surface_refined_faces(; dz_surface, z_uniform, z_top, ratio = 1.08,
                      dz_max = Inf, z0 = 0) -> Vector{Float64}

Interior face heights (length Nz + 1, from z0 to z_top) of the standard ABL vertical grid: uniform dz_surface from z0 up to z_uniform, then geometric growth by ratio per level, capped at dz_max, up to z_top. Pass the result as stretching = (:custom, zf) with size[3] = length(zf) - 1 and extent[3] = z_top - z0.

z_uniform - z0 must be a whole number of dz_surface cells. The stretched part takes the smallest number of cells that keeps every cell ≤ dz_max and is then scaled by one common factor f ≤ 1 so that the last face lands exactly on z_top (f ≥ 1 - dz_max/(z_top - z_uniform); the ratio between stretched cells stays ratio, the first stretched cell is f·ratio·dz_surface).

This is the layout of the Berlin nesting parent (#246): dz_surface = 25, uniform to z_uniform ≥ 500 m so that the ~400 m child sits in the uniform region, stretched to z_top = 3000 m. With ratio = 1.08, dz_max = 100 it has 53 levels (20 uniform + 33 stretched). Keep ratio ≲ 1.1 (see grid_stretching_ratio).

Aeolus.StretchedGrids.terrain_following_grid — Method
terrain_following_grid(mesh, h) -> TerrainFollowingGrid

Wrap mesh (a RegularGrid or StretchedGrid, which becomes the computational σ-coordinate) around the orography h and precompute every metric of the Gal-Chen transformation

\[z(x,y,\sigma) = h(x,y) + \sigma\,\frac{H - h(x,y)}{H}, \qquad H = L_z .\]

h may be

  • a function h(x, y) — sampled at cell centres including halos, which gives smooth ghost-cell metrics even for non-periodic terrain;
  • a Real — flat terrain at that elevation;
  • a Matrix of size (Nx, Ny) (halos filled by periodic wrap) or of the full storage size (Nx+2Hx, Ny+2Hy) (used verbatim).

Terrain slopes are evaluated with centred differences on the same mesh the stencils use, so the discrete metric identities the conservative kernels rely on hold exactly rather than approximately.

Throws if the terrain reaches the model lid (max h ≥ H), which would make the Jacobian vanish.
