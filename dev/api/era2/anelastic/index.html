Anelastic and pseudo-incompressible dynamics (Phase 27)

Aeolus.Anelastic — Module
Anelastic

Era II / Phase 27 — anelastic and pseudo-incompressible dynamics as selectable alternatives to the Boussinesq core in Simulations.

The three sound-proof systems shipped by Aeolus differ only in the mass-like constraint that the pressure projection enforces:

SystemConstraintProjection operator
Boussinesq∇·u = 0∇²φ
Anelastic (Lipps–Hemler 1982)∇·(ρ̄ u) = 0∇·(ρ̄ ∇φ)
Pseudo-incompressible (Durran 1989)∇·(ρ̄θ̄ u) = 0∇·(ρ̄θ̄ ∇φ)

Everything else — advection, buoyancy, SGS closure, boundary conditions — follows from that one choice, because the flux-form operators must be weighted by the same profile that appears in the constraint or the projection stops being exact.

Contents

  • ReferenceState and its four constructors (isothermal_reference, isentropic_reference, constant_stability_reference, reference_from_profile).
  • DensityWeightedPoissonSolver — matrix-free Jacobi-preconditioned CG for the variable-coefficient Poisson problem ∇·(w(z) ∇φ) = f.
  • AnelasticSimulation / PseudoIncompressibleSimulation with step_anelastic! / step_pseudo_incompressible!.
  • system_comparison_diagnostics — the instrument that shows the three systems diverging from one another.

Formulation note (which pressure variable?)

Lipps & Hemler write the momentum equation with -cₚθ̄∇π′; Ogura & Phillips and most LES codes instead project the mass flux ρ̄u onto its divergence-free part, which puts a plain scalar potential φ in the momentum equation,

u = u* − ∇φ,     ∇·(ρ̄ u) = 0   ⟹   ∇·(ρ̄ ∇φ) = ∇·(ρ̄ u*).

Aeolus implements the mass-flux projection, so AnelasticSimulation uses the ∇·(ρ̄ ∇·) operator. The -cₚθ̄∇π′ form would instead give the weight ρ̄θ̄ — numerically the same operator family, and exactly the weight used by PseudoIncompressibleSimulation. The two weightings therefore bracket the usual sound-proof formulations, and they coincide (up to an irrelevant constant factor) whenever the base state is neutral, θ̄ = const.

Aeolus.Anelastic.DensityWeightedPoissonSolver — Type
DensityWeightedPoissonSolver(mesh, w_c, w_f; tol=1e-10, maxit=500,
                             bc_x=:periodic, bc_y=:periodic, bc_z=:neumann)
DensityWeightedPoissonSolver(mesh, ref::ReferenceState; weighting=:ρ, kwargs...)
DensityWeightedPoissonSolver(mesh, c::Real; kwargs...)

Matrix-free, Jacobi-preconditioned conjugate-gradient solver for the variable-coefficient Poisson problem

\[\nabla\cdot\left(w(z)\,\nabla\varphi\right) = f\]

with w > 0 a function of height only. Drop-in replacement for the other AbstractPressureSolvers: the interface is the same solve!(p, source, solver). The constant-coefficient FFT/DCT solvers cannot do this — the vertical operator is no longer diagonalised by a cosine transform once w varies with k.

Discrete operator

w depends only on z, so the horizontal faces of cell k all carry the cell value w_c[k], while the two vertical faces carry w_f[k] (bottom) and w_f[k+1] (top):

(Lφ)ᵢⱼₖ = w_c[k] (δ²ₓ + δ²_y)φ
        + [ w_f[k+1](φᵏ⁺¹ − φᵏ) − w_f[k](φᵏ − φᵏ⁻¹) ] / Δz²

Averaging w to faces is what makes L self-adjoint. The coefficient coupling cells k and k+1 is w_f[k+1]/Δz² seen from either side, so L = Lᵀ exactly; had we instead used the cell value w_c[k] in front of a centred second difference, the coupling would be w_c[k] from below and w_c[k+1] from above and CG would be solving a non-symmetric problem. w_f is supplied by the caller — ReferenceState builds it with the logarithmic mean, which is symmetric (so self-adjointness is preserved), strictly positive, and makes the discrete hydrostatic balance exact for exponential density; a plain arithmetic mean is equally admissible for the solver and differs only at O(Δz²).

Boundary conditions

Each axis is :periodic or :neumann. In x and y the default is periodic; bc_x = :neumann / bc_y = :neumann sets the flux w ∂φ/∂n through the two lateral faces to zero (mirror ghost) — the closure of a nested or open-boundary child, whose normal velocity is prescribed on the side faces (#251; before that the solver was periodic in x, y by construction). In z, bc_z = :neumann (default) sets the vertical flux through the two wall faces to zero — the condition consistent with ImpermeableWall/NoFlux halos and with w = 0 at the walls — or bc_z = :periodic, in which case w_f[1] and w_f[Nz+1] describe the same face and are symmetrised for you. The Jacobi preconditioner uses the interior diagonal in every cell; on a Neumann side the boundary cells' true diagonal is smaller by w_c/Δ², which leaves the preconditioner symmetric positive definite (CG still converges, to the same solution).

L is singular with the constants as its null space, so the source is projected to zero mean and the returned solution is the unique zero-mean solution. The preconditioned direction is projected too, which keeps every iterate inside the zero-mean subspace (a deflated Jacobi preconditioner) — without that, M⁻¹ = diag(L)⁻¹ leaks a growing constant into φ because the diagonal varies with k.

Convergence is ‖r‖₂ < tol · ‖b‖₂; inspect with solver_iterations and solver_residual. Apply the operator directly (for manufactured solutions) with apply_weighted_laplacian!.

Aeolus.Anelastic.MassWeightedSimulation — Type
MassWeightedSimulation{S<:MassWeighting, …}

Sound-proof LES driver on a staggered Arakawa-C mesh, parameterised by the MassWeighting S. Two aliases name the concrete systems:

  • AnelasticSimulation = MassWeightedSimulation{AnelasticWeighting}
  • PseudoIncompressibleSimulation = MassWeightedSimulation{PseudoIncompressibleWeighting}

Prognostic state mirrors BoussinesqSimulation (u@FaceX, v@FaceY, w@FaceZ, p@CellCenter, θ@CellCenter) plus the prescribed ReferenceState in sim.ref. θ stores the full potential temperature, initialised to θ̄(z) by set_reference_state!; buoyancy works with θ − θ̄(z) internally.

sim.p holds the projection potential rate φ/Δt, i.e. the sound-proof pressure divided by the reference density — the same convention as the Boussinesq driver.

Aeolus.Anelastic.MassWeighting — Type
MassWeighting

Tag selecting which profile weights the continuity constraint and therefore all the flux-form operators and the pressure projection.

  • AnelasticWeighting — W = ρ̄(z), Lipps–Hemler anelastic.
  • PseudoIncompressibleWeighting — W = ρ̄(z)θ̄(z), Durran pseudo-incompressible.
Aeolus.Anelastic.PseudoIncompressibleSimulation — Type
PseudoIncompressibleSimulation(mesh; ref = constant_stability_reference(mesh), kwargs...)

Durran (1989) pseudo-incompressible LES. Identical structure to AnelasticSimulation but the constraint is on the flux of the pseudo-density P = ρ̄θ̄,

∇·(ρ̄θ̄ u) = 0    ⟹    ∇·(ρ̄θ̄ ∇φ) = ∇·(ρ̄θ̄ u*),

which retains the thermodynamic coupling that the anelastic filter throws away: the constraint responds to the base-state stratification, not only to the mass distribution. Advance with step_pseudo_incompressible!.

Because P and ρ̄ differ by a constant when θ̄ is constant, this system is exactly the anelastic one on a neutral base state; use a stratified base state (e.g. constant_stability_reference) to see them separate, which is why that is the default ref here.

Note: the adiabatic limit is implemented (Q = 0). A diabatic pseudo-incompressible run would put ∇·(ρ̄θ̄u) = (R/cᵥ)(P/π̄)⁻¹·… on the right-hand side of the projection; the hook for that is post_predictor!, which runs after the predictor and before the solve.

Aeolus.Anelastic.PseudoIncompressibleWeighting — Type

W = ρ̄(z)θ̄(z): the pseudo-incompressible constraint ∇·(ρ̄θ̄u) = 0. See MassWeighting.

Aeolus.Anelastic.ReferenceState — Type
ReferenceState{T, M}

Prescribed, horizontally homogeneous base state (ρ̄, θ̄, p̄, T̄)(z) for the sound-proof systems, in discrete hydrostatic balance on the mesh it was built for.

Stored profiles

Cell-centred, length Nz (interior levels only):

FieldMeaning

Each profile also has a halo-padded twin (ρ̄c_pad, ρ̄f_pad, θ̄c_pad, θ̄f_pad, Pc_pad, Pf_pad) whose index is the storage-array k index, so kernels can write ρ̄c_pad[k] next to u.data[i,j,k] with no offset arithmetic. Halo entries are filled by log-linear extrapolation, which keeps them strictly positive.

Discrete hydrostatic balance

The balance that is exact (to round-off) is the one the staggered C-grid actually evaluates in the w-momentum equation — a cell-to-cell pressure difference against the face density:

(p̄[k] − p̄[k−1]) / Δz  =  −g ρ̄_f[k],        k = 2 … Nz

and ρ̄_f[k] is the logarithmic mean of ρ̄[k−1] and ρ̄[k],

ρ̄_f = (ρ̄[k−1] − ρ̄[k]) / log(ρ̄[k−1]/ρ̄[k]).

The logarithmic mean is symmetric (hence the projection operator stays self-adjoint, see DensityWeightedPoissonSolver), lies between the geometric and arithmetic means (hence strictly positive), and — the reason it is chosen here — it makes the discrete balance above identically exact for an exponential density profile. That is what lets isothermal_reference be the exact analytic ρ₀e^{−z/H} and still satisfy the discrete balance to ~1e-13. Check it with max_hydrostatic_residual.

θ̄_f uses the arithmetic mean instead, because the buoyancy kernel interpolates the prognostic θ to the w-face arithmetically; matching the two guarantees that a base-state-only atmosphere produces exactly zero buoyancy. At the two wall faces both face profiles mirror the adjacent cell value, matching the NoFlux/ImpermeableWall halo fill.

See isothermal_reference, isentropic_reference, constant_stability_reference, reference_from_profile.

Aeolus.Anelastic._brunt_vaisala — Method
_brunt_vaisala(θ̄, dz, g) -> N²

N² = g ∂(log θ̄)/∂z on cell centres: a centred difference of log θ̄ in the interior and one-sided differences at the two end levels. Using the logarithmic derivative (rather than g/θ̄ · Δθ̄/Δz) makes the result exact to round-off for the analytic constant-N² profile θ̄ = θ_s e^{N²z/g}, which is what constant_stability_reference prescribes. The difference is the logarithmic form of TurbulenceStability.buoyancy_frequency_squared.

Aeolus.Anelastic._density — Method
_density(p, θ, R, cp, p₀) -> ρ

Density from pressure and potential temperature via the ideal-gas law and the Exner relation: ρ = (p₀/(Rθ)) (p/p₀)^{cᵥ/cₚ}.

Aeolus.Anelastic._integrate_hydrostatic — Method
_integrate_hydrostatic(mesh, θ̄, p_surf; R_d, cp, p₀, g) -> (p̄, ρ̄)

Integrate the discrete hydrostatic equation upward from a surface pressure p_surf prescribed at z = z₀ (the bottom wall face), given a prescribed θ̄(z) at cell centres.

Level 1 sits half a cell above the wall, so it is closed with the half-cell balance p̄[1] = p_surf − g(Δz/2)ρ̄[1]; this only sets the absolute pressure level. Every subsequent level solves the nonlinear two-point balance

p̄[k] = p̄[k−1] − g Δz · logmean(ρ̄[k−1], ρ(p̄[k], θ̄[k]))

by fixed-point iteration (contraction factor ≈ gΔz·cᵥρ̄/(2cₚp̄) ~ 10⁻³, so a handful of sweeps reaches round-off). The result satisfies the discrete balance to machine precision by construction — no O(Δz²) slop.

Aeolus.Anelastic._logmean — Method
_logmean(a, b)

Logarithmic (Lorenz) mean (a-b)/log(a/b), symmetric in its arguments and continuous at a == b where it equals a. Evaluated by a series in x = (a-b)/(a+b) when the arguments are close, so the 0/0 is never formed and full precision is retained.

Aeolus.Anelastic.add_anelastic_buoyancy! — Method
add_anelastic_buoyancy!(tw, θ, ref, mesh=ref.mesh)

Add the sound-proof buoyancy tendency to the w-momentum tendency using the height-dependent reference potential temperature,

b(z) = g · (θ − θ̄(z)) / θ̄(z),

evaluated at FaceZ with θ interpolated arithmetically in z and θ̄ taken from ref.θ̄_f (which uses the matching arithmetic mean, so a base-state-only atmosphere yields exactly zero buoyancy — including at the wall faces, where θ̄_f mirrors the adjacent cell just as the halo fill does).

Contrast with the Boussinesq add_buoyancy!(tw, θ, θ₀, g), which divides by a single constant θ₀; over a deep domain the difference in 1/θ̄(z) is a leading-order error.

Aeolus.Anelastic.anelastic_divergence! — Function
anelastic_divergence!(div, u, v, w, refstate, mesh=refstate.mesh) -> div

(1/ρ̄) ∇·(ρ̄ u) in flux form — the anelastic continuity residual, in s⁻¹ so it is directly comparable with the Boussinesq ∇·u. Note that a correct anelastic solution has this equal to zero while ∇·u = −w ∂log ρ̄/∂z ≠ 0; that difference is the anelastic approximation.

Aeolus.Anelastic.apply_weighted_laplacian! — Method
apply_weighted_laplacian!(out::Field, φ::Field, solver) -> out

Evaluate the discrete operator ∇·(w ∇φ) of solver on the interior of φ, writing the result to the interior of out. Halos of φ are ignored — the operator applies its own periodic / Neumann closure, exactly as during solve! — so this is the right tool for a manufactured-solution test: build f = apply_weighted_laplacian!(f, φ, solver), solve for φ, and the recovery error is purely the CG tolerance.

Aeolus.Anelastic.bubble_top_height — Method
bubble_top_height(sim; frac = 0.5) -> Real

Height of the highest interior cell level whose maximum horizontal potential-temperature perturbation still exceeds frac times the global maximum perturbation, where the perturbation is measured against the horizontal mean at each level. Returns the bottom of the domain when the field is perturbation-free.

Defined for any simulation type carrying a θ field, so the three dynamical cores can be compared with one number. frac = 0.5 is the conventional half-amplitude contour of the warm-bubble benchmark.

Aeolus.Anelastic.constant_stability_reference — Method
constant_stability_reference(mesh; N=0.01, θ_surf=300.0, p_surf=1e5, …)

Constant-stability base state, the standard stratified test atmosphere:

θ̄(z) = θ_surf exp(N² ζ / g),      ζ = z − z₀

which is the exact solution of N² = g ∂ log θ̄/∂z for constant N². The N² diagnostic stored in the returned ReferenceState reproduces N² = N^2 to round-off because it is evaluated as a difference of log θ̄ (see _brunt_vaisala). N is the frequency in s⁻¹; pass N = 0.01 for the usual mid-tropospheric value N² = 10⁻⁴ s⁻².

Aeolus.Anelastic.constraint_residual — Method
constraint_residual(sim) -> Real

The continuity residual appropriate to sim's own dynamical core: ∇·u for Boussinesq, (1/ρ̄)∇·(ρ̄u) for anelastic, (1/ρ̄θ̄)∇·(ρ̄θ̄u) for pseudo-incompressible. Lets system_comparison_diagnostics hold all three cores to their own standard.

Aeolus.Anelastic.constraint_weights — Method
constraint_weights(sim::MassWeightedSimulation) -> (Wc, Wf)

The weight W of the constraint ∇·(W u) = 0 a sound-proof simulation enforces (ρ̄ anelastic, ρ̄θ̄ pseudo-incompressible), as the halo-padded cell and bottom-face vectors its kernels use (Wc[k], Wf[k] at storage index k, see weighted_divergence!). A tracer transported in the flux form ∂(W q)/∂t = −∇·(W u q) with these weights conserves Σ W q and keeps a uniform q uniform to the projection's tolerance.

Aeolus.Anelastic.hydrostatic_residual — Method
hydrostatic_residual(ref) -> Vector

Residual of the discrete hydrostatic equation on the interior faces k = 2 … Nz:

r[k−1] = (p̄[k] − p̄[k−1])/Δz + g ρ̄_f[k]        [Pa m⁻¹]

This is exactly the stencil the w-momentum equation uses (a cell-to-cell pressure difference against the face density), so a zero residual means the base state exerts no spurious vertical acceleration on the model grid. Every constructor in this module drives it to round-off; see max_hydrostatic_residual.

Aeolus.Anelastic.isentropic_reference — Method
isentropic_reference(mesh; θ_const=300.0, p_surf=1e5, …)

Neutral (constant-θ) base state — N² ≡ 0 exactly. The classic dry warm-bubble background. Pressure and density come from the discrete hydrostatic integration, so the balance is exact on the model grid.

With θ̄ = const the anelastic weight ρ̄ and the pseudo-incompressible weight ρ̄θ̄ differ only by a constant factor, so AnelasticSimulation and PseudoIncompressibleSimulation produce identical trajectories on this base state — a useful exactness check, and the reason the two systems must be compared on a stratified base state to see them separate.

Aeolus.Anelastic.isothermal_reference — Method
isothermal_reference(mesh; T_surf=288.0, p_surf=1e5,
                     R_d=287.0, cp=1004.0, p₀=1e5, g_accel=9.81)

Isothermal base state — the one case with a closed-form answer, so it is evaluated analytically rather than integrated:

p̄(z) = p_surf e^{−ζ/H},   ρ̄(z) = p̄/(R T),   H = R T / g,   ζ = z − z₀

with θ̄ = T (p₀/p̄)^{R/cₚ} following from the definition of potential temperature. Because the face density uses the logarithmic mean (see ReferenceState), this exact exponential also satisfies the discrete hydrostatic balance to round-off — the two requirements are not in tension. reference_scale_height(ref) is not stored; use R_d*T_surf/g.

Aeolus.Anelastic.max_anelastic_divergence — Method
max_anelastic_divergence(sim) -> Real

max |(1/ρ̄)∇·(ρ̄u)| over the interior — the anelastic continuity residual in s⁻¹. After step_anelastic! this sits at the Poisson-solver tolerance. Compare with max_divergence(sim), which measures the plain ∇·u and is deliberately not zero: ∇·u = −w ∂log ρ̄/∂z.

Defined for both weightings (it always measures the ρ̄ constraint), so a pseudo-incompressible run can be asked how far it is from the anelastic constraint and vice versa.

Aeolus.Anelastic.step_anelastic! — Method
step_anelastic!(sim::AnelasticSimulation, dt; body_force! = nothing, post_predictor! = nothing)

Advance an anelastic simulation by dt with the density-weighted fractional step:

  1. fill halos; zero tendencies;
  2. ρ̄-weighted flux-form momentum and θ advection;
  3. molecular / SGS diffusion, anelastic buoyancy g(θ−θ̄)/θ̄, Coriolis, surface flux, sponge, then body_force!(sim, Δt);
  4. predictor u* = uⁿ + Δt F, then post_predictor!(sim, Δt);
  5. refill halos (which re-imposes w = 0 on the walls) and form D = ∇·(ρ̄u*);
  6. solve ∇·(ρ̄∇φ) = D with the DensityWeightedPoissonSolver;
  7. correct u = u* − ∇φ, advance θ, store p = φ/Δt, advance the clock.

Steps 5–7 are discretely consistent: the divergence in 5 and the operator in 6 are built from the same face weights and the same differences, so the post-step ∇·(ρ̄u) is bounded by the CG tolerance, not by truncation error. The callbacks have the same contract as for step!, and so does the SGS closure: the keyword sgs (any AbstractSGSClosure, or a function f(νt, u, v, w); the legacy alias eddy_viscosity! is accepted) replaces the built-in Smagorinsky closure (C_s), its coefficients go into sim.νt / sim.κt, its tendencies are added in step 3 where the built-in ones are, and its memory is committed once after the projection; sgs_stress = :laplacian | :symmetric selects the form of its momentum divergence as for step!. The SGS stress is applied in the unweighted form, like the built-in closure (the O(∂ln ρ̄/∂z) weighting correction is omitted for the same reason as for the molecular term).

Aeolus.Anelastic.system_comparison_diagnostics — Method
system_comparison_diagnostics(sims::NamedTuple) -> NamedTuple

Reduce a family of simulations — typically the same initial condition integrated by Boussinesq, anelastic and pseudo-incompressible cores — to one comparable diagnostic record each, and quantify how far they have drifted apart.

sims = (boussinesq = b, anelastic = a, pseudo = p)
d = system_comparison_diagnostics(sims)
d.anelastic.max_w            # 4.71
d.anelastic.rel_ke           # KE relative to the first entry

Each entry is a NamedTuple with

FieldMeaning
tsimulation time
kinetic_energyeach core's native KE — density-weighted for the sound-proof cores
kinetic_energy_unweighted½∫(u²+v²+w²)dV, the cross-system-comparable KE
max_wmaximum vertical velocity, m s⁻¹
θ_variancevolume-mean horizontal θ variance, K²
bubble_topbubble_top_height, m
constraint_residualeach core's own continuity residual
plain_divergence`max
rel_ke, rel_max_w, rel_θ_variancesigned relative difference from the first entry

rel_ke is built from kinetic_energy_unweighted, not from the native KE, precisely so that it measures a difference in the flow rather than a difference in the definition.

The rel_* fields are the validation instrument: they should be ~0 for the reference entry, O(Lz/H) for a sound-proof core in a shallow domain, and grow as the domain deepens.

Aeolus.Anelastic.unweighted_kinetic_energy — Method
unweighted_kinetic_energy(sim) -> Real

½∫(u²+v²+w²)dV over the interior, with no density weighting — the same definition for every dynamical core, hence the only kinetic-energy number that may be compared across systems. (kinetic_energy of a sound-proof core carries a ρ̄ factor and so differs from the Boussinesq value even when the velocity fields are identical.)

Aeolus.Anelastic.weighted_divergence! — Method
weighted_divergence!(out, u, v, w, Wc, Wf, mesh; normalize=false)

Flux-form divergence of the weighted velocity, at cell centres:

out = ∇·(W u) = Wc[k] (δₓu + δ_y v) + ( Wf[k+1]w[k+1] − Wf[k]w[k] ) / Δz

With normalize = true the result is divided by Wc[k], giving (1/W)∇·(W u). Wc/Wf are the halo-padded weight vectors of a ReferenceState.

This is the exact discrete adjoint-partner of the projection operator in DensityWeightedPoissonSolver: substituting u ← u − ∂ₓφ etc. reproduces ∇·(W∇φ) term for term, which is why the projection drives the constraint to the solver tolerance rather than to truncation error.

Aeolus.Anelastic.weighted_momentum_advect! — Method
weighted_momentum_advect!(tu, tv, tw, u, v, w, Wc, Wf, mesh)

Add the weighted flux-form momentum advection −(1/W) ∂(W uⱼuᵢ)/∂xⱼ to the three face tendencies.

Under the discrete constraint ∇·(Wu) = 0 this equals the advective form −u·∇uᵢ; the unweighted Boussinesq flux form does not, because it is off by uᵢ ∇·u, and ∇·u ≠ 0 in a sound-proof system with a varying base state. Getting this term right is what keeps a deep anelastic run from spuriously accelerating as the density drops.

Control volumes: u/v live at cell-centre height, so their own weight is Wc[k] and their vertical flux faces carry Wf[k], Wf[k+1]. w lives at a face, so its own weight is Wf[k] and its vertical flux faces sit at cell centres k−1, k carrying Wc[k−1], Wc[k].

Aeolus.Anelastic.weighted_scalar_advect! — Method
weighted_scalar_advect!(tϕ, u, v, w, ϕ, Wc, Wf, mesh)

Add −(1/W) ∂(W uⱼ ϕ)/∂xⱼ to a cell-centred scalar tendency, with 2nd-order centred face interpolation of ϕ. Discretely conservative for W ϕ, and equal to −u·∇ϕ under the discrete ∇·(Wu) = 0 constraint — i.e. the correct adiabatic θ equation for the anelastic (W = ρ̄) and pseudo-incompressible (W = ρ̄θ̄) systems.

Aeolus.Anelastic.θ_variance — Method
θ_variance(sim) -> Real

Volume mean of the horizontal variance of θ, ⟨⟨(θ−⟨θ⟩)²⟩⟩ (K²). A single scalar measuring how much thermal structure survives — the cleanest way to see two dynamical cores predicting different mixing.

Aeolus.PressureSolvers.solve! — Method
solve!(p, source, solver::DensityWeightedPoissonSolver)

Solve ∇·(w ∇p) = source on the interior. source is projected to zero mean (the compatibility condition of the singular operator) and p is returned with zero mean; halos of p are untouched.

Aeolus.Simulations.kinetic_energy — Method
kinetic_energy(sim::MassWeightedSimulation) -> Real

Density-weighted kinetic energy ½∫ρ̄(u²+v²+w²)dV over the interior. u, v use the cell density ρ̄[k], w the face density ρ̄_f[k] — each component weighted by the density at its own location. This is the energy that the anelastic system conserves in the absence of forcing; the unweighted Boussinesq kinetic_energy is not comparable in absolute terms across systems, which is why system_comparison_diagnostics also reports max_w and the θ variance.

Aeolus.Simulations.max_divergence — Method
max_divergence(sim::MassWeightedSimulation) -> Real

max |∇·u| — the Boussinesq constraint evaluated on a sound-proof solution. Non-zero by design; its size relative to max_anelastic_divergence is a direct measure of how far the run is from the Boussinesq limit.

Aeolus.TimeIntegration.adaptive_dt — Method
adaptive_dt(sim::MassWeightedSimulation, constraints; dt_prev = nothing,
            sgs = nothing, kwargs...)

The next time step of a sound-proof simulation: the smallest of the advective limit C_advective / max_cell Σ|uᵢ|/Δxᵢ (the per-cell rate of the Boussinesq cfl_diagnostics), the diffusive limit C_diffusive Δ²_harm / (ν + max(νt, κt)) with the SGS coefficients recomputed (pure compute_sgs!) from the current state, the buoyancy limit C_buoyancy / N from the horizontal-mean θ profile, and the Coriolis limit C_coriolis / |f|. Then, as for the Boussinesq core, limited to growth_max · dt_prev and clamped to [dt_min, dt_max]; a non-finite state collapses to dt_min. The other keywords of the Boussinesq method (scheme, sgs_stress, diagnostics) are accepted and ignored, so a driver can pass the same keywords to either core.
