Cut-cell IBM and ghost-cell walls (Phases 21–22)

Buildings intersect grid cells at arbitrary angles. A cut-cell treatment carries the fluid volume and wetted area of each partial cell, so the discrete divergence remains conservative on a body-fitted boundary; ghost-cell reconstruction then imposes the wall condition to second order rather than by first-order masking.

Aeolus.CutCell.CutCellGeometry — Type
CutCellGeometry{T,M}

Discrete description of a solid body immersed in a RegularGrid. All arrays have the shape of a Field on the mesh (interior + halos) so that geometry and field indices coincide.

FieldMeaning
vol_fracwetted volume fraction of each cell, in [0,1]
vol_effvol_frac with the small-cell floor applied (see vol_min)
ax, ay, azopen-area fraction of the west / south / bottom face, [0,1]
nx, ny, nzunit surface normal (into the fluid) — zero in full/empty cells
areawetted surface area inside the cell, m² (zero if not cut)
philevel-set value sampled at the cell centers
is_cut0 < vol_frac < 1
is_solidvol_frac == 0
is_small0 < vol_frac < vol_min (needs merging/stabilization)
merge_targetlinear index of the merge partner of a small cell (0 if none)
vol_minsmall-cell threshold
nsubsub-samples per direction used to build the fractions

area and the discrete normal are mutually consistent with the apertures: area * n = ((axₑ-ax_w)ΔyΔz, (ay_n-ay_s)ΔxΔz, (az_t-az_b)ΔxΔy), i.e. the discrete Gauss theorem holds cell by cell, which is why the cut-cell divergence of a uniform flow is exactly zero. The stored n comes from ∇φ (smoother, and defined even for degenerate aperture patterns); area is the norm of the aperture-balance vector.

Build one with cut_cell_geometry or cut_cell_geometry_from_heightmap.

Aeolus.CutCell.GhostCellStencil — Type
GhostCellStencil{L,T}

Precomputed wall reconstruction data for one sample point of a field at Location L. Built by build_ghost_stencils.

FieldMeaning
i, j, kstorage index of the sample point being reconstructed
dnsigned wall-normal distance of the sample point (< 0 ⇒ in solid)
probedistance from the wall foot point to the image point (> 0)
nunit wall normal, pointing into the fluid
xwfoot point on the true surface
ximgimage point out in the fluid
donorslinear indices of the (≤ 8) interpolation donors, 0 = unused
weightsinterpolation weights, normalized to sum to 1
exact_linearall eight tri-linear donors are fluid ⇒ interpolation exact for linear fields
ghostthe sample point lies in the solid, so its value must be reconstructed
validat least one usable donor was found

The reconstruction line is (xw + dn·n) → xw → (xw + probe·n); the one-dimensional profile along n is what carries the boundary condition, so the condition is imposed on the surface, not on a Cartesian face.

Aeolus.CutCell._normal_from — Method
_normal_from(φ, mesh, i, j, k) -> (nx, ny, nz, |∇φ|)

Unit normal of the level-set field φ at index (i,j,k), obtained from central differences (one-sided at array edges) and normalized. The normal points into the fluid (direction of increasing φ). Returns the gradient magnitude as well, which converts a level-set value into a wall-normal distance: d ≈ φ / |∇φ|.

Degenerate gradients (flat φ) fall back to (0,0,1) with magnitude 1.

Aeolus.CutCell.apply_cutcell_velocity_bc! — Function
apply_cutcell_velocity_bc!(u, v, w, geom, mesh = geom.mesh)
apply_cutcell_velocity_bc!(sim, geom)

Enforce no-penetration on the immersed surface exactly.

  • Every face with aperture == 0 gets exactly zero normal velocity. That covers all faces of a solid cell and every face the surface closes completely, so no flux can cross a dry face.
  • Partially open faces keep their value: by convention a face value is the velocity of the open part of the face, so the flux it carries is a · A · u and is already consistent with what cutcell_divergence! sums. As a safety net the aperture of any face whose neighbouring cell is dry is re-zeroed here as well (it should already be zero after the geometry's consistency sweep), which guarantees the flux into a solid cell is identically zero even if the geometry was edited by hand.

Nothing needs to be done on the surface: because the wall is not a face of the control volume, the cut-cell divergence contains no wall-flux term, so the no-penetration condition u·n = 0 is satisfied by construction. The tangential (no-slip / wall-model) condition is the job of the Phase-22 ghost-cell reconstruction — see reconstruct_wall_velocity!.

Typical use with the fractional-step driver:

step!(sim, dt; post_predictor! = (s, Δt) -> apply_cutcell_velocity_bc!(s, geom))
Aeolus.CutCell.build_ghost_stencils — Method
build_ghost_stencils(geom, mesh = geom.mesh, loc = CellCenter();
                     probe_factor = 1.0, search_radius = 2)

Precompute a GhostCellStencil for every sample point of a field at loc that the immersed surface touches, namely

  • points inside the solid (or behind a closed face) that have at least one fluid neighbour — the classic ghost points: they appear in fluid stencils, so they must carry the wall condition; and
  • points hosted by a cut cell, even when they lie on the fluid side — these are not overwritten by the reconstruction routines but give wall_shear_stress a probe on every piece of wetted surface.

Points buried deep in the solid get no stencil: no fluid stencil reads them, and extrapolating that far from the wall would be meaningless.

For each point the routine computes

  • the unit normal from ∇φ at the sample point, pointing into the fluid;
  • the signed wall distance dn = φ/|∇φ|, and the foot point xw = x - dn·n on the true surface;
  • the image point ximg = xw + probe·n with probe = max(|dn|, probe_factor·hₙ), hₙ = |(nₓΔx, n_yΔy, n_zΔz)|. With probe = |dn| this is the textbook mirror point, ximg = x + 2|dn|·n, and the reconstruction reduces to f_ghost = 2·f_wall - f_image. The floor at one grid spacing keeps the image far enough from the surface that its interpolation donors are all fluid, which is what preserves the second-order (linear-exact) behaviour for cut cells whose center is only just inside the solid;
  • tri-linear interpolation weights at ximg on the loc sample grid. Donors that are not fluid are dropped and the remaining weights renormalized (this loses linear exactness, flagged by exact_linear = false); if that leaves nothing, the routine falls back to inverse-distance weighting over the nearest fluid samples inside a (2·search_radius+1)³ box.
Aeolus.CutCell.cut_cell_count — Method
cut_cell_count(geom) -> (cut, solid, small)

Number of interior cells that are cut, fully solid, and below the small-cell threshold.

Aeolus.CutCell.cut_cell_geometry — Method
cut_cell_geometry(mesh, levelset::Function; nsub=4, vol_min=1e-3)

Build a CutCellGeometry on mesh from a level set levelset(x,y,z) with the convention φ < 0 = solid, φ > 0 = fluid. A signed distance function is ideal but any smooth level set works (distances are normalized by |∇φ|).

Volume and area fractions are computed by sub-cell sampling: nsub^3 equally spaced sub-samples per cell and nsub^2 per face, counting the fraction that lies in the fluid. The fractions are therefore quantized in steps of nsub^{-3} (nsub^{-2} for apertures) and converge like O(1/nsub) for a smooth surface. nsub = 4 (the default, 64 samples per cell) is a good accuracy/cost compromise for LES; increase it for sharply curved bodies. An exact marching-cubes polyhedron intersection is a future refinement — nothing downstream depends on how the fractions were obtained.

Robustness passes applied after sampling (in this order, iterated to a fixed point):

  1. Aperture/volume consistency — a face is closed (aperture = 0) whenever either cell it separates is dry. Independent sampling of volumes and areas can otherwise produce a face that leaks mass into a cell with no volume.
  2. Isolated-pocket removal — a wetted cell with no open face cannot exchange mass with anything and would leave the pressure matrix singular, so it is reclassified as solid.
  3. Small-cell bookkeeping — cells with 0 < vol_frac < vol_min are flagged in is_small and given a merge partner (merge_target, the most open neighbour); vol_eff floors their volume at vol_min. See cutcell_divergence! for how they are treated.

The level set is also sampled at cell centers into geom.phi, including halo cells (evaluated at the extrapolated coordinates) — that array is what the Phase-22 ghost-cell machinery uses.

Aeolus.CutCell.cut_cell_geometry — Method
cut_cell_geometry(mesh, sm::SolidMask; vol_min=1e-3)

Staircase geometry from an existing SolidMask (Phase-13 IBM interop): vol_frac is 0/1 and apertures are 0/1, so the cut-cell operators reduce exactly to the Cartesian ones with blocked faces. Useful for A/B-testing the cut-cell machinery against the old penalization IBM on the same obstacle.

Aeolus.CutCell.cut_cell_geometry_from_heightmap — Method
cut_cell_geometry_from_heightmap(mesh, height::AbstractMatrix; nsub=4, vol_min=1e-3)

Convenience constructor for terrain and flat-roofed buildings: builds the level set φ(x,y,z) = z - h(x,y) where h is taken piecewise-constant over each grid column (height[i,j] is the solid top of column (i,j)).

height may be given on the interior (Nx × Ny) or on the full storage array (Nx+2Hx × Ny+2Hy); interior-sized input is extended to the halos by edge replication.

Piecewise-constant columns are the right model for a flat roof: the vertical cut is resolved exactly (vol_frac measures how much of the cell is above the roof) while the horizontal outline stays on grid lines. For smooth orography give cut_cell_geometry a bilinearly interpolated level-set closure instead, so the cut-cell normals pick up the slope.

Aeolus.CutCell.cutcell_cell_inner — Method
cutcell_cell_inner(a, b, geom, mesh = geom.mesh)

Wetted-volume inner product of two cell-centered fields, Σ_interior V·vol_eff·a·b. This is the inner product in which the cut-cell operators are adjoint:

cutcell_cell_inner(p, D·u)  ==  -cutcell_face_inner(G·p, u)

(exactly, to round-off) whenever the velocity vanishes on the domain-boundary faces or the geometry closes them. That identity is what makes the fractional-step projection discretely divergence-free on a cut geometry, and it is verified numerically in the Era II test set.

Aeolus.CutCell.cutcell_divergence! — Function
cutcell_divergence!(div, u, v, w, geom, mesh = geom.mesh; redistribute = true)

Conservative cut-cell divergence

(∇·u)_ijk = 1/(V vf) [ (aₑuₑ - a_w u_w)ΔyΔz + (a_n v_n - a_s v_s)ΔxΔz
                                             + (a_t w_t - a_b w_b)ΔxΔy ]

i.e. the aperture-weighted face-flux balance divided by the wetted volume V vf. The wall itself contributes no flux term — no-penetration is built into the operator rather than imposed on it — so a flow tangential to the surface is exactly divergence-free.

Solid cells (vol_frac == 0) are set to zero.

Small cells. Dividing by a vanishing vol_frac would destroy the time step and the conditioning of D·G, so two mechanisms act together:

  • vol_eff = max(vol_frac, vol_min) floors the volume (this is what the operator actually divides by). It keeps (D, G) an exact adjoint pair with respect to the vol_eff-weighted inner product, hence keeps the projection exact — see cutcell_cell_inner.
  • redistribute = true (default) additionally merges each flagged small cell with its most open neighbour (geom.merge_target): the two cells' fluxes are summed and the cluster's mean divergence is written back to both. The move conserves Σ V·vol_eff·(∇·u) exactly, so global mass conservation is untouched, and it removes the O(1/vf) amplification from the small cell. Note that flux redistribution is only adjoint-consistent on the subspace where the pressure is constant across a merged cluster (true cluster merging would make the cluster a single unknown — a future refinement that needs the pressure solver to carry the cluster map). Pass redistribute = false for the strict adjoint operator; with sampled geometry small cells are rare, since vol_frac is quantized in steps of nsub^{-3}.
Aeolus.CutCell.cutcell_face_inner — Method
cutcell_face_inner(au, av, aw, bu, bv, bw, geom, mesh = geom.mesh)

Face inner product of two staggered vector fields, Σ_faces V·a·b, summed over the interior faces (those separating two interior cells) of all three directions. Paired with cutcell_cell_inner it expresses the discrete adjointness of the cut-cell divergence and gradient.

Aeolus.CutCell.cutcell_gradient_x! — Function
cutcell_gradient_x!(out, p, geom, mesh = geom.mesh)
cutcell_gradient_y!(out, p, geom, mesh = geom.mesh)
cutcell_gradient_z!(out, p, geom, mesh = geom.mesh)

Aperture-scaled pressure gradient at the faces,

(G p)_face = a_face · (p⁺ - p⁻) / Δ ,

which is exactly zero on a fully blocked face (a = 0) — the face then carries neither flux nor pressure force. Together with cutcell_divergence! this forms a negative adjoint pair; see cutcell_cell_inner.

Following the convention of the Cartesian kernels in FiniteVolume.jl, face index i holds the west face of cell i, and only interior indices are written (the opposite domain-boundary face lives in the halo and is supplied by the boundary conditions / geometry).

Aeolus.CutCell.cutcell_laplacian! — Function
cutcell_laplacian!(out, f, geom, mesh = geom.mesh; aperture_power = 2)

The cut-cell Poisson operator — the operator the pressure solve must invert:

(L f)_ijk = 1/(V vf) Σ_faces a_face^q A_face (f_nbr - f_ijk) / Δ .

Blocked faces drop out of the sum, which is exactly a homogeneous Neumann (zero normal derivative) condition on the immersed surface — the correct boundary condition for the projection pressure. Solid cells give zero. V·vol_eff·L is symmetric for either exponent, so L is self-adjoint and negative semi-definite in the vol_eff-weighted inner product (see cutcell_cell_inner) and stays solvable by CG / multigrid.

The exponent q = aperture_power selects which operator you get:

  • q = 2 (default) is exactly the composition D·G of cutcell_divergence! with cutcell_gradient_x! &co. One aperture comes from the flux weighting in D, the other from the aperture scaling of G. This is the operator to use in the fractional-step projection, because the projection is discretely exact only when the Poisson operator is the composition of the same D and G that are applied to the velocity: solving D G φ = D u* and correcting u = u* - G φ then gives D u = 0 to round-off. In operator terms this is the aperture-weighted projection u = u* - A ∇φ, an oblique projection in the A-weighted metric.
  • q = 1 is the classical flux-form cut-cell Laplacian ∇·(a ∇f), i.e. the second-order approximation of ∇²f on the wetted volume. Use it for diffusion of a scalar (heat, species) across a cut geometry, where the physical flux through a partially open face is a·A·κ·∂f/∂n and must carry a single aperture.

Aeolus.CutCell.reconstruct_wall_scalar! — Method
reconstruct_wall_scalar!(field, stencils, geom; wall_value = nothing,
                         wall_flux = nothing, κ = 1)

Ghost-cell reconstruction for θ and tracers. Exactly one of the two boundary conditions must be given:

  • wall_value (number or (x,y,z) -> value) — Dirichlet, identical to reconstruct_wall_velocity!.

  • wall_flux (number or function) — Neumann. The imposed condition is κ ∂φ/∂n |_wall = wall_flux with n pointing into the fluid, so a positive wall_flux means the scalar increases away from the surface (e.g. a surface heat flux warming the air above). The ghost value follows the same normal line,

    φ_ghost = φ_image + (dn - probe) · wall_flux / κ ,

    which for the mirror image is φ_ghost = φ_image - 2|dn|·flux/κ; written with the ghost-to-wall distance it is the familiar φ_ghost = φ_image + flux·Δn/κ.

κ is the diffusivity that makes wall_flux a flux; pass κ = 1 (the default) to prescribe the normal derivative directly.

Aeolus.CutCell.reconstruct_wall_velocity! — Method
reconstruct_wall_velocity!(field, stencils, geom; wall_value = 0.0)

Second-order ghost-cell reconstruction of a Dirichlet condition on the true surface. For every ghost sample point (one inside the solid) the value is set from the linear profile along the wall normal through the wall value and the image value,

f_ghost = f_wall + (dn / probe) · (f_image - f_wall) ,

which for the mirror image (probe = |dn|, dn < 0) is exactly the familiar f_ghost = 2·f_wall - f_image. The scheme is exact whenever the field varies linearly along the normal, so the imposed wall value is second-order accurate.

wall_value may be a number or a function (x,y,z) -> value evaluated at the foot point on the surface — the latter is how a moving wall or a prescribed surface velocity distribution is imposed. For no-slip pass 0.0 (the default).

Apply it to each velocity component with stencils built at that component's location:

su = build_ghost_stencils(geom, mesh, FaceX())
reconstruct_wall_velocity!(sim.u, su, geom)

Sample points that lie on the fluid side of the surface are left untouched, and so are points for which no usable donor exists (isolated pockets) — those keep whatever value they had.

Aeolus.CutCell.wall_shear_stress — Method
wall_shear_stress(u, v, w, stencils, geom, nothing; ν) -> Vector

The ν-linear stress ν |u_t(ximg)| / probe of wall_shear_stress(u, v, w, stencils, geom, ν): the law = nothing case of the wall-model method wall_shear_stress(…, law; ν) of Aeolus.WallModels.

Aeolus.CutCell.wall_shear_stress — Method
wall_shear_stress(u, v, w, stencils, geom, ν) -> Vector

Wall shear stress τ_w = ν |∂u_t/∂n| (m²/s²) at every stencil point, evaluated on the true normal rather than on a Cartesian face.

For each stencil the cell-centered velocity is interpolated to the image point, its wall-normal component removed, and the remaining tangential speed divided by the probe distance:

τ_w = ν · |u_t(ximg)| / probe .

The returned vector is indexed like stencils, so it pairs with geom.area[s.i,s.j,s.k] (the wetted area of that cut cell) to integrate a drag force, and with stencils[m].n to give the stress its direction.

stencils must be built at CellCenter(). The cell-centered velocity is obtained by aperture-weighted averaging of the two bounding faces, which is the consistent reconstruction on a cut cell (a blocked face contributes neither value nor weight).

This is the resolved (ν-linear) stress. The modelled stress at the same probe point is the method wall_shear_stress(u, v, w, stencils, geom, law; ν) of Aeolus.WallModels (Phase 57, #179), for any WallModels.AbstractWallLaw (Spalding, rough log law, MOST); with law = nothing it is this ν-linear stress. WallModels.wall_slip_velocity turns the modelled stress into the slip wall_value of reconstruct_wall_velocity!.
