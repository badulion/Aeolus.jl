Solar geometry (Phase 38)

Aeolus.SolarGeometry.AbstractSolarPosition — Type
AbstractSolarPosition

Solar-position algorithm selector. Concrete types:

  • MeeusPosition — the standard astronomical series, ~0.01°.

A SPAPosition carrying the full NREL/VSOP87 periodic tables (±0.0003°) is the documented refinement; see the module header for why it is not included here.

Aeolus.SolarGeometry.BirdClearSky — Type
BirdClearSky(; aod380, aod500, precipitable_water, ozone, albedo, ba)

Bird & Hulstrom (1981) clear-sky broadband model. Returns direct-normal, diffuse-horizontal and global-horizontal irradiance from the solar position plus a small number of atmospheric parameters. It is the standard compact clear-sky reference: accurate to a few percent against rigorous spectral calculations, with no spectral integration required.

Parameters:

  • aod380, aod500 — aerosol optical depth at 380 and 500 nm
  • precipitable_water — cm
  • ozone — atm-cm (0.3 is typical)
  • albedo — ground albedo (feeds the multiple-reflection term)
  • ba — forward-scattering fraction, 0.85 in the original paper
Aeolus.SolarGeometry.ClearSkyModel — Type
ClearSkyModel

Clear-sky irradiance model selector. Concrete: BirdClearSky.

Aeolus.SolarGeometry.MeeusPosition — Type
MeeusPosition()

Solar position from the standard astronomical series (Meeus, Astronomical Algorithms 2nd ed.). Achieved accuracy ~0.01° in elevation and azimuth for 1950-2050.

Aeolus.SolarGeometry.SolarPosition — Type
SolarPosition{T}

Computed solar geometry:

  • elevation_true — geometric elevation above the horizon, degrees
  • elevation — apparent elevation, i.e. refraction-corrected
  • zenith — apparent zenith angle, degrees (90 - elevation)
  • azimuth — degrees clockwise from north (N=0, E=90, S=180, W=270)
  • μ₀ — cos(zenith) of the apparent position, clamped at 0
  • declination, right_ascension, hour_angle — degrees
  • eot_minutes — equation of time in minutes
  • distance_au — Earth-Sun distance in AU
  • air_mass — relative optical air mass (Kasten-Young), Inf below the horizon

The azimuth convention (clockwise from north) matches the meteorological standard and sun_direction_enu below.

Aeolus.SolarGeometry.SolarTime — Type
SolarTime

An instant specified in UTC plus the observer's geographic position, which is everything the position algorithm needs.

  • year, month, day — calendar date (Gregorian)
  • hour, minute, second — UTC
  • latitude, longitude — degrees, north and east positive
  • elevation — metres above sea level
  • pressure — station pressure in hPa (for refraction)
  • temperature — station air temperature in °C (for refraction)
  • delta_t — TT − UT1 in seconds (≈ 69 s in the 2020s); affects the position by < 0.001° so the default is fine for radiation

Longitude sign convention is east-positive, the ISO convention. Note the original NREL SPA paper uses east-positive too, but many meteorological datasets use west-positive — a silent sign flip here moves the sun by hours, so it is worth checking against a known sunrise.

Aeolus.SolarGeometry.air_mass — Method
air_mass(h_apparent; pressure = 1013.25) -> relative air mass

Kasten & Young (1989) relative optical air mass:

\[m = \frac{1}{\cos z + 0.50572 (96.07995 - z)^{-1.6364}}\]

with z the apparent zenith angle in degrees, scaled by P/1013.25 to give the absolute (pressure-corrected) air mass. Equals 1 at the zenith and ≈ 38 at the horizon — the plane-parallel 1/μ₀ would diverge there, which is why this formula exists.

Aeolus.SolarGeometry.clear_sky_irradiance — Method
clear_sky_irradiance(p::SolarPosition, m::BirdClearSky; S₀ = SOLAR_CONSTANT)
    -> (; dni, dhi, ghi)

Bird clear-sky irradiance: direct-normal (dni), diffuse-horizontal (dhi) and global-horizontal (ghi), all W/m². Returns all zeros when the sun is below the horizon.

Aeolus.SolarGeometry.clearness_index — Method
clearness_index(ghi, p::SolarPosition; S₀ = SOLAR_CONSTANT) -> kt

Clearness index kt = GHI / (E₀ μ₀) — the fraction of the extraterrestrial horizontal irradiance that reaches the ground. Near 0.75 for a clear sky, ~0.2 for heavy overcast. The standard predictor for the diffuse fraction. Returns 0 when the sun is below the horizon.

Aeolus.SolarGeometry.cloud_optical_depth_column — Method
cloud_optical_depth_column(ql, qi, ρ, dz; k_liq = liquid_mass_extinction(10),
                           k_ice = ice_mass_extinction(30))

Per-cell cloud optical depth from the condensate field, τ = k · WC · dz with the mass extinction k in m²/g and the water content WC in g/m³. The defaults are the RadiationCore coefficients for effective radii of 10 μm (liquid, 0.15 m²/g) and 30 μm (ice, 0.033 m²/g), the same ones TwoStream.MieCloudOptics uses, so a column scheme and the 3-D shadow see the same cloud. (Before #130 the defaults were 0.06 and 0.02 m²/g, which the docstring called "~10 μm" but correspond to 25 μm liquid.) This lightweight form exists so shadow geometry can be computed without instantiating the whole optics pipeline.

ql, qi are mass mixing ratios (kg/kg), ρ is air density (kg/m³), a scalar, a per-level vector or a 3-D array, dz the layer thickness (m). Returns a 3-D array of optical depths: one pointwise launch! on backend (KA-n, #305).

Aeolus.SolarGeometry.cloud_shadow_map — Method

Allocating form of cloud_shadow_transmittance!.

Aeolus.SolarGeometry.cloud_shadow_transmittance! — Method
cloud_shadow_transmittance!(trans, τ_cloud, mesh, sun_dir; k_start = nothing)

Fill trans[i,j] ∈ (0,1] with the direct-beam transmittance reaching each surface cell, by tracing the ray from the centre of the cell's bottom face (z = 0) toward the sun through the 3-D cloud optical-depth field τ_cloud and accumulating the slant optical depth:

\[T_{i,j} = \exp\!\left(-\sum_\text{cells c on the ray} \frac{τ_c}{Δz}\,ℓ_c\right)\]

τ_c is the vertical optical depth of cell c (its extinction coefficient is τ_c/Δz) and ℓ_c the exact length of the ray inside it, found by a grid traversal (Amanatides & Woo 1987). For a horizontally uniform layer of vertical optical depth τ this is exactly exp(-τ/μ₀), with μ₀ the up component of sun_dir: the slant amplification comes from the path lengths, and is not applied again.

sun_dir = (east, north, up) is the unit vector toward the sun (from sun_direction_enu). Lateral boundaries wrap periodically, matching the LES. If the sun is at or below the horizon, trans is filled with 0.

k_start[i, j] (optional) starts the ray of column (i, j) at height k_start·Δz instead of the ground: on a roof, pass the number of solid cells under it, so the roof sees only the cloud above it. Nothing blocks the ray (for opaque buildings see combined_shadow_transmittance!).

This is genuinely 3-D: a cloud displaced horizontally from a surface cell shadows it when the sun is low, which no column-by-column (independent column) calculation can represent.

The rays are the work items of one launch_weighted! over the columns on backend (KA-n, #305); each ray reads τ and writes its own trans[i, j], so the map is identical at every thread count.

Aeolus.SolarGeometry.combined_shadow_transmittance! — Method
combined_shadow_transmittance!(trans, τ_cloud, solid_mask, mesh, sun_dir)

Direct-beam transmittance including both cloud attenuation and opaque building blocking, in a single ray traversal. The ray starts at the top of the column's solid stack (the roof, or the ground at z = 0), at the centre of the cell, and runs toward the sun until it leaves the top of the domain. A cell is fully shadowed (trans = 0) if the ray enters a solid cell; otherwise the cloud slant optical depth attenuates it, with the exact path length in every cell as in cloud_shadow_transmittance!.

solid_mask is a Bool array with the halo layout of the mesh (i.e. the .mask field of an IBM.SolidMask); pass nothing for no buildings, and τ_cloud = nothing for no cloud. Doing the two effects in one march is not just an optimisation — it is also the only way to get the ordering right when a cloud shadow and a building shadow overlap.

Aeolus.SolarGeometry.diffuse_fraction_erbs — Method
diffuse_fraction_erbs(kt) -> DHI/GHI

Erbs, Klein & Duffie (1982) diffuse-fraction correlation — the most widely used decomposition of a measured global irradiance:

kt ≤ 0.22 :  1 − 0.09 kt
0.22 < kt ≤ 0.80 : 0.9511 − 0.1604kt + 4.388kt² − 16.638kt³ + 12.336kt⁴
kt > 0.80 : 0.165
Aeolus.SolarGeometry.diffuse_fraction_reindl — Method
diffuse_fraction_reindl(kt, μ₀) -> DHI/GHI

Reindl, Beckman & Duffie (1990) correlation, which adds a solar-elevation dependence to Erbs and performs better at low sun — the regime that matters for urban canyons.

Aeolus.SolarGeometry.earth_sun_distance_factor — Method
earth_sun_distance_factor(p::SolarPosition) -> (R₀/R)²

Inverse-square correction to the solar constant for the Earth-Sun distance. Varies ±3.4% over the year (perihelion early January).

Aeolus.SolarGeometry.equation_of_time — Method
equation_of_time(st) -> minutes

Difference between apparent and mean solar time. Ranges ±16 minutes over the year; this is the term the original sinusoidal approximation omitted entirely, and it is why the analemma exists.

Aeolus.SolarGeometry.partition_global_irradiance — Method
partition_global_irradiance(ghi, p; method = :reindl, S₀ = SOLAR_CONSTANT)
    -> (; dni, dhi, ghi, kt, diffuse_fraction)

Split a measured or modelled global horizontal irradiance into its direct and diffuse components. method is :erbs or :reindl.

This is the routine a real driven simulation needs: observations and reanalysis give GHI, but a radiative-transfer solver needs the direct beam separately because only the direct beam casts shadows.

Aeolus.SolarGeometry.refraction_bennett — Method
refraction_bennett(h_true; pressure = 1013.25, temperature = 15.0) -> degrees

Atmospheric refraction correction to add to the true elevation, from Bennett (1982):

\[R = \frac{1.02}{\tan\left(h + \frac{10.3}{h + 5.11}\right)} \text{ arcmin}\]

with h in degrees, scaled by (P/1010)(283/(273+T)) for non-standard conditions. At the horizon this is ~34 arcmin — slightly more than the solar diameter, which is why the sun appears to rise before it geometrically does.

Returns 0 for elevations below −1° (the sun is well set and refraction is meaningless there).

Aeolus.SolarGeometry.shade_direct_beam! — Method
shade_direct_beam!(sw, removed, fdir, trans) -> sw

Take the cloud shadow off the surface shortwave: in every column the direct-beam share fdir of the absorbed shortwave sw is attenuated by the slant-path cloud transmittance trans (from cloud_shadow_transmittance!), $sw ← sw − sw f_{dir} (1 − T)$; removed receives what was taken off (W m⁻², ≥ 0 for 0 ≤ fdir, T ≤ 1). The diffuse part is left as it is. Moved from the urban case's cloud_shadow coupling (ONE-e, #454).

Aeolus.SolarGeometry.solar_declination_accurate — Method
solar_declination_accurate(st) -> degrees

Apparent solar declination. Compare against the original approximation 23.44 sin(2π(284+doy)/365), which is in error by up to ~0.5°.

Aeolus.SolarGeometry.solar_position — Method
solar_position(st::SolarTime, alg = MeeusPosition()) -> SolarPosition

Compute the apparent solar position. Includes the equation of time, nutation in longitude, apparent obliquity, aberration, the topocentric parallax correction, and Bennett atmospheric refraction scaled by the station pressure and temperature.

Aeolus.SolarGeometry.sun_direction_enu — Method
sun_direction_enu(p::SolarPosition) -> (east, north, up)

Unit vector pointing from the observer toward the sun, in a local east-north-up frame — the convention the shadow ray-marchers use. up > 0 when the sun is above the (apparent) horizon.
