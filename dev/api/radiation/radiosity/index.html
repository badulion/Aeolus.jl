Urban radiosity (Phase 35)

Aeolus.Radiosity.URBAN_CLASS_MIXTURES — Constant
URBAN_CLASS_MIXTURES

Material composition of each urban surface class, as class => (material => area fraction, ...). Fractions within a class sum to one. Materials are names from SurfaceOptics.material_names.

The default is a warm-summer European city centre. Pass a modified mapping to default_urban_materials to describe a different building stock — a North American downtown with flat bitumen roofs and curtain-wall glazing has a very different roof and wall composition, and that difference is worth several degrees of surface temperature.

Aeolus.Radiosity.URBAN_STREET — Constant

Surface-class indices. These deliberately coincide with the SURFACE_* constants of Aeolus.InputDrivers (the surface classes of the static driver), so a Facet's material index can be used directly as an index into that example's SurfaceParams arrays and a RadiosityScene can be driven from an existing surface_class_map.

Aeolus.Radiosity.Facet — Type
Facet{T}

One planar rectangular radiating patch.

  • centroid — centre of the rectangle, metres, in the scene frame (x east, y north, z up)
  • normal — outward unit normal, i.e. pointing into the air
  • u, v — orthonormal in-plane axes with u × v = normal
  • lu, lv — extents along u and v, metres
  • area — lu · lv
  • kind — :ground, :roof or :wall
  • material — index into a UrbanMaterials
  • temperature — surface temperature, K (the longwave source)
  • col — the horizontal grid column (i, j) this facet's energy is attributed to when aggregating back onto the LES grid

Walls are attributed to the open column they face, not to the building column they belong to. That matches tag_building_adjacent! in src/components/urban/site/geometry.jl, which tags the street cell next to a building as SURFACE_WALL precisely so a 2-D surface scheme can carry the wall's heat load.

The type is immutable; set_temperature! rebuilds a facet in place inside a scene, which is the only field that changes during a simulation.

Aeolus.Radiosity.LongwaveResponse — Type
LongwaveResponse{T}

The longwave radiosity system written as a linear map from the facet emission to the net longwave each facet absorbs. Returned by longwave_response; per unit facet area,

\[N_i \;=\; \varepsilon_i Q_i - \varepsilon_i\sigma T_i^4 \;=\; \sum_j J_{ij}\,E_j + j^{sky}_i\,L_{sky}, \qquad E_j = \varepsilon_j \sigma T_j^4 .\]

  • dN_dE — J, n × n: the change in facet i's net longwave gain per W m⁻² of extra emission by facet j
  • dN_dLsky — j^{sky}, length n: the same for the sky irradiance
  • emissivity — the facet emissivities it was built with; J depends on geometry and emissivity only, not on temperature, so it is valid until the emissivities change

N = -LongwaveSolution.net_loss for any temperatures and sky.

Aeolus.Radiosity.LongwaveSolution — Type
LongwaveSolution{T}

Result of solve_longwave, per facet, W m⁻² of facet area:

  • radiosity — B_i, emitted plus reflected longwave leaving the facet
  • incident — Q_i, longwave arriving from other facets and from the sky
  • emitted — ε_i σ T_i⁴
  • absorbed — ε_i Q_i
  • net_loss — ε_i(σT_i⁴ - Q_i) = B_i - Q_i, positive when the facet is cooling radiatively. This is the term the surface energy balance needs.
  • from_sky — F_sky,i · L_sky, the sky's share of incident
  • totals in W, and escaping_to_sky = Σ A_i B_i F_sky,i
Aeolus.Radiosity.RadiosityFactorCache — Type
RadiosityFactorCache{T}()

The factorisation of the radiosity operator M = I − diag(ρ)F, kept between calls of solve_radiosity_direct (and of solve_shortwave / solve_longwave with cache = c). A dense LU of M costs O(n³) — 1.67 s for the 5639 facets of the Berlin scene on one BLAS thread, 0.20 s on the 12 of Backends.dense_blas_threads() (#496) — while solving with a stored LU costs O(n²). The factorisation depends on the view factors F and on the reflectance ρ only, not on the source E, so it can be reused for as long as both are unchanged (KA-n, #305):

  • longwave ρ = 1 − ε is fixed for a scene, so its LU is computed once;
  • shortwave ρ = α: the urban class albedos depend on the solar zenith angle (default_urban_materials(; μ₀)), so the LU is refactorised whenever α changes, and reused while it does not.

The cache is keyed on the identity of F (===; F must not be mutated in place while it is cached) and on the values of ρ (==). On a hit the solve is the same LAPACK getrs on the same factors as a refactorisation. The factorisations and solves run on Backends.dense_blas_threads() BLAS threads (with_dense_blas, #496). nfactor counts the factorisations. Use one cache per band: a shortwave and a longwave solve sharing one cache would refactorise every time. The cache holds one n × n matrix (254 MB at n = 5639).

Aeolus.Radiosity.RadiosityScene — Type
RadiosityScene{T}

A facet list plus the heightmap it came from. The heightmap is retained because it is the occluder: every ray test in this module — facet-pair occlusion, direct-beam shadowing, hemispherical sky sampling — marches against it with a 2-D digital-differential-analyser traversal, which is exact for piecewise-constant columns and far cheaper than voxel marching.

  • facets — the radiating patches
  • heightmap — Nx × Ny, heightmap[i,j] is the solid top of column (i,j) in metres above z = 0; 0 means open ground
  • dx, dy — horizontal cell size, metres
  • x0, y0 — coordinate of the column-(1,1) lower corner
  • hmax — maximum(heightmap); a ray above this can no longer be blocked, which terminates the sky march
  • materials — the optical-property table the facets index into

Lateral boundaries are open: the world outside the heightmap is empty, so a ray leaving the domain reaches the sky. That is the right convention for an isolated block of city, and it is what makes the energy budget close exactly (everything that leaves is counted as leaving). For a periodic LES domain, pad the heightmap with a copy of the opposite edge before building the scene.

Aeolus.Radiosity.ShortwaveSolution — Type
ShortwaveSolution{T}

Result of solve_shortwave, all per facet and in W m⁻² of facet area unless noted:

  • radiosity — B_i, the diffusely reflected flux leaving facet i
  • incident — total shortwave arriving: direct + sky diffuse + all inter-reflections
  • absorbed — (1-α_i)·incident_i, the surface-energy-balance term
  • direct — the direct-beam component of incident
  • diffuse_sky — the sky-diffuse component of incident
  • interreflected— Σ_j F_ij B_j, the part that came off other facets; this is the term the original scheme omitted entirely
  • total_incident, total_absorbed, total_escaping — scene totals in W
  • effective_albedo — total_escaping / total_incident
Aeolus.Radiosity.UrbanMaterials — Type
UrbanMaterials{T}

Grey broadband optical properties per surface class:

  • albedo — shortwave hemispherical reflectance α ∈ [0, 1)
  • emissivity — longwave emissivity ε ∈ (0, 1]; the longwave reflectance is 1 - ε by Kirchhoff's law
  • name — human-readable class label

Reflection is assumed Lambertian in both bands, which is what makes the radiosity formulation valid: a Lambertian surface's outgoing radiance is direction-independent, so a single scalar B_i per facet is a complete description of its emission. Specular facades (glazing) violate this and need a directional method.

A subtype of RadiationCore.AbstractSurfaceOptics; material_albedo and material_emissivity are the accessors — methods of the generics declared in RadiationCore, which SurfaceOptics also extends with its spectral versions. Same name, dispatched on argument type: ask for the albedo of a Symbol and you get a spectral object, ask for the albedo of an UrbanMaterials class and you get the grey scalar the radiosity solve needs. The greys are derived from the spectral curves, so they agree by construction.

Aeolus.Radiosity.UrbanRadiationForcing — Type
UrbanRadiationForcing{T}

Facet-level radiation aggregated onto the (Nx, Ny) LES horizontal grid, in the form a flat-plate surface balance R_n = (1-α)·SW + ε·LW - εσT⁴ consumes (the Berlin case passes them as SurfaceExchange.SurfaceForcing sw_down / lw_down to its per-column surfaces).

All flux arrays are per unit plan area — facet fluxes are multiplied by facet area and divided by dx·dy. That is the correct normalisation for a column-integrated urban surface energy balance: a cell containing a tall wall legitimately absorbs more than 1000 W m⁻² of plan area, and hiding that by normalising per facet area would silently discard the wall's heat load, which is the very thing this phase exists to capture.

  • sw_absorbed — absorbed shortwave, W per m² of plan area
  • lw_net — net longwave loss, W per m² of plan area (positive = cooling)
  • area_density — Σ A_facet / (dx·dy) per column; 1 for flat ground, larger where walls stand. The "urban rugosity" factor.
  • svf — facet-area-weighted sky-view factor per column
  • sw_effective — the value to pass as the downwelling shortwave of the surface balance so that its (1-α)·sw_in reproduces sw_absorbed exactly, given the column's class albedo
  • lw_down_effective — the value to pass as lw_in[i,j] so that ε·lw_in - ε σ T⁴ reproduces -lw_net
  • class — the material class used for each column when forming the two effective fields

The two *_effective fields exist so this module can be dropped into the existing surface scheme without rewriting it: they invert that scheme's own algebra so the energy is right even though the scheme still thinks it is doing a flat-plate balance.

Aeolus.Radiosity.ViewFactors — Type
ViewFactors{T}
  • F — F[i,j], the fraction of radiation leaving facet i (diffusely) that arrives at facet j. F[i,i] = 0 for planar facets.
  • F_sky — F_sky[i] = 1 - Σ_j F[i,j], the sky-view factor. It is the closure residual, deliberately, so that it inherits the accuracy of F rather than being an independent approximation.
  • area — facet areas, cached because every energy identity needs them.

Two identities hold and are the correctness backbone of the whole phase:

\[A_i F_{ij} = A_j F_{ji} \qquad\text{(reciprocity)}\]

\[\sum_j F_{ij} + F_{sky,i} = 1 \qquad\text{(closure)}\]

Reciprocity holds to machine precision because the symmetric exchange area is what is actually computed; closure holds exactly by construction. Together they make the shortwave and longwave energy budgets close identically — see solve_shortwave.

Aeolus.Radiosity._edge_log_integral — Method
_edge_log_integral(P1, e1, L1, P2, e2, L2, ngauss) -> T

\[\int_0^{L_1}\!\!\int_0^{L_2} \ln\lVert (P_1 + t_1 e_1) - (P_2 + t_2 e_2)\rVert \, dt_2\, dt_1\]

the inner kernel of the double contour integral. Two cases:

  • Parallel edges — evaluated in closed form, for any perpendicular offset d including d = 0. Two parallel segments have r² = (s₁-s₂)² + d² with d constant, so

    \[\int_{a_1}^{b_1}\!\!\int_{a_2}^{b_2}\ln\sqrt{(s_1-s_2)^2+d^2}\;ds_2\,ds_1 = \tfrac12\big[G(b_1-a_2) - G(b_1-b_2) - G(a_1-a_2) + G(a_1-b_2)\big]\]

    with G from _parallel_edge_antiderivative. This case matters enormously: it is collinear (d = 0, logarithmically singular) for a street patch and the wall rising out of its edge, and nearly collinear for closely spaced facades — and Gauss-Legendre is poor on both. In grid-aligned heightmap geometry every edge pair that contributes at all is parallel (perpendicular pairs have dr₁·dr₂ = 0 and are discarded by the caller), so taking this branch makes the whole contour integral exact to round-off.

  • Everything else — Gauss-Legendre in both parameters, for the non-axis-aligned facets a future extension might introduce.

Aeolus.Radiosity._parallel_edge_antiderivative — Method
_parallel_edge_antiderivative(x, d) -> T

G(x) with G''(x) = ln(x² + d²), namely

\[G(x) = \tfrac12 (x^2 - d^2)\ln(x^2+d^2) - \tfrac32 x^2 + 2 d x \tan^{-1}(x/d),\]

which degenerates correctly to 2f(x) = x^2\ln|x| - \tfrac32 x^2 as d → 0.

Aeolus.Radiosity.aggregate_to_grid — Method
aggregate_to_grid(scene, vf; sw = nothing, lw = nothing,
                  T_surface = nothing) -> UrbanRadiationForcing

Project the facet-level solution back onto the LES horizontal grid.

Each facet's flux is weighted by its area and deposited in the column given by Facet.col — the open column a wall faces, the column itself for ground and roof. The result is divided by the cell plan area dx·dy.

The columns are filled by one launch! over (i, j) on backend (KA-n, #305): each column gathers its facets through facet_column_index, in ascending facet order, so every sum has the order of a serial facet loop and the result is the same at every thread count. The dominant class of a column is the material with the largest facet area; an exact tie goes to the lowest material index.

Either solution may be omitted (nothing), in which case the corresponding arrays are zero. T_surface is the temperature used when inverting the longwave algebra for lw_down_effective; it defaults to the area-weighted mean facet temperature in each column, which is the consistent choice when the caller's surface scheme carries one temperature per column.

Aeolus.Radiosity.build_facets — Method
build_facets(heightmap, dx, dy; kwargs...) -> Vector{Facet}

Decompose a building heightmap into explicit radiating facets.

heightmap[i,j] is the solid top of grid column (i,j) in metres, the same piecewise-constant-column convention used by CutCell.cut_cell_geometry_from_heightmap. A column with height ≤ 0 is open ground.

Produces

  • one ground facet per open column, at z = ground_level, normal +ẑ, area dx·dy;
  • one roof facet per built column, at z = heightmap[i,j], normal +ẑ;
  • for each built column and each of its four sides, a wall rectangle spanning the height step against that neighbour — from the neighbour's height up to this column's height — with the outward normal pointing at the neighbour, subdivided into wall_subdivisions equal vertical strips.

Using the height step rather than the full height is what makes the decomposition watertight and non-overlapping on stepped geometry: where a tall building abuts a short one, only the exposed part of the tall building's wall is a facet, and the short building's roof closes the gap. Outside the domain the height is taken as ground_level, so perimeter walls are fully exposed.

Keyword arguments

  • wall_subdivisions = 4 — vertical strips per wall rectangle. Walls carry strong vertical gradients in both sky-view factor and direct-beam shadowing, so 1 is rarely enough; 4 resolves a canyon of H/W ≈ 2 well.
  • x0, y0, ground_level = 0 — scene-frame origin.
  • ground_material, roof_material, wall_material — class indices, defaulting to URBAN_STREET, URBAN_ROOF, URBAN_WALL.
  • class_map — optional Nx × Ny integer array overriding the ground class per column (to mark parks and water), used only for open columns.
  • temperature = 288.15 — initial facet temperature, K.
  • min_height = 1e-9 — a column is "built" when its height exceeds this.

Aeolus.Radiosity.canyon_centreline_sky_view_factor — Method
canyon_centreline_sky_view_factor(H_over_W) -> ψ

Sky-view factor at the centreline of an infinitely long canyon floor,

\[\psi_c = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 + 4(H/W)^2}},\]

the a = b = W/2 case of the 2-D differential view factor above.

This is the maximum over the canyon floor, and therefore always larger than the floor average sqrt((H/W)^2+1) - H/W: the centreline is the point furthest from both walls, so it is the point that sees the most sky. The minimum sits hard against a wall, at 1/(2\sqrt{1+(H/W)^2}) — half the centreline value in the shallow-canyon limit, because one entire side of the hemisphere is masonry. That cross-street profile is exactly the structure a single canopy-average sky-view factor throws away, and having the centreline, the wall-side value and the average as three independent closed forms is a much sharper test than the average alone: a facet decomposition that is subtly mis-registered in x reproduces the mean but not the profile.

Aeolus.Radiosity.canyon_sky_view_factor — Method
canyon_sky_view_factor(H, W, a1, a2) -> ψ

Sky-view factor of an infinitely long canyon floor averaged over the strip a ∈ [a1, a2], measured from one wall across a canyon of width W between walls of height H. With the 2-D differential sky fraction

\[\psi(a) = \tfrac12\left(\frac{a}{\sqrt{a^2+H^2}} + \frac{W-a}{\sqrt{(W-a)^2+H^2}}\right)\]

the strip average integrates in closed form,

\[\bar\psi = \frac{1}{2(a_2-a_1)}\Big[\sqrt{a_2^2+H^2}-\sqrt{a_1^2+H^2} + \sqrt{(W-a_1)^2+H^2}-\sqrt{(W-a_2)^2+H^2}\Big].\]

Setting (a1, a2) = (0, W) recovers sqrt((H/W)²+1) - H/W. This per-strip form is the sharp test: it must be matched by every individual floor facet of a long modelled canyon, not merely by their mean, so it catches a facet decomposition that is right on average but mis-registered in x.

Aeolus.Radiosity.canyon_sky_view_factor — Method
canyon_sky_view_factor(H_over_W) -> ψ

Floor-averaged sky-view factor of an infinitely long street canyon of height-to-width ratio H/W:

\[\psi = \sqrt{(H/W)^2 + 1} - H/W\]

This is the standard urban-canopy result (Oke, Boundary Layer Climates 2nd ed. §8; Johnson & Watson 1984). It follows from the 2-D differential view factor: a point on the floor at distance a from one wall and b from the other sees sky fraction ½(a/\sqrt{a^2+H^2} + b/\sqrt{b^2+H^2}), and averaging that over a ∈ [0, W] gives the expression above.

Aeolus.Radiosity.canyon_wallside_sky_view_factor — Method
canyon_wallside_sky_view_factor(H_over_W) -> ψ

Sky-view factor of the canyon floor immediately against a wall,

\[\psi_w = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{1+(H/W)^2}},\]

the a = 0 case. The minimum over the floor; see canyon_centreline_sky_view_factor.

Aeolus.Radiosity.default_urban_materials — Method
default_urban_materials(T = Float64; μ₀ = 0.7, mixtures, spec, lw_spec)

Urban material properties for a warm-summer European city centre, derived from the Phase 36 spectral library by band-averaging a mosaic of real materials — see URBAN_CLASS_MIXTURES.

μ₀ is the cosine of the solar zenith angle. Every class brightens toward grazing incidence — the Phase 36 library carries a Briegleb direct-beam shape function, and rough surfaces really do get brighter at low sun — but water does so by a different mechanism and by an order of magnitude more:

street0.1190.1381.15
wall0.2120.2621.24
roof0.1980.2571.30
park0.1780.2731.53

The roof emissivity drop from 0.90 to 0.85 is the metal fraction asserting itself, as it should.

Aeolus.Radiosity.diffuse_sky_irradiance — Method
diffuse_sky_irradiance(vf, dhi) -> Vector

Diffuse irradiance from an isotropic sky on each facet, E^dif_i = DHI · F_sky,i, where DHI is the diffuse horizontal irradiance.

The identity is exact for an isotropic sky: an unobstructed horizontal surface under sky radiance L receives πL = DHI, and a facet with sky-view factor F_sky receives πL F_sky by the definition of the view factor. Note this is where the closure property earns its keep — an independently computed sky-view factor would break the energy budget at this step.

Aeolus.Radiosity.direct_absorbed_share! — Method
direct_absorbed_share!(fdir, scene, sol::ShortwaveSolution, facet_col) -> fdir

The share of each ground column's absorbed shortwave that arrived as direct beam, $Σ A_f (1 − α_f) E_{dir,f} / Σ A_f Q_{abs,f}$ over the facets f of the column (facet_col[f] = (i, j)), 0 where nothing was absorbed. What a cloud shadow on the beam (SolarGeometry.shade_direct_beam!) attenuates. Moved from the urban case's cloud_shadow coupling (ONE-e, #454).

Aeolus.Radiosity.direct_beam_irradiance — Method
direct_beam_irradiance(scene, sun; dni, samples = 2) -> Vector

Direct-beam irradiance on each facet, W m⁻² of facet area:

\[E^{dir}_i = I_{DNI}\,\max(0, \hat{n}_i\cdot\hat{s})\,\sigma_i\]

with I_DNI the direct normal irradiance (measured perpendicular to the beam, e.g. SolarGeometry.clear_sky_irradiance(...).dni) and σ_i the sunlit fraction from the ray-cast shadow test.

Summing A_i E^dir_i over an opaque closed scene recovers I_DNI · μ₀ · (plan area) exactly, because the facets tile the horizon as seen from the sun — a useful check that the shadow test is neither double-counting nor leaking.

Aeolus.Radiosity.effective_albedo — Method
effective_albedo(scene, vf, sun; dni = 900, dhi = 100, kwargs...) -> T

The effective albedo of the scene: the fraction of the shortwave energy entering it that eventually escapes back to the sky, after every bounce.

\[\alpha_{eff} = \frac{\sum_i A_i B_i F_{sky,i}}{\sum_i A_i Q^{ext}_i}\]

For a flat surface this is exactly the material albedo α. For any scene with relief it is strictly smaller, and it decreases monotonically as the canyon deepens: each reflection off a facade is aimed mostly at another facade, so the photon gets more chances to be absorbed before it finds the sky. The approximate canopy-scale scaling is α_eff ≈ α·ψ_sky/(1 - α(1-ψ_sky)), the geometric series of the bounces; the full solve here resolves the facet-level structure that the single-ψ approximation smears out.

This ratio is the quantitative signature of urban radiative trapping and the headline validation of this phase. Reported by ShortwaveSolution too.

Caveat on scene edges. The denominator counts what actually entered the scene, so the budget closes exactly whatever the geometry. But with open lateral boundaries a scene's perimeter walls face empty space: they see nothing but sky, reflect straight back to it, and so behave like a flat surface of albedo α, diluting the canyon signal. Their area grows with building height, which can make α_eff non-monotone in H/W for a small test domain under an oblique sun. Two ways to avoid the artefact: illuminate from the zenith with no diffuse component, which leaves vertical perimeter walls receiving exactly nothing; or pad the heightmap so the region of interest is interior. The zenith configuration is the controlled experiment used in the test suite.

The two-argument convenience form effective_albedo(scene, sun; ...) assembles the view factors itself — do not use it in a loop, since assembly is the expensive part.

Aeolus.Radiosity.exchange_area — Method
exchange_area(scene, i, j; kwargs...) -> T

The exchange area A_i F_ij = A_j F_ji between two facets of a scene, including occlusion by intervening buildings.

Algorithm:

  1. Back-face cull. If either facet lies entirely behind the other's plane, the exchange is exactly zero. This alone removes most pairs in an urban scene (all ground-ground, all roof-roof, all ground-roof pairs — every co-facing parallel pair) and it removes them exactly, not approximately.
  2. Occlusion ratio. A subdivided area quadrature is accumulated twice in one pass: once ignoring obstructions, once with a ray test between every sub-patch pair. The subdivision is adapted to the size-to-separation ratio, so touching facets get the finest sampling and distant ones a single sample.
  3. Exact unoccluded value. The fully-visible exchange area comes from the contour integral, which is exact; it is then multiplied by the occluded/unoccluded ratio from step 2. When nothing is blocked the ratio is 1 and the answer is exact; when everything is blocked it is 0. The approximation is confined to partially shadowed pairs, where it is the quadrature's estimate of the blocked fraction weighted by the transfer kernel — which is the right weighting, and much better than scaling by a bare unweighted visible-sample count.

Keyword arguments

  • ngauss = 8 — Gauss nodes per contour edge.
  • nsub_max = 3 — cap on the per-axis subdivision used for the occlusion ratio (so at most nsub_max^4 rays per pair).
  • subdiv_density = 1.5 — target sub-patch size relative to the facet separation; larger means finer sampling.

Aeolus.Radiosity.heightmap_from_solid_mask — Method

Aeolus.Radiosity.longwave_response — Method
longwave_response(scene, vf) -> LongwaveResponse

The Jacobian of the longwave radiosity solution with respect to the facet emission and the sky irradiance. Eliminating the radiosity B = E + (1-ε)Q from Q = F B + F_sky L_sky gives

\[Q = (I - F\,\mathrm{diag}(1-\varepsilon))^{-1}\,(F E + F_{sky} L_{sky}),\]

so the net absorbed longwave N = diag(ε) Q - E is exactly linear in the emission E; the only nonlinearity of longwave exchange is E = εσT⁴. Hence

\[J = \mathrm{diag}(\varepsilon)\,(I - F\,\mathrm{diag}(1-\varepsilon))^{-1} F - I, \qquad j^{sky} = \mathrm{diag}(\varepsilon)\,(I - F\,\mathrm{diag}(1-\varepsilon))^{-1} F_{sky},\]

one LU factorisation of the same operator solve_longwave solves.

This is what an implicit surface scheme needs: with $∂E_j/∂T_j = 4ε_jσT_j^3$, $∂N_i/∂T_j = J_{ij}\,4ε_jσT_j^3$ couples every facet's energy balance to every other facet's temperature within one time step. Two properties make it conservative (both are asserted in the test suite):

  • Reciprocity of total exchange areas (Hottel): for i ≠ j, A_i J_ij ε_j = A_j J_ji ε_i.
  • Closure: Σ_i A_i J_ij = -A_j × (fraction of j's emission that escapes to the sky); in an enclosure with no sky the area-weighted column sums vanish, so extra emission by one facet is absorbed, in full, by the others.
Aeolus.Radiosity.longwave_trapping_factor — Method
longwave_trapping_factor(scene, vf, lw; kinds = (:ground,)) -> T

The ratio

\[f = \frac{\text{net longwave loss from the selected facets}} {\text{loss the same facets would have with } F_{sky}=1}\]

i.e. actual cooling divided by the cooling of an exposed flat surface of the same material at the same temperature under the same sky. f = 1 for an open plain; f → 0 for a facet buried in an isothermal cavity.

This is the cleanest single number for canyon longwave trapping: it isolates geometry, because the material, the temperature and the sky are held fixed between numerator and denominator.

Aeolus.Radiosity.ray_blocked — Method
ray_blocked(scene, origin, dir, tmax; ztol = 0) -> Bool

Aeolus.Radiosity.sky_view_factor_raycast — Method
sky_view_factor_raycast(scene, i; nθ = 16, nφ = 32) -> T

The sky-view factor of facet i by cosine-weighted hemispherical ray casting: nθ × nφ stratified directions are drawn about the facet normal with density cos θ / π, each is marched against the heightmap, and the plain (unweighted) mean of the unobstructed fraction is returned.

Cosine weighting is not cosmetic. The sky-view factor is

\[F_{sky} = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_{2\pi} V(\hat{\omega})\cos\theta \, d\omega,\]

so sampling with density cos θ/π turns it into an unweighted average and removes the cos θ from the estimator — the same variance-reduction trick a Monte-Carlo renderer uses. Sampling uniformly in (θ, φ) and weighting afterwards, as the original compute_sky_view_factor does, converges far more slowly near the horizon, which is exactly where urban obstruction lives.

This is a genuinely independent estimate: it never touches the view-factor matrix. Comparing it against ViewFactors.F_sky (the closure residual) is the external validation of the whole pair-integration machinery.

Aeolus.Radiosity.solve_longwave — Method
solve_longwave(scene, vf, r::LongwaveResponse; L_sky) -> LongwaveSolution

The same LongwaveSolution as solve_longwave(scene, vf; L_sky), evaluated from a precomputed longwave_response instead of a new factorisation: the net absorbed longwave is exactly linear in the emission, N = J E + j^{sky} L_sky, so one matrix–vector product (O(n²)) replaces the O(n³) LU of the direct solve. The other fields follow from N by Kirchhoff (absorbed = εQ = N + E, B = E + (1-ε)Q), which requires every facet emissivity to be positive.

This is the longwave solve for a scene whose geometry and emissivities are fixed while the temperatures change every step (UrbanSurface's facet scene). r must have been built with the scene's current emissivities.

Aeolus.Radiosity.solve_longwave — Method
solve_longwave(scene, vf; L_sky, solver = :direct, tol = 1e-12) -> LongwaveSolution

Solve the longwave radiosity system

\[B_i = \varepsilon_i\sigma T_i^4 + (1-\varepsilon_i)\Big(\sum_j F_{ij}B_j + F_{sky,i} L_{sky}\Big),\]

where L_sky is the downwelling sky longwave irradiance on a horizontal unobstructed surface (W m⁻²) and σ is RadiationCore.STEFAN_BOLTZMANN. The facet temperatures come from scene.facets[i].temperature; set them with set_temperatures! before each call.

The primary source passed to the solver is E_i = ε_i σ T_i⁴ + (1-ε_i) F_sky,i L_sky and the reflectance is 1 - ε_i (Kirchhoff).

Canyon trapping. The physically important output is net_loss. A facet on an exposed roof loses ε(σT⁴ - L_sky), typically 80-120 W m⁻² on a clear night. The same material at the same temperature on a canyon floor exchanges most of its emission with walls that are almost as warm, so its net loss collapses — by a factor of 3 or more for H/W = 2. See longwave_trapping_factor for the diagnostic.

Energy closes as an identity, again from reciprocity and closure: absorbed = emitted + sky_in - sky_out.

Aeolus.Radiosity.solve_radiosity_direct — Method
solve_radiosity_direct(F, ρ, E; cache = nothing) -> B

Dense LU solve of the radiosity system

\[B_i = E_i + \rho_i \sum_j F_{ij} B_j \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad (I - \mathrm{diag}(\rho) F)\,B = E .\]

ρ is the facet reflectance (shortwave albedo α, or longwave 1 - ε) and E the primary source (see solve_shortwave and solve_longwave for what goes into it).

The system is always non-singular for ρ < 1: diag(ρ)F is non-negative with row sums ρ_i(1 - F_{sky,i}) ≤ max ρ < 1, so its spectral radius is below one and the Neumann series Σ_k (diag(ρ)F)^k E — physically, the sum over bounce orders — converges geometrically. That is the mathematical statement of "radiation eventually gets absorbed".

With cache (a RadiosityFactorCache) the LU factorisation of I − diag(ρ)F is kept and reused while F and ρ are unchanged: the solve then costs O(n²) instead of O(n³), with bits identical to a refactorisation at the same BLAS thread count.

Aeolus.Radiosity.solve_radiosity_iterative — Method
solve_radiosity_iterative(F, ρ, E; method = :gauss_seidel, tol = 1e-12,
                          maxiter = 10_000) -> (B, iters, residual)

Iterative solve of the same system, for scenes too large for a dense O(n³) factorisation.

  • :gauss_seidel — sweep B_i ← E_i + ρ_i Σ_j F_ij B_j in place, using already-updated values within the sweep. Because the iteration matrix is non-negative with spectral radius ≤ max ρ, Gauss-Seidel converges monotonically from B = E and every iterate is a strict under-estimate: after k sweeps the solution is bounded below by the first k bounce orders. Convergence is geometric at rate ≈ max ρ, so a facade albedo of 0.2 needs ~18 sweeps for 1e-12, while a snow-covered α = 0.8 scene needs ~125.

  • :progressive — progressive refinement (Cohen, Chen, Wallace & Greenberg 1988): repeatedly pick the facet holding the largest unshot energy A_i ΔB_i and distribute it to all others through the column of the exchange-area matrix. Each shot costs O(n) rather than O(n²), and because the largest contributor is always chosen first, the visually and energetically important transport happens in the first few shots. This is the method of choice when n is large and a few significant digits suffice.

Returns the radiosity, the iteration/shot count, and the final residual ‖B - E - diag(ρ)FB‖_∞.

Aeolus.Radiosity.solve_shortwave — Method
solve_shortwave(scene, vf, sun; dni, dhi, solver = :direct, samples = 2,
                tol = 1e-12) -> ShortwaveSolution

Solve the shortwave radiosity system for a scene.

The external (uncollided) irradiance on facet i is

\[Q^{ext}_i = I_{DNI}\max(0,\hat n_i\cdot\hat s)\,\sigma_i + \mathrm{DHI}\,F_{sky,i},\]

the primary source is E_i = α_i Q^ext_i — the first reflection — and the system solved is B = E + diag(α) F B. Total incident is then Q_i = Q^ext_i + Σ_j F_ij B_j and the absorbed flux is (1-α_i) Q_i.

Energy conservation is an identity here, not an approximation. With B_i = α_i Q_i,

\[\sum_i A_i Q^{ext}_i \;=\; \sum_i A_i (1-\alpha_i) Q_i \;+\; \sum_i A_i B_i F_{sky,i},\]

which follows from reciprocity (Σ_i A_i F_ij = A_j(1 - F_sky,j)) and closure alone. So the shortwave budget closing to round-off is a test of the view factors, and its failure would localise the bug immediately.

solver is :direct (dense LU), :gauss_seidel, or :progressive.

Aeolus.Radiosity.surface_irradiance! — Method
surface_irradiance!(sw, lw, forcing, T_surface) -> (sw, lw)

The irradiance per unit of actual surface of each column, from the per-plan-area fluxes of aggregate_to_grid (forcing): a column holding a tall building reports its walls' energy per unit of footprint, area_density times the incident flux, which is the right column energy but not what a one-temperature surface of the column absorbs per m². With $a = \max(\mathrm{area\_density}, 1)$:

\[SW = \frac{SW_{\mathrm{eff}}}{a}, \qquad LW = σ T_s^4 + \frac{LW_{\downarrow,\mathrm{eff}} - σ T_s^4}{a},\]

i.e. the net longwave of the column is spread over its surface area, the emission $σ T_s^4$ of the column's temperature T_surface kept. (Moved from the urban case's radiosity coupling, ONE-e #454; one temperature per facet, UrbanFacetScene, removes the approximation.)

Aeolus.Radiosity.total_sky_exchange_area — Method
total_sky_exchange_area(vf) -> T

Σ_i A_i F_sky_i — the total exchange area between the scene and the sky.

For a scene that is closed from above (every vertical line through the footprint ends on a facet, which is true of any heightmap scene with ground at z = 0), reciprocity with the sky "facet" forces this to equal the plan area of the footprint, Nx·Ny·dx·dy, independent of how much building surface the scene contains. That is a strong, cheap, global test of the whole view-factor matrix — it fails immediately if any pair is double-counted or dropped. Open lateral boundaries add a small positive bias, because facets near the domain edge can see "sky" through directions that a larger domain would have blocked.

Aeolus.Radiosity.view_factor_contour — Method
view_factor_contour(fi, fj; ngauss = 8) -> T

The exchange area A_i F_ij of two fully mutually visible planar rectangles, by the double contour integral (Modest, Radiative Heat Transfer 3rd ed. eq. 4.42; originally Mitalas & Stephenson 1966):

\[A_1 F_{12} = \frac{1}{2\pi}\oint_{C_1}\oint_{C_2} \ln r \; d\vec{r}_1\cdot d\vec{r}_2\]

Exact up to the Gauss truncation on the non-singular edge pairs. The result is symmetric in the two arguments by inspection, which is where machine- precision reciprocity comes from: the caller computes it once and divides by A_i and A_j respectively.

Note that it returns the exchange area (m²), not the view factor; divide by fi.area for F_ij.

Aeolus.Radiosity.view_factor_parallel_rectangles — Method
view_factor_parallel_rectangles(a, b, c) -> F

Closed-form view factor between two identical parallel rectangles, a × b, directly opposed (coaxial), separated by c. Howell, A Catalog of Radiation Heat Transfer Configuration Factors C-11; Modest eq. (4.41):

With X = a/c, Y = b/c,

\[F_{12} = \frac{2}{\pi X Y}\Bigg\{ \ln\sqrt{\frac{(1+X^2)(1+Y^2)}{1+X^2+Y^2}} + X\sqrt{1+Y^2}\,\tan^{-1}\!\frac{X}{\sqrt{1+Y^2}} + Y\sqrt{1+X^2}\,\tan^{-1}\!\frac{Y}{\sqrt{1+X^2}} - X\tan^{-1}X - Y\tan^{-1}Y \Bigg\}\]

Sanity value: two unit squares one unit apart give F = 0.199825.

Aeolus.Radiosity.view_factor_perpendicular_rectangles — Method
view_factor_perpendicular_rectangles(l, w, h) -> F

Closed-form view factor between two perpendicular rectangles sharing a common edge. Surface 1 lies in the z = 0 plane, spans the common edge of length l and extends a width w away from it; surface 2 stands on the same edge and rises to height h. F is F_{1→2}. Howell C-14; Modest eq. (4.40):

With W = w/l, H = h/l,

\[\begin{aligned} \pi W F_{12} =\;& W\tan^{-1}\frac{1}{W} + H\tan^{-1}\frac{1}{H} - \sqrt{H^2+W^2}\,\tan^{-1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{H^2+W^2}} \\ &+ \tfrac14\ln\Bigg[\frac{(1+W^2)(1+H^2)}{1+W^2+H^2} \left(\frac{W^2(1+W^2+H^2)}{(1+W^2)(W^2+H^2)}\right)^{W^2} \left(\frac{H^2(1+H^2+W^2)}{(1+H^2)(H^2+W^2)}\right)^{H^2}\Bigg] \end{aligned}\]

Sanity value: two unit squares sharing an edge (two faces of a cube) give F = 0.200044.

Aeolus.Radiosity.view_factors — Method
view_factors(scene; kwargs...) -> ViewFactors

Assemble the full facet-to-facet view-factor matrix and the sky-view factors.

Each unordered pair is visited once; the symmetric exchange area is computed by exchange_area and written into both F[i,j] = X/A_i and F[j,i] = X/A_j. Reciprocity is therefore exact to rounding, and the sky-view factor is the closure residual 1 - Σ_j F[i,j].

Cost is O(n²) pair tests, but the back-face cull disposes of the majority in a few dozen flops. A scene of ~300 facets assembles in well under a second.

The pair loop is one launch_weighted! on backend (KA-n, #305): work item p owns the rows p and n − p of the upper triangle, so every item tests n − 1 pairs and the threads share the triangle evenly. Every entry is computed once, from the same inputs, so F and F_sky are bit-identical at every thread count.

Keyword arguments

  • ngauss, nsub_max, subdiv_density — passed to exchange_area.
  • backend = default_backend() — where the pair loop runs.
  • clamp_sky = true — clamp F_sky into [0, 1]. Quadrature error on a facet that sees almost nothing but other facets (the bottom strip of a deep canyon wall) can push the residual a few times 1e-4 negative; a negative sky-view factor is unphysical and would inject energy. The un-clamped residual magnitude is a useful diagnostic — see closure_error and the raw_sky return of sky_view_factors_raycast for the independent check.
