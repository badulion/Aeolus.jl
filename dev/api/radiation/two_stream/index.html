Two-stream solver and cloud optics (Phase 33)

Aeolus.TwoStream.TWOSTREAM_CHUNK — Constant

Columns solved per launch by the multi-column two-stream solves (bounds the workspace).

Aeolus.TwoStream.MieCloudOptics — Type
MieCloudOptics <: AbstractCloudOptics

Parameterised cloud optical properties. Liquid follows Hu & Stamnes (1993) in form — τ ∝ LWP/r_eff, with ssa and g weak functions of r_eff — and ice follows Fu (1996) in form, using effective diameter. Coefficients are representative values chosen to reproduce the well-known benchmarks the test suite checks (a 100 g/m² LWP at 10 μm gives visible τ ≈ 15, ssa > 0.999, g ≈ 0.85).

Aeolus.TwoStream.TwoStreamSolver — Type
TwoStreamSolver{T} <: AbstractRTSolver

Two-stream solver. sw_variant defaults to DeltaEddington, lw_variant to Quadrature — the standard operational pairing.

Aeolus.TwoStream.TwoStreamVariant — Type
TwoStreamVariant

Selects the closure used for the two-stream γ coefficients.

  • DeltaEddington — Eddington closure with delta-scaling. The standard shortwave choice; most accurate for forward-peaked scattering (clouds).
  • PIFM — Practical Improved Flux Method (Zdunkowski 1980). Better behaved than Eddington at large zenith angle, which matters for urban low-sun geometry.
  • Quadrature — the classical quadrature (Gauss) closure, the standard longwave choice.
Aeolus.TwoStream.TwoStreamWorkspace — Type
TwoStreamWorkspace{T}()

Scratch of the multi-column two-stream solves (KA-n, #305): per g-point and column, the layer reflectances / transmittances / sources and the level fluxes of one (g, column) work item, for up to TWOSTREAM_CHUNK columns at a time. Pass the same workspace to every call (workspace = ws) and a solve allocates nothing; it grows when a call needs more layers, g-points or columns than it holds.

Aeolus.RadiationCore.compute_optics! — Method
compute_optics!(optics, cloud::MieCloudOptics, col)

Fill cloud optical properties from col.ql, col.qi and the effective radii. The extinction is geometric-optics-like in the solar (τ ≈ 3·LWP/(2ρ_w r_eff)), with absorption rising into the near-infrared and dominating in the thermal infrared — which is why clouds are bright in the visible and black in the longwave.

Aeolus.RadiationCore.compute_optics! — Method
compute_optics!(optics, cloud::MieCloudOptics, cols::AbstractVector{<:AtmosphericColumn};
                backend = default_backend())

Multi-column form (KA-n, #305): optics of shape (nlay, ngpt, ncol), one work item per (layer, column); column c gets the bits of the single-column compute_optics! on cols[c].

Aeolus.RadiationCore.solve_rt! — Method
solve_rt!(fluxes, solver::TwoStreamSolver, optics, col; kwargs...)

AbstractRTSolver interface. Dispatches to solve_sw! or solve_lw! on the spectral grid's kind.

Aeolus.TwoStream.adding_solver! — Method
adding_solver!(flux_up, flux_dn, flux_dir, R_dif, T_dif, R_dir, T_dir,
               T_beam, sfc_alb_dif, sfc_alb_dir, F_top, μ₀; F_dif_top = 0)

Combine per-layer reflectance/transmittance into level fluxes by the standard adding (doubling) equations, layers ordered bottom-up.

F_top·μ₀ is the direct beam on a horizontal plane at the top level and F_dif_top the diffuse flux entering from above (zero at the top of the atmosphere; the sky diffuse when the "column" is a plant canopy, see PlantCanopy.canopy_shortwave).

The recursion builds the albedo of the composite "surface + all layers below level i" upward, then propagates the downward flux back down. The 1/(1 - R_dif·A) factors are the infinite series of multiple reflections between a layer and everything beneath it summed in closed form.

Aeolus.TwoStream.cloud_optical_depth — Method
cloud_optical_depth(col, cloud; band = 10) -> τ

Column cloud optical depth in a band (band 10 of the default shortwave grid is in the visible).

Aeolus.TwoStream.delta_scale — Method
delta_scale(τ, ω, g) -> (τ′, ω′, g′)

Joseph-Wiscombe-Weinman delta-scaling. Strongly forward-peaked phase functions (cloud droplets, g ≈ 0.85) violate the two-stream assumption badly; delta-scaling removes the forward spike into the unscattered beam and rescales what remains:

\[f = g^2,\quad τ' = (1-ωf)τ,\quad ω' = \frac{(1-f)ω}{1-ωf},\quad g' = \frac{g}{1+g}\]

Without this, cloud albedo is overestimated by tens of percent.

Aeolus.TwoStream.ice_water_path — Method

Column ice water path, g m⁻².

Aeolus.TwoStream.layer_reflectance_transmittance_lw — Method
layer_reflectance_transmittance_lw(variant, τ, ω, g, B_bot, B_top)
    -> (R, T, S_up, S_dn)

Longwave layer properties with a linear-in-τ Planck source. B_bot and B_top are the source at the layer's lower and upper boundary; the source is taken to vary linearly in optical depth between them, which is the Toon et al. (1989) treatment.

The constant-source alternative evaluates the Planck function once per layer and is biased cold wherever the layer spans a significant temperature change — the test suite measures the difference against a sub-layered reference.

Aeolus.TwoStream.layer_reflectance_transmittance_sw — Method
layer_reflectance_transmittance_sw(variant, τ, ω, g, μ₀)
    -> (R_dif, T_dif, R_dir, T_dir, T_beam)

Shortwave layer properties: diffuse reflectance/transmittance, the direct-beam source reflected and transmitted into the diffuse streams, and the unscattered direct-beam transmittance T_beam = e^{-τ/μ₀}.

The three removable singularities are handled explicitly:

  • τ → 0 — everything reduces to pass-through (R = 0, T = 1).
  • ω → 1 (conservative) — k → 0, so sinh(kτ)/k → τ and the general formula's 0/0 is replaced by its limit.
  • k μ₀ → 1 — the direct-beam denominator 1 - k²μ₀² vanishes; the beam source terms are evaluated at μ₀(1 ± 10⁻⁴) (each with its own beam transmittance) and averaged, which is accurate to ~1e-8.
Aeolus.TwoStream.liquid_water_path — Method
liquid_water_path(col) -> g m⁻²

Aeolus.TwoStream.maximum_random_overlap — Method
maximum_random_overlap(cf) -> total cloud fraction

Maximum-random overlap of a layer cloud-fraction profile: vertically contiguous cloudy layers overlap maximally, separated groups randomly. This is the standard operational assumption and sits between the two extremes, both of which are wrong for real clouds.

Aeolus.TwoStream.solve_lw! — Method
solve_lw!(fluxes, solver, optics, col; sfc_emissivity,
          workspace = TwoStreamWorkspace{T}(), backend = default_backend())

Longwave solve. Source terms come from the Planck function evaluated at the layer boundary temperatures, so the linear-in-τ treatment has the gradient information it needs.

Multi-column form (KA-n, #305): optics of shape (nlay, ngpt, ncol), cols a vector of ncol columns, fluxes of shape (ncol, nlev); sfc_emissivity a scalar, one value per column or an (nband, ncol) matrix. The band Planck sources are one launch over (level, column), the (g-point, column) sweeps a second, the g-point sums a third, as in solve_sw!. Column c gets the bits of the single-column solve on cols[c], at every thread count.

Aeolus.TwoStream.solve_sw! — Method
solve_sw!(fluxes, solver, optics; μ₀, sfc_albedo_direct, sfc_albedo_diffuse,
          workspace = TwoStreamWorkspace{T}(), backend = default_backend())

Shortwave solve over all g-points, accumulating broadband fluxes. sfc_albedo_* may be a scalar or a per-band vector.

Multi-column form (KA-n, #305): optics of shape (nlay, ngpt, ncol) (τ[k, g, c]) and fluxes of shape (ncol, nlev) (allocate_fluxes(T, (ncol,), nlev)). μ₀ is a scalar or one value per column; sfc_albedo_* a scalar, one value per column, or an (nband, ncol) matrix. Every (g-point, column) is one work item of a launch_weighted! on backend: the adding recurrence runs sequentially over the layers inside the item, on the item's slice of workspace; a second launch sums each column's g-points in ascending order. Column c gets the bits of the single-column solve, and the single-column form is this solve on one column, so the fluxes are identical at every thread count.

Aeolus.TwoStream.two_stream_coefficients — Method
two_stream_coefficients(variant, ω, g, μ₀) -> (γ₁, γ₂, γ₃, γ₄)

The four two-stream coupling coefficients (Meador & Weaver 1980 Table 1). γ₁, γ₂ couple the two diffuse streams; γ₃, γ₄ couple the direct beam into them. For the longwave only γ₁, γ₂ are used.
