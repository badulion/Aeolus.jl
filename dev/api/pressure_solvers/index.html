PressureSolvers

Pluggable Poisson-solver interface used by the fractional-step driver. See Discrete Poisson & FFT for the mathematics and Pluggable pressure solvers for the design.

Aeolus.PressureSolvers.AbstractPressureSolver — Type
AbstractPressureSolver

Pluggable Poisson-solver interface. Every concrete solver implements a single method

solve!(p, source, solver)

where p and source are cell-centered Fields living on the mesh the solver was constructed with. The solver populates p's interior from source's interior; halos of p are left untouched.

Phase 1a ships FFTPoissonSolver for triply-periodic RegularGrid. Future planned backends:

  • CosineFFTPoissonSolver — FFT in horizontal, DCT-II in vertical for channel-like domains (Phase 1b).
  • CGPoissonSolver — matrix-free preconditioned conjugate gradient for arbitrary BCs and immersed boundaries (Phase 3).
  • SEMPoissonSolver — element-local mass + matrix-free CG for the spectral-element backend (Phase 5).
Aeolus.PressureSolvers.FFTPoissonSolver — Type
FFTPoissonSolver(mesh::RegularGrid)

Direct Poisson solver for triply-periodic boundary conditions on a RegularGrid, with source and solution both at cell centers.

The FFT is diagonalized using the exact eigenvalues of the discrete 2nd-order centered 7-point Laplacian:

λ_h(m_x, m_y, m_z) = - 4/dx² sin²(π m_x/Nx)
                     - 4/dy² sin²(π m_y/Ny)
                     - 4/dz² sin²(π m_z/Nz)

so the returned p satisfies the discrete equation L_h p = source to machine precision (up to projection of source onto its zero-mean part, which is the periodic-Poisson compatibility condition).

Aeolus.PressureSolvers.CosineFFTPoissonSolver — Type
CosineFFTPoissonSolver(mesh::RegularGrid;
                       bc_x = :periodic, bc_y = :periodic, bc_z = :neumann)

Direct Poisson solver on a RegularGrid whose three axes are each either periodic or homogeneous Neumann (both faces of the axis), with cell-centred source and solution. The default is the channel (periodic x, y, walls in z); bc_x = bc_y = bc_z = :neumann is the closed box of a nested or open-boundary child, whose normal velocity is prescribed on every face so that ∂φ/∂n = 0 everywhere.

Each axis is diagonalised separately (Schumann & Sweet 1988):

  • a periodic axis by the real FFT, eigenvalue $4/Δ^2 \sin^2(π m/N)$, $m = 0 … N-1$;
  • a Neumann axis by the DCT-II (FFTW REDFT10, inverse REDFT01 = DCT-III), eigenvalue $4/Δ^2 \sin^2(π m/2N)$. The DCT-II even-extends the data about the half-integer positions $i = 1/2$ and $i = N + 1/2$, which are the faces of the cell-centred grid, so the mirror ghosts φ₀ = φ₁, φ_{N+1} = φ_N (zero face gradient) are enforced implicitly. The 2N is the period of that even extension.

\[λ_{m,n,l} = -\frac{4}{Δx^2}\sin^2\frac{π m}{N_x^*} -\frac{4}{Δy^2}\sin^2\frac{π n}{N_y^*} -\frac{4}{Δz^2}\sin^2\frac{π l}{N_z^*}, \qquad N^* = N \text{ (periodic)},\quad 2N \text{ (Neumann)}.\]

The eigenvalues are those of the discrete 7-point Laplacian with that closure, so L_h p = source is satisfied to round-off. The cost is one O(N log N) transform pair; a DCT costs about the same as a real FFT of the same length.

Compatibility. With no Dirichlet axis the (0,0,0) mode has zero eigenvalue: p is fixed up to a constant (the solver returns the zero-mean solution) and only the zero-mean part of source can be matched. The discrete compatibility condition is $\sum_{ijk} s_{ijk} = 0$ (Gauss: the net boundary flux of ∇φ, zero for homogeneous Neumann and periodic faces). An incompatible source with mean $\bar s = Q/V$ — a net source Q in volume V, e.g. a non-zero net inflow through prescribed boundary velocities — is solved as L_h p = s − \bar s: the residual L_h p − s is exactly the uniform −\bar s. That is why the open-boundary mass-flux correction must make the net boundary volume flux vanish before the projection.

The default (:periodic, :periodic, :neumann) builds exactly the plans, eigenvalues and normalisation of the original channel solver, so results are bit-for-bit unchanged.

Reference: Schumann, U. & Sweet, R. A. (1988). Fast Fourier transforms for direct solution of Poisson's equation with staggered boundary conditions. J. Comput. Phys., 75, 123-137.

Aeolus.PressureSolvers.solve! — Function
solve!(p, source, solver::MultigridPoissonSolver)

AbstractPressureSolver interface: solve ∇²p = source in place. Drop-in replacement for the FFT and CG solvers.

solve!(p, source, solver::MGPreconditionedCG)

CG preconditioned by mg_cycles V-cycles, through the one CG driver Aeolus.Backends.pcg! (KA-g, #300): canonical reductions, so a solve and its iteration count are identical at every thread count. For a singular operator (periodic / Neumann on every axis) the mean is removed from the source, from every preconditioned residual and from the solution. A breakdown (p·Ap ≤ eps·|r·z|) stops the iteration silently, as before.

solve!(p, source, solver::DensityWeightedPoissonSolver)

Solve ∇·(w ∇p) = source on the interior. source is projected to zero mean (the compatibility condition of the singular operator) and p is returned with zero mean; halos of p are untouched. The iteration is the one CG driver Aeolus.Backends.pcg! (KA-g, #300): the operator fused with p·Ap, the Jacobi step and its mean projection fused with the residual update, so three sweeps and two canonical reductions per iteration, identical at every thread count.

solve!(p, source, solver::DirectWeightedPoissonSolver)

Solve ∇·(w ∇p) = source − mean(source) on the interior, exactly to round-off; p is returned with zero mean and its halos untouched.

Aeolus.PressureSolvers.poisson_axis_bcs — Function
poisson_axis_bcs(solver) -> (bc_x, bc_y, bc_z)

The per-axis boundary kinds (:periodic / :neumann) a pressure solver was built with.

poisson_axis_bcs(bcs::FieldBoundaryConditions) -> (bc_x, bc_y, bc_z)

The per-axis pressure-Poisson kinds implied by the six velocity faces of bcs, each read through axis_poisson_bc. The two faces of an axis must give the same kind; a periodic face opposite a Neumann one is not a well-defined Poisson problem for these solvers and throws an ArgumentError. Splat the result into a solver constructor: CosineFFTPoissonSolver(mesh; poisson_axis_bcs(bcs)...).

Aeolus.PressureSolvers.solver_stats — Function
solver_stats(solver) -> (iterations, residual)

The work and the convergence of the last solve! of solver, as the solver itself tracks them: iterations (CG iterations, multigrid cycles) and residual, the solver's own stopping measure (the absolute ‖r‖₂ of CGPoissonSolver and of the anelastic DensityWeightedPoissonSolver, the ‖r‖₂/‖b‖₂ of the multigrid solvers). A direct solver (the FFT, cosine-FFT and stretched-z solvers) gives (0, NaN): no iteration, no residual computed. The per-step diagnostics of the step cores read it after every pressure solve (#336).

A spectral solver's source buffer can be filled by the caller (the step cores write the divergence straight into it, SC-d #404):

Aeolus.PressureSolvers.source_buffer — Function
source_buffer(solver) -> Union{Nothing, AbstractArray{T,3}}

The array a spectral solver copies the source's interior into before it transforms it (buf[i, j, k] is the interior point (i, j, k), the storage point (i + Hx, j + Hy, k + Hz)), or nothing for a solver without one. A caller that can compute the source straight into it (the divergence of the step cores, SC-d #404) does so and then calls solve_source_buffer!: the same bits as solve! from a source field, without writing the field and copying it in (two passes over the domain per solve).

solve!(p::Field{CellCenter}, source::Field{CellCenter}, solver::FFTPoissonSolver)

Solve L_h p = source in-place. Both fields must live on the mesh the solver was constructed with. The mean of source is projected out; if your source has a large mean you have a compatibility issue, not a solver bug.

Aeolus.PressureSolvers.solve_source_buffer! — Function
solve_source_buffer!(p, solver) -> p

solve! from the source already in source_buffer(solver) (which the solve overwrites). Defined for the solvers whose source_buffer is not nothing.

Column-separable direct solver

The FFT/DCT + tridiagonal solver behind StretchedPoissonSolver and the anelastic DirectWeightedPoissonSolver (#309).

Aeolus.PressureSolvers.ColumnTridiagonalSolver — Type
ColumnTridiagonalSolver(T, (Nx, Ny, Nz), (dx, dy); lower, upper, hweight,
                        measure, inv_coupling, gauge,
                        bc_x = :periodic, bc_y = :periodic)

The direct FFT–tridiagonal solver (Schumann & Sweet 1988; Press et al. 2007, Numerical Recipes 3rd ed. §20.4) of every cell-centred Poisson problem whose horizontal operator is the uniform 3-point stencil and whose coefficients depend on height only:

\[h_k\,(δ_x^2 + δ_y^2)φ_k + a_k(φ_{k-1} - φ_k) + c_k(φ_{k+1} - φ_k) = b_k,\]

with a_k = lower[k], c_k = upper[k] (a_1 = c_{N_z} = 0: zero flux through the bottom and top faces), h_k = hweight[k] > 0. It is the one implementation behind two operators:

  • the conservative stretched-z Laplacian of StretchedPoissonSolver (h = 1, a_k = 1/(Δz_c[k] Δz_f[k]));
  • the density-weighted operator ∇·(w(z)∇φ) of the sound-proof cores, DirectWeightedPoissonSolver (h = w_c, a_k = w_f[k]/Δz²).

x and y are each :periodic (half-complex DFT, FFTW R2HC/HC2R) or :neumann (DCT-II/III, REDFT10/REDFT01), diagonalised with the exact 3-point eigenvalues λ = 4/Δ² sin²(πm/N) or 4/Δ² sin²(πm/2N); each horizontal wavenumber leaves the tridiagonal system a_k φ̂_{k-1} − (a_k + c_k + λ h_k) φ̂_k + c_k φ̂_{k+1} = b̂_k, which is strictly diagonally dominant for λ > 0 and is solved by the Thomas algorithm with the factorisation computed once at construction.

The mean mode (λ = 0) is singular. It is the 1-D problem G_{k+1} − G_k = m_k b̂_k for the face flux G_k = (φ̂_k − φ̂_{k−1}) / ι_k (m = measure, ι = inv_coupling, so a_k = 1/(m_k ι_k) and c_k = 1/(m_k ι_{k+1})) with G_1 = 0: the m-weighted mean of the source is removed (the compatibility condition of the closed column), the flux is integrated up, and the result is shifted to zero gauge-weighted mean, Σ_k gauge_k Σ_{ij} φ_{ijk} = 0.

Cost: one 2-D transform pair and O(N_x N_y N_z) for the column solves; exact to round-off. Periodic z is not supported.

Iterative solver

Aeolus.PressureSolvers.CGPoissonSolver — Type
CGPoissonSolver(mesh::RegularGrid; tol=1e-8, maxit=200,
                bc_top=:neumann, bc_bottom=:neumann,
                bc_xy=:periodic, bc_x=bc_xy, bc_y=bc_xy)

Matrix-free conjugate-gradient Poisson solver on the 7-point discrete Laplacian. Every axis is periodic or homogeneous Neumann: bc_x, bc_y (bc_xy sets both) and bc_bottom, bc_top in z (any value other than :periodic there is Neumann; the two z faces must agree). A Neumann face uses the mirror ghost φ₀ = φ₁, i.e. zero flux through the face — the same discrete operator CosineFFTPoissonSolver diagonalises with the matching bc_x, bc_y, bc_z. Slower than the direct solvers; its use is as an independent check and for operators they cannot diagonalise.

Before #251 bc_xy was stored but ignored: x and y were wrapped periodically whatever it said, so bc_xy = :neumann silently returned the periodic solution.

Preconditioner: none (the diagonal of the uniform-grid Laplacian is constant in the interior, so Jacobi would only rescale).

The iteration is Aeolus.Backends.pcg!, the one CG driver (KA-g, #300): the 7-point operator is a point kernel fused with p·Ap, the reductions are canonical plane sums, so a solve (and its iteration count) is identical at every thread count. The mean is removed from the source, from every residual direction and from the solution.

Convergence: ‖r‖ ≤ tol · ‖b‖. Not guaranteed within maxit; a warning is emitted if the iteration hits the cap.

Aeolus.PressureSolvers.fill_unit_halo! — Function
fill_unit_halo!(a, bc_x, bc_y, bc_z) -> a

Fill the one-cell halo of a (size (nx+2, ny+2, nz+2), interior 2:n+1): per axis :periodic wraps, anything else mirrors (the homogeneous-Neumann ghost φ₀ = φ₁), x then y then z. The halo kernels of BoundaryConditions (KA-f, #297): the halo of the CG iterates of CGPoissonSolver and DensityWeightedPoissonSolver, and of the multigrid levels.

Aeolus.PressureSolvers.copy_unit_halo_in! — Function
copy_unit_halo_in!(backend, buf, f::Field) -> buf
copy_unit_halo_out!(backend, f::Field, buf) -> f

Copy f's interior into the interior 2:n+1 of the unit-halo array buf, or back. Pointwise kernels (KA-g, #300).

CGPoissonSolver, MGPreconditionedCG, DensityWeightedPoissonSolver and solve_implicit_diffusion! all iterate with the one CG driver pcg! (KA-g, #300).

Kernels of the spectral solvers

The FFT, CosineFFT and stretched solvers run their pointwise steps as launch! kernels and their transforms through apply_transform! from the host (KA-h, #298); see The kernel port.

Write buf (interior-sized), times s if given, into f's interior; f's halos are left untouched. A pointwise kernel (KA-h, #298).
