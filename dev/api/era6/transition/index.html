Transition diagnostics — Transition

Transition owns the laminar/turbulent and intermittency diagnostics of Era VI: the turbulent fraction $γ(z)$ of the vorticity-fluctuation indicator (turbulent_fraction, turbulent_indicator!, laminar_mask!), the time-series intermittency_factor, the flux Richardson number with resolved and SGS fluxes (flux_richardson), the buoyancy Reynolds number, the Sun et al. (2012) $V_{TKE}$, the grid Reynolds number, the supercritical fraction of a $Ri_g$ field, and a log-linear growth_rate fit. It defines no Richardson number: $Ri_g$ comes from TurbulenceStability. The theory and the Kelvin–Helmholtz / Miles–Howard validation are on Laminar–turbulent transition.

using Aeolus
γ  = turbulent_fraction(sim)                    # γ(z), plane-normalised |ω′|/⟨|ω|⟩ > 0.2
r  = flux_richardson(sim)                       # r.Ri_f, r.B, r.P (resolved + SGS)
fit = growth_rate(ts, A)                        # fit.rate, fit.r2, fit.window
intermittency_factor(series; threshold = 0.1)

It also owns the spatially developing flat-plate boundary layer (P59-d, #185): the Blasius solution (blasius_solution, blasius_profile, blasius_velocity), the fringe region (FringeRegion, add_fringe!, fringe_forcing, blasius_target, blasius_freestream), free-stream turbulence (FreeStreamDisturbance, disturbance_velocity!, disturbance_fringe_update), the integral diagnostics as functions of x (skin_friction, displacement_thickness, momentum_thickness, shape_factor, boundary_layer_edge) and the onset finder (transition_onset, with the Mayle and Abu-Ghannam & Shaw correlations); theory in Flat-plate transition.

c = flat_plate_setup()                          # 2-D Blasius plate, Re_x 1.35e4 → 1e5
c.sim.u.data .= 1
for n in 1:1200; step_rk3!(c.sim, 1.0; body_force! = c.forcing!); end
cf = skin_friction(c.sim; U_e = 1.0); H = shape_factor(c.sim)

Synthetic inflow turbulence (N-inflow-a, #252): the generator contract (AbstractInflowGenerator, unit_fluctuations!, generator_state, restore_generator_state!), the Lund transform (lund_coefficients, apply_lund!), the Xie & Castro digital filter (DigitalFilterGenerator, digital_filter_kernel) and free-stream turbulence as a second provider (FreeStreamInflow, disturbance_velocity, disturbance_covariance); theory in Synthetic inflow turbulence.

gen = DigitalFilterGenerator(64, 40, 15.625, 6.25; L_s = 62.5, L_z = 25.0, T = 30.0)
a   = lund_coefficients([0.514 0 -0.09; 0 0.332 0; -0.09 0 0.141])   # R_ij, m² s⁻²
u, v, w = zeros(64, 40), zeros(64, 40), zeros(64, 40)
unit_fluctuations!(u, v, w, gen, Δt); apply_lund!(u, v, w, a)

Inflow statistics (N-inflow-b, #258): the contract (InflowStatistics, inflow_stresses, inflow_lengths, update_inflow_statistics!, inflow_projection_log, realizable_stress, inflow_lund_coefficients), the statistics of an Aeolus LES parent (ParentResolvedStatistics, inflow_stress_parts: resolved, scale-gap band, SGS from Operators.sgs_tke!) and the similarity profiles for a parent without resolved turbulence (ParametrizedStatistics, parametrized_scales); theory in Inflow statistics.

                               levels = 1:20)
update_inflow_statistics!(src, Δt)              # after every parent step (or at a cadence)
z   = [(k - 0.5) * 6.25 for k in 1:64]          # the child's levels
gen = DigitalFilterGenerator(64, 64, 15.625, 6.25; inflow_lengths(src, z, t)...)
a   = inflow_lund_coefficients(src, z, t)       # realizable, projections booked
unit_fluctuations!(u, v, w, gen, Δt); apply_lund!(u, v, w, a)

Inflow faces (N-inflow-c, #264): InflowTurbulence wraps the open boundary's data, applies the perturbations on the inflow levels of every lateral face with zero added mass flux (added_mass_flux), and books the injected kinetic-energy flux (injected_ke_flux). Related functions are inflow_normal_length, set_integral_time!, refresh_inflow_lengths!, inflow_perturbation, feedback_exclude_width and remove_inflow_turbulence!. The theory is in Applying the perturbations on the inflow faces.

run = NestedRun(parent, child, nest; feedback = :one_way)
it  = InflowTurbulence(run)                     # ParentResolvedStatistics per face
step_nested!(run)                               # perturbations enter at every stage time
added_mass_flux(it).west.relative               # round-off
injected_ke_flux(it).total                      # W m⁻²

Development length (N-inflow-d, #266): FetchStatistics, accumulate_fetch! and fetch_profiles average the resolved moments of an inflow–outflow domain over y and time per streamwise column; development_length is the distance from the inflow plane beyond which a profile stays within a band around its target. The inflow–outflow half channel is half_channel_case(; extent, inflow, outflow) (WallModels), the x band of the total stress total_stress_profile(…; irange) (Operators). Theory and measured fetches in Development length.

Aeolus.Transition.DigitalFilterGenerator — Type
DigitalFilterGenerator(ns, nz, Δs, Δz; L_s, L_z, T, seed = 1)

The digital-filter inflow generator of Xie & Castro (2008), as in PALM (Kadasch et al. 2021), on an ns × nz inflow plane with spacings Δs (along the face) and Δz (vertical). Implements AbstractInflowGenerator.

  • L_s, L_z (m) are the integral lengths along the face and in the vertical, T (s) the integral time; each may be a number, a vector over the nz levels, a 3-tuple (one entry — number or vector — per component $ũ, ṽ, w̃$) or a 3 × nz matrix.
  • Space. A fresh plane of Gaussian white noise $r$ is filtered with the separable kernel of digital_filter_kernel, $ψ_{\rm new}(j,k) = \sum_{j'}\sum_{k'} b^s_{j'}(k)\,b^z_{k'}(k)\,r(j+j', k+k')$, the coefficients those of the output level $k$ (so the scales may vary with height). The kernels are calibrated so the measured $∫_0^∞R\,ds = L_s$ and $∫_0^∞R\,dz = L_z$ (the X&C nominal $n = L/Δ$ gives 1.11-1.17 L; see digital_filter_kernel).
  • Time. $ψ(t) = ψ(t-Δt)\,ρ + ψ_{\rm new}\sqrt{1-ρ^2}$, $ρ = e^{-Δt/T}$ — the blend of X&C (2008, Eq. 13), $e^{-πΔt/(2T_{XC})}$ and $(1 - e^{-πΔt/T_{XC}})^{1/2}$, with $T_{XC} = πT/2$: their $T_{XC}$ is not the integral time ($∫_0^∞e^{-πτ/(2T_{XC})}dτ = 2T_{XC}/π$), so $T$ here is, and the correlation is $R(τ) = e^{-τ/T}$ for any sequence of (adaptive) steps. $ρ^2 + (1-ρ^2) = 1$ keeps unit variance.
  • The first planes are drawn spatially filtered (stationary start). Components are independent, so $⟨ũ_iũ_j⟩ = δ_{ij}$; impose $R_{ij}$ with apply_lund!.

seed seeds a Random.Xoshiro. generator_state / restore_generator_state! snapshot the three planes and the random-number generator. Cost: one step on a 64 × 40 face with $L = 4Δ$ is quoted in docs/src/theory/transition.md.

References: Xie, Z.-T. & Castro, I. P. (2008). Efficient generation of inflow conditions for large eddy simulation of street-scale flows. Flow Turbul. Combust., 81, 449-470. Kadasch, E. et al. (2021). Mesoscale nesting interface of the PALM model system 6.0. Geosci. Model Dev., 14, 5435-5465. Klein, M., Sadiki, A. & Janicka, J. (2003). A digital filter based generation of inflow data for spatially developing direct numerical or large eddy simulations. J. Comput. Phys., 186, 652-665.

Aeolus.Transition.FetchStatistics — Type
FetchStatistics(sim; jrange = nothing) -> fs

Running span- and time-averaged moments of the resolved velocity of sim (a simulation with u, v, w on a RegularGrid) per streamwise column $i$ and level $k$, for a flow that is homogeneous in $y$ and in time but develops in $x$ (an inflow–outflow domain). Add samples with accumulate_fetch!, read them with fetch_profiles.

jrange (default: all of 1:Ny) restricts the span average to the cells j ∈ jrange (and their y-faces, the two end faces at half weight), e.g. the centre of a domain whose open lateral faces carry the mean profile only, so that the side layers they leave do not enter the fetch.

The variances are taken at each staggered component's own points, as Operators.resolved_tke_profile and ParentResolvedStatistics do: $u$ on the two x-faces of the column, $v$ on its y-faces, $w$ on the bottom and top face of the level, each about the time-and-span mean of its own face; the column value is the mean of the two faces' variances (a wall face, $w ≡ 0$, contributes zero). Interpolating to the cell centre first would multiply the variance of a mode by $\cos^2(kΔ/2)$. The shear stress $\overline{u'w'}$ needs both components at one point: it is formed from the cell-centred (two-point-mean) $u$ and $w$. Fluctuations are deviations from the time-and-span mean of the same point, so a steady, spanwise-uniform developing flow has zero TKE.

Applied to a horizontally periodic precursor, the same statistics averaged over $x$ are the target the developing flow is compared with (same operator, same grid), so no definition difference enters the comparison.

Aeolus.Transition.FreeStreamDisturbance — Type
FreeStreamDisturbance(Tu, L, nmodes; seed = 1, U∞ = 1, κ_min = 0.25/L,
                      κ_max = 12/L, period_y = nothing, ndir = 10)

Homogeneous isotropic free-stream turbulence of intensity Tu ($u'_{\rm rms}/U_∞$, a fraction, e.g. 0.033) and longitudinal integral length L ($L_{11}$), represented by nmodes random Fourier modes (Jacobs & Durbin 2001; Brandt, Schlatter & Henningson 2004).

  • Wavenumbers lie on $⌈nmodes/ndir⌉$ spherical shells, equispaced in $\ln κ$ between κ_min and κ_max; on each shell ndir directions — one of each antipodal pair of the 20 vertices of a dodecahedron, randomly rotated per shell (Brandt et al. 2004) — with uniformly random phases and polarisations $σ ⊥ k$. period_y quantises $k_y$ to multiples of $2π/L_y$ so the field is periodic in a spanwise-periodic domain.
  • Each shell carries the energy $E(κ_s)Δκ_s$ of von_karman_spectrum; the amplitudes are then scaled so the total kinetic energy is exactly $q = \tfrac32 (Tu\,U_∞)^2$, and the spectrum length is iterated so that the integral scale of the discrete mode set, $\tfrac{π}{2u'^2}\sum_s E_sΔκ_s/κ_s$, equals L (the finite $κ$ range otherwise biases it). An ArgumentError is thrown if no spectrum length can give L with modes in $[κ_{\min}, κ_{\max}]$ (e.g. an integral length close to the grid spacing when κ_max is set by the grid).
  • Fields are sampled with disturbance_velocity! (divergence-free to round-off on the staggered grid) and fed into a spatially developing boundary layer through the fringe target with disturbance_fringe_update, advected at $U_∞$ (Taylor's frozen turbulence), as in Brandt et al. (2004).

References: Jacobs, R. G. & Durbin, P. A. (2001). Simulations of bypass transition. J. Fluid Mech., 428, 185-212. Brandt, L., Schlatter, P. & Henningson, D. S. (2004). Transition in boundary layers subject to free-stream turbulence. J. Fluid Mech., 517, 167-198.

Aeolus.Transition.FreeStreamInflow — Type
FreeStreamInflow(fst::FreeStreamDisturbance, ns, nz, Δs, Δz;
                 origin = (0, 0), x = 0, t = 0)

FreeStreamDisturbance as an AbstractInflowGenerator: the frozen random-mode field advected at fst.U∞ (Taylor) through an inflow plane at x, with plane points $s_j = s_0 + (j - ½)Δs$, $z_k = z_0 + (k - ½)Δz$ (origin = (s_0, z_0); $s$ is the $y$ of the mode set). Each step evaluates the continuous mode sum (disturbance_velocity) at $x - U_∞t$ and whitens it with the Cholesky factor of the exact mode-set covariance (disturbance_covariance, lund_coefficients): $ũ = a^{-1}u'$. The planes are then exactly zero-mean, unit-variance and uncorrelated over a long sweep, whatever the anisotropy of the finite mode set; since $a$ is lower triangular, $ũ$ is $u'$ rescaled, so its longitudinal integral scale is that of fst ($L_{11}$). Requires Tu > 0 and U∞ > 0. The state is the clock t. Unlike DigitalFilterGenerator it is isotropic with a single scale.

Aeolus.Transition.FringeRegion — Type
FringeRegion(; x_start, x_end, rise, fall = rise, λ_max)

Fringe region for a spatially developing flow in an x-periodic domain: the relaxation term $-λ(x)(\mathbf u - \mathbf u_{\rm target})$ with

λ(x) = λ_max [ S((x − x_start)/rise) − S((x − x_end)/fall + 1) ]

(Nordström, Nordin & Henningson 1999, eq. 2.3; S is smooth_step). λ is zero outside [x_start, x_end] and λ_max on the plateau [x_start + rise, x_end − fall]. Apply it with add_fringe! or through body_force! with fringe_forcing.

The rate is an Operators.DampingProfile with a NordstromRamp: the one damping-profile implementation that add_sponge! also uses.

λ_max must make the relaxation fast against advection through the plateau: Nordström et al. require $λ_{\max} L_{\rm plateau}/U ≫ 1$; λ_max · (x_end − x_start − rise − fall)/U ≈ 10–20 recovers the target to $< 1\%$ of $U$.

Reference: Nordström, J., Nordin, N. & Henningson, D. (1999). The fringe region technique and the Fourier method used in the direct numerical simulation of spatially evolving viscous flows. SIAM J. Sci. Comput., 20(4), 1365-1393.

Aeolus.Transition.InflowStatistics — Type
InflowStatistics

Supertype of the sources of synthetic-inflow statistics (N-inflow-b, #258). An implementer provides, at heights z (m, a number or a vector) and time t (s),

methodreturns

Aeolus.Transition.InflowTurbulence — Type
InflowTurbulence(run; generator = DigitalFilterGenerator,
                 statistics = ParentResolvedStatistics, faces = :lateral,
                 u_min = 0.5, ρ_ref = 1.2, seed = 1,
                 length_interval = 3600, statistics_interval = 60,
                 statistics_kwargs = (;))

Synthetic turbulence on the inflow parts of the lateral open faces of a nest child (N-inflow-c, #264): $u_{b,i} = u_{{\rm data},i} + a_{ij}ũ_j$, the unit planes $ũ_j$ of one generator per face (DigitalFilterGenerator, Xie & Castro 2008) and the Lund et al. (1998) amplitudes $a$ of the statistics source (inflow_lund_coefficients). run is a NestedRun (its child gets the perturbations; the default statistics are a ParentResolvedStatistics of its parent per face, in scale-gap mode with the nest's refinement) or a simulation with OpenBoundary faces (then statistics must be given). The object wraps the open boundary's data and installs itself as that data, so the perturbations enter inside update_open_boundaries!, after the data fill and before the mass-flux correction, at every stage time of every core. Nothing is copied into the step cores.

Faces. faces = :lateral takes every open west/east/south/north face (a tuple of sides selects some). The top face is excluded, as in PALM (Kadasch et al. 2021, §2.4): the perturbations enter with the mean flow, which crosses the top of a boundary-layer nest only weakly.

Inflow detection (per face and level). $U_n(z)$ is the face-mean outward normal velocity of the data over the fluid faces. Perturbations are multiplied by the ramp $r = \min(1, \max(0, -U_n/u_{\min}))$: zero on outflow levels, full where the inflow exceeds u_min (default 0.5 m s⁻¹), linear in between, so a wind that veers through a face switches its turbulence on and off continuously. The integral time is Taylor's $T = L_n/\max(|U_n|, u_{\min})$ with the normal length of inflow_normal_length.

Time. The perturbation is piecewise linear in time between knots. A knot is a complete perturbation: the generator advanced to the knot time (always, whatever the wind, so its state does not depend on the direction), Lund-transformed, ramped with the current $U_n$, restricted to the receiving faces and stripped of its per-level mean. For data with a time window (a NestBoundaryData: the parent blended linearly over the parent step $[t_0, t_0+Δt]$) the knots are the window ends, so the perturbation is blended exactly like the data (a stage time past the end extrapolates, as the data does); otherwise every refresh at a new time makes a knot. The first knot (installation) is zero. This is what keeps the child's divergence at round-off: a multistage core combines the step-start state with its stages, and the open-boundary buffers with them (SSP-RK3: $u^{n+1} = frac13u^n + frac23u^{(2)}$); boundary values linear in time make the combined buffers equal to a fresh refresh at the step's end, so the refresh at the start of the next parent step (the window update of a NestedRun) finds exactly the faces the projection saw. Planes held constant over a step, a ramp re-evaluated at every stage, or perturbations switched on at the installation time each left a divergence of $O(10^{-3})$ s⁻¹ that the combination only damps by 1/3 per substep (measured, test/era6_inflow_turbulence.jl).

Zero added mass flux. On every face and level the area-weighted mean of the normal perturbation over the receiving fluid faces is removed, so the perturbation adds no mass, whatever the core's constraint weight of the level ($ρ̄(z)$, $ρ̄θ̄(z)$). added_mass_flux reports the check. The removal reduces the normal variance by the variance of the face mean, $σ_n^2\,n^{-2}\sum_{ij}R(|i-j|Δ_s) ≈ 2(L_s/L_{\rm face})σ_n^2$ for a face of length $L_{\rm face} ≫ L_s$ (and $\overline{u_n'w'}$ by the same factor); see docs/src/theory/transition.md.

Corners. The cell next to two open faces is owned by exactly one: the x-face (west/east) at levels where that face carries turbulence (ramp > 0, decided at each knot), the y-face otherwise. The y-face's corner column then receives no perturbation and is excluded from its mean removal. The along-face (staggered) component of an x-face is not applied at the slot that is the adjacent open y-face's normal-velocity face, and the vertical component not at the ground face: those slots belong to the other face's condition.

Ledger. injected_ke_flux gives the flux of perturbation kinetic energy through each face, $ρ_{\rm ref}⟨\tfrac12|u'|^2\max(-U_n,0)⟩$ (W m⁻², face mean). Solid faces (fluid = false in the normal plane, set by NestBoundaryData's solid mask) receive nothing.

Statistics. statistics is an InflowStatistics (one source for every face), a function side -> source, a NamedTuple by side, or the type ParentResolvedStatistics (the default, for a NestedRun; one per face, statistics_kwargs passed to its constructor, with the parent levels the child spans). A source with memory is folded with the parent's elapsed time once the parent clock has advanced by statistics_interval seconds (default 60 s, against the default running-mean time of 600 s: one fold costs 65 ms per face on the 192 × 192 Berlin parent, docs/src/theory/transition.md). The stresses (Lund factors) and normal lengths are re-evaluated at every generator advance; the generators' L_s, L_z every length_interval seconds (refresh_inflow_lengths!), lengths below one grid spacing raised to one spacing (shorter scales are not representable).

generator is the type DigitalFilterGenerator (seeded seed, seed+1, … per face) or a function (side; ns, nz, Δs, Δz, L_s, L_z, T, seed) -> generator for another AbstractInflowGenerator. generator_state / restore_generator_state! snapshot and restore everything the next perturbations depend on (bit-identical continuation). Two-way runs: the feedback should skip a frame of feedback_exclude_width next to the open faces.

Aeolus.Transition.ParentResolvedStatistics — Type
ParentResolvedStatistics(parent; closure = nothing, face = :west, τ_avg = 600,
                         region = :plane, footprint = nothing, ring_width = 2,
                         mode = :scale_gap, refine = 2, c_gap = 4, c_len = c_gap/2,
                         U_min = 0.1, levels = 1:Nz, lag_z = 16, c_k = 0.1)

The inflow statistics of an Aeolus LES parent (any simulation with u, v, w, νt, bcs on a RegularGrid, e.g. a BoussinesqSimulation), implementing InflowStatistics. Call update_inflow_statistics!(src, Δt) after every parent step (or at a cadence, with the elapsed Δt).

Running mean. Each sample $x$ enters the exponential average with the exact kernel weight of its interval, $S ← ρS + w\,x$, $W ← ρW + w$, $ρ = e^{-Δt/τ_{\rm avg}}$, $w = τ_{\rm avg}(1 - ρ)$; the statistic is $S/W$. The memory e-folds in exactly $τ_{\rm avg}$ for any sequence of (adaptive) steps and the first sample is not biased towards zero. τ_avg = Inf is the uniform time average ($w = Δt$).

Resolved part. $R^{\rm res}_{ij} = ⟨u_i'u_j'⟩$ with $⟨·⟩$ the mean over the region and $'$ the deviation from it: region = :plane, the whole horizontal plane; region = :ring, the parent columns within ring_width cells of the edge of the child footprint = (i_range, j_range) (parent cell indices), on both sides of it (periodic wrap).

The variances are taken at each staggered component's own points, as Operators.resolved_tke_profile does: $u$ over the two x-faces of each region column, $v$ over its two y-faces, and for $w$ the mean of the variances on the bottom and top face of the level. The two-point mean to the cell centre would multiply the variance of a mode by $\cos^2(k_iΔ_i/2)$ along the component's own direction, ¼ at $λ = 3Δ$, i.e. inside the scale gap. On the plane, the column sum of $\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}R^{\rm res}$ equals that of resolved_tke_profile (and level by level for $u, v$), which the test asserts. A cross term needs both components at one point: it is the correlation coefficient of the cell-centred (two-point-mean) fields times $\sqrt{R_{ii}R_{jj}}$ of the native variances. That is exact for a single Fourier mode (each component attenuated by its own $\cos(k_iΔ_i/2)$) and keeps the tensor realizable (a correlation matrix scaled by a positive diagonal); for a broadband field the centre correlation is a spectrally weighted mean of the modes' correlations, weighted by the products of the two transfer functions. The statistic is computed in one pass over the fluctuation planes that also feed the spectra, the band-pass and the vertical lags, so Operators' _plane_means / resolved_tke_profile (one scalar per level) and total_stress_profile (the solver's $u$-$w$ edge flux, including the mean flow) are not called; their conventions are matched instead.

SGS part. $R^{\rm SGS}_{ij} = \tfrac23 e\,δ_{ij} - 2ν_t\bar S_{ij}$ with $e$ from sgs_tke!(e, closure, parent) (the TKE closure's $e$, or the closure's own estimate), $ν_t$ the closure's viscosity (νh for the horizontal pairs, νv for those with $z$), and $\bar S_{ij}$ the deviatoric part of the cell-centred strain rate (Operators._strain_at), so $\operatorname{tr}R^{\rm SGS} = 2e$ exactly.

Scale gap (mode = :scale_gap, the default). The child receives the parent's large eddies through the interpolated boundary values; the generator must add only what is missing,

\[R^{\rm gen} = R^{\rm SGS} + R^{\rm res}(k_c < |k_h| ≤ r\,k_c), \qquad k_c = \frac{2π}{c_{\rm gap}Δ_p},\]

the resolved stress of the horizontal wavenumbers between the parent's effective resolution (wavelength $c_{\rm gap}Δ_p$; c_gap = 4, within the 2–4 $Δ_p$ of the issue, $Δ_p = \max(Δx, Δy)$) and the child's ($c_{\rm gap}Δ_p/r$, refine = r, the horizontal refinement), obtained by a spectral band-pass of each native plane (FFT, the parent laterally periodic); the band's variances and cross terms are formed as for the resolved part. For $r = 1$ the band is empty and $R^{\rm gen} = R^{\rm SGS}$. mode = :full gives $R^{\rm SGS} + R^{\rm res}$ (a child whose boundary values carry no parent turbulence).

Lengths and time. The horizontal autocovariances of every level come from the spectra of the native planes (Wiener–Khinchin, whole plane, running-averaged; $w$'s the mean of its two faces'); the vertical ones from the plane covariance of the cell-centred fields of a level with the lag_z levels above it (a correlation coefficient, normalised by the same centre variances). Each integral length is the integral of the correlation coefficient to its first zero crossing; $L_s$ is along the face, $L_z$ vertical and $T = L_n/\max(|U_n|, U_{\min})$ with $L_n$ the length along the face normal and $U_n$ the region-mean normal velocity (Taylor; U_min = 0.1 m s⁻¹ keeps a calm parent finite). In scale-gap mode the lengths are those of the band-passed field (of the full field where the band is empty) and are capped at $c_{\rm len}Δ_p$ (c_len = c_gap/2: half the cutoff wavelength).

levels restricts the statistics to the parent levels the child needs; the work arrays hold $2 × 3 × (\text{lag\_z} + 1)$ horizontal planes.

inflow_stress_parts returns the resolved, band, SGS parts separately.

Aeolus.Transition.digital_filter_kernel — Method
digital_filter_kernel(L, Δ) -> (b, n, N)

The normalised filter coefficients $b_k ∝ \exp(-π|k|/(2n))$, $k = -N … N$, $\sum b_k^2 = 1$ (Xie & Castro 2008, Eq. 11) whose filtered white noise has the integral length $∫_0^∞ R\,dr = L$ on a grid of spacing Δ (trapezoid on the discrete correlation $R_r = \sum_k b_kb_{k+r}$).

The length convention. Xie & Castro take $n = L/Δ$ and state $R(r) = \exp(-πr/(2nΔ))$. Neither holds for the integral length: the autocorrelation of the exponential filter is (continuous limit) $R(r) = (1 + πr/(2nΔ))\exp(-πr/(2nΔ))$, with $∫_0^∞ R\,dr = 4nΔ/π$, so the nominal $n = L/Δ$ gives $4L/π ≈ 1.27L$ untruncated, and 1.11 L to 1.17 L (for $L/Δ$ = 2 to 16) with X&C's truncation $N = 2n$. The width n is therefore calibrated (bisection to 1e-13) so the discrete integral is L to 1e-10. The support is $N = ⌈2L/Δ⌉$ (≈ 2.4 n); it is fixed by L, not n, so the integral is continuous in n during the calibration. The calibrated widths are $n/(πL/(4Δ))$ = 1.14, 1.07, 1.05, 1.04 for $L/Δ$ = 2, 4, 8, 16 (the truncation shortens the correlation slightly), and the correlation shape is $R(r) ≈ (1 + 2r/L)\,e^{-2r/L}$ (RMS difference 0.012 to 0.019 over $r ≤ 2L$ for $L/Δ ≥ 1$).

An ArgumentError is thrown for $L ≤ Δ/2$: the white-noise limit ($n → 0$) already has the trapezoid integral $Δ/2$.

Aeolus.Transition.flux_richardson — Method
flux_richardson(u, v, w, θ; θ₀, g = 9.81, νt = nothing, Pr_t = 1/3)
    -> (Ri_f, B, P, uw, vw, wθ)
flux_richardson(sim::BoussinesqSimulation)

The flux Richardson number per level,

\[Ri_f = -\frac{B}{P}, \qquad B = \frac{g}{θ_0} ⟨w'θ'⟩, \qquad P = -⟨u'w'⟩\frac{∂U}{∂z} - ⟨v'w'⟩\frac{∂V}{∂z},\]

the ratio of the buoyancy destruction of turbulence kinetic energy to its shear production (Ellison 1957; Ivey & Imberger 1991, where the irreversible part is the mixing efficiency). $⟨·⟩$ is the plane mean and $U, V, Θ$ the plane-mean profiles. The fluxes are the resolved plus the SGS fluxes: with an eddy viscosity νt (a CellCenter field) the SGS parts

\[τ_{13} = -2ν_t S_{13}, \quad τ_{23} = -2ν_t S_{23}, \quad ⟨w'θ'⟩_{\rm sgs} = -\frac{ν_t}{Pr_t}\frac{∂θ}{∂z}\]

are added ($S_{ij}$ from the Smagorinsky stencil Operators._strain_at, $∂θ/∂z$ centred over $2Δz$, as in add_eddy_diffusion_scalar!). Resolved fluxes use u, v, w interpolated to the cell centre; $∂U/∂z$ is centred (one-sided at the first and last level).

Returns profiles: Ri_f (NaN where $P = 0$), B, P and the total kinematic fluxes uw, vw, wθ. Halos of u, v, w, θ (and νt) must be filled. The sim method uses sim.θ₀, sim.g_accel, sim.Pr_t and, when the SGS closure is on, sim.νt, and fills the halos first.

Aeolus.Transition.growth_rate — Method
growth_rate(t, A; window = nothing, r2_min = 0.999, rate_tol = 0.01, min_points = 10)
    -> (rate, intercept, r2, window, npoints)

Exponential growth rate $σ$ of an amplitude series $A(t) ∝ e^{σt}$ from the least-squares fit of $\ln A = σ t + c$.

  • window = (t₁, t₂): fit the samples with $t₁ ≤ t ≤ t₂$.
  • window = nothing (default): the fit window is chosen automatically as the longest contiguous run of at least min_points samples whose fit has $R^2 ≥ r_{2,\min}$ (default 0.999) and whose three thirds, fitted separately, give rates that agree to rate_tol (relative, default 1 %). The second condition is what makes the window the stretch of clean exponential growth: $R^2$ alone grows with the span of $\ln A$, so a long window that includes the adjustment of the seed or the onset of saturation still passes 0.999 with a rate biased by a few per cent (measured: 0.1859 instead of the 0.1864 of the linear stage of the Kelvin–Helmholtz test, and 2.8 % low on a synthetic saturating series), while the rates of the thirds of a bent $\ln A$ differ (thirds rather than halves: a seed transient at the start and saturation at the end bias both halves alike, but not the middle third). An error is thrown if no window qualifies.

Returns the rate, the intercept, the $R^2$ of the fit, the time window used and the number of samples in it. Non-positive amplitudes are not allowed.

Aeolus.Transition.lund_coefficients — Method
lund_coefficients(R; rtol = 1e-12) -> a::Matrix{Float64}

The lower-triangular amplitude matrix of Lund, Wu & Squires (1998, Eq. 13), the Cholesky factor of the Reynolds-stress tensor $R = a a^{\rm T}$:

\[a = \begin{pmatrix} \sqrt{R_{11}} & 0 & 0\\ R_{21}/a_{11} & \sqrt{R_{22} - a_{21}^2} & 0\\ R_{31}/a_{11} & (R_{32} - a_{21}a_{31})/a_{22} & \sqrt{R_{33} - a_{31}^2 - a_{32}^2} \end{pmatrix},\]

so that $u_i = a_{ij}ũ_j$ has $⟨u_iu_j⟩ = R_{ij}$ when $⟨ũ_iũ_j⟩ = δ_{ij}$ (apply_lund!). R is a symmetric 3 × 3 matrix or the 6-tuple $(R_{11}, R_{22}, R_{33}, R_{12}, R_{13}, R_{23})$ (the ordering of PrognosticSGS), components (normal, along-face, vertical) — for a west face $(u, v, w)$, so $R_{13} = \overline{u'w'}$.

The radicands are the leading principal minors divided by the previous ones, so they are all non-negative exactly when R is positive semi-definite — Schumann's (1977) realizability conditions. A radicand below $-\,\text{rtol}\cdot\operatorname{tr}R$ (or a negative trace) throws an ArgumentError naming the violated condition. A radicand within round-off of zero (a perfectly correlated or vanishing component, e.g. $w' = 0$ at a wall) gives a zero pivot; the column below it must then vanish too, otherwise R is not realizable.

References: Lund, T. S., Wu, X. & Squires, K. D. (1998). Generation of turbulent inflow data for spatially-developing boundary layer simulations. J. Comput. Phys., 140, 233-258. Schumann, U. (1977). Realizability of Reynolds-stress turbulence models. Phys. Fluids, 20, 721-725.

Aeolus.Transition.relaminarisation_criteria — Method
relaminarisation_criteria(sim::BoussinesqSimulation; heat_flux = sim.θ_flux_bottom,
                          u_τ = nothing, h = sim.mesh.Lz)
    -> (h_over_L, L_plus, Ri_b, L, u_τ, Re_τ)

The three parameters that the stable-channel collapse studies use to predict relaminarisation of a channel of depth h cooled at the bottom by the kinematic heat flux heat_flux $= \overline{w'θ'}_0$ (K m s⁻¹, negative for cooling; default the simulation's prescribed bottom flux):

  • $h/L$ with the Obukhov length of the surface flux, $L = u_τ^2 θ_0/(κ g θ_*)$, $θ_* = -\overline{w'θ'}_0/u_τ$ (SurfaceExchange.obukhov_length with the simulation's $g$ and $θ_0$; positive for cooling). This $L$ includes $κ$. Nieuwstadt (2005) defines $L$ without $κ$ and finds that the turbulence of an open channel collapses for $h/L ≳ 1.23$ at $Re_τ = 360$ in his convention, i.e. $h/L ≳ 1.23 κ ≈ 0.49$ in this one;
  • $L^+ = L u_τ/ν$, the Obukhov length in wall units ($L^+ = Re_τ/(h/L)$, with the same $κ$-including $L$); Flores & Riley (2011) find collapse for $L^+ ≲ 100$, which at $Re_τ = 360$ is not consistent with Nieuwstadt's threshold ($L^+ ≈ 730$ there; see the theory page, "The cooled open channel");
  • the bulk Richardson number $Ri_b = g h (Θ_{top} - Θ_{bot})/(Θ_{top} U_b^2)$ (SurfaceExchange.bulk_richardson) of the plane-mean θ of the top and the bottom cell layers and the bulk (volume-mean) streamwise velocity $U_b$.

u_τ defaults to the measured friction velocity of the bottom wall (see friction_reynolds); pass the nominal value to evaluate the criteria of a case set up by its forcing, as the published thresholds are. Needs buoyancy ($θ_0 > 0$). A neutral flux gives $h/L = 0$ and $L^+ = Inf$.

turbulent_indicator!(ind, u, v, w; indicator = :vorticity_fluctuation,
                     threshold = 0.2, fluid = nothing,
                     normalization = :plane) -> ind

Pointwise laminar/turbulent indicator on the interior cells: ind[i,j,k] (a Bool array of the interior size) is true where

\[\frac{\lvert ω' \rvert}{⟨\lvert ω \rvert⟩} > \text{threshold}, \qquad ω' = ω - ⟨ω⟩,\]

with $ω = ∇×u$ at the cell centre (the centred stencil of the Smagorinsky strain rate, Operators._vorticity_at) and $⟨·⟩$ the mean over the fluid cells of the level (normalization = :plane, the streamwise–spanwise average of Brandt, Schlatter & Henningson 2004) or over all fluid cells of the domain (normalization = :volume, for a domain whose free stream is quiet). The fluctuation $ω'$ is always taken about the plane mean, so a laminar parallel shear flow ($ω$ uniform in each plane) is laminar everywhere: $ω' = 0$ up to round-off, far below any threshold. A plane (or domain) without vorticity is laminar.

fluid (a Bool array of the interior size, or nothing for all fluid) marks the cells that count; solid cells are never turbulent. The halos of u, v, w must be filled.

von_karman_spectrum(κ, L, q) -> E(κ)

The von Kármán energy spectrum in the normalisation of Jacobs & Durbin (2001) and Brandt, Schlatter & Henningson (2004, eq. 2.6):

E(κ) = (2/3) a (κL)^4 / (b + (κL)^2)^{17/6} · L q,   a = 1.606, b = 1.350,
