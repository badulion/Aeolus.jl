Era VI — High-fidelity turbulence closure

Era VI replaces the single Smagorinsky closure by a family of subgrid-scale (SGS) models: Lagrangian-dynamic and scale-dependent coefficients, anisotropic and minimum-dissipation closures, prognostic TKE and Reynolds-stress transport, stability-aware mixing. They all plug into the dynamical cores through one interface, the SGS closure contract (P54-a, #167), defined once in Aeolus.Operators (src/operators/sgs_closures.jl). The design and the numbers it was tested at are in Smagorinsky-Lilly SGS → Closure contract.

Shared contract — SGS closures (P54-a)

Every core (step!, step_rk3!, step_imex!, step_adaptive!, and the anelastic / pseudo-incompressible step!) takes the keyword sgs = closure. A closure fills the eddy viscosity and diffusivity (sim.νt, sim.κt) with the pure sgs_coefficients!, adds its tendencies with add_sgs_momentum! / add_sgs_scalar!, and commits its memory with advance_sgs! exactly once per accepted step; step_adaptive! rolls the memory back on a rejected step through sgs_state / restore_sgs_state!.

using Aeolus
sim = BoussinesqSimulation(RegularGrid(; size = (32, 32, 32), extent = (1.0, 1.0, 1.0)))
step!(sim, 1e-3; sgs = StaticSmagorinsky(0.17))           # == BoussinesqSimulation(…; C_s = 0.17)
step!(sim, 1e-3; sgs = DynamicSmagorinsky(sim.mesh))       # Germano–Lilly
step_rk3!(sim, 1e-3; sgs = (νt, u, v, w) -> compute_smagorinsky_νt!(νt, u, v, w, 0.1))  # a plain function
Aeolus.Operators.AbstractSGSClosure — Type
AbstractSGSClosure

Supertype of the subgrid-scale closures every dynamical core accepts through its sgs keyword (step!, step_rk3!, step_imex!, step_adaptive!, and the anelastic / pseudo-incompressible step!). A closure implements

genericwhen the core calls itdefault
sgs_coefficients!(f, c, sim)each time the tendencies are formed (every RK substage, every IMEX step, the CFL estimate)required
advance_sgs!(c, sim, dt)once per accepted step, after the projectionno-op
sgs_state(c), restore_sgs_state!(c, s)around a step step_adaptive! may rejectno-op
add_sgs_momentum!(tu, tv, tw, c, f, sim; stress)momentum tendencyLaplacian eddy-viscosity form with f.νh (stress = :laplacian), or the stress form with f.νh, f.νv (:symmetric)
add_sgs_scalar!(tϕ, ϕ, c, f, sim)θ tendencydiagonal eddy diffusion with f.κh (x, y faces), f.κv (z faces)
sgs_fields(c, sim)the storage f the coefficients go intoSGSFields(sim.νt, sim.κt)

sgs_coefficients! must be pure in the closure's memory: it may read the state and the memory last committed by advance_sgs!, never change it. That is what lets a core call it several times per step (three RK3 substages, the adaptive stepper's CFL estimate, a retried step) while a stateful closure (Lagrangian dynamic, prognostic TKE, Reynolds-stress transport) still advances exactly once per accepted step.

Built-in closures: StaticSmagorinsky, DynamicSmagorinsky, ML.MLSGS, and FunctionSGS (the adapter that keeps the legacy eddy_viscosity! = f(νt, u, v, w) keyword working). The design is in the theory page Smagorinsky-Lilly SGS, section "Closure contract".

Aeolus.Operators.SGSFields — Type
SGSFields(νh, νv, κh, κv)
SGSFields(ν, κ)

The cell-centred eddy coefficients a closure writes and the tendency generics read: horizontal and vertical eddy viscosity νh, νv and eddy diffusivity κh, κv (m² s⁻¹, Field{CellCenter}). An isotropic closure aliases them, νv === νh and κv === κh (the two-argument form); the halos are filled by the core (with the velocity boundary conditions) after sgs_coefficients! returns, once per distinct field.

Aeolus.Operators.sgs_fields — Function
sgs_fields(c::AbstractSGSClosure, sim) -> SGSFields

The storage the closure's coefficients go into. Default: the simulation's own SGSFields(sim.νt, sim.κt) (isotropic). An anisotropic closure that needs separate vertical coefficients overrides it with its own fields.

Aeolus.Operators.sgs_coefficients! — Function
sgs_coefficients!(f::SGSFields, c::AbstractSGSClosure, sim) -> f

Fill the interior of the eddy coefficients f from the current state of sim (u, v, w with filled halos; θ, qv, mesh, the buoyancy reference) and the memory c last committed. Pure: it must not change the closure's memory, because the cores call it more than once per step (see AbstractSGSClosure). Every closure implements it.

Aeolus.Operators.advance_sgs! — Function
advance_sgs!(c::TKEClosure, sim, dt)

Integrate the TKE equation over the accepted step dt (called once per step, or once per accepted piece of an adaptive step, after the projection; the sum over the pieces is the step):

  1. the coefficients $ν_t, κ_t, l, s$ from $e^n$ and the new state's $N^2$; $P_s = ν_t D$ (dual_strain!), with the facade production of attach_immersed_walls! when walls are attached, $P_b = −κ_t N^2$;
  2. with transport: $T = −∇·(\mathbf u e)$ (flux-limited) $+ ∇·(2ν_t∇e)$;
  3. $e^* = e^n + Δt (T + P_s + \max(P_b, 0))$, clipped at 0 if negative (booked: tke_statistics);
  4. the sinks (dissipation, buoyant destruction), linearised implicit or exact (dissipation), so $e^{n+1} ≥ 0$;
  5. $e = 0$ in solids; the first air cell of each column set to tke_surface_value when surface is given (booked).
advance_sgs!(c::ReynoldsStressClosure, sim, dt)

Integrate the stresses over the accepted step dt (once per step, after the projection):

  1. with transport: the explicit tendency $T_{ij}$ of the advection (flux-limited for the diagonals) and of $∇·(2ν_t∇τ_{ij})$, $ν_t$ of $τ^n$;
  2. per cell, $τ^* = R(τ^n + Δt\,T)R^T$ with the exact Coriolis rotation coriolis_rotation;
  3. $(I − Δt\,L)τ^{n+1} = τ^*$ with $L$ the (linear) source operator of reynolds_stress_terms at the cell's velocity gradient (dual_velocity_gradient), $∇θ_v$, and $ε/e$, $c_θ$ of $τ^n$;
  4. $τ^{n+1}$ projected onto the realizable set, the correction booked; $τ = 0$ in solids.
advance_sgs!(c::AbstractSGSClosure, sim, dt) -> c

Commit the closure's memory over an accepted step of length dt: called exactly once per step by every core, after the pressure projection (so sim holds the new state), and never on an RK substage. The default is a no-op (a stateless closure). When step_adaptive! rejects a step, the commit made inside it is undone through sgs_state / restore_sgs_state!.

advance_sgs!(c::LagrangianDynamicSmagorinsky, sim, dt)

Commit the Lagrangian memories over an accepted step of length dt: the Germano identity at the new state (the shared kernel of the dynamic procedure; the velocity halos are filled first), then one relaxation step along the pathlines (MLC96 eqs. 3.1–3.3), and the new cell-centred velocity is kept for the next departure point. The first commit initialises the memories ($\mathcal I_{LM} = C_0^2 \mathcal I_{MM}$, $\mathcal I_{MM} = M_{ij}M_{ij}$).

Aeolus.Operators.sgs_state — Function
sgs_state(c::TKEClosure) / restore_sgs_state!(c, s)

Snapshot of the memory ($e$ and the booked statistics) into buffers the closure owns, and its restoration: a step step_adaptive! rejects leaves $e$ bit for bit as it was. The snapshot (e, stats) is self-contained (restoring reads nothing but s), so a deep copy of it is what a checkpoint stores (#245) and restores into a freshly built closure.

sgs_state(c::ReynoldsStressClosure) / restore_sgs_state!(c, s)

Snapshot of the six stresses and the booked statistics into buffers the closure owns, and its restoration (a rejected adaptive step leaves them bit for bit as they were). The snapshot (τ, stats) is self-contained (restoring reads nothing but s), so a deep copy of it is what a checkpoint stores (#245).

sgs_state(c::AbstractSGSClosure) -> snapshot

A copy of the closure's memory, taken by step_adaptive! before it tries a step. Default nothing (stateless). A stateful closure returns a deep copy (or copies into a buffer it owns) so that restore_sgs_state! can roll a rejected step back bit for bit.

Serialisable (#245): the snapshot must be self-contained — plain arrays, numbers and tuples of them, and restore_sgs_state!(c, s) must read nothing but s — because a checkpoint stores a deep copy of it and restores it into a freshly constructed closure in another process (checkpoint!). Every memory the closure's next step reads (not only what advance_sgs! writes) belongs in it.

Aeolus.Operators.restore_sgs_state! — Function
restore_sgs_state!(c::AbstractSGSClosure, snapshot) -> c

Restore the memory saved by sgs_state (no-op by default).

Aeolus.Operators.add_sgs_momentum! — Function
add_sgs_momentum!(tu, tv, tw, c::ReynoldsStressClosure, f, sim; stress)

$−∂_jτ^{dev}_{ij}$ with Operators.add_stress_divergence! (P55-b) and the edge stresses sgs_coefficients! placed. stress is ignored: the closure's momentum flux is its own stress.

add_sgs_momentum!(tu, tv, tw, c::AbstractSGSClosure, f::SGSFields, sim;
                  stress = :laplacian)

Add the SGS stress divergence to the momentum tendencies. stress is the sgs_stress keyword of the cores:

  • :laplacian (default): the Laplacian eddy-viscosity flux form ∂/∂xⱼ(ν ∂uᵢ/∂xⱼ) of add_eddy_viscosity_u! (and _v!, _w!) with f.νh — the pre-Era-VI operator, bit for bit;
  • :symmetric: the stress form ∂/∂xⱼ(2νᵢⱼSᵢⱼ) of add_sgs_stress_divergence! with νh = f.νh, νv = f.νv (P55-b, #175).

A closure with its own stress (an explicit Reynolds stress) adds a method and may call add_stress_divergence!. Halos of sim.u, sim.v, sim.w and of f are filled.

Aeolus.Operators.add_sgs_scalar! — Function
add_sgs_scalar!(tϕ, ϕ, c::ReynoldsStressClosure, f, sim)

The Daly–Harlow flux divergence $∂_i(c_θ τ_{ij}∂_jϕ)$ with Operators.add_tensor_diffusion_scalar! (P55-c; walls from the scalar boundary conditions).

add_sgs_scalar!(tϕ, ϕ, c::AbstractSGSClosure, f::SGSFields, sim)

Add the SGS scalar flux divergence ∂/∂xⱼ(κⱼₖ ∂ϕ/∂xₖ) to tϕ. Default: the diagonal operator add_eddy_diffusion_scalar!(tϕ, ϕ, f.κh, f.κv) (P55-c, #181), κh on the x and y faces and κv on the z faces; for an isotropic closure (f.κv === f.κh) that is the three-argument form (tϕ, ϕ, f.κh), bit for bit the pre-P55-c operator. A closure with a full diffusivity tensor adds a method that calls add_tensor_diffusion_scalar! (see IsentropicDiffusion). Halos of ϕ and of f are filled.

Aeolus.Operators.sgs_explicit_stress — Function
sgs_explicit_stress(c::AbstractSGSClosure) -> Bool

true for a closure whose momentum tendency is its own stress (an add_sgs_momentum! method that is not an eddy viscosity, e.g. the prognostic Reynolds stress of P56-d, #188). step_imex! treats the SGS momentum implicitly as the eddy viscosity f.νh and has no explicit hook for such a stress, so it refuses these closures instead of silently replacing the stress with ν_t. Default false.

Aeolus.Operators.sgs_tracer_diffusivity! — Function
sgs_tracer_diffusivity!(κ, c::AbstractSGSClosure, f::SGSFields, sim, ϕ) -> Field

The cell-centred SGS diffusivity of a scalar ϕ other than θ (on the Boussinesq core: qv, in the explicit SGS flux of step! / step_rk3! and the implicit diffusion of step_imex!), after compute_sgs! has filled f. Default: f.κh, returned as is and κ untouched, which is right for every closure whose κ_t is a property of the flow alone (κ_t = ν_t/Prₜ). A closure whose κ_t is built from θ's own gradient (the AMD scalar model, AMDClosure) computes ϕ's into the scratch field κ (halos filled with sim.bcs) and returns κ.

Aeolus.Operators.sgs_scalar_face_diffusivity — Function
sgs_scalar_face_diffusivity(c::AbstractSGSClosure, f::SGSFields, sim) -> (Kh, Kv, s) or nothing

The face form of add_sgs_scalar! for the closures whose scalar flux is diagonal: the SGS flux through a face normal to x or y is −s · ½(Kh_L + Kh_R) (ϕ_R − ϕ_L)/Δ, and through a z face the same with Kv (the cell-centred coefficient fields, halos filled, averaged linearly to the face — the kernel of add_eddy_diffusion_scalar!). Default (f.κh, f.κv, 1); an eddy-viscosity closure gives (νt, νt, 1/Prₜ) (its tendency is formed with the factor after the flux divergence, so a flux built from this differs from the tendency's by round-off only). nothing for a closure whose scalar flux is a full tensor (IsentropicDiffusion, the Reynolds-stress closure): it has no two-point face form.

Every closure that adds an add_sgs_scalar! method must add the matching method here. Used by the flux registers of a two-way nest (Nesting, N-nest-e #263), which must book exactly the flux the tendency used.

Aeolus.Operators.compute_sgs! — Function
compute_sgs!(c::AbstractSGSClosure, sim) -> SGSFields

sgs_coefficients! into sgs_fields(c, sim), then the halos of each distinct coefficient field (with sim.bcs). The velocity halos must already be filled. This is the one call every core and the CFL estimate make; like sgs_coefficients!, it changes no closure memory.

Aeolus.Operators.sgs_closure — Function
sgs_closure(x) -> Union{Nothing, AbstractSGSClosure}

Normalise an sgs keyword: a closure is returned as is, nothing stays nothing, and a plain function f(νt, u, v, w) (the legacy eddy_viscosity! hook) is wrapped in a stateless FunctionSGS.

Aeolus.Operators.AbstractEddyViscosityClosure — Type
AbstractEddyViscosityClosure <: AbstractSGSClosure

An isotropic closure that computes an eddy viscosity νt with sgs_viscosity!(νt, c, sim) and takes the scalar diffusivity from a turbulent Prandtl number, κt = νt / Prₜ (Prₜ = turbulent_prandtl(c, sim): the closure's own Pr_t, or the simulation's when that is nothing).

κt is stored in f.κh (so the CFL limit and the implicit IMEX operator see it), but the scalar tendency is formed as (1/Prₜ) ∂/∂xⱼ(νt ∂ϕ/∂xⱼ) by the (tϕ, ϕ, νt, Pr_t) method of add_eddy_diffusion_scalar!: the same kernel, with the factor applied after the flux divergence, which is the order of operations of the pre-contract code. Diffusing with the stored κt instead would differ from it in the last bit (face averages of νt/Prₜ are not bitwise 1/Prₜ times face averages of νt).

sgs_viscosity!(νt, c::AbstractEddyViscosityClosure, sim) -> νt

Aeolus.Operators.sgs_tke! — Function
sgs_tke!(e, c::TKEClosure, sim) -> e

sgs_tke!(e, c::ReynoldsStressClosure, sim) -> e

Half the trace of the transported subgrid stress, $e = \tfrac12τ_{ii}$ (N-inflow-b, #258). See Operators.sgs_tke! for the contract.

sgs_tke!(e::Field{CellCenter}, c, sim; c_k = 0.1) -> e

Fill the interior of e with the closure's subgrid turbulent kinetic energy $e = \tfrac12\overline{u_i'u_i'}^{\,\rm SGS}$ (m² s⁻²) for the current state of sim. Every closure answers through this one generic, so a consumer (the inflow statistics of Transition, N-inflow-b #258) never needs to know which closure the parent runs:

  • a prognostic closure returns its memory: PrognosticSGS.TKEClosure its $e$, PrognosticSGS.ReynoldsStressClosure $\tfrac12τ_{ii}$;
  • every other closure (the default method, and c = nothing for a simulation's built-in Smagorinsky) returns its own estimate: the Deardorff (1980) eddy viscosity $ν_t = c_k\,l\,\sqrt e$ with the neutral mixing length $l = Δ = (ΔxΔyΔz)^{1/3}$ inverted, $e = (ν_t/(c_kΔ))^2$, $c_k = 0.1$ (Moeng 1984). $ν_t$ is the horizontal viscosity the closure last wrote (sgs_fields(c, sim).νh, or sim.νt), so call it after a step (or after sgs_coefficients!). For Smagorinsky, $ν_t = (C_sΔ)^2|S|$ and the estimate is the local equilibrium value $e = (C_s^2Δ|S|/c_k)^2$.

References: Deardorff, J. W. (1980). Stratocumulus-capped mixed layers derived from a three-dimensional model. Boundary-Layer Meteorol., 18, 495-527. Moeng, C.-H. (1984). A large-eddy-simulation model for the study of planetary boundary-layer turbulence. J. Atmos. Sci., 41, 2052-2062.

Aeolus.Operators.turbulent_prandtl — Function
turbulent_prandtl(c::AbstractEddyViscosityClosure, sim) -> Prₜ

Aeolus.Operators.StaticSmagorinsky — Type
StaticSmagorinsky(C_s = 0.17; Pr_t = nothing)

The Smagorinsky–Lilly closure νt = (C_s Δ)² |S| (compute_smagorinsky_νt!), κt = νt/Prₜ; Pr_t = nothing takes the simulation's Pr_t. step!(sim, dt; sgs = StaticSmagorinsky(sim.C_s)) is, bit for bit, the built-in closure a simulation built with C_s ≠ 0 runs. Stateless.

References: Smagorinsky (1963), Mon. Wea. Rev. 91, 99; Lilly (1967), IBM Sci. Comp. Symp.

Aeolus.Operators.DynamicSmagorinsky — Type
DynamicSmagorinsky(workspace::DynamicSmagorinskyWorkspace; averaging = :plane,
                   filter_ratio = 3, cs_max = 0.5, Pr_t = nothing, mask = nothing)
DynamicSmagorinsky(mesh::RegularGrid; kwargs...)

The Germano–Lilly dynamic Smagorinsky closure, compute_dynamic_smagorinsky_νt! with these keywords and the workspace (whose cs2 holds the coefficient of the last call), and κt = νt/Prₜ. Stateless in the sense of the contract: the coefficient is recomputed from the resolved field at every call, so advance_sgs! is a no-op. step!(sim, dt; sgs = DynamicSmagorinsky(ws)) is bit for bit the legacy eddy_viscosity! = (νt, u, v, w) -> compute_dynamic_smagorinsky_νt!(νt, u, v, w; workspace = ws).

averaging is :plane or :local; the Lagrangian average along pathlines is the stateful LagrangianDynamicSmagorinsky. mask (optional, storage-sized cell mask, true = solid, e.g. IBM.SolidMask.mask) makes the procedure obstacle aware (see compute_dynamic_smagorinsky_νt!); its halos are filled from the simulation's boundary conditions on first use (wrapped where periodic).

References: Germano, Piomelli, Moin & Cabot (1991), Phys. Fluids A 3, 1760; Lilly (1992), Phys. Fluids A 4, 633.

Aeolus.Operators.FunctionSGS — Type
FunctionSGS(f)

Stateless adapter for a plain function f(νt, u, v, w) that fills the eddy viscosity (the pre-contract eddy_viscosity! keyword), with κt = νt / sim.Pr_t. A function passed as sgs (or as the legacy eddy_viscosity!) is wrapped in one automatically, so existing user code keeps working unchanged.

Aeolus.Operators._velocity_gradient_at — Function
_velocity_gradient_at(u, v, w, i, j, k, idx, idy, idz) -> g

The full resolved velocity-gradient tensor at the centre of cell (i, j, k) (indices into the halo-padded data arrays of the staggered u @ FaceX, v @ FaceY, w @ FaceZ), with idx = 1/Δx etc. Returns the 3 × 3 tensor as a tuple of rows, g[a][b] = ∂u_a/∂x_b:

  • diagonal: the face-to-face difference through the cell (∂u/∂x = (u[i+1] − u[i])/Δx), exact for a linear field and the stencil of the discrete divergence;

LagrangianDynamicSmagorinsky(mesh; filter_ratio = 3, cs_max = 0.5, C0 = 0.16,
                             θ = 1.5, memory_time = nothing, Pr_t = nothing,

The Lagrangian-averaged dynamic Smagorinsky closure of Meneveau, Lund & Cabot (1996): the Germano identity is averaged backwards along fluid pathlines instead of over homogeneous planes, so the coefficient is local and needs no direction of statistical homogeneity (an urban canopy has none). With $L^d_{ij} M_{ij}$ and $M_{ij} M_{ij}$ from the dynamic procedure (compute_dynamic_smagorinsky_νt!: 3 × 3 × 3 box test filter, test-to-grid width ratio filter_ratio), the two memories obey (MLC96 eqs. 2.10–2.13, their time discretisation 3.1–3.3)

\[\mathcal I_{LM}^{n+1}(\mathbf x) = ε\,[L_{ij}M_{ij}]^{n+1}(\mathbf x) + (1-ε)\,\mathcal I_{LM}^{n}(\mathbf x - \mathbf u^n Δt), \qquad \mathcal I_{MM}^{n+1}(\mathbf x) = ε\,[M_{ij}M_{ij}]^{n+1}(\mathbf x) + (1-ε)\,\mathcal I_{MM}^{n}(\mathbf x - \mathbf u^n Δt),\]

\[ε = \frac{Δt/T}{1 + Δt/T}, \qquad T = θ\,Δ\,(\mathcal I_{LM}\mathcal I_{MM})^{-1/8}, \qquad C_s^2 = \frac{\mathcal I_{LM}}{\mathcal I_{MM}},\]

with $θ = 1.5$, $Δ = (Δx Δy Δz)^{1/3}$, the upstream values found by trilinear interpolation (periodic directions wrap; across a non-periodic boundary the departure point is clamped to the boundary cell), and $\mathcal I_{LM}$ clipped at $≥ 0$ after every update. $T$ is evaluated with the upstream memories; where $\mathcal I_{LM}$ has been clipped to 0 it is evaluated with $\mathcal I_{LM} ≥ 10^{-8}\mathcal I_{MM}$ so the point can recover (otherwise $T = ∞$ freezes it), and where $\mathcal I_{MM} = 0$ (no strain upstream) $ε = 1$. memory_time (s) replaces $T$ by a fixed relaxation time (Inf: pure transport). $C_s^2$ is clipped to $[0, c_{s,\max}^2]$ and $ν_t = C_s^2 Δ^2 |S|$, $κ_t = ν_t/\mathrm{Pr}_t$. No damping function is applied anywhere.

Contract (AbstractSGSClosure): the memory advances only in advance_sgs! (once per accepted step, from the state after the step; the departure point uses the cell-centred velocity committed with the previous memory, $\mathbf u^n$), and sgs_coefficients! is pure: it takes $C_s^2$ from the committed memory and $|S|$ from the current state, so the three RK3 substages and the CFL estimate see one coefficient. sgs_state / restore_sgs_state! copy the memory, so a step step_adaptive! rejects is rolled back bit for bit. Initialisation (MLC96 §3): the first commit sets $\mathcal I_{MM} = M_{ij}M_{ij}$ and $\mathcal I_{LM} = C_0^2 \mathcal I_{MM}$ with $C_0 = 0.16$; until then the coefficient is $C_0^2$.

Solids (mask, a storage-sized cell mask, true = solid, e.g. IBM.SolidMask.mask; halos filled from the simulation's boundary conditions on first use): $ν_t = 0$ and the memories are 0 in solid cells; the dynamic procedure reads the velocity with the no-slip value 0 on every face that touches a solid and test-filters over fluid neighbours only (renormalised, the masked box_filter!); in the upstream interpolation a departure point whose nearest cell is solid is replaced by the cell itself, and solid corners of the trilinear stencil are dropped with the remaining weights renormalised. Nothing stored inside an obstacle can reach a fluid coefficient.

Scale dependence (scale_dependent = true; LASD, Bou-Zeid, Meneveau & Parlange 2005; P54-e, #186). The scale-invariant model above (LASI) assumes $C_s$ is the same at the grid and the test-filter scales; near a wall, where the filter widths reach the distance to it, the coefficient falls toward the grid scale and LASI under-predicts it. LASD adds a second test filter (the 5 × 5 × 5 box, width filter_ratio2 $= α_2 = 5$ from its second moment; see _second_test_filter!) and two more memories

\[\mathcal I_{QN}^{n+1} = ε\,[Q^d_{ij}N_{ij}]^{n+1} + (1-ε)\,\mathcal I_{QN}^{n}(\mathbf x - \mathbf u^n Δt), \qquad \mathcal I_{NN}^{n+1} = ε\,[N_{ij}N_{ij}]^{n+1} + (1-ε)\,\mathcal I_{NN}^{n}(\mathbf x - \mathbf u^n Δt),\]

with the same $ε$ and $T$ (from $\mathcal I_{QN}\mathcal I_{NN}$) and the same pathlines. $\mathcal I_{LM}/\mathcal I_{MM}$ and $\mathcal I_{QN}/\mathcal I_{NN}$ are the coefficients at $αΔ$ and $α_2Δ$; a power law between them gives the grid-scale coefficient (_scale_dependent_cs2!): $φ = \ln(C^2_{α_2Δ}/C^2_{αΔ})/\ln(α_2/α)$, $β = 2^φ$ clipped to [beta_min, beta_max] (Bou-Zeid's $β ≥ 0.125$; the upper clip 8 is ours, symmetric in $φ$), $C^2_Δ = C^2_{αΔ} α^{-φ}$. For Bou-Zeid's filters ($α = 2$, $α_2 = 4$) this is their $β = C^2_{4Δ}/C^2_{2Δ}$, $C^2_Δ = C^2_{2Δ}/β$; here $β$ is the coefficient ratio per doubling of the filter width. closure.beta holds the $β$ of the last call. beta (a number) fixes $β$; beta = 1 is LASI bit for bit. The memories initialise with $\mathcal I_{QN} = C_0^2 \mathcal I_{NN}$ ($β = 1$). No wall damping is applied: the near-wall coefficient comes from the scale dependence, the wall stress from the wall model.

Cadence (cadence = N, default 1): the Germano identities (the expensive part) are evaluated every N commits; at the commits between, the memories are only transported along the pathlines ($ε = 0$), and at the next evaluation $ε$ uses the time since the last one. This is the usual practice of Lagrangian dynamic codes; N = 1 is the exact model.

The coefficient of the last call is in closure.workspace.cs2, the memories in closure.I_LM, closure.I_MM (and closure.I_QN, closure.I_NN; interior-sized arrays).

(2005), A scale-dependent Lagrangian dynamic model for large eddy simulation of complex turbulent flows, Phys. Fluids 17, 025105; Porté-Agel, F., Meneveau, C. & Parlange, M. B. Aeolus.Operators._scale_dependent_cs2! — Function

The coefficient of the scale-dependent model (Bou-Zeid, Meneveau & Parlange 2005) from the committed memories: $C^2_{αΔ} = \mathcal I_{LM}/\mathcal I_{MM}$ and $C^2_{α_2Δ} = \mathcal I_{QN}/\mathcal I_{NN}$ are the Lagrangian coefficients at the two test-filter scales; assuming the power law $C^2(aΔ) = C^2(Δ)\,a^φ$ between them,

\[φ = \frac{\ln(C^2_{α_2Δ}/C^2_{αΔ})}{\ln(α_2/α)}, \qquad β = 2^φ \in [β_{\min}, β_{\max}], \qquad C^2_Δ = C^2_{αΔ}\,α^{-φ} = C^2_{αΔ}\,β^{-\log_2 α},\]

Where $C^2_{αΔ} = 0$ the coefficient is 0 and $β$ is stored as 1 (no scale information); where only $C^2_{α_2Δ} = 0$, $β = β_{\min}$. Aeolus.Operators.add_tensor_diffusion_scalar! — Function
add_tensor_diffusion_scalar!(tϕ, ϕ, K::DiffusivityTensor;
                             bounded = (false, false, false), part = :full)

Add the divergence of the down-gradient flux of a symmetric diffusivity tensor, ∂/∂xᵢ(Kᵢⱼ ∂ϕ/∂xⱼ), to the cell-centred scalar tendency tϕ, with the six components of K at cell centres.

Discretisation (triads, Griffies et al. 1998). Each cell is split into eight octants; octant (a, b, c) pairs one x-face, one y-face and one z-face gradient of the cell, g = (gx, gy, gz). The operator is the gradient of the discrete functional

F[ϕ] = −½ Σ_cells (ΔV/8) Σ_octants gᵀ K_cell g,   ΔV ∂ϕₙ/∂t = ∂F/∂ϕₙ,

which in flux form puts on each face the mean of (K g)ₙ over the eight octants that touch it. Three properties follow from the functional and hold to round-off for any K, independently of resolution:

  • conservation: Σ ΔV ∂ϕ/∂t = 0 (periodic or no-flux boundaries);
  • down-gradient: Σ ΔV ϕ ∂ϕ/∂t = 2F ≤ 0 when every cell's K is positive semi-definite (Griffies et al. 1998, Sec. 3) — the scalar variance cannot grow, however strongly K is rotated;
  • the operator is symmetric.

The diagonal part is exactly the face-averaged operator of add_eddy_diffusion_scalar! with K11 on the x faces, K22 on the y faces and K33 on the z faces, so K = diag(κh, κh, κv) reproduces add_eddy_diffusion_scalar!(tϕ, ϕ, κh, κv) to round-off. For a constant K a plane wave cos(k·x) decays at K11 k̂ₓ² + K22 k̂_y² + K33 k̂_z² + 2K12 sₓs_y + 2K13 sₓs_z + 2K23 s_y s_z, k̂ = 2 sin(kΔ/2)/Δ, s = sin(kΔ)/Δ.

bounded[d] marks direction d (x, y, z) as walled (bounded_directions(bcs) derives it from the boundary conditions of ϕ): the wall faces' gradients are left out of the cross terms, and the wall flux keeps only its diagonal part K_nn ∂ϕ/∂n from the halo, as in the diagonal operator (zero for a no-flux halo, with which the three properties hold with walls). Prescribed surface fluxes are added separately (add_surface_flux!).

part selects :full (default), :diagonal (the K11, K22, K33 terms only) or :offdiagonal (the cross terms only); :diagonal + :offdiagonal = :full. The IMEX cores treat the diagonal implicitly and the off-diagonal part explicitly (see the theory page Smagorinsky–Lilly SGS, section "Tensorial scalar diffusivity").

Halos of ϕ and of the components of K must be filled (a periodic direction needs K in the first halo cell; corners included, which fill_halos! provides).

References: Redi (1982), J. Phys. Oceanogr. 12, 1154; Griffies, Gnanadesikan, Pacanowski, Larichev, Dukowicz & Smith (1998), J. Phys. Oceanogr. 28, 805.

Aeolus.Operators.isentropic_tensor! — Function
isentropic_tensor!(K::DiffusivityTensor, θ, κh, κv; slope_max = 1.0,
                   grad_min = 1e-6, bcs = nothing) -> K

The rotated (isentropic, Redi 1982) diffusivity tensor

K = κh (I − n nᵀ) + κv n nᵀ,   n = ∇θ / |∇θ|,

κh along the isentropes and κv across them, written as K = κh I − f (κh − κv) n nᵀ with the taper factor f ∈ [0, 1] that blends it toward the isotropic κh I:

  • small-slope taper (the (S_max/S)² slope clipping of Gerdes, Köberle & Willebrand 1991; Danabasoglu & McWilliams 1995 discuss tapering the rotated tensor at steep slopes): with the isentropic slope S = |∇ₕθ| / ∂θ/∂z, f = 1 for S ≤ slope_max and f = (slope_max/S)² above it;
  • isotropic fallback: f = 0, K = κh I, where N² ∝ ∂θ/∂z ≤ 0 (n is not a stable-stratification direction; this is the limit of the taper as S → ∞, so K is continuous across N² = 0), and f ramps linearly to 0 as |∇θ| → 0 below grad_min (K m⁻¹, a numerical floor under which the direction of ∇θ is noise).

∇θ is the centred gradient at the cell centre, so the halos of θ must be filled. κh, κv are Field{CellCenter}s or numbers (m² s⁻¹; κv = 0 is the pure isentropic operator). The interior of K is set; with bcs the halos are then filled with it. Every cell's K has the eigenvalues κh, κh, κh − f (κh − κv), so it is positive semi-definite for κh, κv ≥ 0 and add_tensor_diffusion_scalar! dissipates variance.

Applied to θ itself with κv = 0 the operator gives zero tendency where ∇θ is uniform (the discrete triad gradients are then the cell's ∇θ, which K annihilates): diffusion along the isentropes does not move them.

For n = ẑ (horizontally uniform θ) K = diag(κh, κh, κv), the diagonal operator. The flagship's daytime convective boundary layer has N² ≤ 0 over most of the domain, where this is the isotropic κh.

References: Redi (1982), J. Phys. Oceanogr. 12, 1154; Gerdes, Köberle & Willebrand (1991), Clim. Dyn. 5, 211; Danabasoglu & McWilliams (1995), J. Climate 8, 2967; Griffies et al. (1998), J. Phys. Oceanogr. 28, 805.

Aeolus.Operators.StabilityCorrected — Type
StabilityCorrected(base::AbstractEddyViscosityClosure, mesh;
                   scheme = BrownEtAl1994(), length_scales = :anisotropic,
                   qv = nothing, qc = nothing, p = nothing, S2_min = RI_SHEAR2_FLOOR)

A closure that corrects the eddy viscosity ν_base of base (StaticSmagorinsky, DynamicSmagorinsky, FunctionSGS, ML.MLSGS, or any other AbstractEddyViscosityClosure) for the local stratification and splits it into horizontal and vertical coefficients:

\[ν_h = ν_{base}\,ℓ_h, \qquad ν_v = ν_{base}\,ℓ_v\,f_m(Ri), \qquad κ_h = \frac{ν_{base}\,ℓ_h}{Pr_t}, \qquad κ_v = \frac{ν_{base}\,ℓ_v\,f_h(Ri)}{Pr_t}.\]

  • Ri. The gradient Richardson number at the cell centre, gradient_richardson!(Ri, N², u, v, w; S2_min), with $|S|^2 = 2S_{ij}S_{ij}$ (the strain the Smagorinsky closure sees) and $N^2$ from buoyancy_frequency_squared!: of θ, or of the virtual potential temperature when a humidity is known (qv, a Field{CellCenter} such as a tracer, or the core's own sim.qv when it carries moisture). With a condensate field qc (cloud or fog water, Field{CellCenter}) and the pressure p (Pa; a number or a storage-indexed vector) $N^2$ is that of cloudy air (#239): condensate loading in unsaturated cells, the saturated $N^2$ of Durran & Klemp (1982) where $q_c > 0$, so that moist-neutral fog or cloud is not damped as if it were stable. The reference is sim.θ₀ on the Boussinesq core and the profile θ̄(z) (sim.ref) on the anelastic / pseudo-incompressible cores, the reference their buoyancy uses.
  • fm, fh are the stability factors of the TurbulenceStability contract, stability_factor_m(scheme, Ri) and stability_factor_h(scheme, Ri) (P58-a, #170): both 1 at Ri = 0, so a neutral flow is not changed. scheme is any AbstractRiStability: BrownEtAl1994() (default; the LEM / MONC functions, zero above $Ri_c = 1/4$, enhanced mixing for $Ri < 0$), LillyCutoff() (Lilly 1962; Mason 1989), LongTail(), ShortTail(), SurfaceExchange.MOSTConsistent(…). The neutral turbulent Prandtl number is base's (turbulent_prandtl), not neutral_prandtl(scheme): the factors are normalised to 1 at Ri = 0, so the base closure keeps the one neutral value of the run.
  • Length scales. length_scales = :anisotropic (default) uses the horizontal filter width Δh = √(dx dy) for the horizontal and Δv = dz for the vertical coefficients instead of Δ = (dx dy dz)^{1/3} (Bryan & Fritsch 2002; CM1), through the factors anisotropic_length_factors (exact for ν ∝ Δ², true of every Smagorinsky-type closure). :isotropic keeps ℓh = ℓv = 1. On cubic cells the two are identical.
  • Dynamic bases (dynamic_sgs_coefficient): no f_m multiplier on the momentum, ν_v = ν_{base}ℓ_v (the dynamic coefficient already responds to the stratification; a second factor would double count it). Only the scalar is corrected, through $Pr_t(Ri) = Pr_t f_m/f_h$: $κ_v = ν_{base}ℓ_v (f_h/f_m)/Pr_t$, and $κ_v = 0$ where $f_m = 0$ (beyond a critical Ri).

Not for every closure. A closure with its own buoyancy term (the anisotropic minimum-dissipation model, P55-d) or its own length scale (a prognostic-TKE closure, P56-b) already accounts for the stratification and is not wrapped; the constructor takes AbstractEddyViscosityClosures only.

νh and κh are stored in sim.νt, sim.κt (the fields the diagnostics and the CFL estimate read), νv and κv in the closure's own fields c.νv, c.κv; c.N2 and c.Ri hold the last N² and Ri_g. The scalar tendency is the diagonal operator (1/Prₜ) ∂ⱼ(aⱼ ∂ⱼϕ) with a = ν_base ℓh on the x, y faces and ν_base ℓv f_h on the z faces (the base closure's order of operations, so that with N² = 0 and ℓh = ℓv = 1 the closure is base bit for bit: coefficients, momentum and scalar tendencies, and every core's step). Under step_imex! the vertical coefficients are part of the implicit operator. base's memory and commit cadence are untouched (advance_sgs!, sgs_state, restore_sgs_state! forward to it).

It composes with IsentropicDiffusion: IsentropicDiffusion(StabilityCorrected(base, mesh), mesh) diffuses θ with the rotated tensor, κh along the isentropes and this closure's κv across them.

Aeolus.Operators.sigma_singular_values — Function

Aeolus.Operators._sym3_eigvals — Function
_sym3_eigvals(G11, G22, G33, G12, G13, G23) -> (λ₁, λ₂, λ₃)

Eigenvalues λ₁ ≥ λ₂ ≥ λ₃ of the real symmetric 3 × 3 matrix with the given entries, in closed form: no LAPACK call.

  1. The trigonometric solution of the characteristic cubic (Smith 1961; the form for the σ-model in Hasan, Basri & Kopp 2010 and Nicoud et al. 2011, appendix). It is written in the deviator G' = G − (tr G/3) I, so that α₁ = tr(G'²)/6 and α₂ = det(G')/2 are formed without cancellation and G ∝ I gives exactly α₁ = 0 (three equal eigenvalues).
  2. One deflation step where the smaller gap is below 1 % of the spread λ₁ − λ₃. Near a double eigenvalue the cubic determines the gap between the pair only to √ε (the arccos argument is near ±1), far too coarse for D_σ, a product of such gaps; above 1 % the gap error is ≲ ε/(gap/spread)² ≤ 1e-12 relative. The simple eigenvalue (the one further from the other two) is well conditioned, so its eigenvector is taken as the largest cross product of two rows of G − λI, and the pair is recomputed from the 2 × 2 compression of G on the orthogonal complement, μ± = (p + r)/2 ± ½√((p − r)² + 4q²), whose gap is accurate to ε‖G‖. The simple eigenvalue is replaced by its Rayleigh quotient.

The theory is developed in Transition and wall-bounded flow. The no-slip / moving-wall condition NoSlipWall lives in BoundaryConditions (API, together with is_wall and homogeneous_bcs). Its plane-mean wall stress is a method of the cut-cell generic wall_shear_stress:

Aeolus.CutCell.wall_shear_stress — Method

The gradient is the one the discrete viscous operator uses: the halos are filled with sim.bcs, and the one-sided difference across the wall face is (u₁ − u_ghost)/Δz. d/dt ∫ ū dz = τ(top) − τ(bottom) + ∫ forcing dz .

Reference: Pope (2000), Turbulent Flows, §7.1.

wall_shear_stress(u, v, w, stencils, geom, ν) -> Vector

Wall shear stress τ_w = ν |∂u_t/∂n| (m²/s²) at every stencil point, evaluated on the true normal rather than on a Cartesian face.

For each stencil the cell-centered velocity is interpolated to the image point, its wall-normal component removed, and the remaining tangential speed divided by the probe distance:

τ_w = ν · |u_t(ximg)| / probe .

The returned vector is indexed like stencils, so it pairs with geom.area[s.i,s.j,s.k] (the wetted area of that cut cell) to integrate a drag force, and with stencils[m].n to give the stress its direction.

stencils must be built at CellCenter(). The cell-centered velocity is obtained by aperture-weighted averaging of the two bounding faces, which is the consistent reconstruction on a cut cell (a blocked face contributes neither value nor weight).

This is the resolved (ν-linear) stress. The modelled stress at the same probe point is the method wall_shear_stress(u, v, w, stencils, geom, law; ν) of Aeolus.WallModels (Phase 57, #179), for any WallModels.AbstractWallLaw (Spalding, rough log law, MOST); with law = nothing it is this ν-linear stress. WallModels.wall_slip_velocity turns the modelled stress into the slip wall_value of reconstruct_wall_velocity!.

wall_shear_stress(u, v, w, stencils, geom, law::AbstractWallLaw; ν) -> Vector

The modelled wall shear stress (kinematic, m² s⁻²) at every CellCenter ghost-cell stencil of a cut-cell geometry: the method of CutCell.wall_shear_stress for a wall law. As the ν-linear method, the cell-centred velocity is interpolated to the stencil's image point and its wall-normal component removed; the tangential speed $u_t$ there, at the image-point probe distance $h$ = s.probe from the true surface, gives

\[τ_w = u_τ^2, \qquad u_τ = \texttt{friction\_velocity}(law, u_t, h; ν).\]

This is the immersed-boundary wall model of Tessicini et al. (2002): the law is applied at a probe point off the true surface along its normal, not at a Cartesian face.

Aeolus.Operators.phi_m_profile — Function
phi_m_profile(u::Field{FaceX}, v::Field{FaceY}, u_star; κ) -> (z, Φ)
phi_m_profile(U::AbstractVector, dz, u_star; κ)              -> (z, Φ)

The non-dimensional mean shear of the surface layer,

\[Φ_m(z) = \frac{κ z}{u_*}\,\frac{∂⟨U⟩}{∂z},\]

in the staggered-consistent form: the plane-mean wind speed $⟨U⟩_k = |(⟨u⟩_k, ⟨v⟩_k)|$ lives at the cell centres $z_k = (k - ½)Δz$, so its derivative is the centred difference $(⟨U⟩_{k+1} - ⟨U⟩_k)/Δz$ at the w-face between them, $z = kΔz$ ($k = 1, …, N_z - 1$), which is where Φm is returned. No interpolation of the derivative is involved, so for the log law ``⟨U⟩ = (u*/κ)\ln(z/z_0)`` the result is

\[Φ_m(z) = \frac{z}{Δz}\ln\frac{z + Δz/2}{z - Δz/2} = 1 + \frac{1}{12}\Big(\frac{Δz}{z}\Big)^2 + O\Big(\frac{Δz}{z}\Big)^4,\]

independent of $z_0$: 1 to second order, $\ln 3 = 1.0986$ at the first face. Heights are above the bottom of the mesh (mesh.z0). In the neutral surface layer Φm = 1; Porté-Agel, Meneveau & Parlange (2000, Fig. 7) and Mason & Thomson (1992) show the Smagorinsky model's overshoot to Φm ≈ 1.5-2 near the wall.

κ (the von Kármán constant) has no default here: the Era V contract SurfaceExchange.von_karman() owns it, and the case builders of WallModels pass it.

References

  • Porté-Agel, F., Meneveau, C. & Parlange, M. B. (2000). A scale-dependent dynamic model for large-eddy simulation: application to a neutral atmospheric boundary layer. J. Fluid Mech., 415, 261-284.
  • Mason, P. J. & Thomson, D. J. (1992). Stochastic backscatter in large-eddy simulations of boundary layers. J. Fluid Mech., 242, 51-78.
Aeolus.Operators.total_stress_profile — Function
total_stress_profile(u, v, w, νt; ν = 0, τ_wall_bottom = 0,
                     τ_wall_top = 0, component = :x, irange = nothing,
                     jrange = nothing)
    -> (z, resolved, sgs, viscous, wall, total)

The plane-mean vertical flux of horizontal momentum (kinematic, m² s⁻²), counted positive downward (the wall-ward flux of a flow in +x), at the Nz + 1 w-faces $z = (k-1)Δz$, $k = 1, …, N_z + 1$:

\[τ(z) = \underbrace{-⟨u\,w⟩}_{\text{resolved}} + \underbrace{⟨ν_t\,∂u/∂z⟩}_{\text{SGS}} + \underbrace{ν\,∂⟨u⟩/∂z}_{\text{viscous}} + τ_{\text{wall}} .\]

Each term is formed with the stencil the solver integrates, so the discrete momentum budget of a horizontally periodic run closes exactly: the resolved flux is the centred flux-form product of momentum_advect! ($w$ averaged in x to the x-z edge, $u$ averaged in z), and the SGS flux is the z-flux of add_eddy_viscosity_u! ($ν_t$ averaged over the four cells around the edge), i.e. the default eddy-viscosity stress of the SGS contract (add_sgs_momentum!). Pass a closure's vertical viscosity (f.νv) as νt for an anisotropic closure; a closure that overrides add_sgs_momentum! with another stress needs its own term. The halos of u, v, w, νt must be filled (with the velocity boundary conditions).

At a free-slip (ImpermeableWall) z-wall the resolved, SGS and viscous fluxes are zero, and the modelled wall stress (from WallModels) is the whole flux: τ_wall_bottom is added at $k = 1$ and -τ_wall_top at $k = N_z + 1$ (momentum leaves through the top wall upward). With component = :y the same is returned for $v$ (on y-z edges).

For the pressure-driven half channel of height $H$ and forcing $u_*^2/H$ in statistically steady state the total is $u_*^2(1 - z/H)$ (Porté-Agel et al. 2000, Fig. 3); for the two-wall channel it is $u_τ^2(1 - z/δ)$.

irange (default: all of 1:Nx) restricts the mean to the columns i ∈ irange (the x-faces / y-z edges of those columns), for the streamwise-developing flow of an inflow–outflow domain, which is homogeneous only in y (N-inflow-d, #266: the total stress a few boundary-layer depths behind a synthetic inflow). The budget statement above is then local to the x band. jrange likewise restricts it to the rows j ∈ jrange (the centre of a domain with open lateral faces).

References

  • Porté-Agel, F., Meneveau, C. & Parlange, M. B. (2000). J. Fluid Mech., 415, 261-284.
  • Bou-Zeid, E., Meneveau, C. & Parlange, M. B. (2005). A scale-dependent Lagrangian dynamic model for large eddy simulation of complex turbulent flows. Phys. Fluids, 17, 025105.
Aeolus.Operators.streamwise_spectrum — Function
streamwise_spectrum(u::Field, levels; remove_mean = true) -> (k1, E11)

The one-sided streamwise (x) energy spectrum of the field u (normally sim.u) at the levels levels (indices k of the interior), averaged over the Ny lines of each level:

\[E_{11}(k_{1,n}) = \frac{c_n\,L_x}{2π\,N_x^2}\,\big⟨|\hat u_n|^2\big⟩_y, \qquad k_{1,n} = \frac{2π n}{L_x},\; n = 0, …, N_x/2,\]

with $\hat u_n = \sum_i u_i e^{-2π\mathrm{i}\,n i/N_x}$ (FFTW's rfft), $c_n = 2$ for the modes that stand for a ± pair and $c_n = 1$ for $n = 0$ and the Nyquist mode of an even $N_x$. The normalisation is Parseval's theorem: $\sum_n E_{11}(k_{1,n})\,Δk_1 = ⟨u'^2⟩$ (the plane variance; $Δk_1 = 2π/L_x$), exactly up to round-off. With remove_mean = true the line mean is subtracted first ($E_{11}(0) = 0$); with false, the sum is $⟨u^2⟩$. Returns k1 (length $N_x/2 + 1$) and E11, a matrix with one column per level.

Used for the inertial-range $k_1^{-5/3}$ check of the neutral-ABL runs (Porté-Agel et al. 2000, Fig. 9; Bou-Zeid et al. 2005, Fig. 6).

Aeolus.Operators.resolved_tke_profile — Function
resolved_tke_profile(u::Field{FaceX}, v::Field{FaceY}, w::Field{FaceZ}) -> e

The plane-averaged resolved turbulent kinetic energy per level,

\[e_k = \tfrac12\left(⟨u'^2⟩_k + ⟨v'^2⟩_k + ⟨w'^2⟩_k\right), \qquad ϕ' = ϕ - ⟨ϕ⟩_k ,\]

(m² s⁻²), the fluctuations taken about the plane mean of each staggered component on its own level: u, v at the cell-centre height of level k, w on the bottom face of cell k (zero on a wall face). Mean flow and its shear do not count, so a laminar parallel flow has $e = 0$ to round-off. The volume mean of a uniform grid is sum(e)/Nz.

Used by the turbulence-collapse runs of P58-e (#193): the resolved TKE of a flux-cooled half channel relative to the neutral run (Donda et al. 2015, Fig. 3).

Anisotropic minimum-dissipation and Vreman closures (P55-d)

Two closures on the contract that weight each direction with its own grid spacing and vanish in laminar flow (src/operators/sgs_amd_vreman.jl, #176). The theory, the constants and the measured numbers are in Smagorinsky-Lilly SGS → AMD and Vreman.

step!(sim, dt; sgs = VremanClosure(0.17))     # c = 2.5 C_s², κ_t = ν_t/Pr_t
Aeolus.Operators.AMDClosure — Type
AMDClosure(; C = 0.3, Pr_t = nothing, buoyancy = true)

The anisotropic minimum-dissipation closure (compute_amd_coefficients!) as an AbstractSGSClosure: ν_t and its own κ_t (not ν_t/Pr_t; Pr_t is only the fallback where the resolved θ gradient vanishes, nothing → the simulation's Pr_t). With buoyancy = true the buoyancy parameter is the core's: g/θ₀ on the Boussinesq core (0 when it was built with θ₀ = 0), g/θ̄(z) of the reference state on the anelastic / pseudo-incompressible cores (0 with buoyancy = false). The θ gradient is that of the simulation's θ (dry: moisture does not enter the buoyancy term), and the stored κ_t is θ's. Another scalar gets its own AMD diffusivity from its own gradient (sgs_tracer_diffusivity! → compute_amd_scalar_diffusivity!); that is how qv is diffused by step!, step_rk3! and step_imex!. Stateless. Do not stack a Richardson-number stability correction on it: the buoyancy term is its stratification response.

References: Rozema et al. (2015), Abkar, Bae & Moin (2016), Abkar & Moin (2017); see compute_amd_coefficients!.

Aeolus.Operators.VremanClosure — Type
VremanClosure(C_s = 0.17; Pr_t = nothing)

The Vreman (2004) closure (compute_vreman_νt!, c = 2.5 C_s²) as an AbstractEddyViscosityClosure: κ_t = ν_t/Pr_t (nothing → the simulation's Pr_t). Stateless.

Aeolus.Operators.compute_amd_coefficients! — Function
compute_amd_coefficients!(νt, κt, u, v, w, θ; C = 0.3, Pr_t = 1/3,
                          buoyancy = 0.0) -> (νt, κt)

Eddy viscosity and diffusivity of the anisotropic minimum-dissipation (AMD) model of Rozema, Bae, Moin & Verstappen (2015), with the scalar diffusivity of Abkar, Bae & Moin (2016) and the buoyancy term of Abkar & Moin (2017), on the interior of the cell-centred fields νt, κt:

\[ν_t = C\,\frac{\max\!\left(0,\; -(δ_k ∂_k u_i)(δ_k ∂_k u_j) S_{ij} + \frac{g}{θ_0}(δ_k ∂_k w)(δ_k ∂_k θ)\right)}{∂_l u_m\, ∂_l u_m}, \qquad κ_t = C\,\frac{\max\!\left(0,\; -(δ_k ∂_k u_i)(δ_k ∂_k θ)\, ∂_i θ\right)}{∂_l θ\, ∂_l θ},\]

with δ_k = (Δx, Δy, Δz) (summation over the repeated k inside each product) and S_ij the resolved strain rate. Where ∂_l θ ∂_l θ = 0 (no resolved θ gradient) κ_t = ν_t/Pr_t; where ∂_l u_m ∂_l u_m = 0, ν_t = 0.

  • C — the modified Poincaré constant; 0.3 for a second-order central discretisation (Rozema et al. 2015, their decaying-turbulence tests; 1/12 for a spectral method, 0.212 for fourth order), the value of this code's stencil.
  • buoyancy — g/θ₀ [m s⁻² K⁻¹], a number or a function k -> g/θ̄(z_k) of the interior level; 0 switches the buoyancy term off. The term is the gradient-model estimate of the subfilter buoyancy flux: in stable stratification it lowers ν_t and switches it off where it cancels the shear production, which is AMD's own stratification response (no separate Richardson-number correction is applied).

The velocity gradient is _velocity_gradient_at, the stencil of every Era VI closure; the θ gradient is the centred difference over two cells. Halos of u, v, w, θ must be filled.

References: Rozema, Bae, Moin & Verstappen (2015), Phys. Fluids 27, 085107; Abkar, Bae & Moin (2016), Phys. Rev. Fluids 1, 041701(R); Abkar & Moin (2017), Boundary-Layer Meteorol. 165, 405 (eq. 10–11 as restated by Gadde, Stieren & Stevens 2021, Boundary-Layer Meteorol. 178, 363).

Aeolus.Operators.compute_amd_scalar_diffusivity! — Function
compute_amd_scalar_diffusivity!(κt, νt, u, v, w, ϕ; C = 0.3, Pr_t = 1/3) -> κt

The AMD eddy diffusivity of a scalar ϕ (Abkar, Bae & Moin 2016) on the interior of κt: the κ_t of compute_amd_coefficients! with ϕ in place of θ,

\[κ_t = C\,\frac{\max\!\left(0,\; -(δ_k ∂_k u_i)(δ_k ∂_k ϕ)\, ∂_i ϕ\right)}{∂_l ϕ\, ∂_l ϕ},\]

and κ_t = ν_t/Pr_t (from the interior of νt) where ϕ has no resolved gradient. Each scalar gets its own diffusivity: the model bounds the subfilter variance of that scalar, so qv is not diffused with θ's κ_t. κ_t is unchanged under ϕ → aϕ + b (a ≠ 0). Halos of u, v, w, ϕ must be filled.

Aeolus.Operators.compute_vreman_νt! — Function
compute_vreman_νt!(νt, u, v, w, C_s = 0.17) -> νt

The eddy viscosity of Vreman (2004) on the interior of νt:

\[ν_t = c\,\sqrt{\frac{B_β}{α_{ij} α_{ij}}}, \qquad α_{ij} = ∂_i u_j,\quad β_{ij} = Δ_m^2 α_{mi} α_{mj},\quad B_β = β_{11}β_{22} - β_{12}^2 + β_{11}β_{33} - β_{13}^2 + β_{22}β_{33} - β_{23}^2,\]

c = 2.5 C_s² (Vreman's eq. 9 relation to the Smagorinsky constant; C_s = 0.17 gives c = 0.0723), Δ_m = (Δx, Δy, Δz); ν_t = 0 where α_ij α_ij = 0. B_β vanishes for every flow whose velocity varies in one direction only, e.g. the shear flow u(z), v(z) (the laminar switch-off); it is evaluated in a Cauchy–Binet form that makes this zero exact. The gradient is _velocity_gradient_at. Halos of u, v, w must be filled.

Reference: Vreman, A. W. (2004), An eddy-viscosity subgrid-scale model for turbulent shear flow: algebraic theory and applications, Phys. Fluids 16, 3670.
