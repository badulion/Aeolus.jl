Wall models — WallModels

Aeolus.WallModels (Phase 57, #169) owns every modelled wall stress of Era VI. Theory: Wall models.

  • Laws: AbstractWallLaw with friction_velocity, wall_stress (kinematic, m² s⁻²) and wall_drag ($τ_w/U$); SpaldingLaw (smooth wall; LEE_MOSER_2015 constants), RoughLogLaw and MOSTWallLaw (κ and $ψ_m$ delegated to SurfaceExchange), and matching_obukhov_length.

  • Operator: apply_wall_stress! on the z-walls of a RegularGrid, implicit, :local / :schumann / :moeng, with Bool or level masks. A post_predictor! hook; the Berlin ground drag is this operator.

  • Immersed walls (P57-d, #179): immersed_walls (the tangential walls of an IBM solid mask), remove_stencil_wall_flux! (a body_force! hook: the stencil's own facade flux out) and apply_immersed_wall_stress! (a post_predictor! hook: the modelled stress, implicit, corners summed). The Berlin facade drag is this. immersed_wall_drag! is the one evaluation of the law per (face, wall) pair (the drag rate $a$); the TKE closure books $a q^2$ as the facade shear production (#232, attach_immersed_walls!).

  • Cut-cell walls (P57-d, #179): the method wall_shear_stress(u, v, w, stencils, geom, law; ν) of CutCell.wall_shear_stress (modelled stress at the image-point probe), wall_slip_velocity and slip_wall_value (the slip wall value of reconstruct_wall_velocity!).

  • Benchmark cases (P54-d, #174): half_channel_case (neutral ABL half channel) and channel_case (two-wall channel at a given Reτ), a WallBoundedCase advanced by `advancechannel!/runchannel!` (time-averaged U, Φm, stress budget, ⟨τw⟩), with the step size of `channeldt`. Closure, wall law, formulation and stepper are keyword arguments; the diagnostics are Operators' (see Era VI).

  • Wall-model input (P57-b, #183): MatchingInput (matching height h_wm / h_wm_cells, exponential time filter T_f, 3 × 3 wall-parallel filter), matching_input! (the wind / height of apply_wall_stress!), update_input_filter!, matching_distance, exponential_filter_weight, wall_parallel_filter!; the case builders take h_wm, h_wm_cells, filter, T_f. Theory: Wall models → Log-layer mismatch.

  • Non-equilibrium wall model (P57-c, #189): AbstractWallModel (WallModel and TBLEWallModel share the operator's implicit face loop); TBLEWallModel (the thin-boundary-layer equations under every wall column, terms = :equilibrium_ode | :pressure_gradient | :full, dynamic $κ_{wm}$ of Wang & Moin 2002) with update_wall_model!, tble_kappa, tble_columns; the column solver TBLEColumn, tble_solve!, tble_grid, tble_column_momentum; the eddy-viscosity generic AbstractTBLEEddyViscosity / tble_eddy_viscosity with DampedMixingLength and NoEddyViscosity. channel_case(wall_model = :nonequilibrium, tble = (...)). Theory: Wall models → Non-equilibrium wall model.

  • Dynamic roughness (P57-e, #190): SubfilterHeight / subfilter_height (sub-filter height statistics at the grid and test-filter scale), DynamicRoughness ($z_0 = \sqrt{z_{0,f}^2 + α^2σ_Δ^2}$, α from total-drag invariance, Anderson & Meneveau 2011), dynamic_alpha, update_dynamic_roughness!, roughness_drag_residual, roughness_surface_drag, surface_gradient_drag, and the parameter provider with_roughness / dynamic_wall_laws(!) for RoughLogLaw and MOSTWallLaw; half_channel_case(; roughness). Theory: Wall models → Dynamic roughness.

  • Wall-resolved, stratified channel (P55-f, #191): channel_case(bottom_bc = :noslip, θ_walls = (θ_b, θ_t)) with stratified_channel_walls(Ri_τ) and channel_richardson(case); the validation against the DNS of García-Villalba & del Álamo (2011) is in Stable boundary layers.

  • Flux-cooled half channel (P58-e, #193): half_channel_case(surface_heat_flux = Q) with MOSTFluxWallLaw, the flux-prescribed Monin–Obukhov wall law that exerts no stress beyond the maximum sustainable heat flux (SurfaceExchange.max_sustainable_heat_flux; theory: Maximum sustainable heat flux).

  • Flux-cooled open channel (P59-e, #195): channel_case(bottom_bc = :noslip, walls = :bottom, extent = (…, …, δ), surface_heat_flux = Q), the resolved stable channel of Nieuwstadt (2005); the relaminarisation study is in Transition → Relaminarisation.

Aeolus.WallModels — Module
WallModels

Era VI, Phase 57: the modelled wall stress of wall-modelled LES. Owner of every modelled wall stress in the code base: the channel harness, the stable-boundary-layer cases, the TKE surface value and the Berlin ground drag all call this module rather than writing their own.

The parts, each in its own file under src/wall_models/ so the later Phase 57 issues (matching height and filtering, TBLE, facades, dynamic roughness) only add files and include lines:

  1. Laws (laws.jl) — AbstractWallLaw and the generics friction_velocity, wall_stress and wall_drag, with three equilibrium laws: SpaldingLaw (smooth wall, viscous sublayer to log layer in one formula), RoughLogLaw (fully rough, neutral) and MOSTWallLaw (rough, Monin–Obukhov stability correction). κ of the rough laws and the stability functions are delegated to the Era V contract SurfaceExchange; nothing is restated.
  2. The operator (operator.jl) — apply_wall_stress!, which applies the stress of a law to the first velocity level next to the z-walls of a RegularGrid, implicitly (stable for any Δt), in the local (Schumann 1975 / Grötzbach 1987), plane-mean (Schumann 1975) or Moeng (1984) formulation. It is a post_predictor! hook; halos and the finite-volume operators are untouched, so the wall model is the only momentum flux through a free-slip (ImpermeableWall) wall face.
  3. The wall-model input (matching.jl, P57-b #183) — MatchingInput: the velocity the law is evaluated with, sampled at a matching height off the first cell (Kawai & Larsson
    1. and filtered in time (Yang, Park & Moin 2017) and along the
    wall (Bou-Zeid, Meneveau & Parlange 2005), against log-layer mismatch.
  4. The non-equilibrium model (tble.jl, P57-c #189) — TBLEWallModel: the thin-boundary-layer equations on an embedded wall-normal grid under every wall column (Balaras, Benocci & Piomelli 1996; Wang & Moin 2002; Kawai & Larsson 2012; Park & Moin 2014), its upper boundary condition the MatchingInput velocity, with the dynamic $κ_{wm}$ of Wang & Moin; an AbstractWallModel applied by the same face loop as the equilibrium WallModel.
  5. Immersed walls (immersed.jl, P57-d) — immersed_walls, remove_stencil_wall_flux! and apply_immersed_wall_stress!: the stress of a law on the tangential walls of an IBM solid mask (Tessicini et al. 2002; Chester, Meneveau & Parlange 2007), the stencil's own wall flux removed so the two are not counted twice. The Berlin facades.
  6. Cut-cell walls (cutcell.jl, P57-d) — the method wall_shear_stress(u, v, w, stencils, geom, law; ν) of CutCell.wall_shear_stress (the law at the image-point probe) and wall_slip_velocity / slip_wall_value, the slip wall value of CutCell.reconstruct_wall_velocity!.
  7. Dynamic roughness (dynamic_roughness.jl, P57-e #190) — DynamicRoughness: the roughness length of partially resolved relief, $z_0 = \sqrt{z_{0,f}^2 + α^2σ_Δ^2}$ with α from the invariance of the total surface drag between the grid and the test filter (Anderson & Meneveau 2011), as a parameter provider for RoughLogLaw / MOSTWallLaw (with_roughness).
  8. The benchmark cases (channel_cases.jl, P54-d #174) — half_channel_case (the neutral ABL half channel of Porté-Agel et al. 2000 / Bou-Zeid et al. 2005) and channel_case (the two-wall channel at a given Reτ), with [`advancechannel!](@ref) and [runchannel!`](@ref): the shared wall-bounded harness every Era VI closure is validated on. They add no wall physics; the diagnostics they report (Φm, the stress budget, E11) are Operators.phi_m_profile, total_stress_profile and streamwise_spectrum.

Aeolus.WallModels.AbstractTBLEEddyViscosity — Type
AbstractTBLEEddyViscosity

The eddy viscosity $ν_{t,wm}(y)$ of the thin-boundary-layer equations of TBLEWallModel. A concrete model implements

tble_eddy_viscosity(model, y, u_τ, ν, κ, z0) -> ν_t    (m² s⁻¹)

at the distance y (m) from the wall, for the friction velocity u_τ of the wall model's own wall stress, the kinematic viscosity ν, the (possibly dynamic) constant κ and the column's roughness length z0 (m; 0 = smooth). It is a generic so that a later phase (P58, buoyancy) adds a stability-corrected model by a new subtype, without touching the solver.

Aeolus.WallModels.AbstractWallLaw — Type
AbstractWallLaw

An equilibrium wall law: the relation between the wall-parallel speed U (m s⁻¹) at a distance h (m) from the wall and the friction velocity $u_τ = \sqrt{τ_w/ρ}$. A concrete law implements

friction_velocity(law, U, h; ν) -> u_τ        (m s⁻¹)

and inherits

wall_stress(law, U, h; ν) = u_τ²             (kinematic, m² s⁻²)
wall_drag(law, U, h; ν)   = τ_w / U          (m s⁻¹, the implicit drag coefficient)

ν is the kinematic viscosity (m² s⁻¹); laws of a fully rough wall ignore it. The stress acts against the wall-parallel velocity, $τ_{w,i} = τ_w\,u_i/U$ (Piomelli & Balaras 2002).

Aeolus.WallModels.AbstractWallModel — Type
AbstractWallModel

A wall model: whatever decides the stress apply_wall_stress! applies at a wall. WallModel is the equilibrium (algebraic) one — a wall law evaluated at the matching point; TBLEWallModel (P57-c, #189) integrates the thin-boundary-layer equations under every wall column and carries per-column state. Both hand per-column coefficients $τ_{w,i} = a\,u_i + b_i$ to the same implicit, face-by-face application, so the operator exists once.

Aeolus.WallModels.DampedMixingLength — Type
DampedMixingLength(; A_plus = 17)

The eddy viscosity of Cabot & Moin (2000) and Wang & Moin (2002): the mixing-length viscosity with van Driest damping,

\[ν_{t,wm} = κ\,y\,u_τ\,\left(1 - e^{-y^+/A^+}\right)^2, \qquad y^+ = y u_τ/ν,\]

with $A^+ = 17$. Over a rough wall (z0 > 0) it is the undamped $κ u_τ (y + z_0)$, whose equilibrium solution is the rough log law $U = (u_τ/κ)\ln[(y + z_0)/z_0]$ (u = 0 at y = 0).

Aeolus.WallModels.DynamicRoughness — Type
DynamicRoughness(stats::SubfilterHeight; z0_floor, C_d = 1.0,
                 α = nothing, α_fallback = 0.0, mask = nothing,
                 α_max = nothing, c = DEFAULT_SURFACE_CONSTANTS)

The dynamic surface roughness model of Anderson & Meneveau (2011) for a surface that is partially resolved: the relief larger than the grid ($h_Δ$) is resolved, the rest enters the wall law as a roughness length proportional to the RMS sub-filter height,

\[z_{0,Δ}(x, y) = \sqrt{z_{0,f}^2 + α^2 σ_Δ^2(x, y)} ,\]

where $z_{0,f}$ (z0_floor, m; a number or an (Nx, Ny) matrix) is the roughness of the scales below the height map itself (the per-class table of the Berlin case; for the pure Anderson & Meneveau model a negligible value). A flat surface ($σ_Δ ≡ 0$) returns $z_{0,f}$ exactly.

The dynamic α. The total surface drag — modelled wall stress plus the form drag on the resolved relief (surface_gradient_drag) — must not depend on how much of the surface is resolved. Evaluated at the grid scale Δ and at the test-filter scale $\hat Δ$ (the filter of stats), the plane-mean drag along the mean wind $\hat{\mathbf e}$,

\[D_Δ(α) = \Big\langle \Big[\frac{κ}{\ln(z/z_{0,Δ}(α))}\Big]^2 |\tilde{\mathbf u}|\,\tilde{\mathbf u}\cdot\hat{\mathbf e} + \mathbf f(\tilde{\mathbf u}, ∇h_Δ)\cdot(-\hat{\mathbf e}) \Big\rangle ,\]

and $D_{\hat Δ}$ the same with the test-filtered velocity, $h_{\hat Δ}$ and $σ_{\hat Δ}$, gives α as the root of

\[D_{\hat Δ}(α) - D_Δ(α) = 0\]

(Anderson & Meneveau 2011, their eq. 3.3, with the neutral log law of RoughLogLaw), solved per plane with SurfaceExchange's Illinois iteration on the bracket $(0, α_{max})$, $α_{max}$ the largest α for which every $z_0 < z$. A plane-mean residual with no sign change in the bracket keeps α_fallback (default 0, i.e. $z_0 = z_{0,f}$) and marks the solve unconverged.

α = <number> fixes α instead (the non-dynamic model, e.g. a value tuned at one resolution). mask ((Nx, Ny) Bool, true = included) restricts the plane means to the columns with a wall. κ is SurfaceExchange.von_karman(c).

Fields after update_dynamic_roughness!: α, converged, z0 (the (Nx, Ny) roughness lengths, m), nsolves, nfail. dynamic_wall_laws / with_roughness turn them into a per-column RoughLogLaw or MOSTWallLaw.

For an array of obstacles that the grid does not resolve at all, the morphometric roughness length and displacement height of Macdonald et al. (1998) are Aeolus.SurfaceTiles.macdonald_roughness (the one implementation in Aeolus, used by the Berlin parent's tiled surface); this model does not use them.

Aeolus.WallModels.ImmersedWalls — Type
ImmersedWalls

The walls of an immersed solid mask as a wall model sees them; built by immersed_walls. Fields: faces (a vector of ImmersedWallFace), ν (the kinematic viscosity the laws are evaluated with, m² s⁻¹), the grid spacings, and work arrays. The last call's diagnostics are kept in stats:

Aeolus.WallModels.MOSTFluxWallLaw — Type
MOSTFluxWallLaw(sf, z0m, Q, θ_ref; U_min = 0.1, c = DEFAULT_SURFACE_CONSTANTS)
MOSTFluxWallLaw(z0m, Q, θ_ref; kwargs...)   # sf = BusingerDyerBeljaarsHoltslag()

The rough-wall Monin–Obukhov law for a prescribed surface buoyancy flux: Q is the upward kinematic flux $\overline{w'θ'}$ (K m s⁻¹, negative = surface cooling) and θ_ref (K) the reference potential temperature of $g/θ_{ref}$. Unlike MOSTWallLaw, which holds the Obukhov length, the stability follows the wind: $L = -u_τ^3 θ_{ref}/(κ g Q)$ with the $u_τ$ of the law itself, so

\[u_τ = \frac{κ\,\max(U, U_{min})}{\ln(h/z_{0m}) - ψ_m(h/L) + ψ_m(z_{0m}/L)}, \qquad L = -\frac{u_τ^3 θ_{ref}}{κ g Q},\]

solved by SurfaceExchange.flux_friction_velocity (one source of truth). Under cooling the relation has a turbulent solution only while $-Q$ is at most the maximum sustainable heat flux of the wind at height h (SurfaceExchange.max_sustainable_heat_flux, Van de Wiel et al. 2012); then $u_τ$ is on the weakly stable branch. Beyond it the law gives $u_τ = 0$: the wall stops exerting stress — the collapse of the surface layer. This is the wall law of the flux-cooled half channel (half_channel_case(surface_heat_flux = Q), P58-e, #193).

Aeolus.WallModels.MOSTWallLaw — Type
MOSTWallLaw(sf, z0m, L; U_min = 0.1, c = DEFAULT_SURFACE_CONSTANTS)
MOSTWallLaw(z0m, L; kwargs...)          # sf = BusingerDyerBeljaarsHoltslag()

The rough-wall law with the Monin–Obukhov stability correction for a given Obukhov length L (m; Inf neutral, > 0 stable):

\[u_τ = \frac{κ\,\max(U, U_{min})}{\ln(h/z_{0m}) - ψ_m(h/L) + ψ_m(z_{0m}/L)}.\]

Everything here is delegated to SurfaceExchange: κ is its von_karman, and the denominator is the stability-corrected log term of solve_surface_layer evaluated with the stability functions sf (their psi_m), so for the same ζ = h/L, wind and roughness the result is solve_surface_layer(...).u_star bit for bit. U_min is the minimum wind of that solver (default the same 0.1 m s⁻¹).

L is held: the law is the momentum half of Monin–Obukhov similarity with the buoyancy of the last surface-layer solve. matching_obukhov_length recovers that L from a solve's u_*.

Aeolus.WallModels.MatchingInput — Type
MatchingInput(; h_wm = nothing, h_wm_cells = 1, T_f = 0, spatial_filter = false)

The input of a wall model at one wall: where the velocity is sampled and how it is filtered before the wall law is evaluated with it. Pass it to matching_input!, which returns the wind and height keywords of apply_wall_stress!.

Matching height (Kawai & Larsson 2012). The LES velocity in the first one or two cells next to a wall is under-resolved and dominated by the numerical errors of the SGS model there, and an equilibrium law fed with it gives a wall stress that is wrong by the same amount: the log-layer mismatch, a shift of the mean profile against the log law. Kawai & Larsson sample the velocity a few cells away, at $h_{wm}$, where the LES resolves the turbulence, and apply the stress of the law at $h_{wm}$ at the wall face.

  • h_wm (m) — the matching distance from the wall; the velocity is linearly interpolated between the two cell centres around it (so $h_{wm} ≥ Δz/2$);
  • h_wm_cells — otherwise the centre of the h_wm_cells-th cell from the wall, $h = (n - ½)Δz$; an integer, or an (Nx, Ny) integer matrix (one per column; counted from the column's wall level with a level mask). h_wm_cells = 1 with no filter is the classical first-cell matching.

Temporal filter (Yang, Park & Moin 2017). With T_f > 0 (s) the cell-centre velocity components $(u, v)$ at the matching point are passed through the exponential (single-pole) filter

\[\tilde u^{n+1} = \tilde u^n + ε\,(u^{n+1} - \tilde u^n), \qquad ε = 1 - e^{-Δt/T_f},\]

once per accepted step (update_input_filter!), and the law is evaluated at $|\tilde{\mathbf u}|$. The weight is the exact one of the continuous filter $T_f\,\dot{\tilde u} = u - \tilde u$ for an input held over the step, so a unit step is followed by $1 - e^{-t/T_f}$ at every step whatever the Δt (exponential_filter_weight). The filter removes the instantaneous fluctuation that the equilibrium law (a relation between mean quantities) does not hold for; Yang et al. show that the input filtering alone removes most of the log-layer mismatch.

Wall-parallel filter (Bou-Zeid, Meneveau & Parlange 2005). With spatial_filter = true the horizontal velocity is first filtered along the wall with the 3 × 3 box of Operators.box_filter! (n = (3, 3, 1); the test filter of the dynamic model, so the two cannot drift), the discrete form of the test filter of twice the grid width that Bou-Zeid et al. apply to the wall-model input. Its second moment is $2Δ^2/3$ per direction; it preserves the plane mean of a periodic field.

Filtered state: uf, vf ((Nx, Ny), the filtered components at the matching point) and primed (false until the first sample).

Aeolus.WallModels.TBLEWallModel — Type
TBLEWallModel(; ν = 1.5e-5, κ = SurfaceExchange.von_karman(),

              N = 30, r = 1.2, z0 = 0.0, pressure = true,
              dynamic_kappa = :off, T_κ = 0.0, κ_range = (0.05, 0.6),
              tol = 1e-10, maxiter = 50)

The non-equilibrium wall model: a TBLEColumn under every wall column, integrated with tble_solve! (Balaras, Benocci & Piomelli 1996; Wang & Moin 2002; Kawai & Larsson 2012; Park & Moin 2014) between the wall ($u = 0$) and the matching height, where the profile equals the LES velocity sampled by P57-b's MatchingInput (matching height, time and wall-parallel filters). An AbstractWallModel: apply_wall_stress! applies the columns' wall stress with the operator's implicit face loop.

Use. Per step, apply_wall_stress!(sim, wm, Δt; side, mask, input) as the post_predictor! hook (every substage applies the stress of the columns' current state, implicitly) and, once per accepted step after the projection, update_wall_model!(sim … ) to advance the columns to the end of the step with the end-of-step LES velocity and pressure. The first call of either primes the columns with the equilibrium solution (:equilibrium_ode) for the current LES velocity. State is kept per wall (:bottom, :top) and per column.

Stress direction. The columns' stress $\boldsymbol τ_w$ need not point along the resolved first-cell velocity $\mathbf u$ (a pressure gradient turns it). It is applied as $τ_{w,i} = a u_i + b_i$ with the implicit part $a = \max(\boldsymbol τ_w·\hat{\mathbf u}, 0)/U$ and the explicit rest $b_i = τ_{w,i} - a u_i$ (the linear form of the Moeng formulation), so a stress along the flow keeps the unconditional stability of the equilibrium operator.

Keywords. ν (m² s⁻¹), κ (the initial or fixed $κ_{wm}$, default SurfaceExchange.von_karman()), eddy (AbstractTBLEEddyViscosity), terms (see tble_solve!), N and r (the grid, tble_grid), z0 (the roughness length, m, a number or an (Nx, Ny) matrix; 0 smooth), pressure (add the resolved pressure gradient $∂p/∂x_i$ of sim.p at the matching cell; a gradient across a solid neighbour is one-sided).

Dynamic κ (Wang & Moin 2002). dynamic_kappa = :total sets $κ_{wm}$ of each wall so that the TBLE eddy viscosity at the matching height equals the LES's effective eddy viscosity there: the plane-mean resolved plus SGS stress (the momentum flux the TBLE eddy viscosity stands for) over the resolved mean shear, both projected on the mean wall-stress direction $\hat e$:

\[κ_{wm} = \frac{⟨-u'w' + τ_{sgs}⟩(h)·\hat e} {(∂_z⟨\mathbf u⟩(h)·\hat e)\;⟨h\,u_τ D(h^+)^2⟩}.\]

:sgs matches the SGS viscosity alone, $ν_{t,wm}(h) = ⟨ν_{sgs}⟩(h)$ (Wang & Moin's original form: the resolved eddies are carried by the convective terms of the full TBLE). :off keeps κ. The target is relaxed with the exponential filter of time scale T_κ (s; 0 instantaneous) and clamped to κ_range; κ_feedback = false computes and relaxes it (tble_kappa(…; dynamic = true)) without handing it to the columns. Plane walls only (mask = nothing).

Matching the stress itself ($⟨ν_{t,wm}∂_y u⟩(h)$ = LES stress) does not determine κ: in a statistically steady flow both sides equal $τ_w + h\,∂p/∂x$ by the momentum balance whatever κ is (the first version did this and drifted to the lower clamp). Measured in the Reτ = 1000 channel (`test/era6wallmodelstble.jl): the effective LES viscosity ath = 0.375δgives κ_wm ≈ 0.23, close to0.41(1 - h/δ) = 0.26(the outer-layer decrease of the stress that the mixing-lengthν_{t,wm}lacks) and to Wang & Moin's ≈ 0.2. Fed back into:pressuregradient(which has no convective terms to carry the difference) it lowers`τw`` near the wall too; see the theory page.

Not modelled. No Monin–Obukhov stability correction is carried into the TBLE eddy viscosity (neutral; P58 adds buoyancy through AbstractTBLEEddyViscosity): over the Berlin ground the non-equilibrium option is neutral where the equilibrium MOSTWallLaw is stability-corrected.

Aeolus.WallModels.apply_immersed_wall_stress! — Method
apply_immersed_wall_stress!(sim, iw::ImmersedWalls, Δt) -> (Fx, Fy, Fz)

Apply the modelled stress of every wall of iw to the velocity of sim, implicitly, over the step Δt. A post_predictor! hook, run after the direct forcing of the IBM (so the solid faces are already zero) and before the projection:

iw = immersed_walls(sim, sm, RoughLogLaw(0.05))
step!(sim, dt;
      body_force!     = (s, Δt) -> remove_stencil_wall_flux!(s, iw),
      post_predictor! = (s, Δt) -> (implicit_ibm_correction!(s, sm; dt = Δt);
                                    apply_immersed_wall_stress!(s, iw, Δt)))

At each (face, wall) pair the law is evaluated at the speed parallel to that wall, $U = |(q, \bar q_o)|$ (the face's component q and the other tangential component interpolated to the face), at $h = Δ_d/2$, giving the drag coefficient $a = τ_w(U)/U$ (wall_drag). The stress on the face's component is $τ = a\,q$ (along the wall, against the flow; Piomelli & Balaras 2002), applied implicitly through the face's control volume, $Δq = -Δt\,τ/Δ_d$:

\[q ← \frac{q}{1 + Δt \sum_{walls} a/Δ_d},\]

so a face between two walls (a corner, a one-cell gap) gets the sum of both, the stress is evaluated with the end-of-step velocity, the face velocity never changes sign and the update is stable for any Δt. The momentum removed is exactly $\sum τ_w ΔA Δt$ ($ΔA = ΔV/Δ_d$).

Returns the volume-integrated kinematic force $\sum Δq\,ΔV/Δt$ per component (m⁴ s⁻²); iw.stats also holds $\sum τ_w ΔA$, the wall area and the smallest $τ_w$ of the step.

This is the IBM wall-stress model of Tessicini et al. (2002) and Chester, Meneveau & Parlange (2007) in its simplest, equilibrium form: the stress of a wall law at the first fluid point replaces the stencil's unresolved no-slip flux (which remove_stencil_wall_flux! removes).

Aeolus.WallModels.apply_wall_stress! — Method
apply_wall_stress!(sim, wm, Δt; side = :bottom, mask = nothing,
                   formulation = :local, wind = nothing, height = nothing,
                   ustar = nothing, wind_out = nothing) -> (Fx, Fy)

Apply the modelled wall stress of wm (a WallModel, or a bare AbstractWallLaw with the default ν) to the horizontal velocity of sim next to its z-walls, over the step Δt. Use it as the post_predictor! hook of a step (on the predicted velocity, before the projection):

wm = WallModel(SpaldingLaw(); ν = 1e-5)
step!(sim, dt; post_predictor! = (s, Δt) -> apply_wall_stress!(s, wm, Δt))

Geometry. The wall-parallel speed of a column is the speed at the centre of its wall-adjacent cell, $U = |(\bar u, \bar v)|$ (faces averaged to the centre), at the distance $h = Δz/2$ from the wall. side is :bottom (level 1), :top (level Nz) or :both. mask restricts the wall: nothing (the whole plane), an (Nx, Ny) Bool matrix (columns with a wall), or an (Nx, Ny) integer matrix giving, per column, the level of the first cell above an immersed floor (:bottom only; a level outside 1:Nz means no wall — the roof-aware ground of the Berlin case). A velocity face is shared by two columns; it gets the mean of the two columns' stresses, a column contributing only at its own wall level (half of the face's area is each column's).

Formulations (Piomelli & Balaras 2002 review all three):

  • :local — Schumann (1975) as generalised by Grötzbach (1987): the law is applied locally, $τ_{w,i} = τ_w(U)\,u_i/U$.
  • :schumann — Schumann (1975): the plane-mean stress from the plane-mean speed, $τ_{w,i} = ⟨τ_w⟩\,u_i/⟨U⟩$ with $⟨τ_w⟩ = τ_w(⟨U⟩)$.
  • :moeng — Moeng (1984): $τ_{w,i} = ⟨τ_w⟩\,[⟨U⟩(u_i - ⟨u_i⟩) + U⟨u_i⟩]/(⟨U⟩\,|⟨\mathbf u⟩|)$, whose plane mean is $⟨τ_w⟩$ along the mean wind. With no mean wind ($|⟨\mathbf u⟩| = 0$) it is undefined; the step then uses :local.

Plane means are over the columns (or faces) with a wall.

Time stepping. Each stress is linear in the face velocity, $τ_{w,i} = a\,u_i + b$ ($b ≠ 0$ only for :moeng), with $a$ and $b$ from the velocity at the start of the step, and is applied implicitly:

\[u_i ← \frac{u_i - Δt\,b/Δz}{1 + Δt\,a/Δz},\]

so the wall stress is evaluated with the end-of-step velocity: for :local and :schumann the velocity decays monotonically and never changes sign, for any Δt (the explicit form is unstable beyond $Δt = 2Δz/a$). The momentum the step removes is exactly $\sum τ_{w,i}\,ΔA\,Δt$.

Halos and the finite-volume operators are not modified: on a free-slip (ImpermeableWall) z-wall, whose eddy-viscosity flux is zero, the wall model is the only momentum flux through the wall face.

Keywords. wind — an (Nx, Ny) matrix of speeds to evaluate the law at instead of the resolved $U$ (a held or filtered wind, or the speed at a matching height off the first cell: see MatchingInput). The stress then has the magnitude of the law at that speed and the direction of the resolved first-cell velocity, $τ_{w,i} = τ_w(U_{in})\,u_i/U$ (:local; :schumann: $τ_w(⟨U_{in}⟩)\,u_i/⟨U⟩$), so the implicit coefficient is $a = τ_w(U_{in})/U$. (Before P57-b it was $τ_w(U_{in})/U_{in}$, which scaled the magnitude by $U/U_{in}$.) :moeng uses the input speeds for $U$ and $⟨U⟩$. height — the distance from the wall (m) at which the law is evaluated, a number or an (Nx, Ny) matrix (default $Δz/2$, the first cell centre; the matching height of MatchingInput); the stress is still applied at the wall face, to the first velocity level (Kawai & Larsson 2012). ustar / wind_out — (Nx, Ny) matrices that receive, for every column with a wall, the friction velocity of the law and the speed it was evaluated at (one side only).

A TBLEWallModel (P57-c) is applied by the same face loop with the stress of its columns (apply_wall_stress!(sim, wm::TBLEWallModel, Δt; side, mask, input, ustar, wind_out); input a MatchingInput, formulation = :local only).

Returns the volume-integrated kinematic force of the step, $\sum (u' - u)/Δt\,ΔV$ (m⁴ s⁻²) per component, negative for a flow in the positive direction; divided by the wall area it is the mean kinematic stress.

Its columns take the input of the matching-height and filter keywords, the case's mean pressure gradient $∂p/∂x = -u_τ^2/δ$ plus the resolved one, and are advanced once per step after the projection. formulation must be :local.

  • bottom_bc = :noslip — resolved NoSlipWalls instead of the wall model: no-slip at the bottom and, with walls = :both, at the top (walls = :bottom keeps a free-slip top, an open channel). Aeolus.WallModels.half_channel_case — Method
half_channel_case(; N = 24, closure = StaticSmagorinsky(0.1),
                  wall_law = nothing, formulation = :local,
                  z0_over_H = 1e-4, h_wm = nothing, h_wm_cells = 1,
                  filter = :none, T_f = nothing, H = 1.0,
                  u_star = 1.0, noise = 3.0, seed = 1,
                  stepper = :rk3, roughness = nothing,
                  surface_heat_flux = nothing, θ_ref = 300.0,
                  stability = BusingerDyerBeljaarsHoltslag(),
                  bottom_bc = :wall_model,
                  θ_walls = nothing, extent = nothing,
                  inflow = nothing, outflow = :convective,
                  lateral = :periodic, lateral_outflow = outflow) -> WallBoundedCase

The neutral, pressure-driven half channel of Porté-Agel, Meneveau & Parlange (2000) and Bou-Zeid, Meneveau & Parlange (2005): the canonical high-Reynolds-number surface layer for testing an SGS closure's log law.

  • Domain $2πH × 2πH × H$ on N cells per direction (N an integer or a 3-tuple), periodic in x and y.
  • Forcing $-∂p/∂x/ρ = u_*^2/H$, a uniform body force on $u$ (body_force!), so the steady total stress is $u_*^2(1 - z/H)$.
  • Free-slip top (ImpermeableWall); at the bottom, the modelled wall stress of wall_law through apply_wall_stress! (implicit, post_predictor!), in the formulation :local (Schumann-Grötzbach), :schumann or :moeng. wall_law = nothing is RoughLogLaw(z0_over_H * H).
  • Inviscid (ν = 0), no Coriolis, no buoyancy. closure is any AbstractSGSClosure (no wall-damping function is applied; that is the point of the case).
  • Initial state: the law's profile $U(z)$ (the speed for which the law gives $u_*$ at height z, at every cell centre), plus seeded uniform noise of amplitude noise·u_star on all three components (rms 0.58·noise·u). The default noise = 3 (rms 1.7 u, the level of the surface-layer turbulence it stands for) makes the flow turbulent within about 5 H/u* at 24³ with StaticSmagorinsky(0.1); with noise = 0.3 the same run stays laminar (the eddy viscosity damps the noise) and its wall stress sits at 0.5-0.7 u² for 30 H/u.

Domain and inflow (N-inflow-d, #266). extent = (Lx, Ly) replaces the default $2πH × 2πH$ horizontal extent. inflow — an AbstractBoundaryData (e.g. a ProfileBoundaryData with the mean profile of a periodic precursor) — opens the x direction: the west face takes the data (Dirichlet), the east face the outflow condition of N-open-c (:convective by default, :orlanski or :dirichlet), and the walls, forcing, closure and wall model are those of the periodic case. This is the inflow–outflow half channel on which the development length of a synthetic inflow is measured against the periodic precursor (Transition.development_length; Xie & Castro 2008; Kim, Castro & Xie 2013). Neutral only.

lateral = :periodic (default) keeps y periodic; lateral = :open opens the south and north faces as well, as in a nest child: they take the same data, with the lateral_outflow condition (default: that of the east face) at points where the flow leaves the domain and the data (Dirichlet) where it enters. With a mean-profile inflow ($v = 0$) the lateral faces carry no turbulence of their own, so the side layers of the domain are quieter than its centre; the fetch diagnostics (FetchStatistics(sim; jrange)) can exclude them.

Wall-model input (P57-b, #183; see MatchingInput):

  • h_wm (m) or h_wm_cells — the matching height: the law is evaluated with the velocity there and its stress applied at the wall face (Kawai & Larsson 2012). Default: the first cell centre;
  • filter — :none, :temporal (the exponential time filter of Yang, Park & Moin 2017, time scale T_f, default $0.5\,H/u_*$), :spatial (the 3 × 3 wall-parallel box of Bou-Zeid et al. 2005) or :both.

The defaults reproduce the first-cell, unfiltered input bit for bit.

Partially resolved roughness (P57-e, #190): roughness — a DynamicRoughness on the (N, N) surface grid (periodic, the grid's spacing). The resolved relief $h_Δ$ of its statistics exerts surface_gradient_drag on the first cell (a body force $f_i/Δz$ on the faces, the mean of the two adjacent columns), and the wall law (wall_law, a RoughLogLaw or MOSTWallLaw, as a template) gets, per column, the dynamic roughness $\sqrt{z_{0,f}^2+α^2σ_Δ^2}$, with α solved once per step from the first-cell (or matching-height) velocity (Anderson & Meneveau 2011). τ_form holds the form drag.

Keyword hooks reserved for later Era VI issues — documented, not implemented (any value other than the default raises an ArgumentError):

  • bottom_bc = :wall_model and θ_walls = nothing — the half channel is inviscid, so it has no resolved wall; the resolved no-slip, θ-walled flow is channel_case(bottom_bc = :noslip, θ_walls = …) (P55-f, #191).

Surface heat flux (P58-e, #193). surface_heat_flux = Q (K m s⁻¹, the upward kinematic flux $\overline{w'θ'}$; negative cools the surface) turns the case into the flux-cooled (or heated) half channel of the stable-boundary-layer collapse studies (Nieuwstadt 2005; Flores & Riley 2011; Donda et al. 2015):

  • buoyancy on, $g(θ - θ_{ref})/θ_{ref}$ with θ_ref (default 300 K) and $g$ of SurfaceExchange; θ starts uniform at θ_ref;
  • Q enters the first cell as the prescribed bottom θ flux (θ_flux_bottom of the simulation); the top and the bottom are otherwise adiabatic;
  • wall_law = nothing becomes MOSTFluxWallLaw(stability, z0, Q, θ_ref): the wall stress of Monin–Obukhov similarity with the stability set by Q and the wall's own $u_τ$, which drops to zero where $-Q$ exceeds the maximum sustainable heat flux of the local wind (max_sustainable_heat_flux, Van de Wiel et al. 2012);
  • the initial velocity is the neutral log law (RoughLogLaw(z0)) plus noise, so a neutral and a cooled run start from the same state.

max_sustainable_heat_flux(stability, U₁, Δz/2, z0, z0, θ_ref) with the neutral first-level wind $U_1 = (u_*/κ)\ln(Δz/(2z_0))$ is the MSHF the collapse sweep is normalised by (examples/sbl/collapse_channel.jl, test/era6_collapse.jl).

Measured with StaticSmagorinsky(0.1) at 24³ (test/era6_neutral_abl.jl) and StaticSmagorinsky(0.17) (the Mason & Thomson 1992 overshoot): see the Wall models theory page, section "Wall-bounded benchmark cases".

Aeolus.WallModels.immersed_wall_drag! — Method
immersed_wall_drag!(a, iw::ImmersedWalls, u, v, w) -> a

The drag rate $a_n = τ_w(U)/(U\,Δ_d)$ (s⁻¹) of every (face, wall) pair n of iw, for the velocity data arrays u, v, w (halos filled): the law of the pair evaluated at the speed parallel to that wall, $U = |(q, \bar q_o)|$, at $h = Δ_d/2$ (wall_drag), divided by the face's width $Δ_d$ across the wall. The modelled stress on the face's component q is $τ = a_n Δ_d\, q$ and its kinematic force per unit volume $a_n q$, so $a_n q^2$ is the rate per unit volume at which the wall stress takes resolved kinetic energy out of that face.

The one evaluation of the immersed wall law: apply_immersed_wall_stress! applies it, and the facade production of the prognostic TKE closure (PrognosticSGS.TKEClosure with walls, #232) books $a_n q^2$ as shear production.

Aeolus.WallModels.remove_stencil_wall_flux! — Method
remove_stencil_wall_flux!(sim, iw::ImmersedWalls; ν = sim.ν, νt = sim.νt,
                          sgs = sim.has_sgs) -> (Fx, Fy, Fz)

Remove from the momentum tendencies of sim the flux the viscous and eddy-viscosity stencils put through the walls of iw, so that apply_immersed_wall_stress! is the only momentum flux there. A body_force! hook: it must run inside the step, after the tendencies are formed (it reads the start-of-step velocity and the step's $ν_t$) and before the predictor.

For a face F with a wall across $s\,e_d$ (neighbour face $N$, zeroed by the IBM), the stencils of Simulations.step! (the molecular Laplacian and Operators.add_eddy_viscosity_*!) added

\[\frac{(ν + ν_{t,e})\,(q_N - q_F)}{Δ_d^2},\]

$ν_{t,e}$ the mean of the four cell-centre $ν_t$ around the edge between F and N; this is subtracted. The correction of a step is therefore modelled stress minus stencil stress: no double counting. With sgs = false (no eddy viscosity in the step) only the molecular part is removed; pass sgs = true when step! gets an eddy_viscosity! closure (it fills sim.νt without sim.has_sgs).

Returns the volume-integrated kinematic force the stencil had exerted through the walls (m⁴ s⁻²), also kept in iw.stats[:F_stencil].

With the IMEX stepper the diffusion is implicit and its wall flux is not this explicit expression; the removal is then first-order accurate in time (exact for step!, the explicit projection step).

Aeolus.WallModels.tble_solve! — Method
tble_solve!(col::TBLEColumn, Ut, Vt, dpdx, dpdy, Δt; ν, κ = 0.4,
            eddy = DampedMixingLength(), terms = :pressure_gradient,
            tol = 1e-10, maxiter = 50) -> (τx, τy, u_τ, iterations)

Advance the column col over Δt (s) with the thin-boundary-layer equations

\[\frac{∂u_i}{∂t} + \mathrm{conv}_i = -\frac{∂p}{∂x_i} + \frac{∂}{∂y}\left[(ν + ν_{t,wm})\frac{∂u_i}{∂y}\right], \qquad u_i(0) = 0,\quad u_i(h) = (U_t, V_t)_i,\]

dpdx, dpdy the kinematic pressure gradient (m s⁻²) and ν, κ, eddy those of $ν_{t,wm}$ (tble_eddy_viscosity). terms:

  • :equilibrium_ode — the steady, unforced equation $∂_y[(ν+ν_t)∂_y u_i] = 0$ ($τ$ constant across the layer), the ODE of the equilibrium models, solved directly (Δt and the pressure gradient are ignored);
  • :pressure_gradient — the unsteady equation with the pressure gradient and no convection;
  • :full — plus the convective tendency col.cx, col.cy, set by the caller (update_wall_model! computes it from the neighbouring columns, Park & Moin 2014).

Δt = Inf gives the steady state of :pressure_gradient / :full.

Discretisation. Finite volumes on the stretched nodes, backward Euler in time (L-stable: any Δt), a tridiagonal (Thomas) solve per component. The flux of segment $[y_j, y_{j+1}]$ is $G_{j+1/2}(u_{j+1} - u_j)$ with the conductance $G = [∫ dy/(ν + ν_t)]^{-1}$ integrated exactly (4-point Gauss–Legendre), so the unforced steady solution is the quadrature of the equilibrium ODE at every node whatever the grid, and with $ν_t ≡ 0$ the scheme is exact for quadratic profiles (Poiseuille). The wall stress is the flux of the first segment carried to the wall with the wall value of $∂τ/∂y = ∂p/∂x$: $τ_{w} = G_{1/2}u_1 - (y_1/2)\,∂p/∂x$, so in steady state $τ(y) = τ_w + y\,∂p/∂x$ at every segment midpoint.

Momentum. Over the control volumes $V_j = (y_{j+1} - y_{j-1})/2$ of the interior nodes the step conserves momentum exactly: $Σ V_j (u_j^{n+1} - u_j^n)/Δt = τ_{top} - G_{1/2}u_1 + Σ V_j F_j$ ($τ_{top}$ the flux of the last segment; tble_column_momentum).

Nonlinearity. $ν_{t,wm}$ depends on the column's own $u_τ$; the step is iterated to $|Δu_τ| ≤$ tol·$u_τ$ by a secant iteration on $u_τ = |τ_w(u_τ)|^{1/2}$ (falling back to the fixed-point step), each iterate a full implicit solve from the start-of-step profile.

  • Wang, M. & Moin, P. (2002). Dynamic wall modeling for large-eddy simulation of complex turbulent flows. Phys. Fluids, 14, 2043-2051.
  • Kawai, S. & Larsson, J. (2012). Wall-modeling in large eddy simulation: length scales, grid resolution, and accuracy. Phys. Fluids, 24, 015105.
  • Park, G. I. & Moin, P. (2014). An improved dynamic non-equilibrium wall-model for large eddy simulation. Phys. Fluids, 26, 015108.

Aeolus.WallModels.update_wall_model! — Method
update_wall_model!(wm::TBLEWallModel, sim, Δt; side = :bottom, mask = nothing,
                   input = nothing, dpdx = 0, dpdy = 0) -> wm

Advance the TBLE columns of wm at the wall side (:bottom, :top or :both) over the accepted step Δt (call once per step, after the projection, and after update_input_filter! of a time-filtered input): per column tble_solve! with the LES velocity at the matching point of input (a MatchingInput; nothing is the first cell, unfiltered) as the upper boundary condition and the pressure gradient dpdx, dpdy (m s⁻², a uniform imposed part such as the mean pressure gradient $-G$ of a forced channel) plus, with pressure = true, the resolved gradient of sim.p at the matching cell. With terms = :full the convective tendency $u ∂_x u_i + v ∂_y u_i + w ∂_n u_i$ is formed from the neighbouring columns' profiles (first-order upwind differences between active neighbours of equal matching height — explicit centred advection would be unconditionally unstable; $w$ from continuity, $w = -∫_0^y (∂_x u + ∂_y v)\,dy$), as in Park & Moin (2014). Then the dynamic $κ_{wm}$ is updated (see TBLEWallModel).

Aeolus.WallModels.wall_slip_velocity — Method
wall_slip_velocity(u, v, w, stencils, geom, law; ν, ν_eff = ν) -> Vector{NTuple{3}}

The slip velocity on the true surface that makes the ghost-cell reconstruction carry the modelled stress, per CellCenter stencil. The reconstruction (CutCell.reconstruct_wall_velocity!) makes the velocity linear along the normal between the wall value $u_s$ and the image value $u_t$, so the resolved stress there is $ν_{eff}(u_t - u_s)/h$. Setting it equal to the modelled $τ_w$ (wall_shear_stress with law) gives

\[u_s = u_t - \frac{τ_w\,h}{ν_{eff}},\]

along the tangential direction of the image velocity. ν_eff is the viscosity the resolved stencil applies at the wall ($ν + ν_t$). In the viscous sublayer ($τ_w = ν u_t/h$, ν_eff = ν) the slip is zero: no-slip is recovered. With ν_eff = ν in the log layer it is negative ($u_t - u_τ y^+$, a wall moving against the flow), which is why a wall-modelled LES passes its eddy viscosity. law = nothing gives zero slip (the ν-linear stress is the stencil's own).

Use slip_wall_value to hand it to reconstruct_wall_velocity! as the wall_value of one component.
