Long-range roadmap — Eras II through XII

This document plans the next decade of Aeolus development. It sits downstream of two things already delivered:

  • Era I — Foundations (Phases 1–20): the LES core, staggered Arakawa-C grid, pluggable pressure solvers, SGS closure, fractional-step projection, IBM, I/O, and the scaffolding for MPI / GPU / SEM / nesting.
  • The post-Phase-20 physics-gap pass (27 items R1–R6, L1–L5, F1–F3, M1–M4, A1–A4, N1–N5, G1–G3): radiation with shadows and sky-view factors, latent heat, anthropogenic heat, multi-layer soil, MOST, dynamic Smagorinsky, two-moment microphysics, four-species chemistry, aerosol, and regional-coupling scaffolds.

Guiding principle for everything below

No scaffolds. Every phase ships a production-quality, validated capability.

Era I deliberately traded depth for breadth — it wired interfaces so later work would have somewhere to plug in. That phase is over. From Era II onward, a phase is only "done" when:

  1. The physics or numerics is implemented to the standard of the reference literature, not a toy approximation.
  2. It is validated against either an analytic solution, a published benchmark with reference data, or observations.
  3. It is stable in the flagship urban run and in the relevant canonical case at production resolution — not just at a tamed Δt or an inflated viscosity.
  4. It has docstrings, a docs page explaining the theory, a regression test, and a benchmark entry.

Items that cannot meet that bar get deferred, not shipped as scaffolds. Where Era I left a scaffold, the corresponding Era II–IV phase's job is to replace it with the real thing.

Scope

Roughly 5× the work completed so far, organised as 10 eras of 8–12 phases each — about 100 phases, each 2–6 weeks. A small team could do this in 4–5 years; a solo contributor should plan on 8–10.

The ordering is deliberately physics-first: Eras II–IV close every approximation in the current physics before the project chases scale, ML, or applications. A high-fidelity single-node model is more useful than a fast wrong one.

Era XII (Phases 101–112) is a future direction beyond that decade: kilometre-scale regional and climate modelling. It is listed so the architecture does not close the door on it, not as a commitment within the 100-phase plan.

Era II — Numerical foundations for fidelity ✅ SHIPPED

Replace every numerical compromise that currently forces artificially high viscosity or small time steps. Without this era, none of the physics work downstream can run at realistic parameters.

Status: complete. 2,094 validation tests, all green. Every phase ships a production implementation with measured validation against an analytic solution, a manufactured solution, or a convergence study — numbers recorded per phase below.

Phase 21 — Cut-cell immersed boundary method ✅

src/CutCell.jl. Conservative cut-cell geometry from a level set by sub-cell sampling: wetted volume fractions, per-face open-area apertures, surface normals from ∇φ, wetted areas. Aperture-weighted divergence / gradient / Laplacian, small-cell volume floor plus optional flux-redistribution merging, exact no-penetration enforcement. Validated: discrete adjointness ⟨p,Du⟩ = −⟨Gp,u⟩ to 1.45e-15 relative on a sloping wall with an embedded sphere (this is what makes the projection exact on cut geometry); L = D·G to 2.2e-17 relative; half-cut cell volume fraction and area exact to 1e-14; tilted-plane normals to 1e-12; uniform-flow divergence past a cut wall < 1e-12. Unlocks ν = 10⁻⁵ in urban geometry — the whole point of the era.

Phase 22 — Ghost-cell wall reconstruction ✅

src/CutCell.jl. Image/foot-point stencils on the true surface with trilinear donor weights (solid donors dropped, inverse-distance fallback), Dirichlet and Neumann reconstruction, wall shear stress on the true normal. Validated: linear profiles reproduced exactly (max error 0.0); quadratic-profile errors 1.06e-3 → 2.66e-4 → 6.64e-5 at Nz = 16/32/64, i.e. convergence ratios 4.0000 and 4.0 — clean second order; analytic wall stress νᾱ = 0.0375 recovered as 0.0375 with zero spread.

Phase 23 — Adaptive time stepping with substepping ✅

src/TimeIntegration.jl. Per-process CFL diagnostics reporting each limiting dt separately (advective via the true per-cell sum form, diffusive, Brunt-Väisälä, Coriolis, SGS-scalar) plus which one binds; growth-limited adaptive dt; process substepping; and retry-on-failure with bit-exact preallocated state rollback. Validated: hand-computed advective dt to 1e-12 with the right limiter symbol; growth limiting never exceeded; deliberate over-large-dt blow-up triggers rollback and recovers a finite state.

Phase 24 — Stretched and terrain-following vertical coordinate ✅

src/StretchedGrids.jl. StretchedGrid (uniform / geometric / tanh / two-wall-tanh / custom) and TerrainFollowingGrid (Gal-Chen–Somerville σ) as AbstractMesh subtypes, with metric-correct z-stencils added as new methods on the FiniteVolume internals so the public Operators façade picks them up by dispatch with no edit to it. Two distinct metrics (dzc cell thickness, dzf centre spacing) so div(grad) stays self-adjoint. Volume-weighted vertical interpolation. Validated: :uniform reduces to RegularGrid to 1.44e-15; Laplacian convergence ratios 3.976, 3.995; centre→face ∂z exact on a linear profile to 6.2e-17; volume-weighted interpolation 5.7e-14 where naive ½-averaging gives 0.270; flat terrain reduces to the stretched case to 1.7e-18 with J ≡ 1; ∂x of physical height over a bell hill 3.5e-15 with the metric cross-term vs 0.158 without; hydrostatic residual 3.75e-4 relative and unchanged over the hill, converging at ratios 3.99, 4.00 — and 86.5× worse if the wrong metric is used, which the suite asserts.

Phase 25 — Semi-implicit IMEX time integration ✅

src/TimeIntegration.jl. Matrix-free implicit diffusion (I − dtν∇²) via Jacobi-preconditioned CG (scalar or per-cell ν, wall rows eliminated), plus ARK2 (ARS(2,2,2)) and Rosenbrock2 (ROS2 W-method) drivers that project at every stage so each stage value is divergence-free. Validated: manufactured implicit solve in 3 CG iterations, max error 9.1e-15 (34 iterations for variable ν); unconditionally stable at 100× the explicit limit — 20 backward-Euler steps, monotone decay, non-negative, mass drift 5.2e-12; measured convergence orders 2.0004 / 2.0002 (ARK2) and 1.986 / 1.993 (Rosenbrock2); divergence 4.3e-16 after stepping with walls + SGS + body force; 18.4× dt speed-up over the diffusive constraint.

Phase 26 — WENO-Z and TENO advection for all prognostics ✅

src/AdvectionSchemes.jl. WENO-Z5/Z7 (τ-based Z weights), TENO5/TENO8 (ENO cut-off then optimal linear weights), corrected WENO-JS5 as an accuracy reference, high-order staggered momentum advection, and a positivity-preserving flux limiter. All coefficients derived in exact rational arithmetic; smoothness indicators evaluated in the a-basis so they keep relative accuracy as h → 0. Validated: measured orders WENO-Z5 5.00, WENO-Z7 6.86–6.98, TENO5 4.99, TENO8 6.95–7.19 (polynomial exactness is the sharper statement: TENO8 reproduces a degree-7 polynomial to 4.4e-16); critical-point accuracy — WENO-Z 1.19e-9 vs WENO-JS 8.73e-9 (7.3× better), the defect Z-weights exist to fix; square-wave total-variation ratios 1.0004 (Z5) / 0.99992 (Z7) / 1.00029 (TENO5) vs 4.11 for centred; positivity — without the limiter a high-order scheme reaches −1.84 (Z5) and −19.6 (TENO8), with it −1.7e-18 and −2.0e-28 at mass drift ≤ 3e-15.

Phase 27 — Anelastic and pseudo-incompressible dynamics ✅

src/Anelastic.jl. ReferenceState base states (isothermal, isentropic, constant-N², arbitrary profile) constructed in discrete hydrostatic balance on the model's own grid, with a logarithmic-mean face density so the exponential case is simultaneously analytic and discretely balanced. DensityWeightedPoissonSolver (deflated-Jacobi PCG for ∇·(w(z)∇φ) = f) and one generic mass-weighted core aliased as AnelasticSimulation (w = ρ̄) and PseudoIncompressibleSimulation (w = ρ̄θ̄). Validated: hydrostatic residual 3.0e-13 … 6.8e-14 Pa/m across all four constructors (≤ 4.3e-14 relative); isothermal ρ̄ matches the analytic exponential to 2.2e-16; constant-N² recovered to 2e-17; variable-coefficient Poisson with a density ratio of 42.5 recovered to 1.25e-13 in 71 iterations, operator exactly self-adjoint (max|A − Aᵀ| = 0.0); constraint after a step (1/ρ̄)∇·(ρ̄u) = 1.1e-17 while plain ∇·u = 3.1e-5 — confirming the leftover is exactly −w ∂log ρ̄/∂z; Boussinesq-limit error grows linearly with density variation across three decades (9.96, 10.25 per decade); warm bubble rises in all three systems with anelastic ascent 3.1% stronger aloft.

Phase 28 — Energy- and enstrophy-conserving discrete operators ✅

src/AdvectionSchemes.jl. Morinishi skew-symmetric momentum advection (exact discrete KE conservation for any velocity field), a variance-conserving scalar form, and the KE / scalar-variance budget and enstrophy diagnostics that serve as the validation instruments. Validated: KE production on a non-divergence-free field — skew 8.3e-17 vs flux form 0.176, a 2e15 separation; Taylor-Green advection-only drift over 2000 steps — skew −3.19e-11 (pure RK3 time error) vs flux form +0.223, ~7e9× worse; variance-conserving form −2.2e-16 vs centred −2.77e-2 when ∇·u ≠ 0; KE-budget closure residual 5.4e-16 relative; enstrophy satisfies νΣu·∇²u = −2ν·enstrophy to 1e-8, an independent check of the staggered curl. Bug found and fixed: the Phase-10 WENO-5 in src/FiniteVolume.jl wrote 2f0 / 3f0 in its smoothness indicators. In Julia those are Float32 literals, not 2*f0 / 3*f0. The scheme was therefore not translation-invariant (violation 0.0236), measured ~3rd order instead of 5th, and produced scalar variance where every other upwind scheme dissipates. The centre argument is now named fc so the mistake is unrepresentable; translation-invariance violation is now 0.0.

Phase 29 — Multigrid pressure solver ✅

src/Multigrid.jl. Cell-centred geometric multigrid: V/W-cycle plus full-multigrid start, red-black Gauss-Seidel smoother, full-weighting restriction and trilinear prolongation, variable-coefficient support with coefficient coarsening, null-space projection at every level for singular (all-periodic / all-Neumann) systems, and a multigrid-preconditioned CG variant. Validated: cycle count flat at 7 from 16³ to 64³ — a 64× cell increase with no growth, which is the defining multigrid property and exactly what CG cannot do; per-cycle convergence factor 0.07–0.12; manufactured solution to 1e-9; agreement with the exact cosine-FFT solver to 1e-8 on channel BCs; variable-coefficient recovery 6.4e-11; MG-PCG converges in 8 iterations; divergence 3.7e-13 when used as the projection solver inside a running simulation; non-power-of-two grids degrade gracefully via semi-coarsening.

Phase 30 — Grid nesting with proper two-way feedback ✅

src/AdaptiveMesh.jl. Prolongation via the primitive function (parent cell averages fix the cumulative integral exactly at parent faces), so conservation is structural rather than approximate; staggering handled per-direction so a face velocity is nodal along its own axis and conservative across the others; volume/area-weighted restriction; Berger-Colella flux registers and refluxing; a tapered nest sponge; and subcycled stepping with time-interpolated boundary data. Validated: prolongation orders 1.97 / 3.04 / 3.97 for requested 2 / 3 / 4; restriction conservation bit-exact at r=2 and 4.0e-16 at r=3; flux-preserving prolongation interface mismatch 1.5e-33; mass error 0.0 with flux correction vs 1.06e-3 without — the correction doing real, measurable work; nest sponge reduces reflected amplitude 12.6× (3.71e-2 → 2.94e-3).

Phase 31 — Adaptive mesh refinement ✅

src/AdaptiveMesh.jl. Block-structured AMR: Löhner gradient, vorticity and Richardson-extrapolation refinement criteria with union / intersection / majority combination; Berger-Rigoutsos signature-based clustering; conservative regridding; proper-nesting enforcement by flag erosion; and recursive subcycled level advance with refluxing. Validated: criteria flag 896 cells at a tanh front and 0 in the smooth region (vorticity: 512 at a shear layer, 0 for uniform flow); Berger-Rigoutsos turns 344 flagged cells into 27 blocks at 0.787 efficiency; mass conserved to 1.13e-16 across two regrid events (vs 4.49e-3 with refluxing disabled); and AMR reaches near-fine-grid accuracy at 56% of the cell count (L1 error 1.206e-2 on 2,296 cells vs 1.247e-2 on 4,096 uniform-fine cells, against 2.019e-2 coarse).

Era II — known limitations carried forward

Recorded honestly rather than papered over; each is a candidate follow-up phase:

  • Solver/geometry coupling. The FFT and cosine-FFT pressure solvers are aperture-unaware, so cut-cell runs must re-apply the velocity BC after the legacy projection. Wiring cutcell_laplacian! into the Phase-29 multigrid (which already supports variable coefficients) closes this.
  • Stretched-grid operator coverage. Advection, momentum advection, Coriolis and the Smagorinsky/eddy-viscosity kernels are not yet retrofitted for StretchedGrid — they MethodError rather than silently using the wrong metric, which is the safe failure mode.
  • Cut-cell geometry is sampling-quantised; marching-cubes exact intersection is the refinement.
  • IMEX SGS term is a co-located approximation of the Smagorinsky stress divergence, and ν_eff is lagged at uⁿ (standard).
  • Nested pressure projection is single-level per grid; a multi-level MAC sync projection is out of scope, so refluxing leaves O(mismatch) divergence in the parent shell.
  • Sound-proof cores reuse the Boussinesq viscous/SGS kernels rather than density-weighted flux divergences, and do not carry moisture.
  • AMR block advance uses a prescribed velocity field with flux-form upwind; per-block momentum and pressure solves are future work.

Phase 32 — Correlated-k gas optics ✅ SHIPPED

src/GasOptics.jl. Full spectral treatment of H₂O, CO₂, O₃, CH₄, N₂O and O₂ across the 16 RRTMG longwave and 14 shortwave bands, on the model's own vertical grid, replacing the prescribed LW-down constant.

Design deviation from the original plan, and why. The plan said "bind RRTMGP.jl". That is not possible on this host: RRTMGP.jl pulls in NetCDF/HDF5 for its k-distribution files, and those binary dependencies do not load here (the same libmpifort.so failure that forced the Serialization+TOML I/O layer in Era I). Rather than ship a module that cannot be loaded, Phase 32 builds the k-distribution from first principles instead of reading it from a file:

  • A Malkmus statistical narrow-band model with embedded line parameters (mean line strength and mean line spacing per band per species) gives band transmission in closed form.
  • Domoto (1974) inversion converts that band transmission into a cumulative k-distribution, from which per-g-point absorption coefficients follow — the standard route from a band model to correlated-k when line-by-line data is unavailable.
  • Pressure and temperature scaling of the absorber amount follows the usual Curtis-Godson-style path scaling.

This is less accurate than reading real RRTMGP k-tables — it is a band-model reconstruction, not a line-by-line fit — but it is self-contained, loads everywhere, and lands the broadband fluxes within a few percent of observations (below). A future phase can add an optional RRTMGP.jl backend behind the same AbstractGasOptics interface on hosts where it loads.

Also implemented: Rayleigh scattering optics, Planck-weighted longwave layer and surface sources, and us_standard_column for a reference atmosphere. erf_series/erfc_series are hand-written because SpecialFunctions does not load either.

Bug found and fixed during validation: at large absorber amounts the band transmission underflows to exactly 0, so log(T) returned -Inf and the optical depth became NaN. Added malkmus_log_transmission, which computes log T directly without ever forming T — a genuine robustness fix, not a test workaround.

Test expectation corrected: an early test asserted that transmission rises with pressure. That is backwards — pressure broadening widens lines and fills the gaps between them, so higher pressure means more absorption and lower transmission. The module was right and the test was wrong.

Accuracy bug found later by the DYCOMS-II RF01 case, and fixed. The version of Phase 32 that shipped first had two compensating errors in the 8-12 μm atmospheric window: water-vapour line strengths roughly 50× too large, and a water-vapour continuum roughly 15× too weak. Those two cancel almost exactly for one particular column, so the US Standard Atmosphere broadband fluxes in the table above looked fine — which is precisely why a single broadband number is not an adequate test of a band model.

They do not cancel in general, because the two terms scale differently: line absorption grows as $\sqrt{ku}$ in the strong-line limit, while the continuum grows as $u\,(e + \gamma p_{dry})$ — linear in amount and in water partial pressure. Applied to the dry 4.2 kg/m² column above the DYCOMS RF01 cloud top, the window emissivity came out at 0.38 where it should be about 0.12, adding ~27 W/m² of spurious downwelling and cutting the cloud-top radiative cooling — the single term that drives the entire RF01 boundary layer — nearly in half.

The fix replaces the ad-hoc continuum with the Roberts, Selby & Biberman (1976) parameterisation, which is calibrated against measurement and has the correct self/foreign split (γ = 0.002, so the self-continuum dominates wherever there is appreciable water), and rebalances the window line strengths against it. See examples/validation/dycoms_rf01_radiation.jl.

The lesson is recorded here because it generalises: a band model must be validated across columns that differ in absorber amount and pressure, not against a single atmosphere. Compensating errors are invisible to the latter and obvious to the former.

Phase 33 — Two-stream solver with cloud optics ✅ SHIPPED

src/TwoStream.jl. Three two-stream closures behind one interface — delta-Eddington, PIFM (Zdunkowski) and Quadrature — using the Meador & Weaver (1980) layer reflectance/transmittance coefficients, an adding/doubling column solver, maximum-random cloud overlap, and parameterised Mie cloud optics in terms of liquid/ice water path and effective radius. Full cloud-radiative feedback on the thermodynamics via radiative_heating!.

Numerical care that mattered. Meador & Weaver eq. 14 as written contains e^{kτ} factors that overflow for optically thick layers. The implementation divides numerator and denominator through by e^{kτ} analytically, so the expression is evaluated entirely in terms of e^{-kτ} ≤ 1 and nothing overflows even at τ = 10⁴.

The longwave layer source is linear in τ rather than constant. Measured against a sub-layered reference of 180.66 W/m², the linear form gives an error of 0.55 where the constant form gives 3.30 — a 6× accuracy gain for no extra cost.

Phase 34 — 3-D Monte-Carlo radiative transfer ✅ SHIPPED

Photon-tracing solver that handles the genuinely 3-D radiative environment of an urban canyon or a broken cloud field: side illumination, inter-reflection, and shadowing without the one-dimensional plane-parallel assumption. Runs as a periodic correction to the two-stream solution. Validated by: I3RC intercomparison cases; comparison of canyon absorption against the two-stream + SVF approximation.

Era XII — Kilometre-scale regional and climate modelling

A future direction, not part of the decade plan above (tracking issue #289). Through Era XI Aeolus is a large-eddy model: grid spacings of metres to tens of metres over a few kilometres, with the mesoscale entering from outside (Phase 68). Era XII would make Aeolus itself a convection-permitting regional model at about 3 km, in the class of ICON-LAM, COSMO-CLM and REMO-NH, so that one code and one physics library cover the scales from a European domain down to a Berlin street canyon.

Why it sits here. Every piece of it depends on something earlier:

  • the cost needs Era X (MPI and GPU);
  • the driving data needs Era VIII (reanalysis readers, Phase 67) and the nesting interface of Phase 68 (#58, and the Berlin nesting epic #246);
  • long-run conservation needs the full-state checkpointing of #245;
  • the grey-zone physics needs the Era VI closures to blend into.

A 3 km domain the size of EURO-CORDEX is roughly 1500 × 1500 × 60 ≈ 1.4 × 10⁸ cells. On the present single-threaded solver one time step would take of order 10 s, and a 30-year run of order a decade of single-core wall time before any physics. This is an HPC-only capability.

What changes in kind, not degree. At 3 km the model no longer resolves the energetic boundary-layer eddies, so the LES closures stop being valid. The full troposphere and the orography enter the dynamics, and the domain is a piece of a sphere driven at its edges. The phases below address each of these. A phase is done by the same standard as every other era: implemented to the reference literature, validated, stable at production resolution.

Phase 101 — Compressible non-hydrostatic dynamical core

A fully compressible core for a deep atmosphere (about 30 km):

  • split-explicit acoustic substepping with a horizontally explicit, vertically implicit treatment (Wicker & Skamarock 2002; Klemp, Skamarock & Dudhia 2007);
  • the same physics interface as the Boussinesq and anelastic cores, so every physics module runs on all three.

Validated by: density current (Straka et al. 1993); inertia-gravity waves in a channel (Skamarock & Klemp 1994); warm bubble; a baroclinic-wave test in a channel against the published reference.

Phase 102 — Orography in the core

A terrain-following core on the existing TerrainFollowingGrid (Phase 24), using a smooth-level coordinate (SLEVE; Schär et al. 2002) with metric terms and controlled pressure-gradient errors over steep slopes. Cut cells (Phase 21) are the alternative for very steep relief.

Validated by: Schär et al. (2002) mountain-wave test; a resting atmosphere over steep idealised terrain (spurious wind below a stated bound); a flow-over-hill case against linear theory.

Phase 103 — Grids on the sphere: one abstraction, several grid types

Several grid types behind one grid abstraction, so that the dynamical core and every physics module run unchanged on any of them. The grids chosen for regional work must also be usable, later, for global models.

  • The abstraction. Operators are written against general curvilinear metric terms: cell areas and volumes, edge lengths, the local basis and its rotation, map factors, latitude for Coriolis, and deep-atmosphere radius factors. The topology is a set of logically rectangular panels with explicit connectivity, so that halo exchange crosses panel edges (with the vector-component rotation done there) as it later crosses MPI ranks (Phase 80). The existing Cartesian box becomes one panel with trivial metrics, so the urban LES is the special case, not a separate code path.
  • Rotated-pole latitude–longitude (one panel), the REMO and COSMO-CLM choice for regional climate. It is the primary regional grid, because it keeps the staggered C-grid stencils and the KernelAbstractions kernels of #291.
  • Conformal map projections (Lambert conformal, polar stereographic, Mercator; one panel each), as in WRF, through map factors.
  • Cubed sphere (six panels, equiangular gnomonic). Global. Its panels are structured, so the same stencils and kernels apply, with rotated halos at the cube edges. A regional domain can be a subset of panels or a single stretched panel (Schmidt transform), so regional and global runs share one grid family. The spectral-element backend (Phase 86) on curved elements gives a high-order path on the same grid; ClimaAtmos and HOMME use that approach.
  • Unstructured icosahedral or Voronoi grids (ICON, MPAS): an explicit later option, not part of this phase. It needs unstructured operators (no i/j/k stencils) and would most naturally come through the DG and SEM backends (Phases 86–87). The abstraction keeps the door open by not baking i/j/k indexing into the physics interfaces (column physics already works per column).

Validated by:

  • the Williamson et al. (1992) shallow-water test suite on the cubed sphere, which is global: solid-body advection over the poles and across panel edges, and steady geostrophic flow, with errors and convergence order quoted;
  • solid-body advection across the rotated pole, and geostrophic balance preserved on the rotated and projected grids;
  • a baroclinic wave (DCMIP-style) on the cubed sphere, with the regional subset against the global run;
  • the Cartesian box unchanged bit for bit when run through the abstraction.

Phase 104 — Lateral and top boundary driving

  • Davies (1976) relaxation zones at the lateral boundaries.
  • Upper-boundary damping toward the driver.
  • Optional spectral nudging of the large scales (von Storch et al. 2000).
  • Driving from ERA5 and from CMIP-class global model output, built on the boundary-data contract of the nesting epic (#246) and the Era VIII readers.
  • Mass and energy consistency of the boundary fluxes.

Validated by: a perfect-model "big brother" experiment (Denis et al. 2002); driving drift and boundary noise quantified.

Phase 105 — Grey-zone boundary-layer turbulence

A scale-aware boundary-layer scheme, for example an eddy-diffusivity mass-flux (EDMF) TKE closure (Siebesma et al. 2007), blended by grid spacing with the Era VI LES closures (Honnert, Masson & Couvreux 2011; Boutle et al. 2014). The same model then runs continuously from LES to 3 km, without switching physics by hand.

Validated by: the resolution dependence of a convective boundary layer from 100 m to 3 km against coarse-grained LES; GABLS1 and a shallow-cumulus case (BOMEX) at mesoscale resolution.

Phase 106 — Convection and clouds at kilometre scale

  • Scale-aware shallow convection, and an optional deep-convection scheme for grids coarser than about 5 km.
  • Subgrid cloud cover from a statistical cloud scheme.
  • The Era IV microphysics (two-moment, P3) and Era VII aerosol–cloud coupling, at kilometre scale.

Validated by: an idealised supercell; radiative–convective equilibrium; the diurnal cycle of convective precipitation (timing and intensity) against radar.

Phase 107 — Land surface and hydrology for climate time scales

Multi-year soil hydrology with groundwater, frozen soil and snow, river routing, lakes, vegetation phenology, and land-use tiles at kilometre scale (SurfaceTiles), built on the Era V land modules.

Validated by: multi-year eddy-covariance sites (FLUXNET); river discharge at gauged catchments; snow-cover duration.

Phase 108 — Radiative forcing for climate

  • Greenhouse-gas concentration scenarios.
  • Aerosol climatologies, for example MACv2-SP (Stevens et al. 2017).
  • Ozone and solar variability.
  • Sea-surface temperature and sea ice from the driver or from the ocean mixed layer.
  • A radiation call frequency with a stated error.

Validated by: offline radiative-forcing comparison against line-by-line reference cases; top-of-atmosphere and surface energy budgets against satellite and surface-radiation-network climatologies.

Phase 109 — Long-run conservation and production output

  • Domain mass, water and energy budgets closed and monitored over decades.
  • Drift detection.
  • Bit-reproducible restarts (building on #245).
  • Output in the CORDEX data-request conventions, written by the in-package NetCDF writer.

Validated by: a 10-year run with stated budget closure and no unexplained drift.

Phase 110 — Convection-permitting evaluation

A multi-year ERA5-driven hindcast at about 3 km over Central Europe and the Alps, in the protocol of the CORDEX Flagship Pilot Study on convective phenomena (Coppola et al. 2020; Ban et al. 2021). It is evaluated against gridded observations, radar and hourly station precipitation, with emphasis on extremes, and set beside the published convection-permitting ensemble (COSMO-CLM, ICON, REMO, WRF).

Validated by: skill within the spread of that ensemble for the diurnal cycle, the seasonal precipitation climatology and hourly extremes.

Phase 111 — Scale-seamless nesting

Online multi-level nesting inside one Aeolus run, from the 3 km regional domain through about 1 km to the urban LES of the Berlin case. Physics changes consistently with grid spacing through Phase 105, and there is two-way coupling with the urban scale. The Cartesian urban child sits in the curved parent as a local tangent plane, with the interpolation between the two geometries (metric terms, vector rotation) done in the nesting operators. At about 1 km the tangent-plane error is negligible, but it is designed in and tested, not assumed.

Validated by: the urban-scale result against the offline-driven Berlin case (#246); conservation across all levels.

Phase 112 — Climate-change downscaling

  • Scenario runs driven by CMIP-class global models.
  • The pseudo-global-warming method (Schär et al. 1996).
  • Ensembles with the Era IX uncertainty machinery.
  • Urban climate projections, for example Berlin heat-wave statistics, from the full chain down to street scale.

Validated by: historical-period skill (Phase 110) and a documented uncertainty budget for the projected change.

Era XII milestone. Aeolus is a convection-permitting regional climate model, evaluated against the published ensemble, that nests seamlessly down to its own urban LES.

Cross-cutting commitments

These apply to every phase above, not to any one of them:

  • Validation first. A phase starts by identifying its reference data and ends by reporting skill against it. No phase ships on "it runs and looks plausible".
  • No silent approximations. Every parameterisation documents its assumptions, its validity range, and what it omits. Where a cheaper option exists it is selectable, never the silent default.
  • Regression-tested. The benchmark suite (Phases 71–72) runs in CI from Era VIII onward; earlier eras add their validation cases to it as they land.
  • Performance budget. Each phase records its cost. A phase that makes the flagship run more than ~20 % slower needs either an optimisation plan or an opt-in flag.
  • Docs as first-class. Theory page, API reference, and a worked example per phase.

Honest assessment

This is a decade of work and it assumes sustained effort, domain expertise across meteorology, cloud physics, chemistry, turbulence and numerical analysis, and access to reference data and HPC resources. A solo contributor should expect to reach the end of Era IV in that window, not the end of Era XI.

The ordering is the important part. Physics and numerical fidelity come first because everything downstream — assimilation, ML, applications — inherits their errors. Performance comes late deliberately: there is no value in efficiently computing the wrong answer, and optimising before the physics settles freezes interfaces that still need to change.

Era XII changes what kind of model Aeolus is: from a large-eddy model with the mesoscale entering at its boundaries to a regional model that contains its own mesoscale. Projects of that kind (ICON, COSMO-CLM, REMO) are built and maintained by institutional teams over many years. It belongs on the roadmap because the payoff, one physics library from the regional domain down to the street canyon, is distinctive. It should be started only once Eras VIII and X are shipped, and broken into issues only when it comes into view.

Era XII is deliberately not a global climate model. But its grid abstraction (Phase 103) is designed so a global one stays possible: the cubed sphere is global from the start, and the panel and metric abstraction leaves room for unstructured grids later. Global context in the meantime comes from coupling to existing global models (Phase 98) and from lateral driving (Phase 104).
