Pluggable pressure solvers

The pressure-Poisson solve is the single most consequential part of an incompressible LES code:

  • It runs once per time step (more, with a projected RK3 scheme).
  • Its cost dominates the run.
  • It is the piece most likely to change when boundary conditions or grid geometry change.

Aeolus wraps it in an AbstractPressureSolver interface so different solvers can be swapped in without touching the fractional-step driver.

The interface

abstract type AbstractPressureSolver end

solve!(p::Field{CellCenter}, source::Field{CellCenter}, solver::AbstractPressureSolver)

That's the entire contract. A solver is constructed once — typically at simulation setup — and reused every time step. solve! populates the interior of p from the interior of source; halos are left untouched.

Current: FFTPoissonSolver

The only shipped implementation is FFTPoissonSolver: direct solve via real FFT on a triply-periodic RegularGrid. See Discrete Poisson & FFT for the derivation.

At construction:

  • Preallocate a real buffer (Nx, Ny, Nz) and a complex buffer (Nx÷2+1, Ny, Nz).
  • Plan a real forward FFT (plan_rfft) and a real inverse FFT (plan_irfft).
  • Precompute the discrete-Laplacian eigenvalues on the complex-buffer grid, storing their reciprocals. The (0,0,0) mode's reciprocal is stored as 0 — this projects out the zero-mean component.

At each solve! call:

  • Copy interior of source into the real buffer.
  • Forward FFT.
  • Multiply element-wise by the precomputed reciprocal eigenvalues.
  • Inverse FFT.
  • Copy into the interior of p.

No heap allocation. The forward+inverse FFT pair dominates the cost: O(Nx Ny Nz log(Nx Ny Nz)).

On threads (KA-h, #298). The copies, the eigenvalue multiply and the normalisation are pointwise launch! kernels, bit-identical to the broadcasts they replaced. The transforms are executed from the host between launches through apply_transform!, never inside a kernel, so a GPU backend can substitute its own FFT library. FFTW's plans are threaded (init_threads! sets FFTW to Threads.nthreads()) and give the same bits at every thread count under ESTIMATE or imported wisdom. The stretched solver's tridiagonal solve in z is one launch over slabs of ky rows, serial in z within each work item. Measured speed-ups: The kernel port.

Planned solvers

CosineFFTPoissonSolver (Phase 1b — shipped)

For channel-like domains — periodic in x, y, walls in z. Uses a Type-II discrete cosine transform in z (matches the pressure-Neumann BC at the walls) combined with a real FFT in x, y. Cost is still O(N log N). Passes the manufactured-Poisson test to machine precision; see Discrete Poisson & FFT.

Selecting it is a constructor-time decision:

solver = CosineFFTPoissonSolver(mesh)
sim    = BoussinesqSimulation(mesh; solver = solver, θ_bcs = θ_bcs, bcs = bcs)

The rest of the driver is unchanged. This unlocks the Dry Convective Boundary Layer benchmark and everything downstream in Phase 1b.

Per-axis boundary kinds (#251)

CosineFFTPoissonSolver(mesh; bc_x = :periodic, bc_y = :periodic, bc_z = :neumann): every axis is :periodic (rfft) or :neumann (DCT-II). Neumann sides are an option of this one type, not a new solver — one source of truth for the transform solve. The default keeps the channel plans, eigenvalues and normalisation bit for bit. poisson_axis_bcs(solver) reports the axes of any solver (FFT, cosine, CG, multigrid, MG-CG, density-weighted CG).

Choosing the axes from the boundary conditions

The pressure condition is not chosen separately: it follows from the velocity faces. BoundaryConditions.axis_poisson_bc(face) returns :periodic for Periodic and :neumann for every face on which the normal velocity is prescribed (ImpermeableWall, NoSlipWall, NoFlux, and every AbstractOpenBoundary: FreeStreamBoundary and the data-driven OpenBoundary of N-open-b). A face type with no method throws, so a solver is never silently built for the wrong problem. PressureSolvers.poisson_axis_bcs(bcs) pairs the two faces of each axis and rejects periodic-against-Neumann.

Simulations._default_solver(mesh::RegularGrid, bcs) uses it: triply periodic gives FFTPoissonSolver, anything else CosineFFTPoissonSolver(mesh; poisson_axis_bcs(bcs)...) — the channel as before, the all-Neumann box for a nested child. The sound-proof cores pass the same axes to their DirectWeightedPoissonSolver (or DensityWeightedPoissonSolver), and the Berlin :pressure_solver providers (src/components/urban/pressure_solver.jl) build every solver from the case's bcs the same way, with a smoke check that the axes match.

CGPoissonSolver (Phase 3)

Matrix-free conjugate gradient on the same 7-point operator, periodic or Neumann per axis (bc_x, bc_y, bc_bottom, bc_top). Until #251 it stored bc_xy but always wrapped x and y periodically — a code bug that silently returned the periodic solution for bc_xy = :neumann. Intended for immersed boundaries, cut cells — anything where a direct transform-based solver fails. Cost per solve is O(N · √cond) ≈ O(N^{4/3}) for a Laplacian with mild preconditioning. The preconditioner will start as a simple diagonal scaling and can be upgraded to geometric multigrid.

A solve allocates nothing (#295): the source, the iterate and the CG work arrays live in the solver, as they do in MultigridPoissonSolver (source buffer) and MGPreconditionedCG.

One CG loop (KA-g, #300). CGPoissonSolver, MGPreconditionedCG, DensityWeightedPoissonSolver and the implicit diffusion of the IMEX steppers all run pcg! in Backends. A solver supplies only its operator (a point stencil, PointOperator, or a function such as the multigrid fine-grid operator), its preconditioner (none, a pointwise Jacobi step, or a V-cycle), whether the constants are projected out, and its pinned rows. The CG and density-weighted solvers keep their iterates in arrays with one halo layer that fill_unit_halo! fills (periodic wrap or Neumann mirror) before each operator application, so the stencil carries no wrap-around index arithmetic. The dot products are canonical plane sums, so a solve and its iteration count are identical at every thread count. Iteration counts, timings and the one-time last-bit change are in The kernel port (section KA-g).

The V-cycle on the kernel layer (KA-i, #303). The multigrid smoother, residual, restriction and prolongation are launch!es of pointwise bodies with the bits of the old loops. The red-black smoother makes one launch per colour over that colour's cells only. The means and norms of the null-space projection and of the convergence test are canonical plane sums, so MultigridPoissonSolver and the V-cycle preconditioner of MGPreconditionedCG give the same solution and cycle count at every thread count. Levels below the launch threshold (32 768 points), including the coarsest solve, run serially. Timings, the per-level split and the last-bit change are in The kernel port (section KA-i).

Column-separable operators: ColumnTridiagonalSolver (#309)

Two operators of the code have uniform horizontal spacing and coefficients that depend on height only: the conservative stretched-z Laplacian of the Boussinesq core on a StretchedGrid (#151) and the density-weighted projection operator ∇·(w(z)∇φ) of the anelastic and pseudo-incompressible cores (w = ρ̄ or ρ̄θ̄). Both are

\[h_k\,(δ_x^2 + δ_y^2)φ_k + a_k(φ_{k-1} - φ_k) + c_k(φ_{k+1} - φ_k) = b_k ,\]

which the horizontal real FFT (periodic side) or DCT-II (Neumann side) reduces to one tridiagonal system per wavenumber (Schumann & Sweet 1988). ColumnTridiagonalSolver is the one implementation: transform, Thomas sweep with the factorisation stored at construction (a launch over slabs of ky rows, see above), the singular mean mode integrated as a column flux, inverse transform. StretchedGrids.StretchedPoissonSolver (h = 1) and Anelastic.DirectWeightedPoissonSolver (h = w_c, a_k = w_f[k]/Δz²) only build its coefficients; the latter is the default projection of the sound-proof cores (pressure_solver = :direct), with DensityWeightedPoissonSolver (CG, pressure_solver = :cg) kept for a periodic column and as the independent check.

Measured (#309, one thread, isothermal 300 K base state over 3 km, random source; CG at the cores' tolerances):

The direct solution satisfies the discrete equation to a relative residual of ~5e-16 and agrees with a tol-1e-14 CG to ~2e-13 (test/anelastic.jl); the weighted constraint after a step is at round-off (~2e-16 s⁻¹ against ~8e-15 s⁻¹ for the CG at tol 1e-12). A solve allocates nothing.

SEMPoissonSolver (Phase 5)

For the spectral-element backend. Element-local mass is not diagonal (GLL weights per element), so a matrix-free CG or a block-Jacobi multigrid is needed. This is what NekRS-family codes use.

What Simulations.step! sees

The driver holds only sim.solver::AbstractPressureSolver. Its call site is a single line:

solve!(sim.φ, sim.div_buf, sim.solver)

No conditional; no interface knowledge. Swapping solvers is a constructor-time decision.

# Default: FFT, triply periodic
sim1 = BoussinesqSimulation(mesh; ν=1e-2)

# Custom solver — e.g. a future channel solver
solver = CosineFFTPoissonSolver(mesh)
sim2   = BoussinesqSimulation(mesh; ν=1e-2, solver=solver)

step! is bit-identical between the two.

Testing new solvers

The unit-test recipe for any new solver is the same as for FFT:

  1. Manufactured solution. Prescribe an analytic φ, apply the discrete Laplacian kernel to get source = L_h φ, call solve! and check ‖φ_num − φ‖∞ < 10⁻¹⁰.
  2. Divergence-removal. Fill u, v, w with random values, call the full projection step, check ‖∇·u‖∞ < 10⁻¹⁰.

Both patterns are in test/runtests.jl for the FFT solver.

When not to build a new solver

If you only need to change boundary conditions on u, v, w (e.g. add a slip wall) but keep the domain topology, extending BoundaryConditions is enough — the pressure BC follows automatically from the discrete Laplacian used in projection. New pressure solvers only earn their keep when the domain topology, BC type on pressure itself, or grid geometry actually changes.
