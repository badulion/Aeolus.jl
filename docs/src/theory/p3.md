# P3 microphysics

The Predicted Particle Properties (P3) scheme of Morrison & Milbrandt
(2015) represents all ice by a single category whose bulk properties —
rime fraction, rime density, and hence the mass–dimension relation — are
prognosed rather than fixed by assigning particles to "cloud ice",
"snow", "graupel" or "hail". This page develops the pieces as they are
implemented in `Aeolus.P3Microphysics` (Phase 40); the API is in
[Era IV — microphysics](../api/era4.md).

## Gamma size distributions and their moments

Every hydrometeor population in P3 is a complete gamma distribution in
maximum dimension ``D``,

```math
N(D) = N_0\, D^{\mu}\, e^{-\lambda D},
```

with intercept ``N_0`` (m``^{-4-\mu}`` for number per m³ of air;
kg``^{-1}``m``^{-1-\mu}`` for number per kg of air, as P3 carries it),
shape ``\mu`` and slope ``\lambda`` (m``^{-1}``). Its
``k``-th moment is

```math
M_k = \int_0^\infty D^k N(D)\,dD = N_0\,\frac{\Gamma(\mu+k+1)}{\lambda^{\mu+k+1}} ,
```

so the number is ``M_0``, the mass of spheres of density ``\rho`` is
``(\pi/6)\rho M_3``, and the Rayleigh reflectivity factor of liquid
spheres is ``M_6``. Given two moments ``M_i, M_j`` and a fixed ``\mu``,
the slope follows from their ratio,

```math
\lambda = \left[\frac{M_i}{M_j}\,
          \frac{\Gamma(\mu+j+1)}{\Gamma(\mu+i+1)}\right]^{1/(j-i)},
```

and ``N_0 = M_0 \lambda^{\mu+1}/\Gamma(\mu+1)``.

### Truncated moments

P3's mass–dimension relation ``m(D)`` is a *piecewise* power law, with
different prefactors and exponents on either side of the thresholds
``D_\mathrm{th}``, ``D_\mathrm{gr}``, ``D_\mathrm{cr}``. Every bulk
integral therefore splits into moments truncated to one regime:

```math
\int_{D_1}^{D_2} D^k N(D)\,dD
  = N_0\,\frac{\Gamma(a)}{\lambda^{a}}
    \left[P(a,\lambda D_2) - P(a,\lambda D_1)\right],
  \qquad a = \mu+k+1,
```

where ``P(a,x) = \gamma(a,x)/\Gamma(a)`` is the regularised lower
incomplete gamma function and ``Q = 1 - P`` its complement. When the
interval sits in the upper tail (``\lambda D_1 > a``) the difference is
formed as ``Q(a,\lambda D_1) - Q(a,\lambda D_2)`` so that a small tail
contribution does not vanish in the cancellation of two numbers near one.

### Numerical evaluation

`SpecialFunctions` does not build on every target, so the functions are
implemented in-package following Press et al. (2007, *Numerical Recipes*
3rd ed., §6.1–6.2):

* ``\ln\Gamma(x)`` by the 14-term Lanczos approximation for ``x \ge 1/2``
  and Euler's reflection ``\Gamma(x)\Gamma(1-x) = \pi/\sin \pi x`` below;
  ``\Gamma`` at positive integers is the exact factorial product.
* ``P(a,x)`` by its power series for ``x < a+1`` and ``Q(a,x)`` by its
  continued fraction, evaluated with the modified Lentz algorithm,
  otherwise. The other value is the complement. The tail that can
  become very small (``P`` for ``x \ll a``, ``Q`` for ``x \gg a``) is
  therefore always computed directly and keeps its relative accuracy.
  One limitation: for ``a < 1`` and ``x < 1``, ``Q`` is of order ``a``
  and is formed as ``1-P``. Its relative error is then about
  ``\epsilon/Q``: 1.8e-12 at ``a = 10^{-3}``, 4.2e-9 at
  ``a = 10^{-6}``. P3 moments have ``a = \mu+k+1 \ge 1``, so they never
  reach this range.

wide, ``P(a,x_2) - P(a,x_1)`` is pure rounding and could otherwise come
out negative, down to ``-1.5\times10^{-14} M_k``.

## Mass–dimension relation and predicted rime density

P3 carries four ice prognostics per kg of air: total ice mass ``q_i``,
number ``N_i``, rime mass ``q_\mathrm{rim}`` and rime volume
``B_\mathrm{rim}``. Two ratios follow,

```math
F_\mathrm{rim} = \frac{q_\mathrm{rim}}{q_i}, \qquad
\rho_\mathrm{rim} = \frac{q_\mathrm{rim}}{B_\mathrm{rim}} ,
```

with ``\rho_\mathrm{rim}`` bounded to 50–900 kg m``^{-3}``. Carrying
the *volume* means density is never combined by a rule: accreting rime
mass ``\delta q`` at density ``\rho_\mathrm{new}`` adds
``\delta q/\rho_\mathrm{new}`` to ``B_\mathrm{rim}``, and the result is
exactly the mass-weighted harmonic mean of the parts.

### Four regimes

| regime | range | ``m(D)`` | ``A(D)`` |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| small spherical ice | ``D \le D_\mathrm{th}`` | ``\tfrac{\pi}{6}\rho_i D^3`` | ``\tfrac{\pi}{4}D^2`` |
| dense nonspherical (unrimed) | ``D_\mathrm{th} < D \le D_\mathrm{gr}`` | ``\alpha_{va} D^{\beta_{va}}`` | ``\gamma D^{\sigma}`` |
| graupel | ``D_\mathrm{gr} < D \le D_\mathrm{cr}`` | ``\tfrac{\pi}{6}\rho_g D^3`` | ``\tfrac{\pi}{4}D^2`` |
| partially rimed | ``D > D_\mathrm{cr}`` | ``\frac{\alpha_{va}}{1-F_\mathrm{rim}} D^{\beta_{va}}`` | ``F_\mathrm{rim}\tfrac{\pi}{4}D^2 + (1-F_\mathrm{rim})\gamma D^{\sigma}`` |

with ``\rho_i = 917`` kg m``^{-3}``, ``\alpha_{va} = 0.0121`` kg
m``^{-\beta}``, ``\beta_{va} = 1.9`` (Brown & Francis 1995, as
corrected by Hogan et al. 2012) and ``\gamma = 0.1315``,
``\sigma = 1.88`` (Mitchell 1996). For unrimed ice only the first two
regimes exist. Each threshold is where the neighbouring masses agree,
so ``m(D)`` is continuous:

```math
D_\mathrm{th} = \left(\frac{\pi\rho_i}{6\alpha_{va}}\right)^{1/(\beta_{va}-3)}
\approx 66\ \mu\mathrm{m},\quad
D_\mathrm{gr} = \left(\frac{6\alpha_{va}}{\pi\rho_g}\right)^{1/(3-\beta_{va})},\quad
D_\mathrm{cr} = \left(\frac{6\alpha_{va}}{\pi\rho_g(1-F_\mathrm{rim})}\right)^{1/(3-\beta_{va})}.
```

The area is *not* continuous at the thresholds (the disc and aggregate
relations are independent fits), so neither is the single-particle fall
speed; this is as in Morrison & Milbrandt (2015).

### Graupel density in closed form

Graupel is rime plus the unrimed mass that filled in around it,
``\rho_g = F_\mathrm{rim}\rho_\mathrm{rim} + (1-F_\mathrm{rim})\rho_d``,
where ``\rho_d`` is the mean aggregate density over
``[D_\mathrm{gr}, D_\mathrm{cr}]``. Morrison & Milbrandt, and the P3
reference code, solve this coupled system by fixed-point iteration. It
is linear in ``\rho_g``: with ``r = D_\mathrm{cr}/D_\mathrm{gr} =
(1-F_\mathrm{rim})^{-1/(3-\beta)}``, ``\rho_d = g\,\rho_g`` where

```math
g = \frac{r^{\beta-2}-1}{(\beta-2)(r-1)}
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\rho_g = \frac{F_\mathrm{rim}\,\rho_\mathrm{rim}}{1-(1-F_\mathrm{rim})\,g}.
```

`p3_mass_dimension` evaluates this directly, summing the numerator's
series for small ``\ln r`` because its ``O(1)`` and ``O(\ln r)`` terms
cancel exactly, and with the common factor ``\ln r`` divided out
analytically so that nothing underflows even at subnormal
``F_\mathrm{rim}``. It agrees with a 256-bit fixed-point iteration to
5.1e-16. Two limits follow. As ``F_\mathrm{rim}\to 0^+``,
``\rho_g \to \tfrac23\rho_\mathrm{rim}`` for any ``\beta``, and the
graupel regime shrinks to zero width. At ``F_\mathrm{rim} = 1``,
``\rho_g = \rho_\mathrm{rim}`` and ``D_\mathrm{cr} = \infty``. Because
``g \le 1``, ``\rho_g \le \rho_\mathrm{rim} \le \rho_i``, which gives
the ordering ``D_\mathrm{th} \le D_\mathrm{gr} \le D_\mathrm{cr}``.

### Size distribution and slope

The mean particle mass of ``N(D)\propto D^\mu e^{-\lambda D}`` is a sum
of one truncated gamma moment per regime (see above), so it is exact to
incomplete-gamma accuracy. It decreases strictly in ``\lambda``, and
`p3_slope` inverts it for ``\lambda`` given ``q_i/N_i`` with ``\mu``
fixed (regula falsi in ``\log\lambda``). The round trip recovers
``\lambda`` to 1.1e-14 over 225 cases. MM15 diagnose ``\mu`` from
``\lambda`` (Heymsfield 2003); that coupling is left to the caller.

### Fall speed

Single-particle fall speeds follow Mitchell & Heymsfield (2005), the
formulation behind the P3 lookup tables. From the Best number
``X = 2 m g \rho_a D^2/(A\eta^2)``,

```math
Re = \frac{\delta_0^2}{4}\left[\left(1+\frac{4\sqrt{X}}{\delta_0^2\sqrt{C_0}}\right)^{1/2}-1\right]^2 - a_0X^{b_0},
\qquad V = \frac{\eta\,Re}{\rho_a D},
```

with ``\delta_0 = 5.83``, ``C_0 = 0.6`` and ``\eta`` from Sutherland's
law. The turbulence correction ``a_0X^{b_0}`` is off by default
(``a_0 = b_0 = 0``), as in the P3 reference lookup-table code. MH05 fit
``a_0 = 1.7\times10^{-3}``, ``b_0 = 0.8`` to aggregates; applied to
dense graupel it outgrows the first term, and 900 kg m``^{-3}`` graupel
would peak at 11 m s``^{-1}`` near 2 cm and fall at zero speed from
5 cm. Without it, ``V(D)`` of such graupel rises monotonically (14.4 m
s``^{-1}`` at 1 cm, 33.8 m s``^{-1}`` at 5 cm, 600 hPa). The price is the
known Stokes-limit bias of the MH05 fit, ``Re \to X/(\delta_0^2C_0)``,
17.7 % above ``X/24``. The bulk
number- and mass-weighted speeds ``V_N``, ``V_M`` are integrated by
composite Gauss–Legendre quadrature, with panels split at the regime
thresholds.

### Continuity: no category jump

The point of P3 is that bulk properties change continuously as ice
rimes. We sweep ``F_\mathrm{rim}`` from 0 to 1 at
``\rho_\mathrm{rim}`` = 50, 400 and 900 kg m``^{-3}``, both at fixed
``\lambda`` and at fixed ``q_i/N_i``. The largest relative step in mean
mass, bulk density or ``\lambda`` between neighbouring samples is
1.6e-2 at ``\Delta F = 10^{-3}`` and 1.6e-3 at ``\Delta F = 10^{-4}``:
it shrinks in proportion to the spacing, so the curves have no jump.
The ``\rho_\mathrm{rim}`` sweep behaves the same way (1.5e-2 and
1.5e-3), and so do ``V_N``, ``V_M`` (1.1e-2 and 1.1e-3 at
``\Delta F`` = 2.5e-3 and 2.5e-4). At fixed ``q_i/N_i = 10^{-7}`` kg,
full riming (``\rho_\mathrm{rim} = 400``) raises ``V_M`` from 1.31 to
3.02 m s``^{-1}``, the snow-to-graupel transition without a category
boundary.

## Process rates

Each microphysical process is written as **one non-negative rate** that
moves mass between exactly two species (vapour ``q_v``, cloud ``q_c``,
rain ``q_r``, ice ``q_i``, with the rime ``q_\mathrm{rim}`` a part of
``q_i``). `p3_tendencies` maps the rates linearly onto every prognostic
variable, so total water is conserved identically, and the heating is
fixed by which phases a rate connects:

```math
c_{pm}\frac{dT}{dt} = -L_v\frac{dq_v}{dt} + L_f\frac{dq_i}{dt},
```

that is ``L_s = L_v + L_f`` for deposition, ``L_f`` for riming,
``-L_f`` for melting and nothing for autoconversion or shedding. Latent
heats, saturation vapour pressures, ``c_{pm}`` and the saturation
adjustment are those of the `MoistThermodynamics` contract; vapour
diffusivity, thermal conductivity, the liquid growth resistances and the
drop fall speed are those of `Superdroplet`; the KK2000 autoconversion
is `AerosolCloud.autoconversion_rate`. The P3 part-file adds only the
integrals over the ice and rain size distributions and the bookkeeping
of number and rime.

### Condensation

Cloud water is condensed or evaporated by saturation adjustment with
respect to liquid (`MoistThermodynamics.saturation_adjustment` on the
``q_v + q_c`` subsystem, at constant ``\theta_{li}`` and pressure),
called with a copy of the constants whose liquid–ice ramp is disabled:
in P3, cloud water stays supercooled liquid and becomes ice only by
explicit processes. Because the adjustment conserves the Bryan–Fritsch
``\theta_{li}`` rather than isobaric enthalpy, ``c_{pm}\Delta T/(L_v
\Delta q_c)`` is 0.998 at 1000 hPa, 1.012 at 900 hPa and 1.064 at
600 hPa. The deviation comes from the moisture-dependent Exner exponent
inside ``\theta_{li}``. It is inherited from the contract rather than
patched locally. Rain and ice are not part of the adjusted subsystem, so
their heat capacity is missing from its ``c_{pm}``: the condensational
``\Delta T`` is too large by 1.5 % with 5 g kg``^{-1}`` of rain present
and by 0.5 % with 5 g kg``^{-1}`` of ice.

### Warm rain

The warm-rain processes follow **Khairoutdinov & Kogan (2000)**, the
formulation P3 uses:

```math
\left.\frac{\partial q_r}{\partial t}\right|_\mathrm{auto} = 1350\,q_c^{2.47}N_c^{-1.79},
\qquad
\left.\frac{\partial q_r}{\partial t}\right|_\mathrm{acc} = 67\,(q_c q_r)^{1.15},
```

(``N_c`` in cm``^{-3}``). Autoconversion is off below
``q_c = 10^{-8}`` kg kg``^{-1}`` and creates drizzle drops of 25 μm radius.
The measured exponents are 2.470000000000 and −1.790000000000.
Rain self-collection and breakup follow Seifert & Beheng (2001) with
the Seifert (2008) breakup efficiency, as in the P3 code:
``\partial N_r/\partial t = -\varphi\,k_{rr}\rho_a q_r N_r`` with
``k_{rr} = 5.78`` and ``\varphi = 2 - \exp(2300(D_m - 280\,\mu m))``
above 280 μm. Breakup balances self-collection at
``D_m = 581.4`` μm.

### Diffusional growth with ventilation

A particle of capacitance ``C`` grows as ``dm/dt = 4\pi C f_v S/(A+B)``.
Here ``S`` is the supersaturation over water (rain) or ice, and ``A``, ``B``
are the heat-conduction and vapour-diffusion resistances. For ice,
``C = D/2`` for spheres and graupel and ``0.48\,D/2`` for nonspherical
ice. The ventilation coefficient ``f_v(Sc^{1/3}Re^{1/2})`` is Hall &
Pruppacher (1976) for ice and Beard & Pruppacher (1971) for drops, in
their piecewise forms, which keep ``f_v \ge 1``. The population rate
``N\,4\pi\,E_N[C f_v]\,S/(A+B)`` is integrated over the P3 PSD, with
``Re`` from the Mitchell–Heymsfield fall speed.

The checks behave as expected. Small ice spheres (mean 3.3 μm) reproduce
the analytic ``N 2\pi(\mu+1)/\lambda \cdot S/(A+B)`` to 1.5e-4. Snow with
a 0.46 mm mean size is ventilated by a factor of 2.32. Near-monodisperse
40 μm drops evaporate at 1.0076 times the unventilated single-drop rate.
Ice grows by deposition only below 0 °C and when ``S_i > 0``. Sublimation
occurs only when ``S_i < 0`` and leaves the rime fraction and density
unchanged. Rain evaporates only when ``S_w < 0``.

### Riming, shedding and Cober & List rime density

Ice collects cloud water with the geometric kernel of the ice particle,
``\partial q_c/\partial t = -E_{ci}\rho_a q_c N_i E_N[AV]``, and rain with
``A|V_i - V_r|``. Below 0 °C the collected water becomes rime:
``q_\mathrm{rim}`` grows, and ``B_\mathrm{rim}`` grows by
``dq/\rho_\mathrm{rime}``. At or above 0 °C, collected cloud water is
shed as 1 mm rain drops. The density of cloud rime follows Cober & List
(1993) in the P3 form, as a function of the Macklin parameter
``R_i = -r_c[\mu m]\,V_\mathrm{impact}/T_s[°C]``:

```math
\rho_\mathrm{rim} = \begin{cases}
(0.051 + 0.114R_i - 0.0055R_i^2)\times10^3 & 1 \le R_i \le 8\\
611 + 72.25(R_i - 8) & 8 < R_i \le 12
\end{cases}
```

The formula is continuous at ``R_i = 8`` (611 kg m``^{-3}``). It is
bounded by 159.5 and 900 kg m``^{-3}``, inside the P3 limits of 50–900.
Rime from rain has a density of 900 kg m``^{-3}``.

### Melting

Above 0 °C, the heat conducted to the particle and the latent heat of
vapour diffusion, both ventilated, plus the sensible heat of collected
water, melt the ice (Mason 1971; Rutledge & Hobbs 1983):

```math
\frac{dq_i}{dt}\Big|_\mathrm{melt} = -\frac{N_i 4\pi E_N[Cf_v]\,[K_a(T-T_0) + L_v D_v(\rho_v - \rho_{vs}(T_0))] + c_w(T-T_0)(\mathrm{coll}_c + \mathrm{coll}_r)}{L_f}
```

The rate is floored at zero. In dry air at +0.5 °C (30 % RH),
evaporative cooling prevents melting. Each melted particle becomes one
drop, and the rime melts in proportion.

### The source-term step

`p3_step` evaluates the rates, limits them, applies a forward-Euler
update, cleans up and then adjusts to saturation. The limiter scales each
process by the factor for the one species it drains. The vapour processes
are also limited so that they cannot overshoot liquid or ice saturation.
This limit includes a psychrometric factor, and the saturation values are
taken after the step's riming and melting heat. Sublimation and rain
evaporation share the ice-saturation deficit (evaporation first, since
ice stops sublimating once the air reaches ice saturation), so the two
vapour sources together cannot overshoot it either.

## Sedimentation, size sorting and radar reflectivity

### One speed per moment

Each prognostic moment falls at its own bulk speed. Number falls at the
number-weighted ``V_N``, mass (and with it rime mass and rime volume) at
the mass-weighted ``V_M``, and a reflectivity moment at ``V_Z``:

```math
V_N = \frac{\int V N\,dD}{\int N\,dD},\qquad
V_M = \frac{\int V m N\,dD}{\int m N\,dD},\qquad
V_Z = \frac{\int V m^2 N\,dD}{\int m^2 N\,dD}.
```

``V(D)`` increases with ``D``, so ``V_N < V_M < V_Z`` for any broad size
distribution, and the three are equal only when all particles have the
same size. The gap ``V_M - V_N`` *is* size sorting. In a falling shaft,
mass runs ahead of number, so the leading edge carries a few large
particles and the small ones are left behind. For exponential rain with
``\lambda = 2000`` m``^{-1}`` the three speeds are 1.86, 6.04 and
8.01 m s``^{-1}``. As ``\mu \to \infty`` at a fixed mean size of 1 mm,
``(V_M-V_N)/V_N`` falls from 2.6e-2 (``\mu = 100``) to 2.6e-3
(``\mu = 1000``), the expected ``O(1/\mu)`` rate.

Ice speeds come from the Mitchell–Heymsfield fall speed above. Rain uses
the drop fall speed of `Superdroplet.terminal_velocity` (Stokes, then
Beard 1976, then Atlas et al. 1973), so the package has a single
raindrop fall speed. It is corrected for air density by
``(\rho_0/\rho_a)^{0.54}`` (Foote & du Toit 1969). The quadrature is
checked against a power law ``V = aD^b``, whose weighted speeds are
gamma-function ratios. They agree to 1e-12.

### Column scheme

The column is updated in flux form with first-order upwinding. Cell
``k = 1`` is at the surface. Over a sub-step ``h``,

```math
\rho_k\psi_k\Delta z_k \leftarrow \rho_k\psi_k\Delta z_k - hF_k + hF_{k+1},
\qquad F_k = \rho_k\psi_k V_k .
```

``h`` is chosen so that the Courant number ``V_k h/\Delta z_k`` stays at
or below 1 in every cell. This makes the scheme conservative and
non-negative. The outflow is also capped at the cell content, so
round-off cannot make a value negative. Fall speeds are recomputed at
every sub-step, as in the P3 reference code.

When a whole column is advanced in one 900 s step the Courant number
reaches 162; the scheme takes 162 sub-steps and conserves mass to 5.7e-16.
With a constant speed and a Courant number of exactly 1, it shifts a
profile by exactly one cell per sub-step. The ice column (``q_i``,
``q_\mathrm{rim}``, ``B_\mathrm{rim}``, ``N_i``) conserves all four
fields to 4.6e-16. It carries ``F_\mathrm{rim}`` and
``\rho_\mathrm{rim}`` unchanged to 1e-12, because rime mass and rime
volume fall with the mass.

Two-moment upwind sedimentation sorts sizes too strongly. At the leading
edge, number trails mass by whole cells, so a few cells hold mass with
almost no number. Their diagnosed drop size then has no upper bound. We
measured 4e4 m after 300 s of a 1 km rain layer (``q_r`` = 1 g kg``^{-1}``,
``N_r = 10^4`` kg``^{-1}``). P3 bounds this by limiting ``\lambda_r`` so
the mean size stays at or below 0.8 mm, and raises ``N_r`` to match
(`p3_rain_limit_number`). Mass stays exact and number is no longer
conserved. With the limiter, the mass-mean diameter falls steadily from
the leading edge to the top of the shaft: 1454, 1416, 1264, 1193, 941,
581, 302, 199 and 114 μm, sampled every 400 m (the initial value was
576 μm). A near-monodisperse shaft (``\mu_r = 2000``) keeps a uniform
size to 4.6e-3.

### Radar reflectivity

For rain, the Rayleigh reflectivity factor is the sixth moment,
``Z = \int D^6 N\,dD = N_0\Gamma(\mu+7)/\lambda^{\mu+7}``, reported in
mm``^6`` m``^{-3}``. It agrees with Simpson quadrature to 3e-14. Ice uses
the equivalent reflectivity (Smith 1984). A particle of mass ``m`` is
treated as a sphere of solid ice with the same mass:

```math
Z_e = \frac{|K_i|^2}{|K_w|^2}\left(\frac{6}{\pi\rho_\mathrm{ice}}\right)^2
      \int m(D)^2 N(D)\,dD,\qquad \frac{|K_i|^2}{|K_w|^2} = \frac{0.176}{0.93}.
```

A particle of density ``\rho_p`` therefore contributes
``(\rho_p/\rho_\mathrm{ice})^2`` of the ``D^6`` that a solid sphere of
the same size would. ``m^2`` is a piecewise power law, so ``Z_e`` is a
sum of truncated gamma moments with no quadrature. It matches quadrature
to 3.8e-15. Melting the same particles into drops raises ``Z`` by
exactly ``(|K_w|^2/|K_i|^2)(\rho_\mathrm{ice}/\rho_w)^{2}``, which is
+6.48 dB.

The standard check is a Marshall–Palmer distribution
(``N_0 = 8\times10^6`` m``^{-4}``), with the slope solved so that the rain
rate computed from our own fall speeds is 5 mm h``^{-1}``. That gives
33.93 dBZ, against 34.19 dBZ from the MP relation ``Z = 200R^{1.6}``. At
1, 10, 25 and 50 mm h``^{-1}`` the differences are +0.67, −0.59, −0.92
and −1.11 dB.

The target in the issue for this part was "a ~5 mm h⁻¹ rain rate gives
35–45 dBZ". The lower edge of that window sits above the standard Z–R
relations themselves: ``200\cdot5^{1.6} = 2627`` mm``^6`` m``^{-3}``
(34.2 dBZ) for Marshall–Palmer, and ``300\cdot5^{1.4} = 2856``
mm``^6`` m``^{-3}`` (34.6 dBZ) for the WSR-88D convective relation. The
expectation was wrong, not the code. The test therefore checks agreement
with the MP value to within 1 dB and ``30 < `` dBZ ``< 40``, rather than
the 35–45 window.

## Terminal-velocity spectrum and sub-grid precipitation fraction (Phase 44)

### No single fall speed

An audit of `src/` and `examples/` for precipitation that fell at one
speed found two places, both now delegating to the P3 rain functions
above (no new fall-speed law):

* **Kessler** (`apply_kessler_step!`, Phase 12) fell at a constant
  `Vt` (default 5 m s⁻¹; the examples used 3, 5 and 6 m s⁻¹), with a
  silent Courant cap of 0.5. Its default is now `Vt = :spectrum`: the
  single-moment rain is closed with the Marshall–Palmer spectrum that
  Kessler (1969) assumes, ``N(D) = N_0 e^{-\lambda D}``,
  ``N_0 = 8\times10^6`` m⁻⁴, so that

  ```math
  \lambda = \left(\frac{\pi \rho_w N_0}{\rho_a q_r}\right)^{1/4},
  \qquad N_r = \frac{N_0}{\lambda \rho_a},
  ```

  and each cell falls at the mass-weighted speed ``V_M`` of that
  spectrum (`kessler_fall_speed`, via
  `p3_rain_sedimentation_velocities`) through the sub-stepped,
  conservative `p3_sediment!`. ``V_M`` = 3.77, 5.66, 6.57 and
  7.48 m s⁻¹ at 0.1, 1, 3 and 10 g m⁻³ of rain, within 10 % of — and at
  1–3 g m⁻³ within 0.2 % of — the Klemp & Wilhelmson (1978) fit
  ``V = 36.34 (\rho_a q_r)^{0.1346}`` (``\rho_a q_r`` in g cm⁻³). A
  numeric `Vt` keeps the legacy kernel bit for bit.
* **The urban two-moment warm rain** (`examples/urban/moisture_addons.jl`)
  used one cell-wise speed ``6.5 (q_r \rho_a/N_r)^{1/6}`` with a
  4 m s⁻¹ floor, and its upwind update took the inflow from above at the
  *receiving* cell's Courant number, which does not conserve mass where
  the speed varies (+0.068 % in 20 s for a 9-level shaft). It now calls
  `p3_sediment_rain!` (mass at ``V_M``, number at ``V_N``).

Not changed, because they are not a single precipitation fall speed:
`Superdroplet` (each droplet at its own `terminal_velocity`), P3 ice
and rain (already per-moment), `inp_sediment!` (Stokes settling of
aerosol) and the aerosol `V_sed` of the urban air-quality add-on.

### Precipitation fraction down the column

The fraction ``f_p`` of a level covered by rain is the union of the rain
area arriving from above, ``P``, and the cloud of the level, ``c``
(where new rain forms), carried from the model top down
(`p3_precipitation_fraction`):

```math
\begin{aligned}
\text{maximum:}\quad & f_p = \max(P, c),\\
\text{random:}\quad & f_p = P + (1-P)\,c,\\
\text{maximum–random:}\quad & f_p = P + (1-P)\,\frac{\max(c,\tilde c) - \tilde c}{1-\tilde c},
\qquad \tilde c = \min(c_\text{above}, P),
\end{aligned}
```

the last being Geleyn & Hollingsworth (1979) as used for precipitation
by Jakob & Klein (2000) and the ECMWF IFS (Tiedtke 1993): maximum
overlap between vertically adjacent cloudy layers, random across clear
gaps. All three are nondecreasing in ``P`` and ordered
max ≤ max-random ≤ random at every level, hence all the way down (for
four gap-separated decks of 0.3, 0.4, 0.5, 0.2: 0.500 ≤ 0.832 ≤ 0.994 at
the surface). Where the rain above has evaporated completely the area is
reset; rain with neither cloud nor rain above it gets ``f_p = 1``.

### Process rates in the precipitating fraction

`p3_fractional_process_rates` follows the P3 code in E3SM/SCREAM: cloud
and rain are maximally overlapped inside the cell, giving four regions —
cloud ∩ rain (area ``\min(f_c, f_p)``), cloud only, rain only, clear.
Each region is given in-cloud ``q_c/f_c, N_c/f_c``, in-rain
``q_r/f_p, N_r/f_p``, and vapour

```math
q_{v,\mathrm{cld}} = \min(q_s, q_v/f_c), \qquad
q_{v,\mathrm{clr}} = \frac{q_v - f_c\, q_{v,\mathrm{cld}}}{1 - f_c},
```

(Tiedtke 1993; grid-mean vapour kept) with the ice uniform, and the cell
rate is the area-weighted sum of `p3_process_rates` of the regions. The
tendency map, and so water conservation, is unchanged. Nonlinear rates
change by the factors their exponents imply: KK2000 accretion
``67 (q_c q_r)^{1.15}`` by ``a\,(f_c f_p)^{-1.15}`` (×1.2311 for rain
on a quarter of a cloudy cell), autoconversion
``\propto q_c^{2.47} N_c^{-1.79}`` by ``f_c^{0.32}`` (×0.6413 at 25 %
cover; the 0.06 % from ``0.25^{0.32}`` is the air density of the cloudy
region, whose condensate P3's ``\rho_a`` counts). Rain under cloud does
not evaporate — the cloudy air is saturated — while rain in the clear
part evaporates at the clear-sky deficit. Rates linear in ``(q_r, N_r)``
at fixed ``\lambda_r`` (evaporation, rain riming) are unchanged by
``f_p`` up to that same density effect (6×10⁻⁵).

The option is off by default: `p3_step(s, dt)` is bit-identical to the
grid-mean step; `p3_step(s, dt; cloud_fraction, precip_fraction)` and
`p3_step_column` switch it on. Sedimentation needs no fraction, since
the fall speeds depend only on ``q_r/N_r``. The saturation adjustment of
step 5 remains the grid-mean, all-or-nothing one.
